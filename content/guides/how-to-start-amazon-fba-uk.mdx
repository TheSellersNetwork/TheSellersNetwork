This is a long one, on purpose. It is everything we would sit down and tell someone who asked "how do I actually start selling on Amazon?" Bookmark it and work through it a section at a time. You do not need it all on day one, but you will need all of it by month three.

It is written for the UK. Amazon's rules, HMRC's rules and the numbers all change, so where a figure matters we link to the official source and you should check it before you rely on it. Nothing here is financial, tax or legal advice. It is what works, from people who do it.

## The short version

1. Decide how you will trade (sole trader or limited company), register with HMRC, and open a separate bank account for the business.
2. Open a Professional seller account on Amazon and get through identity verification.
3. Sort three account settings before you sell anything: overseas shipping off, international listings off, and apply to the dangerous goods programme.
4. Learn to source with online arbitrage: find products where a retailer's price leaves a real profit after every Amazon fee, using price history and a fee calculator.
5. Build a first box of stock, prep it, and send it in as one shipment.
6. Keep your account healthy, keep every invoice, watch your cash, and reinvest.

Now the detail.

## Part 1: How FBA works, and which way in to choose

### What Amazon does and what you do

FBA is Fulfilment by Amazon. You buy stock and send it in bulk to Amazon's warehouses. From then on Amazon stores it, and when a customer orders, Amazon picks it, packs it, posts it, handles the customer service and handles the return. You never post anything to a customer yourself.

That leaves you with two jobs: finding stock that will sell at a profit, and getting it into Amazon in a condition they will accept. Everything else in this guide is about doing those two jobs without tripping over Amazon's rules or running out of money.

A sale, end to end: you find a product, buy it, it arrives at your home (or a prep centre), you label and box it, you create a shipment in Seller Central and send it in, Amazon checks it in and your offer goes live on the existing listing, a customer buys, Amazon does the rest, and about two weeks later the money reaches your bank.

### The four ways to get stock

**Online arbitrage** is buying from UK retailers' websites when a promotion, clearance or voucher puts their price under what the same item sells for on Amazon, then selling it on Amazon. It is where nearly everyone should start, for one reason above all others: if you get a buy wrong, you can usually return the stock to the retailer. No other method gives you that safety net while you are learning. You can do it from a laptop in the evening, the invoices are proper invoices, and you have the whole Amazon catalogue to work with.

**Retail arbitrage** is the same idea in physical shops, scanning products on the shelf with your phone. It is a cheap way to learn how listings behave and it still works, but it is harder than it used to be, it does not scale (you are driving, queuing and carrying), and till receipts are weak evidence if Amazon ever asks you to prove where a product came from. Treat it as a way in, not a business.

**Wholesale** is buying in bulk from brands or distributors. It sounds professional and it can work, but distributors want minimum orders, trade references, VAT registration and a trading history you do not have yet, the listings you can get onto are full of sellers with the same supplier, and one large order in the wrong product ties up cash for months. Come back to it after a year.

**Private label** is making your own branded product. Large minimum orders, packaging, photography, trademarks and an advertising budget before you have sold a unit, all while starting a listing from zero reviews against people who have thousands. Some people do very well at it. Almost nobody does well at it first.

A fifth, once you are running: buying on one Amazon marketplace and selling on another where the same product is priced higher. Worth knowing about, not a starting point.

**Start with online arbitrage.** Learn how Amazon behaves with stock you can send back.

## Part 2: Setting up so nothing bites you later

### Sole trader or limited company

You can sell on Amazon as either. Which is right depends on how much you expect to make, your other income and your appetite for admin, and it is worth an hour with an accountant before you decide. The short comparison:

- **Sole trader**: register with HMRC for Self Assessment, keep records, file a return. Simplest and cheapest. Your business and personal money are legally the same, which is why a separate bank account still matters.
- **Limited company**: register at Companies House (£100 online since February 2026, [Companies House fees](https://changestoukcompanylaw.campaign.gov.uk/changes-to-companies-house-fees/)), a separate legal entity, annual accounts and a confirmation statement, and Amazon, banks and suppliers will want your company number and registered address to match everywhere. More admin, but cleaner as you grow, and some wholesale and credit options only open to companies.

Whichever you choose, the details you give Amazon, your bank and your suppliers must be identical: same name, same address. Mismatches are the root cause of most failed ungating applications and most lost appeals later.

If you register a company, the SIC code that describes this business is 47910, retail sale via mail order houses or via the internet.

### Tell HMRC

If you are a sole trader and your sales in a tax year go over £1,000, you must register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the end of that tax year. Under £1,000 you may be covered by the trading allowance, but read [HMRC's own guidance on buying or making things to sell](https://taxhelpforhustles.campaign.gov.uk/im-buying-or-making-things-to-sell/) rather than assuming.

Two things newer guides miss:

- **Amazon reports you to HMRC.** Since January 2024, marketplaces must send HMRC the details of any seller who passes 30 sales or about £1,700 in a calendar year, and send you a copy. Being reported is not the same as owing tax, but it means the "nobody will notice" approach was never real. Source: [HMRC platform reporting rules](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-tell-hmrc-about-your-income-from-online-platforms).
- **Making Tax Digital** for Income Tax applies from April 2026 to sole traders with qualifying income over £50,000, which means quarterly digital updates. If you are on track for that, get software early.

### A separate bank account

Open a business account before you touch Amazon. It does not need to be a high street bank; the app based business banks are quick to open and connect to accounting software. The point is separation: every purchase of stock, every Amazon payout and every subscription in one place, so your bookkeeping is possible and an HMRC query is answerable. Mixing personal and business money is the most common bookkeeping mistake there is.

When you add a payment card to Amazon, use a proper debit card that works online. Prepaid cards get rejected.

### How much money you need

There is no magic number, so here is how to work it out rather than a figure to take on trust.

Your fixed costs run before you sell anything: the Professional plan at £25 plus VAT a month, a price history tool, a sourcing and fee calculator, and later perhaps a repricer and profit tracker. Call it £60 to £120 a month depending on what you sign up for. Everything else is stock, and stock is the only thing that makes money.

The reason people quote a few thousand pounds as a sensible starting pot is that with less, the fixed costs eat most of it, you can only afford one or two products, and one bad buy stalls you. With more, you can spread across several products, learn faster and keep buying while you wait for payouts. You do not spend it all at once: buy small quantities, sell them, and put the money back in.

Whatever the figure, it should be money you will not need back for six months. Amazon pays out on a delay, and your first two months usually show a small loss on paper because the subscriptions start before the sales do.

### Do you need an accountant on day one?

Not necessarily. Plenty of sellers do their own records at the start and bring an accountant in as they approach the VAT threshold. It works if, and only if, you keep every invoice from day one and a simple "buy sheet" listing every purchase, so it can all be reconciled later. If you would rather not think about it, an accountant who knows Amazon is worth the monthly cost from the start. Either way, keep the invoices.

## Part 3: Your Amazon account

### The plan

The Professional plan is £25 a month plus VAT. There is an Individual plan at £0.75 per item sold with no monthly fee, which is cheaper only if you sell fewer than about 34 items a month and it lacks features you will want. Take the Professional plan. Both plans also pay a referral fee on every sale, mostly between 8% and 15% of the price depending on the category, with a minimum of £0.25 per item, plus FBA fees. All current figures are on [Amazon's pricing page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/pricing).

### Verification

Amazon will ask for a government photo ID (passport or driving licence) and proof of address such as a bank statement or utility bill dated within the last 90 days, and may ask you to join a short video call to hold your documents up to the camera. It is a check that you are real, not an interview. Have the documents ready as clear scans before you start, and make sure the name and address on them match what you typed in. Amazon's own guide is at [sell.amazon.co.uk/verification-compliance](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/verification-compliance).

Use an email address you control and will keep forever for the Seller Central login, and turn on two step verification. Do not use that login for buying stock (more on that below).

### Deposit and charge methods

Your deposit method is the bank account Amazon pays you into. Your charge method is the card Amazon takes fees from. Both are set under the cog icon, Account Info, Payment Information. Enter bank details carefully (a wrong digit delays your first payout), expect a small test transaction on the card, and make sure the card is assigned to the UK marketplace. If the charge method is not assigned, Amazon can block selling.

### Three settings to change before you list anything

**Turn off overseas shipping.** Under Fulfilment by Amazon settings, shipping programmes and export settings, disable every country other than the UK. Left on, Amazon can sell your stock into Europe with different fees, VAT consequences and returns you did not plan for, and new sellers regularly discover this by selling at a loss.

**Make sure Build International Listings is off.** It copies your offers to other marketplaces automatically. That can mean intellectual property complaints and tax questions in countries you never intended to sell in. Check it at Seller Central under listing preferences.

**Apply to the dangerous goods programme.** Aerosols, perfumes, nail polish, anything with a lithium battery and a surprising number of ordinary products get flagged as hazardous. Approval can take a couple of weeks, so apply early even if you are not selling those products yet.

### Learn the dashboard

The home screen tells you most of what you need each day: sales today, open orders (for FBA, the "pending" count), buyer messages (Amazon expects a reply within 24 hours, weekends included), your featured offer percentage (how often you hold the Buy Box), seller feedback, your balance, and your Inventory Performance Index. Check it daily. It takes a minute and it is where problems show first.

## Part 4: Sourcing your first products

This is the part that makes or loses the money, and it is the skill you are really building.

### What you are actually looking for

A product where, after every cost comes off, there is profit left and the product genuinely sells. "Every cost" means the referral fee, the FBA fulfilment fee, storage, any prep cost, the price you paid including delivery, and VAT if you are registered. The gap between what you paid and the Amazon price means nothing on its own.

Two tools do the work. A **price history tool** shows a product's price over time, its sales rank over time and how many sellers are on the listing. Once you can read that chart you can tell whether a product really sells or just has a high price, whether the price is stable or about to collapse, and whether the competition is growing. A **fee and profit calculator** takes the buy price and tells you the net profit and return after Amazon's fees. Amazon's own [FBA calculator](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/pricing) is free; the paid tools are faster.

Early on, work a few products through the fee calculation by hand. After twenty you will be able to look at a listing and know roughly where it lands, and that instinct is what separates people who last from people who do not.

### Reading the signs

- **Sales rank** is relative to the category. Learn what "sells a few a day" looks like in the categories you work, rather than memorising a number.
- **Seller count** matters more than most beginners think. Ten sellers on a listing that sells three a day means each of you waits.
- **Price history** with sudden drops usually means someone dumped stock. Buy on the assumption it happens again.
- **Amazon itself as a seller** on the listing is hard to compete with on price and for the Buy Box.
- **Return rate and reviews** tell you whether the product disappoints people, which becomes your problem when they return it.

### The buy is where the profit is decided

By the time you have bought a product, your profit is largely set. The selling side mostly takes care of itself if the buy was right, and nothing you do later fixes a bad buy. So put your effort there: check the listing is the exact product (variant, size, pack count), check you are allowed to sell the brand before you pay, and check the numbers with delivery included.

The best buys are **replenishable**: products you have sold before and can buy again when the price comes round. Keep a list. Sourcing stops being a blind search and becomes "what on my list is buyable today?"

A return of 20% on your money in your first month is a good result. You made a profit selling on Amazon. Build from there rather than chasing the numbers you see on social media.

### Check restrictions before you buy

Some categories and many brands are "gated": Amazon will not let a new account sell them without approval. Check the listing in Seller Central before you order. Applying for approval usually means a recent invoice (typically within the last 180 days) from a legitimate supplier for a minimum quantity, with your business name and address exactly matching your account. Some brands now ask for larger quantities than they used to, and Amazon increasingly verifies invoices with the supplier. New accounts get refused a lot, often by automated checks. That is normal. Get the paperwork right, apply again, and ask for a human review. It gets easier after a few months of clean selling.

### Buy with the right account

Do not use your Seller Central login to buy stock, from Amazon or anywhere else. If a buying account is ever restricted, you do not want it taking your selling access with it. Set up a separate buying account with a different email and phone number.

## Part 5: Getting stock into Amazon

### Wait until you have a box

Sending three items the day they arrive wastes the courier cost and repeats the admin. Source for a couple of weeks, build a full box, check the numbers again, then send one shipment. You learn the process properly once instead of badly ten times.

### Adding a product

As an arbitrage seller you are not creating listings. You attach your offer to the existing listing (the ASIN) and state your condition, price and fulfilment method. Enter the ASIN, choose New, choose FBA, set your price, answer the battery and dangerous goods questions honestly, and use Amazon's barcodes. Wait a few minutes for it to appear in your inventory before creating a shipment.

### Creating a shipment

Select the products, click Send or Replenish Inventory, confirm the address you are shipping from, enter quantities and any expiry dates, print your SKU labels, tell Amazon how the units are packed and how many boxes, confirm, choose a partnered carrier, print the box labels and attach them. Boxes over 15kg need a "heavy" sticker on each side. Hazardous products go by a separate process with a non partnered carrier.

Schedule a collection rather than driving to a drop off point once you are sending regularly. Enter the tracking numbers exactly as Amazon gives them.

### The kit you need

You do not need a warehouse. You need a thermal label printer (no ink, prints fast, two if you want one for 4 by 6 shipping labels and one for small SKU labels), SKU labels, strong double wall boxes, poly bags in two sizes, packing tape and a tape gun. A barcode scanner and a document scanner are nice later, not needed now. If you already own an ordinary printer, A4 label sheets get you started before you buy a thermal printer.

Members share what they use, with prices, on the [Setups](/kits) page.

### Prep centre or do it yourself

If you have time, space and a tight budget, prep yourself. It keeps money in stock and you learn exactly what Amazon expects. If you have money but no time or space, a prep centre receives, labels and ships for you at a per unit cost. Two catches: it is a cost that comes straight off your margin while you are small, and some retailers will only deliver to the billing address on your account, so a prep centre address can shut some sourcing off. Most people start at home and move to a prep centre when volume justifies it.

### Storage, capacity and fees

Storage is charged per cubic foot per month and rises sharply from October to December. Stock that sits over a year attracts surcharges. New accounts generally get a grace period before Amazon limits how much you can hold, after which limits depend on your Inventory Performance Index. Amazon adjusted fulfilment fees again in 2026, including a lower fee tier for items priced £20 and under and surcharges on fulfilment fees, so always price a product off the current [rate card](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/pricing), not last year's blog post.

## Part 6: The day to day

### Buyer messages

Answer within 24 hours, every day of the year. Most messages are simple questions. If a buyer wants a refund outside Amazon's returns process or is being unreasonable, mark it resolved and let Amazon's process handle it. Keep replies short, polite and factual.

### Feedback

Seller feedback (about you) is different from product reviews (about the listing) and it feeds the Buy Box. Ask for it politely and within Amazon's rules, never with incentives. Feedback about delivery or an Amazon handled return can be struck out: open Feedback Manager, choose the review, and request removal on the grounds it relates to fulfilment Amazon controlled.

### Invoices, for your protection

Your purchase invoices are your proof that stock is genuine and legitimately bought. They have to show your business name and address exactly as Amazon and HMRC know them. Use your billing address on every order, never the prep centre's. A wrong or missing name can fail an ungating application, sink an appeal and cause trouble with a VAT return.

Business customers on Amazon can require an invoice from you within 48 hours of payment settling. Watch for those orders. Once you are VAT registered and have added your VAT number, Amazon can generate them for you.

### Account health

Amazon scores you on customer service (order defect rate, which they want under 1%), policy compliance, and delivery (which is Amazon's problem when you use FBA). The part that ends arbitrage sellers is policy compliance: intellectual property complaints and authenticity complaints. You can buy a genuine product from a real shop and still get one, because brands protect distribution as well as authenticity. Your defence is paperwork: proper invoices, matching details, reputable suppliers. When a complaint lands, stop selling the item, gather the invoice, respond clearly, and keep pushing for a human review if the first answer is a template. Do not open a second account to get round anything. Amazon links accounts well and it is the fastest route to a permanent ban.

### Insurance

Amazon's UK terms require product liability insurance once your gross sales exceed £4,000 a month for three months in a row: a minimum of £400,000 of cover, naming Amazon as an additional insured. Amazon can ask to see the certificate at any time. Get a quote before you cross the line, not after the email arrives.

### Winning the Buy Box

Most sales go through the Buy Box (Amazon now calls it the featured offer). It goes to sellers who are competitively priced, use FBA, have good feedback, keep stock available and have a clean account. You do not have to be cheapest, you have to be in range and trusted. Being early on a listing helps. Consistency wins it over time.

### Pricing

With fewer than about ten products, check and adjust prices by hand once a day. Beyond that, a repricer (software that moves your price within limits you set) earns its monthly fee. Set a floor price for every product so a race to the bottom cannot take you below profit.

### Stranded and unfulfillable stock

Stranded stock is in the warehouse but not for sale because of a listing problem: fix it or remove it, and keep the number near zero. Unfulfillable stock is damaged or returned in unsellable condition: create a removal order to have it sent back or disposed of. Either way, it is your money sitting on a shelf.

### Getting paid

A sale does not become cash immediately. The customer's payment clears, the money sits in deferred transactions for roughly a week to cover returns, then it is included in your next payout, then your bank takes a few days. Sale to bank is often about two weeks. Withdraw available funds every day: money inside Amazon cannot buy stock, and if your account is ever reviewed it can be held.

### A second login

Add a second user to your Seller Central account (a real, trusted person with their own email and phone, verified through Amazon). It does not save you from a suspension, but if your main login is ever lost or locked, it is your way back in. Test it works.

## Part 7: Money and cash flow

### Cash flow is the game

You pay for stock now and get paid weeks later. That gap is where the pressure is. You can be profitable on paper and unable to buy anything, because the profit is sitting in a warehouse or in Amazon's payout queue. So: buy things that sell within weeks, not months; keep subscriptions to the ones you use; withdraw daily; and plan purchases around the payout cycle.

### Net profit is the only number

The big figure in a profit tracker is gross. Net is what is left after referral fees, FBA fees, delivery, prep, software, advertising and everything else. Log every expense every month. A small loss in the first month or two is normal; the aim is for profit to pass overheads by month three, and after that the surplus goes back into stock.

### Credit

Business credit cards with a payment date that lands after Amazon's payout can let you buy the next round of stock while waiting for the last one to pay. That is genuinely useful once you understand your cycle, and dangerous before you do. Stay well away from high interest loans, including those offered inside Amazon: arbitrage margins do not cover the interest. Nothing here is financial advice.

### Cashback

If you buy stock online, a cashback site adds a small percentage to your margin for no effort. Record it; cashback paid to a business is income.

### VAT

If your business is established in the UK, you must register for VAT once your taxable turnover passes £90,000 in any rolling 12 month period, and you have 30 days to do it. Turnover means sales, not profit. Most sellers wait until they are close rather than registering early, because registration changes your pricing and adds quarterly returns, but start preparing around £80,000: accounting software that pulls your Amazon data, an accountant, and every invoice to hand, because you can reclaim VAT on stock bought in the months before registration if the invoices are right. Source: [GOV.UK VAT registration](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat).

Two corrections to things you will read elsewhere. Using FBA does not by itself force a UK business to register early; the threshold applies. It is businesses established outside the UK that must register from their first UK sale. And once you are registered, update your profit calculator to standard VAT so your margins are real.

## Part 8: What six months looks like, and the mistakes to avoid

Progress is slow at the start and compounds later. Month one is setup, learning, rejections from ungating and a small loss. By month three the loop is familiar and profit should be covering costs. By month six, if every pound went back into stock, you have more products, more approved brands, cash flow you understand and systems that run without you thinking. First year for learning, second year for earning is a fair summary.

The mistakes that end most beginners, in order of how often we see them:

1. **Starting with private label or wholesale** before understanding how Amazon behaves.
2. **Confusing gross with net.** The dashboard number is not your profit.
3. **Overbuying one product** and strangling cash flow.
4. **Sloppy invoices.** Wrong name, wrong address, no paperwork, then losing an appeal on a genuine product.
5. **Ignoring account health** until a suspension.
6. **A second account** to get round a problem.
7. **Taking money out too early** instead of reinvesting.
8. **Leaving overseas settings on** and selling into Europe at a loss.
9. **Not telling HMRC**, when Amazon already has.

## Questions people ask

**Can I do this alongside a job?** Yes, and it is the sensible way. Source in the evenings, prep at the weekend, keep the pressure off while you learn.

**Sole trader or company?** Either works to start. Ask an accountant which suits your income and plans, then keep the details identical everywhere.

**How long until it makes money?** Expect a small loss for a month or two and a business that covers its costs by month three if you are consistent. Anyone promising faster is selling something.

**Is it still worth it?** Yes, as a business with real work in it. No, as a way to get rich by Christmas.

**What if I get refused for a brand?** Check your paperwork matches, apply again, ask for a human review. Refusals are the normal path, not the end of it.

**Where do I ask when I am stuck?** The [Amazon forums](/community/c/amazon) here, and specifically [FBA and FBM](/community/c/amazon-fba-and-fbm). Post what you tried, what happened and the numbers.
