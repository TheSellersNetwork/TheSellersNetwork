For sellers who already have stock in FBA and sell on eBay or their own website too, and want to know whether Amazon can post those orders for them. After reading you will know what Multi-Channel Fulfilment (MCF) costs, how to work out whether it beats posting yourself, how to raise an order, and the rules on other platforms that catch people out.

Every fee here comes from Amazon's own rate card and pages, linked where used. MCF fees change at least once a year, so check the rate card before you price on it. Nothing here is financial advice.

## What MCF is

MCF is FBA for orders that did not come from Amazon. Your stock sits in Amazon's warehouses as normal. When it sells somewhere else, you tell Amazon the buyer's address and the speed, and Amazon picks, packs and posts it. Amazon's [MCF page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/fulfilment-by-amazon/fba-multi-channel) describes it as using your FBA inventory to fulfil orders from other sales channels, including your own site and marketplaces such as eBay.

Three things follow from that:

- **It is one pool of stock.** Amazon's [MCF FAQ](https://supplychain.amazon.co.uk/support) says your FBA inventory serves both Amazon customers and customers from your other channels. There is no separate MCF stock.
- **You are still the seller.** The same FAQ says you are responsible for customer service on MCF orders. The eBay buyer's complaint comes to you, not Amazon.
- **Amazon picks the carrier.** Its [tracking page](https://supplychain.amazon.co.uk/blog/how-to-track-your-mcf-orders) says MCF uses a variety of carriers, including Amazon's own delivery network, Amazon Logistics.

## What it costs

### The fulfilment fee

Amazon publishes a separate [UK MCF rate card](https://supplychain.amazon.co.uk/docs/rate-card) (a PDF, dated 29 May 2026 at the time of writing). The fee is per unit, covers pick, pack and postage, and depends on three things: the size tier, the unit weight, and how many units are in the order. There are two speeds, Standard and Expedited.

A selection of lines, for a one unit order:

| Size tier (max dimensions) | Unit weight | Standard | Expedited |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Light envelope (33 x 23 x 2.5cm) | up to 100g | £3.40 to £3.43 | £3.72 to £3.82 |
| Standard envelope (33 x 23 x 2.5cm) | up to 460g | £3.43 to £3.45 | £3.82 to £3.98 |
| Large envelope (33 x 23 x 4cm) | up to 960g | £3.75 | £4.32 |
| Small parcel (35 x 25 x 12cm) | up to 400g | £4.57 | £5.26 |
| Small parcel | up to 1.4kg | £5.98 | £6.88 |
| Standard parcel (45 x 34 x 26cm) | up to 900g | £5.61 | £6.40 |
| Standard parcel | up to 1.9kg | £7.25 | £8.38 |

The rate card has further columns for orders of two units and three or more, where the per unit fee falls. A small parcel up to 400g drops from £4.57 for one unit to £3.41 each for two and £2.52 each for three or more.

### The surcharge on top

The same rate card says a 1.5% fuel and logistics surcharge is applied to UK MCF fulfilment fees. It is listed separately from the fee tables, so add it to every figure above. Our [breakdown of the surcharge](/blog/amazon-fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge) covers when it started and what Amazon has said about how long it will last.

### Storage and everything else

MCF stock is FBA stock, so it pays normal storage and, if it sits too long, the aged inventory surcharge, both covered in the [inventory health guide](/guides/amazon-inventory-health). Optional per unit services such as returns handling and labelling are priced on the rate card. We could not find a festive peak fee for MCF on the UK rate card; check the current version before the season.

### Delivery speeds

Amazon's pages do not quite agree. The [MCF pricing page](https://supplychain.amazon.co.uk/pricing) gives Standard as 2 to 3 calendar days and Expedited as 1, from order to delivery; the [FAQ](https://supplychain.amazon.co.uk/support) says 3 and 1 to 2. Treat them as targets and promise buyers the slower figure.

## Packaging and carriers

Amazon's [unbranded packaging page](https://supplychain.amazon.co.uk/blog/unbranded-packaging-for-amazon-mcf-what-it-is-and-how-it-works) says MCF orders are shipped in unbranded packaging at no extra fee: boxes, paper bags, tape and labels without Amazon branding. There is a limit. In the UK, items that are not sortable and are larger than 45.72 x 35.56 x 20.32cm or heavier than 9kg are not covered by the unbranded packaging programme.

When you create an order you can tick an option to ship only in blank boxes, which Amazon's [Seller Central guide for MCF](https://supplychain.amazon.co.uk/learn/seller-central-guide) describes. With it ticked, an order that cannot go in plain packaging is not fulfilled rather than going out in an Amazon box.

**What we would do.** Tick blank boxes only on every order. An Amazon box on an eBay order makes the buyer think you bought it on Amazon and sent it on, and some will say so in feedback.

Carriers are Amazon's choice. Amazon's [integration guide for one web shop platform](https://supplychain.amazon.co.uk/integrations/shopify/userguide) describes blocking Amazon Logistics for US orders at an extra charge. We could not find that option or its price on a UK Amazon page, so check the Create MCF Order page in your account before assuming it exists.

## Using MCF for eBay orders

### Is it allowed

eBay's [dropshipping policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/posting-items/setting-postage-options/drop-shipping?id=4176) bans listing an item and then buying it from another retailer or marketplace that ships it straight to your buyer. MCF is different: you own the stock and Amazon acts as your warehouse and courier. The same policy says you remain responsible for safe delivery within the time in your listing and for the buyer's satisfaction.

eBay's published policy does not mention fulfilment services, Amazon Logistics or Amazon packaging either way. The practical risk is perception: an Amazon box can look like retail arbitrage to a buyer who then reports you. Blank boxes remove most of that.

### Handling time

eBay defines [handling time](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/posting-items/estimated-delivery-dates-sellers?id=4086) as the time it takes you to hand the item to the carrier after the buyer pays, and recommends no more than one working day. On a one day handling time the parcel must be dispatched and scanned as received by the carrier by the end of the next working day.

With MCF, handling time is the time you take to create the MCF order plus the time Amazon takes to hand it to a carrier. If orders go to Amazon automatically, one working day is usually realistic. If you create them by hand once a day, set two.

### Tracking and late delivery

eBay's [seller performance policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-performance-policy?id=4347) counts a tracked delivery as late only when the delivery scan is after the estimated delivery date and there is no carrier scan within your dispatch time. For Top Rated status, items over £20 must be sent on tracked services with valid tracking uploaded within the dispatch time.

Amazon's [tracking page](https://supplychain.amazon.co.uk/blog/how-to-track-your-mcf-orders) says the MCF order details page shows the tracking number and the carrier. Copy both into eBay with Add tracking number, as eBay's [tracking help](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/posting-items/setting-postage-options?id=4088) describes. A number entered against the wrong carrier does not track, and then it does not protect you on an [item not received claim](/guides/item-not-received-and-cases).

In your eBay listing, choose a delivery service whose speed matches the MCF speed you will actually use. Promising next day on eBay and raising a Standard MCF order is a late delivery waiting to happen.

## Own website orders

This is where MCF fits best: no marketplace rules about carriers or packaging. Amazon's [MCF page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/fulfilment-by-amazon/fba-multi-channel) says it has more than 100 prebuilt integrations.

Integrations of this kind typically do three jobs:

1. Send each new website order to Amazon as an MCF order automatically, with a speed mapped from the delivery option the customer chose.
2. Pull the tracking number back and mark the order dispatched on your site.
3. Push Amazon's stock level to your site so it does not sell what you do not have.

Amazon's own app for one large web shop platform does all three, per its [user guide](https://supplychain.amazon.co.uk/integrations/shopify/userguide), with returns handled on the shop side. Before choosing any integration, check which channels it covers, whether it sets blank boxes on every order, and what it does when Amazon rejects an order.

## Vinted and similar apps

Not a fit. Vinted's [shipping help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/482-shipping-an-item) tells sellers to use the shipping label issued for the order, and says the carrier, label type, address and parcel size cannot be changed after the sale. MCF uses Amazon's carrier and label, so it cannot meet that. Post Vinted orders yourself.

## How to create an order by hand

The [Seller Central guide for MCF](https://supplychain.amazon.co.uk/learn/seller-central-guide) sets out the routes:

- **One order:** Orders, Create MCF Order. Or from Manage Inventory, select the product and choose Create Fulfilment Order.
- **Enter:** the customer's delivery address, the items and quantities, and the shipping speed. Tick blank boxes only.
- **Many orders:** on the MCF Order Creation page, choose Create bulk orders, download the template, fill it in and upload it.

Amazon's [FAQ](https://supplychain.amazon.co.uk/support) says you can cancel an MCF order until the shipping label is generated. Put the eBay order number in the reference field if the form offers one, so you can find the MCF order when the buyer messages.

## Does it beat posting yourself

### The method

Compare like with like, per order:

1. **MCF cost:** the rate card fee for the size tier, weight and speed, plus 1.5%.
2. **Your cost:** the Royal Mail price for the same speed and tracking, plus the packaging, plus what your time is worth to pack and drop it.
3. **The hidden cost of your route:** if the stock is already in FBA, getting it out to post yourself means a removal order, a removal fee per unit and a wait. The [inventory health guide](/guides/amazon-inventory-health) links the removal fee page.

Size your parcel with the [parcel size checker](/tools/parcel-size) first.

### Worked examples

Royal Mail prices below are the online prices from 5 October 2026 in Royal Mail's [online price guide](https://www.royalmail.com/sites/royalmail.com/files/2026-09/online-price-guide-october-2026-v1-ta.pdf) (see our [October breakdown](/blog/royal-mail-prices-october-2026)). MCF figures are one unit orders from the [rate card](https://supplychain.amazon.co.uk/docs/rate-card) with the 1.5% surcharge added and rounded.

| Item | MCF Standard | Royal Mail Tracked 48 | MCF Expedited | Royal Mail Tracked 24 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 80g, flat, fits a large letter | about £3.48 (light envelope) | £2.90 (large letter) | about £3.88 | £3.90 (large letter) |
| 350g boxed item, small parcel | about £4.64 | £3.75 | about £5.34 | £4.80 |
| 1.2kg item, small parcel | about £6.07 | £3.75 | about £6.98 | £4.80 |

On postage alone, Royal Mail wins at almost every size for a single tracked parcel you pack yourself. MCF closes the gap once you count the box, the tape, the label, the trip to the post office and the time it takes you to pack each order, and it wins outright on stock that is already inside FBA, where posting it yourself means paying to get it out first.

### When MCF is the right call

- The stock is already in FBA and sells on eBay or your site as well as on Amazon.
- Small, light items where the MCF premium over Royal Mail is under a pound and your time is worth more.
- You are away, ill, or at capacity. MCF keeps other channels open when you cannot pack.
- Your own website, where there are no platform rules on carriers or packaging.

### When to post it yourself

- Heavier small parcels, where the gap is over £2 an order.
- Anything you hold at home anyway. Sending it into FBA just to post it out again through MCF pays twice.
- Vinted and other apps with their own labels.
- Stock that is slow on every channel, where storage fees will eat the margin before it sells.

For Amazon orders, see [when FBM makes sense](/guides/amazon-fbm-when-it-makes-sense). For Royal Mail services, see [postage and Royal Mail choices](/guides/postage-and-royal-mail-choices).

## Stock shared with Amazon

One pool means an eBay sale and an Amazon sale can hit the same last unit within minutes of each other. If the Amazon order takes the unit first, your MCF order has nothing to send. You cancel the eBay order, and eBay counts a cancellation for being out of stock against you.

The fixes, in order of effort:

- **Hold a buffer.** List on eBay a quantity one or two below what Amazon holds. The last units sell on Amazon only.
- **Sync the stock.** An integration or inventory tool that reads Amazon's stock and updates eBay and your site. Check how often it syncs; every few hours is not good enough for fast lines.
- **Single units on one channel.** A one off item goes on the channel where it sells best, not on both.

The [multi-channel selling guide](/guides/multi-channel-selling) covers stock control in more depth.

## Returns

MCF returns are yours to organise. Amazon's [Seller Central guide](https://supplychain.amazon.co.uk/learn/seller-central-guide) explains creating a customer return from the MCF order details page, which produces a return authorisation and mailing label with no postage on it; you or the customer pay to send it back to Amazon. The [FAQ](https://supplychain.amazon.co.uk/support) says returns can go to an Amazon warehouse or to you.

**What we would do.** Let eBay returns come to you through eBay's own process. You can inspect the item, then relist it or send it back into FBA with your next shipment. Sending it to Amazon adds a second returns process and you lose sight of its condition. The [returns guide](/guides/returns-without-losing-money) covers the eBay side.

## What goes wrong

- **Amazon box on an eBay order.** Blank boxes only was not ticked, or the item was too big for plain packaging. Tick it every time.
- **Tracking entered against the wrong carrier.** It does not validate, and the late delivery protection goes with it. Copy the carrier from the MCF order details.
- **One day handling time with manual orders.** A weekend sale sits in your inbox until Monday. Set two days, or automate.
- **The last unit sells twice.** One pool of stock, two channels. Hold a buffer or sync.
- **Sending stock in just to post it out.** Inbound shipping, storage and MCF fees on something you could have posted from home.
- **Forgetting you own the customer service.** Amazon does not answer your eBay buyer. Watch MCF orders that have not moved for two days and message the buyer first.
