This is for UK eBay sellers of higher-value branded goods and collectables who have seen the blue tick on their listing, or expect to. After reading it you will know which items are caught by Authenticity Guarantee, what happens between the sale and the buyer opening the parcel, how long it takes and when you get paid, and what to do so the item passes. The thresholds below come from eBay's help pages and change from time to time, so check the current page for your category before you list.

## How it works in one paragraph

If an item is eligible, eBay adds it to the programme automatically, free of charge to you, and there is [no opt in or opt out](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/selling-tools/selling-authenticity-guarantee?id=4644). When it sells, you do not post to the buyer. You post to an authenticator at the address eBay gives you in the order details. They check the item matches your listing, inspect it, and if it passes, send it on to the buyer with tracked delivery and an authentication card or tag. If it fails, the buyer is refunded and the item comes back to you. Returns go back through the authenticator too.

## Which items are included in the UK

From eBay's [Selling with Authenticity Guarantee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/selling-tools/selling-authenticity-guarantee?id=4644) page. In every category the item must be sold on eBay.co.uk to a buyer with a UK delivery address, and listings offering only local collection, or going to a PO Box or BFPO address, are not eligible.

| Category | Mandatory authentication | Optional, buyer pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Watches (Wristwatches and Pocket Watches) | Sold for £1,500 or more | Buy It Now £500 to £1,499.99, buyer pays £99 |
| Trainers (eligible brands; men's, women's and kids' categories) | Sold for £200 or more, postage excluded | Buy It Now up to £199.99, buyer pays £20 |
| Fashion (eligible brands and categories) | Sold for £200 or more | Buy It Now up to £199.99, buyer pays £20 |
| Handbags (eligible brands; Women's Bags & Handbags, Men's Accessories > Bags) | Sold for £200 or more | Buy It Now up to £199.99, buyer pays £20 |
| Trading cards (CCG, sports and non-sports singles) | Buy It Now or auction sale of £250 or more | None listed |
| Jewellery (Cartier, Tiffany & Co, Van Cleef & Arpels, Bvlgari, Chopard, Chanel, or unbranded) | Sold for £500 or more | None listed |

The brand lists for trainers, fashion and handbags are linked from that page. Some in-demand trainer models can qualify below £200.

### The Best Offer and auction rules

These catch sellers out:

- **Best Offer below the threshold still counts.** If your Buy It Now price is at or above the threshold and you accept a lower offer, the item is still authenticated. This applies to watches, trainers, fashion, handbags, cards and jewellery.
- **Auctions count on the final price.** For watches, an auction that ends under £1,500 may not be eligible. For cards, a Buy It Now of £250 or more stays eligible even if it sells for less by offer or auction.
- **For optional watch authentication, the Buy It Now price includes the Buyer Protection fee** for private sellers, so a watch listed at £480 may qualify for the £500 optional tier.

### What is excluded

- **Watches:** smartwatches, watch parts and accessories, and anything described as customised (non-original parts, modified dials or bezels, added stones). Vintage watches with commonly replaced parts such as a strap, crystal or spring bars are still eligible.
- **Trainers and fashion:** customised items are not authenticated. Replacement laces noted in the listing are checked against the listing, not authenticated.
- **Trading cards:** graded cards from graders other than Beckett, PSA, Certified Collectibles Group and SGC. Autographs, patches and relics added after manufacture are not verified themselves.
- **Jewellery:** Antique & Vintage, Loose Diamonds & Gemstones, Jewellery Care, Mixed Lots and Loose Beads categories. Items must contain a fine metal (sterling silver 925+, gold 9K+, palladium 500+ or platinum 850+), carry a valid hallmark where UK hallmarking law requires it, and main stones must be from eBay's list. The page has the full stone lists.

If you sell near a threshold, look at the listing after it goes live: eligible listings show the blue tick badge in search, on the listing, at checkout and in the buyer's purchase history.

## What happens after it sells

| Step | What happens | Timing from eBay's pages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | You get the authenticator's address in the order details, plus a confirmation email | At sale |
| 2 | You post it within your handling time | Your stated handling time |
| 3 | Authenticator checks it matches the title, description and photos, then inspects it | Generally within 2 business days of receipt |
| 4a | Pass: tagged or carded and sent to the buyer tracked. Items sold for £450 or more go signature-required | After inspection |
| 4b | Fail: buyer refunded, item returned to you | After inspection |
| 5 | Your funds | Transaction holds typically up to 31 days for authentication items ([payment holds](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/getting-paid/payouts-work-managed-payments-sellers/payments-hold-managed-payments-seller?id=4816)); private sellers typically 24 hours after authentication ([getting paid](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/getting-paid/getting-paid-items-youve-sold?id=4814)) |

A few practical details from the help page and the [watch seller FAQs](https://pages.ebay.co.uk/authenticity-guarantee-watches-seller/faqs/):

- **Private sellers selling an Authenticity Guarantee item for £750 or less post through Simple Delivery.** The authenticator's address is on the label and you choose who pays postage when you list.
- **Each authenticated item is its own order.** Combined postage promotions do not apply.
- **Estimated delivery dates are adjusted automatically** to allow for the detour.
- **Loss or damage.** For watches, eBay says it covers loss or damage while the item is with the authenticator, but the seller is responsible until it reaches them. Pack and insure the leg you control accordingly. See [postage choices](/guides/postage-and-royal-mail-choices) for services with cover at the right value.
- **Trainers.** When eBay provides a free postage label for trainers sold through Authenticity Guarantee, the buyer pays a flat postage charge and there is [no final value fee on postage](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-business-sellers-activated-managed-payments?id=4809).

Your payment will take longer than a normal sale. If you are a business seller buying stock against sales, plan for the authentication items to pay out last. Our [payment holds guide](/guides/ebay-payment-holds) has the detail.

## Why items fail, and how to make sure yours does not

eBay's page says that if the authenticator cannot determine the item is authentic, **or if the item is not as described**, it will not be sent on. The second half is where genuine items fail. The tip on the help page is explicit: if the authenticator decides the condition does not match the condition you chose, eBay refunds the buyer and returns the item to you.

So the listing is the thing being checked, not just the item.

1. **Pick the condition honestly, and err down.** "Pre-owned" with faults described passes. "New" on something tried on does not.
2. **Photograph every flaw.** The watch FAQs ask for photos of defects and cosmetic imperfections, including scratches, on the item, the box and accessories.
3. **List everything included, and send exactly that.** Box, papers, dust bag, tags, spare links, cards. If it is in the photos, it must be in the parcel. If it is not in the parcel, say so in the listing.
4. **Say if it is a display model** and describe the wear from display.
5. **Do not describe it as customised if it is not, and do not hide it if it is.** Customised items are outside the programme; describing a modified item as original is how it fails.
6. **For cards, state the grader and grade exactly,** and for raw cards describe condition carefully. Graded slabs from unapproved graders are not eligible.
7. **Pack as if the authenticator were the buyer.** They are the first person to open it.

Our [spotting fakes guide](/guides/spotting-fakes-before-you-buy) covers checking stock before you buy it. If there is any doubt about what you are holding, the time to find out is before you list it, not when the authenticator does.

If an item cannot be authenticated, eBay says you will cooperate in removing it from circulation. In plain terms: do not relist it. A failed item that reappears on eBay is a route to a restricted account; see [VeRO, restrictions and suspensions](/guides/avoiding-vero-and-suspensions).

## Returns

Your normal returns policy applies. If a buyer returns an authenticated item, it goes back to the authenticator, who checks it is in the same condition and still has everything it left with, including original tags. For watches, the security tag must be attached and untampered. Once re-checked, it comes back to you and the buyer is refunded. The watch FAQs say the authenticator checks returns within 2 business days of receiving them, and that your return policy decides who pays postage back to the authenticator.

eBay may refund the buyer as soon as a return is on its way; if so, it waits until the item is back with you before taking the money from you. This return step is one of the real benefits for sellers: a buyer cannot send back a different item or a damaged one and pass it off as yours without the authenticator seeing it. For how returns work more generally, see [returns without losing money](/guides/returns-without-losing-money).

## Is it good or bad for sellers

Mostly good, with a cost in time. You cannot opt out, so the question is really how to price and list for it.

- **For you:** buyers pay more confidently at higher prices, and returns are checked before they reach you. Swap-and-return fraud on high-value items is much harder.
- **Against you:** slower payment, an extra delivery leg, and a strict reading of your description. Sloppy condition grading costs you a sale that a normal buyer might have kept.

Our recommendation: if you sell regularly above the thresholds, treat Authenticity Guarantee listings as a separate workflow. Longer handling time, a fuller photo set, the condition field chosen conservatively, and a checklist of what goes in the box.

## What goes wrong

- **Accepting a Best Offer and assuming it has dropped out of the programme.** It has not if the Buy It Now price was over the threshold.
- **Condition set too high.** The most common reason genuine items come back.
- **Missing accessories.** Box or papers in the photos but not in the parcel.
- **Posting to the buyer's address out of habit.** Always use the address in the order details.
- **Cheap postage on the leg to the authenticator.** You carry that risk.
- **Relisting an item that failed authentication.**

Questions about a failed authentication or a disputed return belong in [Buyers and disputes](/community/c/ebay-buyers-and-disputes). Post the category, the reason given and your listing's condition field.
