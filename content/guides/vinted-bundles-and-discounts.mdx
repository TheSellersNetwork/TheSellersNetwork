This is for Vinted sellers who want buyers to take three items instead of one. After reading it you will know exactly what a buyer sees when they bundle, how to set a discount that pays for itself, how the parcel size and label work, and how refunds on a bundle differ from refunds on a single item. Every rule links to Vinted's own help page; Vinted changes these pages often, so check the page before relying on a detail.

## The rules in one table

| Thing | What Vinted says | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What a bundle is | Several listings bought together from the same seller in one order | [Buying a bundle](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/260-bundles-policy) |
| Maximum size | Up to 100 items in one bundle order | [Buying a bundle](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/260-bundles-policy) |

## How a bundle works from the buyer's side

Understanding the buyer's screen tells you what to put in your listings.

1. On one of your listings, or on your profile, the buyer presses Create bundle and adds items. Any items of yours they have favourited are shown first, so a buyer who has favourited three of your things is one tap away from a bundle.
2. The bundle price updates as they add and remove items. If you have a bundle discount set, it is applied here.
3. They review the bundle and then either Buy now, message you, or Make an offer on the bundle.
4. At Buy now, the Vinted fee and the shipping fee are added on top of the bundle price.
5. Once paid, every item in the bundle is marked as sold automatically.

Two things follow from this. First, buyers bundle from your profile, so a profile with enough stock in one size or one theme gets more bundles than a profile of one-offs. Second, favourites are the start of most bundles, which is another reason to answer questions and send offers to people who favourite your items (see [handling haggles](/guides/handling-haggles-and-is-this-still-available)).

### Why bundling saves the buyer money

The buyer pays one shipping fee for the parcel rather than one per item. Vinted's [fee page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1654) says the Vinted fee is usually a percentage of the item's or bundle's price plus a fixed amount, and that the calculation varies with order value and whether the order is a single item or a bundle. In practice that means the buyer's extra costs on a bundle are usually lower per item than buying the same things separately. That saving is the main reason buyers bundle, and it is worth saying so in your profile.

## Setting a bundle discount

Standard sellers set this once for the whole profile.

- **In the app:** go to your profile, choose Bundle discounts, turn it on if needed, and set the discount for each number of items.
- **On the website:** profile icon, Settings, Bundle discounts.

You can switch the whole thing off, or leave out a particular quantity by choosing the dash option for that number of items ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/422-set-up-a-discount-for-your-closet)). The discount applies to all buyers who bundle from you; you cannot set it per item.

### How much to offer

You receive the item price in full on Vinted; the buyer pays the Vinted fee and the shipping ([terms, section 9](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions)). So a bundle discount comes straight off your money, and the saving to you is not postage (the buyer pays that) but time: one sale, one parcel, one trip to the drop-off, one set of packaging.

A way to set it that sellers we know use:

| Items in the bundle | Suggested discount | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Small, or none | Two items is often a buyer who would have bought both anyway. |
| 3 | Moderate | This is where a buyer adds the third item because of the discount. |
| 5 or more | Largest | Clears several slow items at once and saves you the most packing time. |

The numbers are yours to choose. Work them out from your own margins, not from what other sellers show. The test is simple: add up the lowest prices you would accept for your typical items, and make sure your biggest discount on a typical bundle still lands at or above that total. If it does not, the discount is too big for your stock.

A worked example with made-up prices, to show the arithmetic only. Three tops listed at £8, £10 and £12 total £30. With a 10% bundle discount the buyer's bundle price is £27. You give up £3 across three sales that each would have needed its own parcel and trip. If your lowest acceptable prices were £6, £8 and £10 (£24 in total), the discount is safe. At 25% off (£22.50) it is not.

### Discounts and offers together

A buyer can make an offer on a bundle that already has the discount applied. Decide in advance whether you accept offers on top of the discount. A reasonable rule: accept offers on bundles only when the offer still clears the total of your lowest acceptable prices, and counter otherwise. If you are sending offers to people who favourite items, remember the bundle discount is a separate tool; do not stack a deep offer on one item with a deep bundle discount on the same order without checking the total.

## Item sets: one listing, several items

A set is different from a bundle. It is one listing containing up to 5 items, sold together. Vinted's [rules for sets](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/257-selling-a-bundle):

- The first photo must show every item in the set in one picture, not a collage. Later photos show each item on its own.
- The title and description must make clear it is a set.
- An item in a set cannot also be listed on its own.
- Choose the category of the main item, or the category that fits similar items.
- Price it for everything in it.

Vinted's [hidden listings page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/62-why-your-listing-was-hidden-or-deleted) says a listing is hidden if it contains several items but you only sell one of them, or sell them separately as well. So a set is a commitment: the items only go together.

**When to use a set rather than a bundle discount.** Use a set for things that genuinely belong together and are worth less apart: a matching outfit, a run of books in a series, a baby clothes lot in one size. Use bundle discounts for everything else, because separate listings each get their own search results and a buyer can still pick them one by one.

## Postage on a bundle

### Parcel size

Vinted chooses the parcel size for a bundle automatically, based on the type and number of items ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/257-selling-a-bundle)). If the size needs to change after the buyer has created the bundle, you get a request to approve a suitable size before they can complete the purchase.

If you get to the drop-off and the automatically selected size is wrong for a bundle, Vinted says you can change it at the drop-off point at no extra cost: hand it to the staff at a parcel shop, or choose another size on the locker screen and do not close the door until told to. If you were charged extra because the real parcel was bigger than the automatic size, Vinted asks you to contact them with the receipt for compensation ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/160-can-i-change-the-parcel-size-at-the-drop-off-point)).

Check each carrier's maximum size and weight on Vinted's [parcel size limits page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1227) before you promise a large bundle. A bundle of coats can go over a limit that single coats never reach. The [parcel size checker](/tools/postage?tab=size) helps with Royal Mail sizes.

### Packing a bundle

Vinted's [packing rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/65) say all items in a bundle go in one parcel, each wrapped so it does not move, with no more than two electronic devices with batteries in one package. Photograph the items and the packed parcel, inside and out, before you seal it. On a bundle this matters more than on a single item, because "an item was missing" is a refund reason on bundles (see below). See [packaging cheaply](/guides/packaging-cheaply) for materials.

You must use the one label Vinted generates for the order. Vinted's [wrong shipping option page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/243) says one label used for parcels from separate purchases to the same buyer causes problems; only a real bundle order goes on one label. If a buyer buys two items separately, post them separately, or ask them to cancel and rebuy as a bundle before you ship.

## Refunds and disputes on a bundle

Bundles have their own refund rules, set out in Vinted's [refund policy](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/465) and [bundle page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/260-bundles-policy).

| Situation | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| One item in the bundle is significantly not as described | The buyer returns only that item and is refunded for that item. They keep the rest. |
| The whole bundle is significantly not as described | Everything is returned. |
| An item is missing from the bundle | This counts as significantly not as described. You may refund the missing item and let them keep the rest, or ask for the rest back. |
| Partial refund while the buyer keeps the item | Not available on bundle orders. The seller's partial refund option is for single item purchases only ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/272-returning-an-item)). |
| Vinted fee on a partial bundle return | Recalculated on the items kept; the buyer gets the difference back ([terms, section 11](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions)). |
| A bundle item confirmed counterfeit | It does not need to be returned for the refund. |

Because partial refunds are not available on bundles, you cannot settle a small bundle complaint with a few pounds back. Either you refund the item in full and let them keep it, or you ask for it back. That is one more reason to photograph every flaw on every item in a bundle. For how disputes are decided, see [fake or not as described claims](/guides/vinted-fake-or-not-as-described-claims).

## When bundles help, and when they do not

**They help when:**

- You have depth in one size or one style, so a buyer finds several things they want.
- Items are low value on their own and the buyer's per-item costs put them off buying one.
- You want slow items gone and would rather give a discount than keep photographing and repricing them (see [stale stock](/guides/stale-stock)).
- Parcels are light and small, so several items still fit the same size band.

**They do not help when:**

- Your items are one-offs in random sizes. Buyers rarely find a second item.
- Items are heavy or bulky, which cannot be bundled anyway.
- Your margins are thin and the discount eats them.

## What goes wrong

- **A discount bigger than your margin.** Check your largest discount against the lowest price you would take for a typical bundle.
- **Accepting a low offer on top of the bundle discount.** Decide your floor for bundles before the offers arrive.
- **Wrong parcel size on a large bundle.** Check the carrier limits and change the size at the drop-off rather than forcing a parcel into a smaller band.
- **Forgetting an item.** Missing items are refund claims. Pack from the order screen, tick each item off, and photograph the open parcel.
- **Listing a set item separately as well.** Vinted hides the listing.
- **Posting two separate orders on one label.** Only one bundle order goes on one label.
- **Expecting to offer bundle discounts as a Pro seller.** Pro accounts cannot. See [Vinted Pro and selling as a business](/guides/vinted-pro-and-selling-as-a-business).

A note on who can sell what. Vinted's [Catalogue Rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules) say standard accounts are for private, non-commercial selling, and that anyone buying stock to resell needs a Pro account. Read the Pro guide linked above if that is you. The October 2026 terms changes are summarised in [Vinted's new UK terms](/blog/vinted-terms-october-2026).
