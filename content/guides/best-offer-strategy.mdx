This is for sellers who list at a fixed price on eBay UK and want Best Offer to work for them rather than against them. After reading it you will know which listings should take offers, how to set the two automatic thresholds so you never answer an offer you should not have to, how to counter without losing the buyer, and how to set a floor as a business seller that survives the fee line. The rules link to eBay's pages; they change, so check before relying on them. Nothing here is tax, legal or financial advice.

## What Best Offer actually is

It is a checkbox on a fixed price listing that lets a buyer name a price, and a workflow behind it that lets you accept, decline or counter, with two optional thresholds that answer offers for you. eBay's listing form asks for the minimum you are willing to accept and offers you the two automatic limits. Buyers see a Make Offer button and a Best Offer label in search. They do not see any of your numbers.

The important thing it is not: it is not a discount. A buyer who offers less than your accept limit and gets declined can still buy at full price, and many do. It is a filter for buyers who need to feel they negotiated, and a way of finding out what the market will pay without lowering the visible price for everyone.

## When to turn it on

The decision by listing type, with what we would do.

| Listing | Best Offer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Used, one-off, priced from a wide spread of comps | On | The spread is where offers live. The buyer who wants the item at the low quarter tells you, and you decide. |
| New, multiples in stock, priced at a tight cluster of comps | On, with a narrow band | The price is known. Offers within a few percent close sales faster than watchers ever will; anything lower is auto-declined and costs you nothing. |
| Priced at the low quarter already, to shift stock | Off, or on with accept and decline set one pound apart | You are already at the floor. Offers here only teach buyers to ask. |
| Collectable where the comps are mostly auctions | On, priced at the high quarter | You are using fixed price with offers as a slow auction. Give it time. |
| Item you are about to put in a sale event | Off during the sale | eBay's [sale event page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/grow-your-sales/using-promotions-to-boost-your-sales/sale-event-markdown) notes automatic offer responses do not run while an item is in a sale, so you would be answering by hand at a price you have already cut. |
| Anything where you expect to make a coupon work | Off | A coupon cannot be applied to a listing with Best Offer or an open offer to buyers. |

The general rule: Best Offer belongs on listings priced at or above the middle of the comps. Below the middle it is an invitation to lose money.

## Setting the two thresholds

Both limits start from a floor you have already worked out. If you have not, do that first: [pricing from sold comps](/guides/pricing-from-sold-comps) takes the sold price, removes fees, postage, packaging and a returns allowance, and leaves what the item is worth to you. Your floor is the lowest sale price at which that leftover still clears the profit you need on this item. Write it down.

**Auto-accept.** Set it at the price you would be pleased to take today. For a listing at the middle comp, that is usually a few percent under the list price, because a buyer who offers within that band would probably have paid full price next week, and you are trading a little margin for cash now and no further admin. Do not set it at your floor. Auto-accept is instant, and you want the region between "pleased" and "acceptable" to come to you as a counter, where you can nudge it up.

**Auto-decline.** Set it just below your floor. Anything under it is a price you would never take, so there is no point reading it. The gap between decline and accept is your negotiating band: every offer inside it lands in your inbox with a 24 hour clock.

A worked band. List price £40. Floor from your margin sheet £30. Auto-accept £36. Auto-decline £29. Offers of £36 or above sell instantly. Offers of £29 to £35.99 come to you and you counter. Offers under £29 disappear without troubling you. Buyers see none of these figures.

Two cautions. The auto-accept limit must sit below your Buy It Now price, which the listing form enforces. And if you promote the listing, an accepted offer is charged the ad fee on the final total, so a promoted listing's floor is the unpromoted floor plus the ad rate on the sale.

For multi-quantity listings, set the thresholds per unit and remember an offer can be for several units at once; the bulk price you accept at ten units should still clear the floor on each.

## Counteroffers

The band between the thresholds is where you make money by hand. You have 24 hours to respond to an offer; your counter lasts 96 hours; each side can counter three times.

What works in practice.

- **Counter towards the accept limit, not the floor.** If the buyer offered £31 on a £40 item with a £36 accept, counter at £37 or £36, not £33. Buyers who opened at £31 expected to end nearer £35 than £31. You will lose a few; you will bank more.
- **Move in small steps.** Three counters each is the limit. Halving the gap each time signals that you have a number in mind and it is not far away.
- **Counter fast.** The buyer is on the site now. An offer answered in an hour converts far better than one answered the next evening.
- **Do not counter above the list price minus the offer's postage.** Buyers compare totals. If your counter plus postage is more than another seller's Buy It Now, you have talked them into buying elsewhere.
- **Use the counter to fix a problem.** If a buyer offers low and says the postage is high, counter at list price with cheaper postage. The item price you keep is what most buyers remember.
- **Know when to decline instead.** If an offer is inside the band but the buyer's feedback or message tells you they will be trouble, decline. A sale that returns is worse than no sale.

If you are a private seller, the counter screen shows you what you will receive and the Buyer Protection fee the buyer pays on top, separately ([information for sellers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/selling/pricing-items/buyer-protection-information-sellers?id=5595)). Counter on the figure you receive.

## Offer messages

Buyers can attach a message to an offer, and eBay's own buyer guidance tells them a friendly message helps. Treat the message as information about the buyer, not about the item.

- A message that describes a genuine use for the item and a modest offer is usually a real buyer. Counter politely; they tend to close.
- A message that lists the item's faults back to you is a negotiation script. Answer the number, not the list.
- A message that asks for something the listing does not offer (a different service, a discount for buying two, a delay in posting) needs a decision on the request, not the price. If you can do it, counter with the terms in your own message.
- A message that asks you to sell outside eBay, or to send to an address other than the checkout address, is a decline and, if it persists, a report. That is in eBay's [selling practices policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/selling-practices-policy?id=4346) rather than here, but it starts in the offer box often enough to mention.

Keep your own counter messages to one line: the price, and one reason if there is one. Long justifications read as weakness.

## Lowball offers

A lowball offer is one under your floor. The auto-decline threshold handles nearly all of them, which is the main reason to set it. For the ones that arrive because you set the threshold too low, or because the buyer offered a fraction above it, a few rules.

- **Do not take it personally and do not reply personally.** Decline or counter at the accept limit. A curt message costs you a sale to that buyer's next offer and possibly a feedback.
- **Counter rather than decline on the first one.** The buyer has a limited number of offers on the listing (3 or 5 in most categories, and expired and declined offers count). A counter keeps the conversation alive without spending one of theirs; a decline nudges them to spend another. Either way, the second offer is usually higher.
- **Watch for the pattern.** If most offers on a listing are lowballs, the list price is too far above the middle comp, and the market is telling you so. Reprice from the comps rather than fighting the offers.
- **Multiples of the same lowball from different accounts** are worth a look at the buyers' feedback left for others. Blocked buyer lists exist for a reason.

## Best Offer and Promoted Listings

The rule is simple and expensive if you forget it. If any buyer clicked a general Promoted Listings ad for the item within the last 30 days and you then accept an offer, the ad fee is charged on the final total sale amount (item, postage and any fees or taxes) at the ad rate in effect when the sale happens ([general campaign strategy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/listing-tips/promoted-listings?id=4164)). eBay's Best Offer page says the same. The ad fee is a percentage of what you actually got, so a discount reduces it in pounds, but it does not go away because the buyer negotiated.

Practical consequences.

- Your floor on a promoted listing is your unpromoted floor divided by one minus the ad rate, roughly. At a 5% ad rate, a £30 floor becomes about £31.58.
- eBay shows during the offer negotiation whether a standard ad rate may apply to the sale, so look before you accept.
- Dynamic ad rates change daily. If you use them, a listing's floor moves with them; give yourself a wider gap between decline and accept.
- If you run a campaign with a high ad rate on slow stock, consider turning Best Offer off on those listings and using [offers to watchers](/guides/offers-to-watchers) instead, where you choose the discount and know the ad position before you send.

## Floors for business sellers

A business seller pays a final value fee on the total sale including postage, a per-order fee, a regulatory operating fee of 0.35%, a listing fee without a Shop, and VAT on all of them ([fees for business sellers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-business-sellers?id=4809)). The final value fee percentage depends on the category and changed for several categories on [12 February 2026](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/news/2026-january/rate-card-change), along with the per-order fee on orders over £10, which went from 30p to 40p. Fees are charged on the accepted offer price, not the list price, which is the one piece of good news.

The floor is built the same way as the price, backwards. Take the profit you need on the item in pounds. Add your buy price, your label, your packaging and your returns allowance. That total has to be what is left after fees. To find the sale price that leaves it, divide by one minus the fee rate as a decimal (with VAT if you cannot reclaim it), then add the per-order fee and any listing fee, also with VAT.

A worked floor, with illustrative costs. Category fee 11.9%, regulatory fee 0.35%, so 12.25% before VAT and 14.7% with it for a seller who is not VAT registered. Per-order fee 40p, listing fee 30p, 84p with VAT. Buy price £9, label £3.20, packaging £0.40, returns allowance £0.80, profit wanted £8: total £21.40. £21.40 divided by 0.853 is £25.09, plus £0.84 is £25.93. That is the floor with free postage. If instead the buyer pays £3.50 postage, the fees run on the total (item plus postage), so the total has to be £25.93 and the item price floor is £22.43, because the buyer's postage is covering the label you already counted. Do it in a spreadsheet, once, with the fee cells editable, and read the floor off it for every listing. A round number like £26 (free postage) is your auto-decline; an accept limit of £30 or £32 sits above it; the list price sits above that at the comp you chose.

Three things business sellers get wrong with floors. They forget VAT on fees, so every floor is a fifth too low on the fee line. They set the floor from the buy price alone, so a lot of sales are profitable before returns and loss-making after them. And they use one floor across categories with different fee percentages. Clothing and books do not carry the same fee, so they cannot carry the same floor.

If you are still registered as a private seller but buying to resell, the [business seller policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/business-seller-policy?id=4710) says you should not be, and your floors will change the day you switch. Build them on business fees now.

## A routine

- When you list, put the floor in the SKU or custom label field so it is in front of you when an offer arrives.
- Set auto-decline a pound under the floor and auto-accept at the "pleased" price.
- Check offers twice a day. The 24 hour clock is short and a fast counter converts.
- Once a month, look at accepted offers as a share of sales and the average discount. If most sales are offers at close to the accept limit, drop the list price a little and lift the thresholds; you are paying in time for a discount buyers would take anyway. If almost no offers arrive, the price is fine or too high; the comps will tell you which.
- When the rate card changes, change the fee cells and re-read every floor.

## What goes wrong

- **Auto-accept at the floor.** You give away the whole negotiating band to the first buyer who guesses it.
- **No auto-decline.** You spend evenings reading offers of a third of the price.
- **Countering down towards the offer instead of up towards the accept limit.** Buyers open low expecting to meet in the middle; meet them near your number.
- **Slow replies.** A counter after a day lands after the buyer has bought elsewhere.
- **Forgetting the ad fee.** An accepted offer on a promoted listing pays the ad rate on the final total. The floor has to include it.
- **Best Offer on a listing already priced to clear.** Every offer is under the floor and every decline is a buyer who leaves.
- **Best Offer during a sale event.** Automatic responses do not run and you are negotiating on a price you already cut.
- **Coupon and Best Offer on the same listing.** The coupon cannot be applied; the buyer who tried it thinks the listing is broken.
- **Treating an accepted offer as a sale before payment.** Unless the buyer offered with automatic payment, the item is still live until they pay. Do not post, do not end other listings, until the money is there.
