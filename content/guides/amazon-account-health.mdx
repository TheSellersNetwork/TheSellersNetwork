This is for anyone selling on Amazon UK who wants to keep selling. Account health is the one part of the business a competitor cannot touch and you can lose in an afternoon. After reading you will know how to read every line of the Account Health page, what each threshold is, how to respond to a violation without making it worse, and what to check each week so that nothing on that page surprises you. The [FBA manual](/guides/how-to-start-amazon-fba-uk) covers the setup and the buying; this goes deep on the one page that decides whether you are still trading next month. Nothing here is legal advice.

## Where the page is and what it shows

Seller Central, Performance, Account Health. Amazon's own [account health policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200205250) describes the page as a snapshot of how far your account is from deactivation, and that is exactly how to treat it. The page has three areas and one score.

**The Account Health Rating** is a number from 0 to 1,000 with a colour behind it. It only measures the policy compliance area: every open violation costs points according to how serious Amazon judges it, and the points come back as violations are resolved or age out. Amazon publishes the bands on the [policy page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200205250); the figures that matter for most sellers are that a healthy account sits comfortably above 200 and that a rating dropping towards 100 means deactivation is on the table. Check the current bands on the page rather than relying on a number you read in a forum, because Amazon adjusts the weighting.

**The three areas** below the score are customer service performance, policy compliance and delivery performance. Each has its own metrics, its own thresholds and its own kind of trouble. A seller who only uses FBA can mostly ignore the third. Nobody can ignore the second.

The page also carries the notifications Amazon has sent you, a button to request a call from the account health team, and, if you qualify, a line saying you are covered by Account Health Assurance. Read all of it, not just the score.

## Customer service performance

This area is about the orders themselves: did the customer get what they paid for, and did they complain. It is driven by one headline number.

### Order defect rate

The [order defect rate](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200285170) is the share of your orders in a rolling 60 day window that picked up at least one of three defects. Amazon's target is under 1%. The three parts are:

| Defect | What counts | How it is triggered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Negative feedback | A one or two star seller rating | Buyer leaves it on your seller profile |
| A-to-z Guarantee claim | A claim Amazon grants against you, or one you failed to answer | Buyer opens it after contacting you, or when Amazon steps in |
| Credit card chargeback | A dispute the buyer raised with their bank that Amazon decides is a service issue | Bank notifies Amazon |

Three things about the arithmetic. It is a percentage of orders, so at 40 orders in 60 days a single negative feedback is 2.5% and you are over. It is 60 days, so a bad week takes two months to fall out. And it counts defective orders, not defects, so one order with a claim and a negative feedback is one defect.

At low volume the only defence is not to have defects. That means: describe condition honestly, answer every message within 24 hours (Amazon's rule, weekends included), refund quickly when something has gone wrong rather than argue, and never let an A-to-z claim sit unanswered. A refunded order can still get negative feedback but it will not usually get a claim.

Negative feedback on an FBA order that is about the delivery or an Amazon handled return can be struck through, which removes it from the calculation. That is covered under feedback below.

### Invoice defect rate

If you sell to Amazon Business customers, and you will whether you meant to or not, Amazon requires an invoice for each business order to be uploaded within one business day of dispatch, and it tracks the share of business orders that missed that window as the [invoice defect rate](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/gp/help/external/44QSYRCU27EW4UK), with a target of under 5%. Business orders are marked in Manage Orders. If you are VAT registered and enrolled in the VAT Calculation Service, Amazon generates the invoices for you. If you are not, Amazon can generate receipts on your behalf from your settings, or you upload one yourself. Whichever you choose, do it, because this metric is the one new sellers do not know exists until the warning email arrives. There is more on business invoices in the [VAT guide](/guides/amazon-vat-invoices-and-business-accounts).

## Policy compliance

This is where sellers who buy from retailers and wholesalers get hurt. The metrics above are about service; this area is about whether Amazon and rights owners believe you are allowed to sell what you are selling, and it is where every point on the Account Health Rating comes from.

Each violation appears as a line with a type, the product it concerns, the date, and a button to acknowledge or appeal. The main types:

**Suspected intellectual property violations.** Amazon's own systems flagged your listing or offer as possibly infringing a trade mark, copyright or patent. No brand has complained yet. These are often generated by keywords, images or a brand name in the wrong field on a listing you did not create.

**Received intellectual property complaints.** A rights owner told Amazon your offer infringes. A genuine product bought from a real shop can still attract one, because some brands object to anyone outside their distribution selling at all. The invoice is your evidence that the goods are genuine; it is not a guarantee the complaint will be withdrawn.

**Product authenticity customer complaints.** A buyer said the item was fake. Amazon takes these seriously and will ask for invoices from your supplier covering the units sold, with your name and address on them, from a supplier it can verify. This is the violation that most often ends arbitrage accounts, because a till receipt or a marketplace order confirmation is not the invoice Amazon wants.

**Product condition customer complaints.** Sold as new, arrived used, opened, damaged or with a retailer's sticker on it. Also counts complaints that the item was not what the listing described, which is usually a listing match error on your side.

**Product safety complaints.** A buyer reported the product as unsafe, or Amazon needs safety documentation. Food and children's products are the usual sources.

**Listing policy violations.** Wrong category, wrong brand name, images or titles that break the style rules, variations that do not belong together, a multipack that is not the brand's own.

**Restricted product policy violations.** You listed something Amazon does not allow, or allows only with approval: medicines, some supplements, some knives, alcohol, and a long list on Amazon's [restricted products pages](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/gp/help/external/G200333160).

**Customer product reviews policy violations.** Anything that looks like buying, incentivising or steering reviews or feedback. Covered in the [feedback and Buy Box guide](/guides/amazon-seller-feedback-and-buy-box); the short version is that an unsolicited offer of a refund for a review ends accounts.

**Other policy violations.** Multiple accounts, fair pricing (an offer priced well above the same item elsewhere), drop shipping outside the rules, selling account misuse.

**Regulatory compliance.** Product documentation Amazon is required to hold under UK or EU law for certain categories: safety test reports, declarations of conformity, labelling, responsible person details. These arrive as requests rather than accusations and are handled through the compliance dashboard described below.

Every one of these has a severity. A single suspected IP line on a listing you can delete costs little and clears fast. An authenticity complaint costs a lot and stays on the page until you have satisfied Amazon. The rating tells you which is which; the notification email tells you what Amazon wants.

## Delivery performance

Only orders you dispatch yourself count here. FBA orders are Amazon's responsibility and do not touch these metrics. If you are FBM, even for a handful of products, the [order performance policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GGJVNFDXQT8C3RA8) sets four targets:

| Metric | Target | Window |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Late dispatch rate | Under 4% | 10 and 30 days |
| Pre-fulfilment cancellation rate | Under 2.5% | 7 days |
| Valid tracking rate | Over 95% | 30 days |
| On-time delivery | Amazon states a target on the page; check it | 30 days |

Late dispatch counts orders confirmed after the promised dispatch date. Cancellations count any order you cancel before dispatch, including ones where you ran out of stock, which is why an FBM offer must never show more units than you can put your hands on. Valid tracking means a tracking number from a carrier Amazon recognises, entered before or at confirmation. Untracked large letter post fails this test by definition, so price FBM products on the assumption that they go tracked.

If you are FBA only, this section will be empty, and that is one of the quieter arguments for FBA.

## Account Health Assurance

Account Health Assurance is Amazon's promise, set out on its [FAQ page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GGQY933W4RDPJT29) and in its [announcement](https://sell.amazon.com/blog/announcements/seller-account-health-assurance), not to deactivate your account for an account health issue as long as you work with Amazon to fix it. In practice it means that instead of a deactivation email you get a call from an account health specialist explaining what is wrong and what to do. It is free and automatic; you cannot apply.

To be eligible, according to the FAQ, you need a Professional selling plan, an Account Health Rating of 250 or above for the previous six months with no more than ten days below 250 in that time, and a valid emergency contact number on the account. Amazon has to be able to reach you within 72 hours of finding the problem, and you have to act on what they tell you. It does not cover suspected fraud, illegal or deceptive activity, or regulatory breaches, and Amazon can remove you from it at any time.

Two practical points. The emergency phone number is a setting most sellers never fill in; it lives under Account Info, and it is the difference between a call and a deactivation. And the rating history is what qualifies you, which means one heavy violation left unresolved for a fortnight can put you below 250 for long enough to lose cover for the next six months. Treat the six month clean run as an asset with a value.

## Acknowledging and appealing

When a violation lands, the line on the page offers one of a few responses, and choosing the right one matters.

**Acknowledge.** Amazon offers this for lower severity violations where it is content for you to confirm you understand the policy and have removed the listing. Acknowledging ends the matter and the points recover with time. It is the right choice when Amazon is correct: you did put a brand name in the wrong field, you did list a pack the brand does not sell. Do not acknowledge something you did not do to make it go away; an acknowledgement is a record.

**Delete the listing.** For suspected IP and some listing violations Amazon accepts deletion as resolution. If the product is a small part of your business, deleting is quicker than appealing and carries no admission. If you have stock in the warehouse, you will need a removal order afterwards.

**Submit an appeal with documents.** For authenticity, received IP and condition complaints Amazon wants evidence: invoices covering the units sold in the period, your supplier's contact details, and sometimes a letter of authorisation from the brand. The invoice requirements are the same as for ungating and are set out in the [ungating guide](/guides/amazon-ungating-uk): supplier name and address, your name and address exactly as on your Seller Central account, dated within the period Amazon asks for, showing the product and quantity. Anything else is decoration.

**Submit a plan of action.** For deactivations and repeated violations Amazon asks for a written plan alongside the evidence. This is the one that takes care.

Whatever you choose, use the buttons on the Account Health page or the link in the notification, not a new case to seller support. Responses submitted through the wrong channel are not linked to the violation and do not count. And do not open a second account while the first is under review; Amazon links accounts by every detail it holds and a second account is treated as a more serious violation than whatever the first one was accused of.

## Writing a plan of action that works

Amazon's account health team reads thousands of these. What gets an appeal accepted is not eloquence, it is the three things the form asks for, answered specifically and in order.

**Root cause.** What actually happened. Not "we take this very seriously", but "the listing for ASIN X was matched to a two pack when the units we sent were singles, because our sourcing checklist did not include pack count". If the cause was a supplier, name the supplier. If the cause was you, say so; Amazon is more likely to believe a seller who admits a process gap than one who blames the buyer.

**What you have done.** Corrective action already taken, in the past tense, with dates. Removed the offer on the date the notice arrived. Created a removal order for the units in stock. Refunded the buyer. Stopped buying from the supplier. Attached invoices for the units sold in the last 60 days.

**What will stop it happening again.** Preventive changes to your process, specific enough that Amazon could check them. A pre-listing checklist that includes pack count and variant. A rule that every product is checked against the gating and restricted lists before purchase. A rule that no unit goes in without a brand new, unopened check at prep. A second person checking listings. A new supplier verification step.

Then the mechanics. Keep it short: a page is plenty. Use headings that match the three questions. Attach documents as PDFs named for what they are. Do not paste the policy back at Amazon. Do not say anything you cannot show. Do not send the same text twice; if it was refused, the refusal usually says which section was thin, and the second version should be visibly different in that section. If the responses are clearly templated, use the call button on the Account Health page and ask the specialist what is missing; sellers we know have had more success from one call than from five rewrites.

## Manage Your Compliance

For anything about product safety and regulation, Amazon has moved the work to a single dashboard, described on its [Manage Your Compliance page](https://sell.amazon.com/blog/manage-your-compliance). It lists each product with an outstanding requirement, what document or information is needed, and the status of anything you have submitted. Common requests for a reseller: a declaration of conformity or test report for a toy or electrical item, the responsible person for a product made outside the UK, safety labelling, and for cosmetics or supplements the ingredient and labelling information.

Two facts that matter. Documents can take up to 15 business days to review, per Amazon's guidance, so a request left until the deadline strands the stock. And for a reseller the documents usually belong to the brand, not you, so the practical routes are: ask your supplier or the brand for them, check whether the listing owner has already supplied them (often the request clears when they do), or accept that a product whose paperwork you cannot get is not a product you can sell, and remove it. Uploading something that is not the document requested, in the hope it passes, creates a compliance violation on top of the request.

## The weekly check

Ten minutes, same day each week, before sourcing. In this order.

1. **Account Health page.** Rating and colour. Any new line under policy compliance. Any line older than seven days still open.
2. **Order defect rate.** The number and the three parts under it. If it moved, find the order and deal with it: reply, refund, or request feedback removal.
3. **Buyer messages.** Nothing older than 24 hours without a reply. Mark "no response needed" on anything that is not a question, so it does not count against you.
4. **A-to-z claims and chargebacks.** Any open claim gets a response today, with the order details, tracking and what you offered the buyer.
5. **Invoice defect rate.** Business orders from the last week have an invoice attached.
6. **Manage Your Compliance.** Any new request, and the status of any submitted document.
7. **Performance notifications.** Read every email Amazon sent that week in full. The subject line is often calmer than the deadline inside.
8. **Emergency contact and two step verification.** Once a month, confirm the phone number is still yours and still on the account.

Keep a one line log of anything you did: date, violation, action. When the same product or supplier appears twice, stop buying it.

## What goes wrong

**Reading the score instead of the lines.** A rating of 300 with an unanswered authenticity complaint is a worse position than 220 with nothing open, because the complaint has a deadline and the score does not.

**Answering the wrong question.** The notice asks for invoices; the seller sends a paragraph about how honest they are. The notice asks for a plan; the seller sends invoices. Read the request, answer that, and only that.

**Invoices that do not match.** Wrong name, home address when Amazon has the company address, a prep centre's address, a marketplace order confirmation, a pro forma. The paperwork has to be right before the stock is bought; it cannot be fixed afterwards. The [ungating guide](/guides/amazon-ungating-uk) has the full requirements.

**Ignoring FBM metrics because "we are mostly FBA".** One FBM product with untracked postage can push valid tracking under 95% on its own.

**Deleting the listing and forgetting the stock.** The violation clears; the units sit in the warehouse as stranded inventory paying storage until a removal order is created.

**Opening a second account.** Always found, always worse.

**Waiting for the email.** The page updates before the email arrives. The weekly check is how you get ahead of it.

## Questions

**How long does a violation stay on the page?** Resolved violations stop affecting the rating on the timescale set out on the [policy page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200205250), which depends on severity. Unresolved ones stay until they are resolved. Check the page for the current periods.

**Can I see what caused a suspected IP violation?** The notice names the product and the type. Common causes are a brand name in a listing field where it should not be, or an image containing another brand's mark. If you did not create the listing, deleting your offer is usually enough.

**Does negative feedback on an FBA order count against me?** Yes, until it is struck through. Request removal through Feedback Manager within 90 days if it is about delivery or an Amazon handled return; the [feedback guide](/guides/amazon-seller-feedback-and-buy-box) covers the rules.

**Who do I ask when the response is a template?** Use the call button on the Account Health page, and post the details (not the buyer's) in [Account health and suspensions](/community/c/amazon-account-health-and-suspensions) here. Say what the notice asked for, what you sent, and what came back.
