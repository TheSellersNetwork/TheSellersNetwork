This is for anyone who lists on eBay, Vinted, Depop or Facebook Marketplace and takes their own photos on a phone, which is nearly everyone. It gives you a setup you can build in an afternoon for very little money, a fixed sequence of shots for each type of item, the photo rules each platform enforces, and a clear line on editing. After reading it your photos will be consistent, your listings will need fewer questions, and you will stop losing sales and returns to pictures that did not show what the buyer got.

## Why the photos decide the sale

On every platform the first photo is the search result. On eBay it is the square thumbnail in a grid of near identical items; on Vinted and Depop it is the tile in a feed; on Marketplace it is the card in a scroll of local listings. The buyer chooses which to open on that one image, and the rest of the set decides whether they buy and whether they send it back. eBay's own [listing best practices](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/listings/create-listings/best-practices) page cites that 43% of consumers said better product images would make them less likely to return an item. In practice, the two things that most reduce "not as described" returns are photographing every flaw and photographing the label.

You do not need a camera, a light box or editing software. You need daylight, a plain background, a way to hold the phone still, and a routine you follow every time.

## The setup

**Light.** Daylight through a window, not direct sun, and not the room light. Put the item near the window with the light falling on it from the side or from behind you. Turn the flash off; every platform's own advice says so, and flash flattens colour and throws hard shadows. Overcast days are the best days. If you must shoot at night, one cheap daylight-balanced LED panel or bulb pointed at a white wall to bounce is enough. Never mix daylight and a warm room bulb in the same shot; the colour cast is what makes a cream jumper look yellow and a navy coat look black.

**Background.** eBay's [photo tips](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/listings/photo-tips) page asks for an uncluttered space and a white or neutral background so the item stands out. A white sheet, a roll of white paper, a plain wall, a wooden floor or a clean table all work. Dark or reflective items (jewellery, black electronics) often read better on a mid grey than on white. The test is simple: the item should be the only thing in the frame. No carpet pattern, no pet, no bed, no feet.

**Steadiness.** Blur is the commonest fault in phone photos and it comes from the hand. Brace your elbows, use the volume button as the shutter, or put the phone on a stack of books and use the timer. A ten pound phone tripod is the best money you will spend on photos.

**The phone.** Any phone from the last five or six years is more than good enough. Clean the lens with your shirt before every session; the smear from your pocket is why photos look hazy. Tap the item on screen to focus and hold, then drag the exposure slider down slightly if the white background is blowing out. Do not zoom; step closer. Turn off any "beauty" or scene modes.

**Clothing.** Three ways to present it: flat lay (on the floor or a table, shot from directly above), hanging (a wooden hanger against a wall or door), or worn. Flat lay is fastest and shows the shape honestly; hanging shows drape; worn shows fit and sells best on Depop. Steam or iron first. Button the buttons. Fold sleeves the same way every time.

## The shot list

Take the same sequence for every item of a type and you will never forget a shot or have to re-photograph after it is packed. The order below is roughly the order buyers want them.

| Shot | What it shows | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Hero | Whole item, straight on, filling the frame | This is the search thumbnail. Square crop, plain background, nothing else in shot. |
| 2. Back | Whole item from behind | Buyers assume you are hiding something if it is missing. |
| 3. Label or brand mark | Size label, care label, maker's mark, model plate | Text readable. This is the single most return-preventing photo for clothing and the most fraud-preventing for branded goods. |
| 4. Details | Fastenings, logo, stitching, buttons, texture, pattern | One or two, close, in focus. |
| 5. Every flaw | Each mark, hole, scratch, bobble, chip | One photo per flaw, close, with a finger or coin for scale. Photograph it even if you think it is minor. |
| 6. Contents | Everything included: box, manual, cables, spare buttons, dust bag | Laid out together in one shot. What is in the photo is what the buyer expects to receive. |
| 7. Proof it works | Screen on, light lit, needle moving, sound bar showing input | For anything electrical or mechanical. |
| 8. Scale | Item next to a tape measure or ruler, or a flat lay with measurements | Ornaments, bags, furniture, anything where size is not obvious. |
| 9. Angles | Sides, underneath, inside | For bags, shoes, furniture, hard goods. Soles of shoes always. |

For clothing, add a measurements photo: the garment flat with a tape across the pit to pit, and one down the length, or list the measurements in the description. For shoes: both shoes together from above, soles, heels from behind (heel wear is what buyers ask about), and the size stamp inside.

Ten to twelve photos of a used item is normal. Four is a listing that will get questions.

## The rules on each platform

Each platform sets a limit on how many photos it accepts and has rules about what is in them. Limits change; check the listing form when you list.

### eBay

- Up to 24 photos per listing, and the first one is required. From the [adding pictures](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/adding-pictures-listings?id=4148) help page.
- Minimum 500 pixels on the longest side; eBay recommends around 1600 by 1600 pixels. Any modern phone exceeds this by a wide margin, so never send a screenshot or a downscaled copy.
- Files up to 12MB in JPEG, PNG, GIF, TIFF, BMP, WEBP, HEIC or AVIF. If your phone shoots HEIC and the upload fails, switch the camera to "most compatible" (JPEG) in settings.
- The [picture policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/listing-policies/picture-policy?id=4370) bans photos that do not accurately show the item, placeholder images used to carry a message, stock photos for used, damaged or defective items, added borders, added text, artwork or marketing material, and watermarks of any kind including ownership marks. Breaking it can mean listing removal, warnings, restrictions or suspension.
- The photo tips page adds that logos, badges and watermarks on images can stop them appearing in third party search results. So the "free postage" banner and the shop logo in the corner are costing you reach as well as breaking policy.
- A photo of a printed message ("please read description") in the gallery is a placeholder image and a breach.
- Stock photos are acceptable only for new items in their original packaging, where the catalogue image is what the buyer will receive. Even then, add your own photo of the actual box.
- eBay keeps copies of photos on completed listings for up to 90 days, which is why relisting old ended items may lose their images. Keep your originals.

### Vinted

- Up to 20 photos per listing. Vinted's [what photos you should upload](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/48-what-photos-you-should-upload) page asks that the first photo shows the entire item, with no collages, and that you avoid flash and filters in favour of daylight.
- The [catalogue rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules) go further than eBay: photos must be taken by you for the purpose of listing on Vinted, no stock photos, no images from adverts, no watermarked images, and no image editing. Defects should be shown.
- For branded items Vinted asks for close-ups of the authenticity points: labels, logos, tags, codes, and proof of purchase where you have it. This is what stops a listing being pulled and what protects you when a buyer disputes.
- Vinted also asks you not to show your face and to keep identifying details out of shot. A hanger, a mannequin or a flat lay avoids the problem.

### Depop

- Depop's help centre on [improving search ranking](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360050410093-Frequently-Asked-Questions-Depop-Search) refers to making use of the eight image feature to showcase the item and highlight details and flaws. Sellers we know still see four slots plus a video on some accounts, so the listing form on your own account is the final word on the count.
- Photos display square in the grid. Shoot square or leave room to crop square; a portrait hero photo gets its top and bottom cut off in the feed.
- Depop's culture is lifestyle: worn shots, styled flat lays, natural settings. That is fine, but the same rules of honesty apply. Depop removes listings that use stock images, screenshots from retail sites or other sellers' photos. Your hero can be styled; your flaw and label shots should be plain.

### Facebook Marketplace

- Marketplace listings take a set number of photos per listing; the [sell something on Marketplace](https://www.facebook.com/help/561376580709359) help page shows the listing form, and the [listing limits](https://www.facebook.com/help/811082570742714) page covers account limits. The count is shown in the form when you add photos, so check there rather than trusting a figure from a blog.
- Marketplace buyers are local and looking for a quick decision. The hero photo does nearly all the work. Take it in daylight, on a plain background, with the item cleaned. Then the flaws, then a scale shot. Furniture sells on a photo in a tidy room with a tape measure in it.
- Marketplace has no formal ban on text or borders, but Facebook's commerce policies prohibit misleading images, and a photo that is not of your actual item is the fastest route to a wasted afternoon and a bad rating.

## Cross-listing the same photos

Take one master set at full resolution and store it in a folder named for the item. Shoot the hero square, because eBay's grid, Depop's grid and Vinted's tiles all favour square, and Marketplace crops to square in the feed. Use the same first photo everywhere; buyers who see the same item on two platforms trust a consistent image and distrust two different ones. Never re-download a photo from one platform to upload to another: you will get a compressed, cropped copy, and on Vinted it can read as a stock or reused image.

## Editing: what is allowed and what misleads

There are two kinds of edit. One corrects the photo so it matches what your eyes saw. The other changes the item. The first is fine on every platform except Vinted, which asks for no editing at all; the second is misleading everywhere and in law for a business seller (see [Descriptions that sell](/guides/descriptions-that-sell)).

Allowed, and usually worth doing in the phone's own editor:

- Crop to square and straighten the horizon.
- Brightness or exposure, to bring a grey background back to white without losing detail in the item.
- Small white balance correction so a white shirt is white. Check it against the real item.
- Rotate so the item is upright.

Not allowed, or a return waiting to happen:

- Filters. Every platform's advice says no filters, and they shift colour.
- Saturation or "vibrance" boosts that make a faded red look new.
- Retouching out a stain, a scratch, bobbling or wear. If it is in the item it is in the photo.
- Background removal tools that also smooth the edges and hide fraying.
- Adding text, borders, arrows, logos, stickers or watermarks (an eBay policy breach, a search visibility loss, and against Vinted's rules).
- Using the manufacturer's photo for a used item.
- Cropping so the missing strap, the chipped corner or the empty box compartment falls outside the frame.

The test for any edit: would a buyer, comparing the photo with the item in their hands, feel the photo told the truth? If not, undo it.

## A ten minute routine

1. Clean the item and the lens.
2. Set up by the window on the white background.
3. Shoot the sequence: hero, back, label, details, every flaw, contents, working, scale, angles.
4. Check each photo on the phone at full size for focus and blur. Reshoot now, not later.
5. Crop the hero square. Fix exposure if the background is grey.
6. Save the set to a folder named for the item. Upload from there to every platform.

Doing it in this order for every item is what makes it fast. The people who take good listing photos are not more talented; they have stopped deciding what to do each time.

## What goes wrong

- **Yellow or blue casts.** Mixed light sources or a warm bulb. Daylight only, or one bulb only.
- **Blur.** Hand shake or a dirty lens. Tripod or timer, and wipe the lens.
- **Grey backgrounds.** Underexposed. Nudge the exposure up, or move closer to the window.
- **The buyer says the colour is wrong.** Usually white balance. Photograph next to something whose colour you know (a white sheet of paper) and describe the colour in words as well.
- **Listing removed for a watermark or text.** Remove the overlay and re-upload clean originals; eBay's policy does not allow ownership watermarks either.
- **Vinted flagged the photo as not yours.** You uploaded a re-downloaded copy or a screenshot. Upload the original from the camera roll.
- **A return for a flaw you knew about.** It was not photographed, or it was photographed from a distance. One close photo per flaw.
- **Photos vanished from an old ended listing.** eBay keeps them about 90 days. Keep originals and re-upload.
