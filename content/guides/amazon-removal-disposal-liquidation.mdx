This is for FBA sellers with stock that is not selling, has come back from customers, or is about to start costing more to store than it is worth. After reading you will know the four exits Amazon offers, the current UK fee for each, which ones are open to a seller who is not VAT registered, and how to choose unit by unit rather than by habit.

Fees here are from Amazon's UK help pages at the time of writing, and Amazon revises them at least once a year; the most recent rises are in [our note on the 2026 fee changes](/blog/amazon-2026-fee-changes-fba-and-clothing). Check the linked page before you rely on a figure. Nothing here is tax or financial advice.

## The four exits

| Exit | What happens | You get back | Main cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Return to you | Amazon posts the units to your address | The units | Per unit return fee |
| Disposal | Amazon disposes of, recycles or donates them | Nothing | Per unit disposal fee |
| Liquidation | Amazon sells them to a wholesale liquidator | A small share of the selling price | 15% of the recovery plus a per unit fee |
| Grade and Resell | Amazon grades customer returns and lists them as used under your account | The used sale price, less normal fees | Per unit processing fee |

There is a fifth option that is not on Amazon's menu and is usually the best: **sell it**. A price cut that clears a slow product in three weeks almost always beats all four. Removal is for stock that will not sell at any price you can accept, or that cannot be sold on Amazon at all.

## Return to you

### What it costs

From the [return-to-seller and disposal fees page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200685050?locale=en-GB), for stock stored and returned in the UK:

| Standard size, shipping weight | Return fee per unit |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 200 g | £0.49 |
| 201 to 500 g | £0.71 |
| 501 to 1,000 g | £1.57 |
| Over 1,000 g | £2.03 plus £0.80 per kg above 1,000 g |

| Oversize, shipping weight | Return fee per unit |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 500 g | £2.13 |
| 501 to 1,000 g | £4.10 |
| 1,001 to 2,000 g | £5.27 |
| 2,001 to 5,000 g | £7.33 |
| Over 5,000 g | £10.49 plus £0.80 per kg above 5,000 g |

Extra weight is rounded up to the next whole kilogram. Since 1 May 2026 the fee is charged when each unit is removed, rather than for the whole order at the end.

### How long it takes

Amazon's [remove inventory overview](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/200280650?locale=en-GB) says removal orders take 10 to 14 working days to leave the fulfilment centre, and up to 30 working days or more during Prime week, October, November and December. Units at different fulfilment centres arrive separately, sometimes by different carriers. Stock that went in as case packs comes back as loose units.

### Rules worth knowing

- **The address cannot be changed** once the order is created. Check it before you submit.
- **Fulfillable stock stays on sale** until it is physically picked. If you do not want it to sell in the meantime, close the listing.
- **Dangerous goods** can only be returned if they are hazmat transport class 8 or 9. Other hazmat can only be disposed of.
- **Cross-border returns between the UK and the EU are no longer possible.** Stock in the UK comes back to a UK address.

### The claim window

A removal that goes missing or arrives damaged is claimable, but only for a short time: lost units from 15 days after the last tracking movement until about 74 days after the order was created, damaged or wrong units within 60 days of delivery. Photograph every removal parcel before you open it. [Lost and damaged stock claims](/guides/amazon-lost-and-damaged-stock-claims#removals) lists the evidence.

## Disposal

### What it costs

From the [same fee page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200685050?locale=en-GB):

| Standard size, shipping weight | Disposal fee per unit |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 200 g | £0.74 |
| 201 to 500 g | £1.15 |
| 501 to 1,000 g | £2.28 |
| Over 1,000 g | £2.73 plus £0.88 per kg above 1,000 g |

| Oversize, shipping weight | Disposal fee per unit |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 500 g | £3.06 |
| 501 to 1,000 g | £6.56 |
| 1,001 to 2,000 g | £9.68 |
| 2,001 to 5,000 g | £12.26 |
| Over 5,000 g | £16.06 plus £0.88 per kg above 5,000 g |

Amazon says it donates or recycles everything it can; donations to selected charities are available for UK stock.

### The number that surprises people

Put the two tables side by side. **In every UK weight band, disposal costs more than having the stock posted back to you.** A 300 g standard unit is £0.71 to return and £1.15 to dispose of. A 1.5 kg oversize item is £5.27 to return and £9.68 to dispose of.

So disposal is not the cheap option. It is the option for stock you genuinely cannot use or sell anywhere: expired food, broken items, products you are not allowed to sell, or units where your time handling them is worth more than the difference. Anything that could fetch even a pound on another platform should come home.

## Liquidation

### How it works

Amazon's [FBA Liquidations overview](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G2RQ9CA5Q83F8N7X?locale=en-GB) explains the programme. You submit units through the removal order workflow with liquidation as the method. Amazon values them using your sales history, the average FBA price and the ASIN's history, then tries to sell them to a contracted wholesale liquidator within 30 days. The liquidator's price is the **gross recovery value**. Amazon deducts its fees and pays you the **net recovery value**, usually within about 60 days after the order shows as completed.

### What it costs and pays

The [liquidation fees page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GR3768HHG3T5X6J2?locale=en-GB) sets two charges: a referral fee of 15% of the gross recovery value, and a per unit processing fee.

| Standard size | Processing fee (UK) |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 200 g | £0.37 |
| 201 to 500 g | £0.49 |
| 501 g to 1 kg | £0.80 |
| Over 1 kg | £1.06 plus £0.53 per kg |

| Oversize | Processing fee (UK) |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 500 g | £0.85 |
| 501 g to 1 kg | £1.73 |
| 1 to 2 kg | £2.59 |
| 2 to 5 kg | £4.32 |
| Over 5 kg | £5.18 plus £0.52 per kg |

The [liquidations FAQ](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GZ2YSGL4Y4M4YEHM?locale=en-GB) puts the maximum recovery at 5% to 20% of the ASIN's average selling price, with no guarantee. Amazon's own worked example is a £20 phone case, 200 g, sold to a liquidator at 7.5%: gross recovery £1.50, less £0.23 referral and £0.37 processing, net £0.90. On a £20 item you would get back less than a pound.

You cannot preview the recovery value before you submit. You find out afterwards, in the Removal Order Detail report.

### Who can use it

This is the part most guides skip. The overview says you must have a **verified VAT registration number** for the country where the stock is stored, and be enrolled in Amazon's VAT Calculation Service. A UK reseller below the VAT threshold and not voluntarily registered cannot use liquidation at all. If the option is missing from your removal workflow, that is usually why.

Also excluded: dangerous goods, recalled stock, gated products you are not approved to sell, and FBA prohibited products. For unfulfillable stock, only units marked customer damaged, carrier damaged or distributor damaged can be liquidated; expired, defective and warehouse damaged units cannot.

### When it makes sense

For a VAT registered seller with a pallet of slow stock, liquidation can beat paying to have it returned and then selling it yourself for pennies. It also frees capacity quickly: the FAQ says units move to reserved the day after the order and stop counting against your storage limit, and pending removals of any kind stop counting against your inventory score. While liquidation is pending, units can still sell to customers at your normal price, and you get the sale instead.

For a small reseller with a handful of units, the recovery is too small and the cash too slow to matter. Return them and sell them elsewhere.

## Grade and Resell

This one only applies to customer returns that Amazon has decided cannot be sold as new. Through [FBA Grade and Resell](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GUA6RV6UA4DR2MFK?locale=en-GB) Amazon inspects each unit, grades it Used Like New, Very Good, Good or Acceptable, and lists it under your account at a percentage of your new price that you set. You enrol through the automated unfulfillable settings, for the whole catalogue or for chosen ASINs; you cannot send units in manually.

The help page gives a processing fee from 3 August 2026 on units graded sellable: £0.37 to £0.39 for sortable items and £0.43 to £0.48 for non-sortable, by weight, with nothing charged for units graded unsellable. Items under £8 are ineligible, and Amazon recommends it for items selling above £15. Consumables, dated, meltable, hazmat and gated products are excluded. Grading and listing can take up to three weeks, longer in the festive season. Storage stops while a unit is being graded, and the storage and aged inventory clocks restart once it is listed.

For non-consumable returns worth more than about £15, it is usually better than paying to have them returned, because you skip the postage, the inspection and the relisting.

## Choosing, unit by unit

Work down this list for each product you want out.

1. **Can it still sell on Amazon at a price above what it will cost to hold?** Reprice and keep it. Check the next aged inventory assessment date first; [inventory health](/guides/amazon-inventory-health#aged-inventory-surcharge) explains the 15th of the month rule.
2. **Is it a customer return, unsellable as new, worth more than about £15, and not a consumable?** Grade and Resell.
3. **Could you sell it anywhere else for more than the return fee plus your postage out?** Return to you.
4. **Are you VAT registered, with a volume of stock that will not sell anywhere at a price worth your time?** Liquidation.
5. **Is it expired, broken beyond use, restricted, or hazmat you cannot have returned?** Disposal.

A quick test for step 3: if the return fee is £0.71 and the item would fetch £3 on a local selling app, you are £2 ahead before your time. If it would fetch nothing, dispose.

## Timing the removal

- **Before the 15th of the month.** Aged inventory surcharges are assessed on the 15th. A removal submitted before then avoids the charge even if it is not complete.
- **Before October.** Storage for standard size stock roughly doubles from October to December on the [monthly storage fee page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200612770?locale=en-GB), and removals slow to 30 working days or more. Anything you already know is dead stock should be on its way out in September. [Q4 on Amazon](/guides/amazon-q4-peak-season) covers the rest of the peak season.
- **In one order per destination.** Removal fees are per unit, so batching does not save money, but it does mean fewer parcels to check and photograph.

## What goes wrong

- **Choosing disposal to save money.** It is dearer than a return in every weight band.
- **Looking for liquidation without a VAT number.** It is not available to you. Return instead.
- **Wrong return address.** It cannot be changed after the order is created, and stranded and unfulfillable stock use separate address settings.
- **Not photographing removal parcels.** A missing or damaged unit is claimable for a short window, and only with photos.
- **Leaving fulfillable stock listed.** It keeps selling until it is picked, possibly at the price you were trying to escape.
- **Removing in November.** Thirty working days or more, while you pay peak storage.
- **Expecting liquidation to be quick money.** Up to 30 days to find a buyer, then up to 60 days to be paid, for 5% to 20% of the price at best.
