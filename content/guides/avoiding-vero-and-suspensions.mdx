This is for anyone selling branded goods on eBay UK who wants to keep the account. After reading you will know how a listing gets reported under VeRO, how to check a brand before you spend money on stock, what happens after a takedown and how to get it reversed, the handful of reasons accounts actually get restricted or suspended, how the appeal process works and what it can and cannot do, and why the "just open another account" advice ends careers.

None of this is legal advice. Policies and their wording change; every figure and rule below links to eBay's own page, and you should read the current version before acting on it.

## What VeRO is

VeRO stands for Verified Rights Owner. It is the programme through which a brand, a designer, a photographer, a record label or anyone else who owns intellectual property can tell eBay that a listing infringes it. eBay's position, set out in its [intellectual property policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/listing-policies/selling-policies/intellectual-property-vero-program?id=4349), is that when a rights owner reports a listing, the listing has to come down quickly to comply with the law. eBay does not weigh the evidence first. The rights owner asserts, eBay removes, and you find out afterwards.

The things a rights owner can report are wider than fakes:

- Counterfeits and replicas, including items honestly described as copies.
- Unauthorised copies of films, music, software and recordings.
- Photos, images, videos and text you did not create and do not have permission to use, including manufacturer stock images and copied descriptions.
- Brand names or trademarks used on items the brand did not make and that are not specifically compatible with it.
- Third party logos added to your own images.
- Some design rights: an item that copies a protected design without copying the name.

Things a rights owner is not supposed to use VeRO for are just as important: disputes about where you bought the item, how much you sell it for, or the fact that the brand would prefer its products were not resold on eBay. Genuine goods bought legitimately can be resold. That is the principle. In practice some brands report anyway and eBay removes anyway, which is why the rest of this guide exists.

## Checking a brand before you list

The cost of a VeRO report is not the listing; it is the stock behind it. So the check happens before you buy, not before you list.

**1. Read the participant profile.** eBay publishes [VeRO participant profiles](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/protection/verified-rights-owner-profiles): pages written by rights owners explaining what they report and why. Search the brand there. If the profile says, for example, that the brand reports any use of its images, or any listing of products sold through unauthorised channels, believe it. The list is not complete; a brand without a profile can still report you.

**2. Search sold listings.** Look at completed listings for the product on eBay UK. Many active sellers with long histories usually means the brand tolerates resale. A product with almost no sold listings but plenty of demand elsewhere is a signal that listings are being removed.

**3. Search the forums.** Search the brand name with "VeRO" on the [eBay community boards](https://community.ebay.co.uk) and here. Sellers post takedowns within hours.

**4. Look at your own listing with the policy in front of you.** Even for a brand that tolerates resale, the listing itself can trigger a report. The four questions:

- Are these my photos, or the brand's? Use your own, or images from eBay's catalogue.
- Is the description my own words?
- If the item is an accessory or part for a branded product, does every mention of the brand say "for" or "fits" or "compatible with" before it, and is there only one brand in the title?
- Have I used a brand name anywhere as a search term for an item that is not that brand?

Fix all four and most reports that were ever going to arrive do not.

**5. Decide on the paperwork before you buy.** If a brand is known to report genuine resellers, the only thing that reverses a takedown is proof you are entitled to sell: an invoice from a legitimate supplier with your business name on it, or written authorisation from the brand. If you cannot get that, decide now whether you want the stock.

## What a takedown does to your account

You receive a message in My eBay explaining why the listing was removed, which part of the listing was the problem, and the name and email address of the rights owner who reported it. The listing is ended. Fees are credited case by case under the [fee credits policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fee-credits?id=4128), and previous violations count against you, so a first removal is usually credited and a fifth may not be.

What happens to the account depends on the count. One removal on an otherwise clean account is a warning. A pattern of removals, especially for the same brand or the same category of problem, moves through the consequences listed in the policy: warnings, then restrictions (a block on listing in a category, a listing limit, a temporary block on selling), then suspension. eBay does not publish the numbers that trigger each step, and sellers who have been through it report different thresholds. The safe assumption is that a second removal for the same reason is already a pattern.

Two things a takedown does not do. It does not add a defect to your seller standards; VeRO is a policy matter, not a transaction matter. And it does not, on its own, hold your payouts, although a restriction that follows repeated removals can.

## After a takedown: the order of operations

1. **Do not relist.** Relisting the same item after a VeRO removal is the fastest known route from warning to restriction. If you believe it was wrong, fix it through the steps below, not by testing whether the bot notices.
2. **Read the notice properly.** It names the policy and the aspect of the listing. If it is an image or text problem, the fix is to relist with your own photos and words once the matter is closed, and that is usually the end of it.
3. **Contact the rights owner.** Use the email in the notice. State the item, the listing number, where you bought it, and attach the invoice. Ask them to retract the report. eBay's own page says you can request a retraction this way, and retractions happen: a form comes back, you fill it in, they email eBay. Be polite and factual; the person reading it deals with fakes all day.
4. **Send the evidence to eBay.** If you have authorisation from the rights owner or proof of legitimate supply, the Seller Centre [intellectual property page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/protection/intellectual-property) says to send it to vero@ebay.com and to appeal through the Resolution Hub. Include the listing number, the notice, and the invoice or the brand's retraction.
5. **Appeal formally if needed.** A rejected VeRO report and a listing removal are both appealable within six months under eBay's [appeals process](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/account/regulatory/ebay-internal-complaint-handling-system?id=5413). Do this if the rights owner will not respond and you have evidence.
6. **Record it.** Date, brand, listing, reason, outcome. When the next one comes, you will need to show you dealt with the last one.

Most sellers we know who have had a takedown reversed did it through step 3. Most who lost the argument skipped step 3 and argued with eBay, which cannot overrule a rights owner's report on its own judgement.

## Why accounts get restricted or suspended

eBay's [restrictions and suspensions page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/account/account-holds-restrictions-suspensions/account-holds-restrictions-suspensions?id=4190) lists the reasons in general terms. In the order they actually happen to working resellers:

| Cause | What it looks like | How you get out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Money owed | A restriction until you pay outstanding selling costs, or repay eBay for a refund it paid a buyer | Pay it. The notice includes a one time payment option. Payouts may be held until it is cleared. |
| Below Standard | Defect rate over 2% (from more than 4 buyers) or cases closed without your resolution over 2 or 0.3%, evaluated on the 20th | Lower search placement, higher fees, no Promoted Listings, possible limits and holds. Fix the metrics; the seller dashboard shows which orders caused it. |
| Unresolved buyer issues or missing tracking | Open cases eBay had to close, items not received with no tracking | Resolve every case yourself before eBay does; post tracked. |
| Policy breaches | VeRO removals, prohibited items, listing policy violations, selling practices breaches, feedback manipulation | Stop the behaviour, read the policy named in the notice, respond with what you changed. |
| Verification | eBay cannot verify your identity, business details or bank | Upload exactly what is asked for, matching the account details character for character. |
| Linked accounts | Another account you own, or one eBay has linked to you, is restricted or suspended | Resolve the original account first. Appeal the link only if it is genuinely wrong. |
| Suspected account takeover | A hold while eBay checks unusual activity | Confirm your identity through the message in My eBay, change your password, turn on two step verification. |

During a restriction you cannot create or revise listings, active listings may be ended, and payouts may be held. A temporary suspension has an end date; an indefinite one does not, and a permanent one is what it says.

Two habits prevent most of this. First, read every message in My eBay Messages, because that is where the real notices are; a suspension email that does not also appear there is a spoof. Second, keep the money side clean: a card on file that works, a bank that matches your registered name, and no refund ever left for eBay to make on your behalf.

## The appeal process

There are two layers.

**The message in your account.** Every restriction or suspension comes with a message saying what happened and what would resolve it. For money, verification and most policy restrictions, doing exactly what that message asks is the appeal. Do not send a long email about your business; send the document or the payment it names.

**The formal appeal.** eBay's [appeals page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/account/regulatory/ebay-internal-complaint-handling-system?id=5413) covers listing removals, account suspensions, rejected reports and failed verification. You can file within six months of being told about the action; it is free; eBay reviews it and reverses the decision if your appeal gives sufficient grounds. If it is rejected, eBay considers its decision correct and points you to mediation or an out of court settlement body where available, or, as the user agreement puts it, a claim for breach of contract. That is the end of the road inside eBay.

What makes an appeal succeed is evidence that speaks to the stated reason. What makes it fail is everything else. The appeal that works:

- Names the action and the date.
- Says in one or two sentences why it was wrong, with reference to the policy named.
- Attaches the proof: the invoice, the tracking, the retraction from the rights owner, the listing text showing the disclosure.
- Says what you have changed so it will not recur.
- Is short.

Do not appeal a Below Standard rating; it is arithmetic, and the route out is next month's numbers. Do appeal a defect or a late delivery that was caused by something outside your control, through the separate [defect appeal](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/selling-seller-performance/appeal-defect?id=4871) route, because those do change the arithmetic.

## One account per person: the reality

eBay's [multiple accounts policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/identity-policies/multiple-accounts-policy?id=4232) allows more than one account. Sellers keep a buying account and a selling account, or one account per product line, and that is fine as long as each account meets the standards on its own. Every account you open is linked to you, and eBay links accounts well: the same name, address, bank, card, device, phone, IP address, or sometimes just the same stock and photos.

The policy is also explicit that registering a new account, or using another existing one, to get around a restriction, a limit or any other policy consequence is prohibited, and that restrictions on one account may be applied to your other linked accounts. In plain terms:

- If your account is suspended and you open another, the new one is usually found and suspended, and the original suspension becomes permanent because you have now breached a second policy.
- If your account is Below Standard and you move your listings to a fresh account, the fresh account inherits the problem when it is linked, and you have lost the history that would have got you back to Above Standard.
- If a family member opens an account "for you" from the same house, on the same broadband, selling the same stock, it is your account in eBay's eyes.
- An account bought from someone else is a breach in itself.

The one legitimate reason to open a second selling account is a genuinely separate business line while the first account is in good standing. Even then, sellers we know keep it simple: one account, one business, one history. A ten year old account with 20,000 feedback is an asset you cannot buy back.

If you are restricted, the way out is through the restriction. Pay what is owed, fix the metrics, send the documents, wait out the suspension, appeal within six months if you have grounds. Slow, but it works, and the alternative does not.

## What goes wrong

- **Listing with the brand's photos.** The commonest VeRO report of all, and the easiest to avoid. Your own photos, every time.
- **"Genuine, not fake" in the title of a replica.** Honest description does not make an infringing item allowed.
- **Relisting after a removal.** Turns a warning into a restriction.
- **Arguing with eBay instead of the rights owner.** Only the rights owner can retract. Email them first.
- **No invoice.** Without paperwork you cannot answer a report, an appeal or a Trading Standards enquiry. Keep every one.
- **Ignoring the Messages tab.** The restriction notice, the payment link and the verification request all live there.
- **Letting eBay close a case.** Each one is a defect and a step towards Below Standard.
- **The second account.** Covered above. It ends the first.
- **Waiting past six months.** The formal appeal window closes.

## Questions

**Can a brand stop me selling their genuine product?** Not under eBay's policy, which excludes distribution and pricing disputes from VeRO. Some do it anyway. Your defence is an invoice from a legitimate supplier and a polite retraction request; if a brand reports every listing regardless, the practical answer is to sell something else.

**Does a VeRO removal affect my seller level?** No. Seller level is defects, cases and late deliveries. Repeated removals affect the account through restrictions, which is a separate track.

**How many strikes before suspension?** eBay does not say. Treat the second removal for the same reason as the last warning.

**I was suspended and the message says permanent. Is there anything left?** The formal appeal, within six months, with evidence. If that fails, there is no route back on a new account; the policy and the linking make sure of it.
