This is for Amazon sellers, FBA and merchant fulfilled, who have had a return come back opened, broken, incomplete or not at all, and want to know whether they can recover the money. After reading you will know how each type of return is refunded, which losses Amazon covers and which it leaves with you, and how to file a claim with the right evidence inside the deadline.

Rules here come from Amazon's UK help pages and announcements, linked where they are used. Returns policy changes often, and in places Amazon's own pages disagree with each other; where they do, we say so. Nothing here is legal or financial advice.

## Two systems, two claim routes

The first thing to get straight is which system your order is in, because the claim process is completely different.

| | FBA order | Merchant fulfilled (FBM) order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who handles the return | Amazon, into its warehouse | You, to your return address |
| Who refunds the customer | Amazon, and debits you | Amazon at first scan for prepaid labels, otherwise you |
| Who grades the item | Amazon | You |
| Where you claim | Inventory reimbursement (portal or Get support) | SAFE-T claims |
| Main deadline | 45 to 105 days after the refund | 60 days after the refund is charged to you |

A SAFE-T claim cannot be used for an FBA order. The FBA route cannot be used for an FBM order. Filing in the wrong place wastes the window.

## FBA returns

### When customers can return

Amazon's [FBA customer returns page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200379860?locale=en-GB) says most items can be returned within 30 days of receipt, with case-by-case exceptions. Three extensions matter:

- **Festive returns.** Most orders placed between 1 November and 31 December can be returned until 31 January. Budget for a wave of returns in January.
- **Baby items.** Eligible FBA baby products can be returned new and unopened within 90 days.
- **Faulty goods.** For functional defects, customers have two years under UK and EU rules. Those units come back as unsellable, and you must tell Amazon within 30 days whether to dispose of them or return them to you; if you do not, Amazon may choose, at your expense.

### When the customer is refunded

Amazon often refunds when the return is requested, and sometimes refunds without asking for the item back at all (a returnless refund). Your account is debited the refund. Returnless refunds do not show in the FBA customer returns report; they appear as a product charge in the refund section of the Payments dashboard. When a return is processed, you also lose part of the referral fee and all of the fulfilment fee; [where the money leaks](/guides/amazon-where-the-money-leaks#what-a-return-actually-costs) sets out the sums, and some products also pay a [returns processing fee](/blog/amazon-returns-processing-fee-2025).

### What happens when it arrives

Amazon's [customer returns reimbursement policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G9N934L7Y4SFWPJ4?locale=en-GB) sets out the outcomes:

| Amazon's finding | What happens to the unit | Do you get paid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sellable | Back into your stock | No, you have the unit |
| Unsellable, Amazon's fault (warehouse or carrier) | Kept by Amazon | Yes, automatically |
| Not returned within 45 days of the refund | Not applicable | Yes, automatically |
| Lost in transit to or from the customer | Not applicable | Yes, automatically |
| Not authentic, or a completely different product | Kept by Amazon | Yes, automatically |
| Customer damaged, defective, recalled or against policy | Into your unfulfillable stock | No |
| Damaged box, missing seal, missing packaging, label over box, different box | Into your unfulfillable stock | No |

That last row is the one sellers argue with most. A sealed product that comes back with a torn box is, for reimbursement purposes, the customer's doing, however it happened.

### Units Amazon disposes of straight away

The same page lists returns Amazon disposes of immediately, whatever your settings say: dangerous goods (perfume, batteries, razors and anything containing alcohol or chemicals), broken glass, opened personal care items, consumables such as food and vitamins, items with expiry dates or lot numbers, bedding, and baby items such as soothers and bottles. If you have not set your automated unfulfillable settings at all, Amazon disposes of every unfulfillable return immediately.

Set those settings now if you have not: [inventory health](/guides/amazon-inventory-health#unfulfillable-inventory) explains the choice between return and dispose.

### When you can claim

Amazon says it reimburses almost all warehouse and customer return cases automatically since January 2025. When it misses one, the [customer return claims page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G84Z6B8BTKVNV53S?locale=en-GB) gives you a window of **45 to 105 days after the refund or replacement**. Check the Eligible for claim tab in the Inventory Defect and Reimbursement portal, then file using the order ID. The reimbursement is the refund amount minus fees and VAT.

Amazon's general returns page still says returnless refunds can be claimed for 18 months. The claims page and Amazon's [January 2025 announcement](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/d0fc997e-de50-4100-9803-76b7993b66df) say 105 days. Use 105.

### Disputing a grade

If Amazon graded a return as customer damaged and you think it was damaged in the warehouse, you cannot argue from a screenshot. Amazon's [removal claims page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G9ZB3H4DP4H72U6R?locale=en-GB) says the unit must be physically returned to you through a removal order, and the claim filed within 60 days of that removal being delivered. Photograph it on arrival: the unit, the damage, the LPN sticker, and the removal parcel's label.

Whether this is worth it depends on value. Removing a unit under 200 g costs £0.49 on the [current fee page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200685050?locale=en-GB). On a £40 item that is sensible. On a £6 item, let it go.

### Grade and Resell

Instead of returning or disposing of unfulfillable returns, you can let Amazon grade them and relist them as used under your account through [FBA Grade and Resell](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GUA6RV6UA4DR2MFK?locale=en-GB). You enrol in the automated unfulfillable settings, either the whole catalogue or chosen ASINs, and set the used price as a percentage of your new price.

The help page says a processing fee applies from 3 August 2026 on each unit graded as sellable: £0.37 to £0.39 for sortable items and £0.43 to £0.48 for non-sortable items, by weight. (The same page's FAQ still says fees are waived until further notice; check your Payments dashboard.) Items under £8 are ineligible, as are dangerous goods, expiry-dated, meltable and gated products, and Amazon recommends only enrolling items that sell above £15.

For a reseller, Grade and Resell is worth switching on for non-consumable products over about £15 where a used copy will still sell. It saves the removal fee and the time. For low-value lines it does not.

## FBM returns

### Prepaid return labels

Amazon's [prepaid returns page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G202072200?locale=en-GB) says every seller with a UK default return address is enrolled in Amazon prepaid return labels. Returns within Amazon's policy are authorised automatically and the customer gets a tracked Royal Mail or Evri label. You are charged the label at first scan, priced from the weight and dimensions on your listing, so keep those accurate.

Exempt from prepaid labels: handmade, dangerous goods, heavy and bulky items, and some categories. You can also exempt individual SKUs priced above £100. For low-value items where return postage is more than the item, you can set up returnless resolution rules instead.

### When the customer is refunded

Returns with an Amazon prepaid label are refunded at first scan, when the carrier picks up the parcel, before you see the item. For everything else you issue the refund.

The deadline for doing so changed on 26 January 2026. Amazon's [announcement](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/11268c92-5a5c-4125-9e59-49e433d0d151) says you now have **four calendar days** from receiving the return, up from two business days, before Amazon may refund automatically. The prepaid returns help page still says two business days. Assume the shorter one if you can, and never leave it past four days.

This matters for claims. If Amazon refunds automatically because you did not act in time, the announcement says you lose the right to a SAFE-T claim, except in cases such as the return being lost in transit or wrongly marked as delivered.

### Partial refunds and deductions

If the item comes back used, damaged or incomplete and you are processing the refund yourself, use **Apply refund reduction** on the refund page ([issue a partial refund](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201650140?locale=en-GB)). You grade the item, accessories and packaging, and you must upload a photo of the damage or difference. You can charge return postage to the buyer only where the return reason is the buyer's responsibility (changed mind, ordered by mistake, found it cheaper) and they used a prepaid label. You cannot deduct postage when you sent the wrong or a damaged item, or on clothing, shoes, jewellery and watches, which have free returns.

The Guided Refund workflow Amazon mentions in its announcement does the same job with evidence attached, and a documented partial refund is usually better than a full refund followed by a claim.

## SAFE-T claims

### What SAFE-T covers

SAFE-T is how you ask Amazon to pay back a refund it issued on your behalf on an FBM order. Amazon's [SAFE-T page for prepaid returns](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G202175000?locale=en-GB) lists the cases:

- The customer misused the return or refund policy.
- The item came back damaged or used and unsellable.
- The item came back materially different or incomplete, or an empty box.
- A replacement was delivered but the original was not returned, or a refund was issued as well.
- A refund was issued outside the return window, or for a non-returnable item for a buyer-responsibility reason.
- You were charged for the return label when the buyer was responsible, were overcharged for it, or the buyer picked a false reason to avoid paying.

Two conditions apply to all of them: Amazon, not you, refunded the customer, and the money has been taken from your account.

### What it does not cover

The same page excludes: orders lost or damaged in transit (claim from the carrier instead), orders you refunded yourself, refunds caused by your own mistakes such as late dispatch or poor packaging, returns you refused, FBA orders, chargebacks, orders under an A-to-z Guarantee claim, and Invoice by Amazon orders.

Note the conflict over lost returns. The help page says a return lost in transit is a carrier claim; the January 2026 announcement says a lost return is one of the few cases still open to SAFE-T after an automatic refund. In practice outcomes vary. Try the carrier first and keep the tracking.

### Deadlines

| Step | Deadline |
| --- | --- |
| File the claim | 60 calendar days from the refund being charged to your account |
| Answer a request for more information | 7 calendar days, or the claim is denied |
| Challenge a decision | 7 calendar days from the decision, once only |
| Amazon's review | Within 7 calendar days of filing |

Do not challenge the same decision again without new evidence; the SAFE-T page warns that repeat challenges can be flagged as misuse, and that misrepresentation in a claim can lead to suspension.

### What you get

Reimbursements exclude VAT and fees such as the referral fee, and may be limited to a percentage of the order value depending on the damage. A mildly used item with resale value will not be paid in full. The claim reasons include "minor damage" and "mildly used" precisely so Amazon can pay part.

### Filing one

1. Orders, Manage SAFE-T claims, File a new SAFE-T claim.
2. Enter the order ID and check eligibility. An error here means the order is not eligible; do not keep trying.
3. Pick the reason and sub-reason that match exactly. The wrong reason can get the claim denied.
4. Explain in two or three plain sentences what you sent, what came back and why you are not responsible.
5. Attach the evidence.
6. Reply only through View message on the claim. Seller support cannot help with SAFE-T.

### The evidence that wins

- Photos of the item as dispatched, if you take them for higher value orders.
- The returned parcel with the return label visible, before opening.
- The item as received, whole, then close-ups of the damage or missing parts.
- For different items or counterfeits: serial numbers, model numbers, the barcode, side by side with your listing.
- For electrical items where damage cannot be seen, a manufacturer's report.
- For label disputes, the return reason the buyer gave and anything in buyer messages that contradicts it.

Set up a place to photograph returns and use it every time, even for returns that look fine. It takes a minute, and without the "before opening" photo most damage claims are hard to win.

## What goes wrong

- **Filing a SAFE-T claim on an FBA order.** It is ineligible. FBA losses go through the inventory reimbursement route.
- **Refunding manually and then claiming.** If you issued the refund, SAFE-T will not pay it back. Use a partial refund with photos instead.
- **Missing the four day refund deadline.** Amazon refunds for you, and your claim rights mostly go with it.
- **No photo before opening.** The most common reason a damage claim fails.
- **Arguing with a torn box.** On FBA, packaging damage is treated as customer damage and is not reimbursed.
- **Removing a £5 item to dispute its grade.** The removal fee and your time cost more than you will recover.
- **Missing the January wave.** Festive orders can be returned until 31 January. Keep cash back for it.
- **Inaccurate listing weights on FBM.** You pay return labels on the listed weight, and overcharges caused by wrong dimensions are not reimbursed.
