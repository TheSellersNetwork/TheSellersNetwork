For sellers who have stock arriving and need it to leave again in a form Amazon will accept. After reading you will know the prep rule for each type of product you are likely to buy, what to own before your first box, how to prep a hundred units in an hour rather than an evening, and how to decide whether a prep centre is a cost or a saving for you. It follows on from decision 5 in [the working manual](/guides/how-to-start-amazon-fba-uk#5-prep-at-home-or-use-a-prep-centre) and pairs with [adding products and creating shipments](/guides/amazon-adding-products-and-creating-shipments).

One change frames everything below. Amazon's [label service page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200483750?locale=en-GB) states that from 1 July 2026 Amazon no longer offers prep and label services for FBA shipments in its European stores, which includes the UK. There is no longer a "let Amazon do it" option at the warehouse. Either you prep, or someone you pay preps, and units that arrive wrong are defective on arrival.

## What Amazon means by prepped

Prep is whatever has to happen to a unit so that it survives Amazon's warehouse, can be scanned, and reaches the customer looking new. Amazon's [product packaging requirements](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200141500?locale=en-GB) page and its sub pages set the standard. The rules that matter for retail stock:

### Every unit

- An Amazon barcode label (FNSKU) on each unit, on a flat surface, covering every other barcode except a serial number or a Transparency code, applied to the outside of any bag or wrap. Since 31 March 2026 this applies to all resellers regardless of whether the product has an EAN ([barcode requirements](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201100910?locale=en-GB)).
- No retailer price tags, security tags, pre priced labels or leaflets. Amazon lists these as marketing materials it will not accept.
- Sold as new means sealed, complete, and in packaging that still looks new after a label is added.

### Poly bags

Anything loose, anything with an opening, anything in flimsy packaging, and anything that would spill if the packaging failed goes in a poly bag. Amazon's requirements: the bag must be transparent, at least 1.5 mil (0.0254 mm) thick, fully sealed, and must not extend more than 7.5 cm beyond the product; if the barcode cannot be scanned through the bag, a label goes on the outside. Any bag with an opening of more than 12 cm must carry a suffocation warning, printed on the bag or on a sticker. Buy bags with the warning printed; it removes a step.

### Boxed products

A boxed unit must be six sided, must not collapse under moderate pressure, and must have a lid or opening that will not come open on its own. If the lid lifts, tape it. If the box has windows or perforations, Amazon expects it to survive a 125 cm drop test (one drop on each face and one on a corner); if it would not, bag it.

### Fragile and glass

Solid six sided box with nothing exposed. In a multi item set every piece individually wrapped, nothing able to move inside, and the whole thing passing a five drop test from 125 cm ([fragile units](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200280160?locale=en-GB)). In practice: bubble wrap, then a box, for anything that would break.

### Liquids

Containers of one litre or less with a double sealed screw cap; glass in a corrugated box; multi unit bundles in a box with the top marked; must survive three drops from 125 cm without leaking ([liquids](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200280130?locale=en-GB)). A pump top or flip cap that is not sealed under the lid is a leak waiting to happen, so bag those as well.

### Sets and bundles

If the listing is a set (a pack of three, a gift set), the units must be sealed together so they cannot separate, labelled to say so ("Sold as set, do not separate"), and the inner barcodes hidden. Amazon also requires a multipack to have its own ASIN; you cannot make your own pack of a brand's product without the brand's barcode or permission, so this applies to retailer multipacks, not to packs you assemble.

### Sharp, small, and other special cases

Sharp edges fully covered so they cannot cut through the packaging ([sharp units](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200280150?locale=en-GB)). Hygiene and topical products (anything used on or in the body: toothbrushes, creams, shavers) need an intact factory seal, because Amazon treats any return without one as unsellable ([hygienic and topical products](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GQFZBRVVEEG3ZE9C?locale=en-GB)). Scented items sealed so the scent does not transfer. Expiry dated products with the date visible on the outside in DD-MM-YYYY or MM-YYYY, stickered over if the printed format differs ([expiry dates](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201003420?locale=en-GB)).

### A decision table

| Product as it arrives | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Sealed box, firm, no windows | Label only |
| Box with a loose lid or a window | Tape the lid, or bag with suffocation warning |
| Blister pack, sealed | Label; bag if the card is flimsy |
| Bottle or tube of liquid, screw cap | Bag; box if glass |
| Anything glass or ceramic | Bubble wrap and box |
| Loose item, no packaging | Bag with suffocation warning, label on the bag |
| Multipack held with a sleeve | Tape or bag so it cannot separate, add a set label |
| Toothbrush, cream, razor | Check the factory seal; if broken or absent, do not send as new |
| Food or anything dated | Check date against the shelf life table, add a date sticker if the format is wrong |
| Retailer sticker on it | Peel it, every time |

When in doubt, over prep. Amazon's requirements are a floor. A bag and a label cost pence; a unit received as damaged costs the unit.

## The kit

Described by type, because brands change and the type does not. Everything here fits in a cupboard and on one table.

**A label printer.** The single purchase that changes prep from a chore to a task. Amazon's [barcode page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200141490?locale=en-GB) says its tools are optimised for thermal printers, that inkjet is not acceptable, and that labels need 300 dpi or better, black on white, non reflective, removable adhesive, sized between 25.4 mm x 50.8 mm and 50.8 mm x 76.2 mm. A direct thermal printer needs no ink, prints one label at a time, and handles the 10.2 cm x 15.2 cm box labels as well. If you have a laser printer already, Amazon supports sheets of 21, 24, 27, 40 or 44 labels on A4; it works, but you print whole sheets and peel.

**Labels.** Rolls of thermal labels at one of Amazon's supported sizes for units, and 10 cm x 15 cm rolls for box and carrier labels. Buy removable adhesive; the customer, and Amazon on a return, may need to peel them.

**Poly bags.** Three sizes cover most stock: small (about 15 cm x 20 cm), medium (about 23 cm x 30 cm) and large (about 30 cm x 40 cm), self seal, with the suffocation warning printed. A heat sealer is nice to have and not necessary.

**Bubble wrap and paper.** A roll of small bubble wrap for glass and fragile items, and kraft paper or air pillows as box filler. Amazon's [routing page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200141510?locale=en-GB) forbids packing peanuts, shredded paper, crinkle wrap and foam strips as filler, so do not reuse those from deliveries you receive.

**Boxes.** Double walled, new or in good condition, in one or two sizes you learn the weight limits of. Reused retailer boxes are fine if every old barcode is covered or struck through and no side is longer than 63.5 cm. Keep the size that fills to about 12 kg; you never need the heavy label and one person can carry it.

**Tape and a dispenser.** Brown packing tape on a gun dispenser for boxes; clear tape for lids and sets. Do not wrap a whole box in tape; Amazon rejects boxes covered in tape because they cannot be recycled.

**Scales.** Digital, to 30 kg, reading to 10 g. Every box weight you type into Send to Amazon comes from these. A bathroom scale is not accurate enough and Amazon compares your figures with what it expects.

**A tape measure**, a **safety knife** with a covered blade (Amazon's safety section asks for exactly this, so a blade never ends up inside a box), a **label peeler or heat gun** for retailer stickers, and **marker pens** for striking out barcodes and pencilling box numbers.

**Optional.** A cheap barcode scanner speeds up scan and pack in the shipment workflow and catches wrong labels before they leave. A phone document scanner for invoices. A small shelf unit so stock waiting to be prepped is not on the floor.

## Keeping prep efficient

Prep is where a home based seller's time goes, and time is the cost nobody puts in the calculator. Sellers we know who prep quickly do these things.

**Prep in batches, not in deliveries.** Let stock accumulate for a few days, then prep and ship once a week. One shipment of eight products beats four shipments of two: fewer labels, fewer collections, fewer chances for Amazon to split.

**Do one operation across every unit before starting the next.** Unbox everything. Peel every sticker. Bag everything that needs a bag. Print all the labels in one run from the shipment's Print all SKU labels button. Label everything. Then box. Switching between tasks per unit is where the hour goes.

**Check the listing before you label.** A label on the wrong variant is a unit going to the wrong ASIN. Scan the EAN, compare to the SKU label's product name, then stick.

**Print the label first, then bag.** The label goes on the outside of the bag, so printing after bagging means handling every unit twice.

**Weigh and measure as you close each box** and write both on the box in pencil. Then the shipment workflow is a copying job, not a search.

**Keep a running count.** Units in from the retailer, units prepped, units in the shipment. Stock goes missing between the doorstep and the box more often than between the box and Amazon, and the count is how you find out which retailer delivery was short before the return window closes.

**Reconcile after receipt.** When a shipment closes, compare received units to sent units on the shipment's contents tab. A shortfall is a reconciliation request; the [claim window is short](/guides/how-to-start-amazon-fba-uk#the-numbers-you-will-need) and you need your counts to claim.

At around a few hundred units a month, a weekly prep session of two to three hours is normal. When that stretches to a full day, or when the spare room has become a warehouse, the next section applies.

## When a prep centre makes sense

A prep centre is a business that receives your stock from retailers, checks it, preps it to Amazon's standard, creates or completes the shipment on your account, and hands it to the carrier. The economics are simple: you are buying back your hours at a per unit price, and you are gaining a delivery address that is not your house.

**It makes sense when** your hourly value is above what prep costs per hour of your time; your volume is high enough that the fixed part of the fee is spread thin; you are buying from retailers that deliver to a business address happily; or you travel, work shifts, or live somewhere a courier collection is a problem.

**It does not make sense when** margins are already thin (the fee comes straight off net profit, and on a £2 margin a £1 prep fee is half your return); you are still learning what Amazon expects (refusals teach you faster than a prep centre's report does); or your stock comes mostly from retailers that only ship to the billing address.

### Pricing models

There are two, and most centres use both.

| Model | How it works | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Per unit | A flat price per unit that covers receiving, a basic check, labelling and boxing; extras (bagging, bubble wrap, sets, expiry stickers, removing stickers) charged on top | Small products in volume, simple prep |
| Per shipment or per box | A price per outbound box or per shipment plus a smaller per unit rate | Bulky or mixed stock, fewer larger units |

On top of either: storage after a free period (usually a few days), a charge for returns and removal orders sent to them, and sometimes a monthly minimum. Ask for the full price list and run last month's real shipments through it before you sign anything. The right question is not "what is the per unit fee" but "what would last month have cost in total".

### The billing address trap

This is the one that catches people. Your Amazon account, your invoices and your retailer accounts should all carry the same business name and address, because that consistency is what makes an invoice usable for brand approval and authenticity complaints. A prep centre's address is a delivery address, not your billing address. Two consequences:

1. Some retailers will only deliver to the card's billing address, or will cancel orders shipping to an address that looks like a commercial forwarding operation. You lose those retailers, or you ship to your home and forward, which defeats the object.
2. Never put the prep centre's address as your billing address to get round it. Then your invoices carry an address that does not match your Amazon account, and every approval and complaint response that depends on an invoice becomes harder.

The workable pattern: billing address is your business address everywhere, delivery address is the prep centre where the retailer allows it, and a small number of "home delivery only" retailers stay home prepped. Most sellers using a prep centre keep a small home prep kit for exactly this.

### Questions to ask before you commit

- What is the turnaround from delivery to shipment created, in working days, and what happens in November?
- Do you check units against the purchase list and report shortages and damage, and how quickly?
- Do you photograph damaged or short deliveries so the retailer claim has evidence?
- What do you charge for bagging, bubble wrap, sets, expiry stickers, and removing retailer stickers?
- What is the free storage period and the daily rate after it?
- Do you handle dangerous goods and expiry dated stock, and what do you charge?
- How does the client see their stock: a portal, a spreadsheet, an email?
- What happens when a delivery arrives with no reference on it?
- Are you insured for stock on your premises, and to what value?
- Do you work inside the client's Seller Central as a user with limited permissions, or does the client create the shipment and you pack to it?
- What happens to returns and removal orders sent to your address?
- What is your notice period?

Then send a small test batch before moving everything.

### How to hand over

1. **Add them as a user** in Seller Central with the minimum permissions they need (typically inventory and shipments, not payments or account settings). Use their own login, never share yours; the [manual's rule](/guides/how-to-start-amazon-fba-uk#staying-on-the-platform) about one account and separate logins applies here.
2. **Send them the purchase list** for every delivery before it arrives: retailer, order number, product, ASIN, quantity, and what prep you expect. This is what they check against, and a prep centre without a list cannot tell you a delivery was short.
3. **Put a reference on the order.** Your name or client code in the delivery address line so the centre can match parcels to you.
4. **Agree the default prep** in writing: poly bag anything loose, bubble wrap glass, peel stickers, label everything, box to a weight limit. Then you only need to specify exceptions.
5. **Set the ship from address** in Send to Amazon to the prep centre. It cannot be changed after a shipment is confirmed, and the partnered carrier quote and collection are built on it.
6. **Reconcile weekly**: their received report against your purchase list, and Amazon's received units against their shipped count. Both gaps are money; the first is a retailer claim, the second is a reconciliation request or a question for the centre.

Keep the home kit. There will always be a retailer that only delivers to you, a return that comes home, and a week the centre is closed.

## What goes wrong

- **Labels on bags that then get bagged again.** The barcode must be on the outside of the final packaging.
- **Lids taped with glossy tape over the barcode.** Tape over a barcode makes it reflective and unscannable. Label on top of tape, never under.
- **Bags with no suffocation warning.** Any opening over 12 cm needs one. Buy printed bags and the problem disappears.
- **Bathroom scale weights.** Wrong box weights mean carrier surcharges and a defect on your inbound performance. Buy the proper scale.
- **Retailer stickers left on.** "Reduced" stickers and security tags arrive in front of customers and come back as not as described.
- **Billing address changed to the prep centre.** Every invoice from that day is weaker evidence. Keep billing at your business address.
- **No purchase list sent to the prep centre.** A short delivery is then your word against the retailer's with no third party count.
- **Assuming Amazon will fix it at the dock.** Since July 2026 there is no paid prep or labelling at the warehouse in the UK; the unit arrives right or it arrives defective.
- **Choosing a centre on per unit price alone.** Extras, storage and minimums decide the real cost. Price a real month.
