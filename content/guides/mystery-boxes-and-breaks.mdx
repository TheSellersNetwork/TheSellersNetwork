This is for UK sellers who sell, or are thinking of selling, mystery boxes, surprise sets, repacks or card breaks in live shows. After reading it you will know what each platform requires, where UK law is clear, where it is not, and how to set these sales up so they are honest with buyers and low risk for you. It is general information from the platforms' UK pages, the legislation and the regulators, not legal advice. Platform rules in this area change often; Whatnot updated its sealed product policy three days before this guide was published. Check the linked pages before every new format.

## What UK law says, and what it does not

### There is no specific ruling

We could not find any Gambling Commission ruling, guidance or statement specifically about physical mystery boxes, repacks or trading card breaks sold by resellers. Nor could we find one from the ASA or the Competition and Markets Authority dealing with them as a category. Anyone who tells you these formats are "definitely legal" or "definitely gambling" in the UK is going further than any official source we could find.

What exists is general law and some adjacent statements.

- **The lottery test.** Under [section 14 of the Gambling Act 2005](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/19/section/14), a lottery is an arrangement where people pay to take part and prizes are allocated to some of them by a process that relies wholly on chance. Lotteries cannot be run for commercial gain, as the Gambling Commission's [guidance on raffles](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/raffling-big-ticket-items-prizes-pitfalls-and-potential-risks) makes clear.
- **The loot box statements.** In 2017 the Gambling Commission said of video game loot boxes that a [key factor](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/loot-boxes-within-video-games) is whether items won by chance can be considered money or money's worth. Physical cards and collectables plainly are money's worth. In 2022 the Government [decided not to change the Gambling Act](https://www.gov.uk/government/calls-for-evidence/loot-boxes-in-video-games-call-for-evidence/outcome/government-response-to-the-call-for-evidence-on-loot-boxes-in-video-games) to cover loot boxes; the response mentions trading cards only as a comparison raised by respondents.

### How the lottery test sits against these formats

The honest position is that a format is lower risk the more it looks like buying a product with a known, fair value, and higher risk the more it looks like paying for a chance at a prize.

| Feature | Lower risk | Higher risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What every buyer gets | Goods worth at least what they paid, described in advance | Most buyers get little; a few get a lot |
| Spread of values | Narrow; items broadly similar | A "chase" item worth many times the price |
| How it is sold | "A box of five pre-owned paperbacks from this list" | "Could you be the one to hit the £500 card" |
| Extras | None, or the same bonus for everyone | Bounties, golden tickets, prizes for the best pull |
| Buyers | Adults | Children able to buy |

Every platform's rules below push sellers towards the left-hand column. That is not a coincidence: it is where the legal argument that a buyer is purchasing goods, not a chance at a prize, is strongest.

### Consumer law still applies in full

Whatever gambling law says, the ordinary rules on selling goods apply. Goods sold by description must [match the description](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/11) when you sell as a trader. The ASA's advice on [mystery gifts and prizes](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/promotional-marketing-mystery-gifts.html) says ads must give enough information about the nature of the item for people to decide whether they want to take part. Claims about value, rarity and past pulls must be true and provable.

## Platform rules

### TikTok Shop UK

TikTok's [Gambling, Gifting and Surprise-based Product Guidelines](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=1514396635760386&default_language=en-GB) are the most detailed of the three.

**Surprise-based products** (mystery boxes, mystery bundles, surprise trading card packs, advent calendars) are restricted, not banned. The requirements:

- Not sold to anyone under 18.
- No gambling terms or imagery: no dice, roulette wheels, "bet", "chance" or "lottery". Never call the contents prizes or say they can be won.
- The nature of the product in the title ("mystery box").
- Images and written descriptions of what the buyer may receive: quantity, category, every variation, and a fixed number of items (for example, 5 from a pool of 10), never a range.
- A statement that selection is random and duplicates are possible.
- Every surprise-based product priced under £100, with the overall value stated, and the price no more than the current retail value of the items inside.

**Surprise Sets and card breaks** are auction formats for eligible sellers only.

- Collectables category only; sealed trading cards only, from the same brand and sub-category, manufacturer-sealed and unmodified.
- A maximum bid of £500 per set.
- The ratio between the lowest and highest value item must not exceed 1:10.
- The probability or quantity of each item shown in the listing and throughout the LIVE.
- The official TikTok randomisation tool for every reveal, all inventory on camera before and during, nothing swapped off camera.
- No value claims: no floors, ceilings, averages, "worth up to", market price or comps, grading-based value, "jackpot" or "golden ticket", investment talk or false scarcity.
- Buyers over 18. Customers with fewer than 25 lifetime orders can buy up to 8 break items a day, and anyone buying 20 or more in a day gets a 15-minute cooling-off period.

### Whatnot

Whatnot has four policies that apply.

**[Surprise Sets Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/34107510260237-Surprise-Sets-Policy)** (a set of items split between several buyers, each assigned an item from a published list):

- The show's primary selling format must be Surprise Sets.
- A full checklist of every item in each set: brand, product name, condition and quantity.
- No prizes, and no floor, ceiling or average value.
- One product type per set; no mixing, for example, lipstick and blush, or two different card games.
- Every item revealed and kept on screen; no wheels or machines; no sets spanning shows; no pre-sales off the platform; no buyers related to the seller.
- Minimum item values in some categories: £150 for Bags, £50 for Accessories and for Sneakers and Streetwear, £35 for Sports Memorabilia.
- **UK only:** you must use Whatnot's in-app Surprise Set Feature. Physical randomisers, third-party randomisers and concealed containers such as bags, boxes or drawers are not allowed, and the checklist must be typed into the feature, not replaced with "item 1".

**Single surprise items** (a bundle, a pallet, a storage unit) sit under the same page: describe the contents accurately (brand, item type, condition, quantity), and never suggest one might contain something rare.

**[Card Breaks Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/34107485220237-Card-Breaks-Policy)**:

- The whole break visible on camera from the sale to the end, including hands and packs. This also applies to a single buyer's pack opened live.
- The break rules in the show notes or listings, including what counts as base or bulk and whether it is sent.
- Cards only; no memorabilia in breaks.
- No prizes, no wheels (physical or digital).
- Every buyer receives at least one card per purchase. If you can guarantee a card from the break, sell no more spots than there are cards.
- All product opened in the same show; no pre-sales off Whatnot; no participants from your household, family or staff.
- If a break does not fill, you can cancel it from Seller Hub and buyers are refunded, with [no cancellation fee](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/360061603971-Cancel-an-order-before-you-ship).

**[Professionally Sealed Surprise Products](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/39618879679757-Professionally-Sealed-Surprise-Products)**: sealed surprise products, including repacks, can only be sold, broken or given away if they are on Whatnot's Identified Product List. From 7 December 2026 they must not contain graded or packaged items with NFC, RFID or similar tags unless each is individually shielded.

**[Gambling and Purchase-Based Prize Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/4410443596813-Gambling-and-Purchase-Based-Prize-Policy)**: no bounties ("an extra box if someone hits a holo"), guaranteed extras only for some spots, guess or mapping games, "wars", golden tickets, cascading sets, raffles for unsold spots, or prizes for the best pull of the night.

### eBay Live

The [eBay Live policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behaviour-policies/ebay-live-policy?id=5478) bans chance-based sales, including sales that give an opportunity to win an item rather than a clearly defined purchase. Within that:

- **Mystery boxes and grab bags are allowed** if the description gives general, accurate information about what is included and does not single out valuable or collectable items unless every purchase includes them.
- **Case, box and pack breaks are only for pre-approved sellers**, and buyers must receive the full pack, box or slot they bought.
- Openings must stay in frame and be clearly visible throughout.
- Buyers lose the change-of-mind return on rip-and-ship packs and case breaks once opened, but keep their other statutory rights.

## Running these formats well

A checklist that satisfies all three platforms and keeps you towards the lower-risk end of the law.

1. **Publish the full list.** Every item, its condition and how many there are. No "and more".
2. **Keep values close together.** Make each box or spot worth at least its price. If one item is worth many times the rest, sell it on its own.
3. **Never market the chase.** No "could be the one", no past big pulls as a selling point, no stated floor or ceiling.
4. **No extras tied to outcomes.** A bonus is fine only if every buyer gets the same one.
5. **Use the platform's randomiser.** On Whatnot in the UK and on TikTok it is required; everywhere, it is the evidence that the assignment was fair.
6. **Keep everything on camera.** From sealed product to the last card in the post pile.
7. **Adults only.** TikTok requires it; it is sensible everywhere.
8. **Refund unfilled breaks.** Do not roll unsold spots into a raffle or give them away to fill the break.

Price the box from the contents, not from the excitement. If you cannot make the numbers work with a fair spread of values, the product is the problem. [Sourcing for resale](/guides/sourcing-for-resale) and [pricing from sold comps](/guides/pricing-from-sold-comps) help with that side.

## What goes wrong

- **Advertising the hit.** It breaks TikTok's and Whatnot's rules and makes the sale look like a prize draw.
- **Concealed containers on Whatnot UK.** Bags, boxes and drawers are not allowed for Surprise Sets in the UK.
- **Selling unlisted repacks.** On Whatnot, sealed surprise products must be on the Identified Product List.
- **Bounties and golden tickets.** Banned on Whatnot as purchase-based prizes.
- **Box value below the price.** It breaks TikTok's pricing rule and invites complaints.
- **Opening off camera.** One accusation of swapping costs more than the break made.
- **Breaks on eBay Live without approval.** Only pre-approved sellers may run them.
