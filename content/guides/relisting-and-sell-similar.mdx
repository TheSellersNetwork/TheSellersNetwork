This is for eBay UK sellers with stock that has not sold and a nagging feeling that they should "refresh" it. It explains the three ways a listing comes back to life, what each does to your fees, your watchers and your sales history, how eBay's Good 'Til Cancelled format renews on its own, and the one policy that catches people who relist carelessly. After reading it you will know, for any listing, whether to leave it, revise it, relist it or start again, and you will be able to say why. Fees quoted are from eBay's own pages and change; check the linked page before relying on them.

## Three actions, three different results

eBay gives you three ways to put an item back in front of buyers, and sellers use the names loosely, which is where the confusion starts.

| Action | What eBay does | Item number | Watchers and history | When it is available |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Automatic renewal (Good 'Til Cancelled) | A fixed price listing renews every month by itself until it sells or you end it | Same | Kept. The listing never stops. | Every fixed price listing, by default |
| Relist | Puts an ended or unsold listing back up with the same details | New | Linked to the old listing; watchers of the ended item see a link to the relisted one | Ended or unsold items in My eBay or Seller Hub, while eBay still holds them |
| Sell similar | Copies an existing listing's details into a brand new listing | New | None. A clean start with no link to the original | Any listing, active, sold or ended |

The [relisting items](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/creating-managing-listings/relisting-items?id=4147) help page describes the first two. Sell similar is a button in the same menus and does what its name says.

## Good 'Til Cancelled: the default you are already using

Every fixed price listing on eBay UK is Good 'Til Cancelled. The [listing durations](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/creating-managing-listings/selecting-listing-duration?id=4652) page says the item is relisted every month on the same day until it sells or you end it; if you listed on the 29th, 30th or 31st the date adjusts in shorter months and reverts afterwards. Auctions run 1, 3, 5, 7 or 10 days (1 day needs a feedback score of at least 10), and that is the only place you choose a duration.

What renewal costs depends on which kind of seller you are.

- **Private sellers.** The [selling fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4364) page gives UK private sellers 300 free listings a month (400 with an eBay Shop), charges 35p for each listing beyond that, and states that when existing listings are automatically renewed or relisted the listing fee is not charged again. There are no final value fees for UK private sellers on domestic sales; buyers pay a Buyer Protection fee on top of your price, set out on the [Buyer Protection for sellers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/selling/pricing-items/buyer-protection-information-sellers?id=5595) page.
- **Business sellers.** The [business seller fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-business-sellers?id=4809) page says listing fees and optional upgrade fees apply each month for Good 'Til Cancelled listings. Free allowances depend on your Shop: none without a Shop, 250 fixed price plus 100 auction a month with Basic, 1,500 plus 600 with Featured, unlimited fixed price plus 1,000 auction with Anchor; beyond the allowance a fixed price listing is 30p with no Shop, 10p with Basic, 5p with Featured, and auctions are 15p. The [multi-quantity listings](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/creating-managing-listings/multiquantity-listings-listings-variations?id=4150) page confirms a listing fee is charged every month from creation until everything sells or the listing ends, at the rate in force when it renews.

So a business seller without a Shop who has 400 slow listings is paying 400 renewals a month, plus any subtitle or other upgrade every month, on stock that is not moving. That is the arithmetic that makes a Shop subscription pay and makes clearing dead listings a monthly job.

Two more Good 'Til Cancelled facts from the multi-quantity page that matter for resellers: the "out of stock" option keeps a listing alive but hidden when quantity hits zero and shows it again when you restock, with a listing fee credit if it sat at zero for a whole billing month; and a listing at zero quantity for 180 consecutive days ends automatically. Turn the option on if you replenish; leave it off for one-offs.

## Relist versus sell similar

Both create a new item number. The difference is the thread back to the old listing.

**Relist** keeps a link. Watchers of the ended item can find the relisted one, and eBay's page says a manual relist uses a listing fee or free listing again but you may get a listing fee credit if the relisted item then sells. Anything you had paid for as an upgrade is applied again and charged again. Relist is the right tool when a listing ended by accident, when an auction did not sell and you want to run it again, or when you ended a fixed price listing to make a change you could not make while live and want the watchers to follow.

**Sell similar** breaks the link. New number, no watchers, no view count, no connection to any sales history. It is a new listing in every sense, and it lands in "newly listed" sorts as such. Use it when you are listing another copy of something you sold, when you want a fresh start for an item whose old listing had a bad history (wrong category, poor photos, a title you have rebuilt), or when the ended listing is too old for eBay to relist and only the details survive.

One difference that is inference rather than official: many sellers believe a sell similar with a genuinely new title and photos gets a better start in Best Match than a relist of the same tired listing. eBay does not say. What eBay does say on the [Best Match page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/listing-tips/optimising-listings-best-match?id=4166) is that popularity, price, listing quality and seller history decide placement. A sell similar that changes none of those is a new number with the same problems.

## Ending and relisting versus leaving it up

This is the real question, and the answer is not "refresh everything every 30 days". Work through it in this order.

### First, is the listing actually stale?

Open Seller Hub, Performance, Traffic, and look at the listing's impressions, page views and sales for the last month.

- **Impressions low, views low.** The listing is not appearing in searches. That is a title, specifics or category problem. Fix those by revising; nothing about relisting changes what the words say.
- **Impressions healthy, views low.** It appears but nobody clicks. The first photo or the price shown in the result is the problem. Revise the photo and the price.
- **Views healthy, no sales.** People open it and leave. Price against sold listings, the condition note, the postage cost or the returns terms. Revise.
- **Everything was fine and has faded.** The listing has been up for months, sales have dried up, and the item still sells for others at your price. This is the case where a fresh start can help, and it is rarer than sellers think.

### Revise first

You can change almost anything on an active fixed price listing with no sales: title, photos, price, quantity, category, postage and specifics, per the [revising a listing](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/creating-managing-listings/revising-listing?id=4356) page, and there is no fee to revise unless you add a paid feature. Revising keeps the item number, the watchers, the views and, for a multi-quantity listing, the sales history that shows "X sold" on the page. That history is a conversion signal to buyers and, on eBay's own account of Best Match, popularity is a ranking factor. Ending a listing that has sold twelve units to relist it as new throws away the strongest thing it had.

### When ending and starting again is the right call

- The listing is a one-off item that has been up for months with falling impressions, and you have rebuilt the title, specifics and photos. A sell similar with the new material is a fair test; keep the old listing's item number in your notes and compare traffic after two weeks.
- The listing is in the wrong category or was created with a product identifier that pulled in the wrong catalogue details, and the fix is messier than starting again.
- Old photos have been lost; eBay keeps images on completed listings for around 90 days, per the [adding pictures](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/adding-pictures-listings?id=4148) page.
- An auction did not sell. Relist it, ideally with a lower start or a Buy It Now, or move it to fixed price. New and occasional sellers get automatic relisting of auctions of five days or more up to eight times at no extra charge, per the [relisting page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/creating-managing-listings/relisting-items?id=4147); if that is running, it is doing the relist for you.

### When it is the wrong call

- A multi-quantity listing with sales history. Revise. Never end.
- A listing with watchers and Best Offer traffic. Watchers are people who might buy on a price drop. Drop the price or send offers to watchers instead.
- Anything you are ending purely because a video said listings "go stale at 30 days". Good 'Til Cancelled already renews at 30 days.
- To dodge a fee. Manual relists cost a listing or a free slot; automatic renewals for private sellers do not.
- An auction with bids. The [ending a listing](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/creating-managing-listings/cancelling-listing?id=4146) page says an auction with bids can be ended only with 12 hours or more to run, and if the reserve is not met the fee is 5% of the highest bid up to £20 for private sellers or final value fees for business accounts; eBay warns that regularly ending listings early can bring account limits.

### A monthly routine that replaces guesswork

Once a month, sort active listings by age and by impressions. For each listing over 90 days old with no sale: check the price against sold comparables and cut it if needed; check the title and specifics against the [titles guide](/guides/ebay-titles-item-specifics-and-search); check the first photo. If all three are already good and traffic is still falling, sell similar with a rebuilt listing, then end the old one. If the item has not sold at any sensible price after two such cycles, it is not the listing; sell it another way or accept the loss.

Send offers to watchers and run a sale on the aged stock before you end anything. They are free, they keep the history, and they often clear the item that "needed relisting".

## The duplicate listings policy

This is where careless relisting turns into a policy problem. eBay's [duplicate listings policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/listing-policies/duplicate-listings-policy?id=4255) says you may not have more than one fixed price listing of an identical item at the same time. Identical means the same item; listing it in two categories, from two accounts, or with an inconsequential bonus thrown in does not make it different. Multiple auctions of the same item are allowed only if they have the same start price, reserve, title and description and no Buy It Now, and only one without bids is shown at a time. Breaches bring listing removal, warnings, restrictions or suspension.

What is allowed instead: one fixed price listing with a quantity, one listing with variations for items that differ by size or colour, and up to five separate fixed price listings for an item that fits several devices, each naming the device.

The three ways sellers break it without meaning to:

1. **Sell similar from a live listing and forget the original.** You now have two identical listings. Sell similar does not end the source. End the old one the moment the new one is live.
2. **Relisting from the Unsold folder something you already relisted.** The ended listing stays in Unsold after you relist it; relist it again and there are two. Delete or mark ended items you have dealt with.
3. **Listing the same product twice to double the search presence.** This is precisely what the policy exists to stop, and the second listing is usually the one that gets removed, taking its history with it.

If you have several units of the same thing, one listing with the quantity set is both the rule and the better listing, because every sale adds to a history buyers can see.

## Fees: the decision in numbers

| Situation | Private seller | Business seller (no Shop) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leave a fixed price listing to renew | No further listing fee | 30p a month beyond allowance, plus any upgrades monthly |
| Manually relist an unsold item | Uses a free listing, or 35p beyond 300 a month; credit possible if it sells | Uses allowance, or 30p; credit possible if it sells |
| Sell similar | Same as a new listing | Same as a new listing |
| End a fixed price listing early | Free | Free |
| End an auction with bids, reserve unmet | 5% of highest bid, up to £20 | Final value fees |
| Subtitle upgrade | £2 per listing | £2 per listing, charged each renewal |

Figures from the [private](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4364) and [business](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-business-sellers?id=4809) fees pages and the [ending a listing](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/creating-managing-listings/cancelling-listing?id=4146) page. Check them before acting on a number.

## What goes wrong

1. **Ending a listing with a sales history to "refresh" it.** History gone, watchers gone, and a new number with the same price and photos. Revise instead.
2. **Two live listings of the same item after a sell similar.** Policy breach and a removal. End the source at once.
3. **Relisting on a loop without changing anything.** If the title, photos and price were the problem, the relist has the same problem.
4. **Paying renewal fees on dead stock.** Business sellers without a Shop pay every month. Clear or consolidate.
5. **Ending auctions with bids to "start again".** A fee if the reserve was not met, disappointed bidders, and account limits if it becomes a habit.
6. **Relisting from a long ended listing and finding the photos gone.** Keep originals; see the [photos guide](/guides/photos-on-a-phone).
7. **Turning on "out of stock" for one-off items.** Sold listings sit hidden and then end themselves at 180 days. Use it for replenishable stock only.
8. **Believing the 30 day reset story.** Good 'Til Cancelled already renews monthly. What moves a listing is price, photos, words and history, not its birthday.
