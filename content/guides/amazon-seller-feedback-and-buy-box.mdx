This guide is for resellers who already have stock live on Amazon and want more of the sales that listing is making. Two things decide that: your seller feedback record, which Amazon reads as a measure of how you serve customers, and the featured offer, which Amazon calls the Buy Box and everyone else calls the sale. After reading it you will know how to build a feedback record within the rules, what to do with a bad one, and how to price so that Amazon keeps handing you a share of the button. If you have not set up yet, start with [the working manual](/guides/how-to-start-amazon-fba-uk) and come back.

Amazon changes its rules and its pages without much notice. Where a figure or a rule matters, it links to Amazon's own page; check that page before relying on this one.

## Feedback and reviews are two different things

Amazon has two scoring systems that customers mix up and sellers must not.

| | Seller feedback | Product review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it rates | You: packing, speed, communication, whether the order was right | The product itself, whoever sold it |
| Where it shows | Your storefront and seller profile, and in your account metrics | The product listing, shared by every seller on it |
| Who it affects | Only you | Every seller on the listing, including you |
| Left through | The order page, "leave seller feedback" | The product page or the order page, "write a review" |
| Feeds order defect rate | Yes, if negative (1 or 2 stars) | No |

As a reseller on other brands' listings you have almost no influence over product reviews and no reason to chase them. Seller feedback is the one that is yours. A customer who is unhappy with the product will often leave you a one star seller rating anyway, and that is exactly the kind of feedback Amazon will remove, which is why the removal section below matters more than the asking section.

A negative seller rating counts towards your order defect rate, which Amazon wants under 1% (the figure is on your Account Health page in Seller Central, and in [the manual's numbers table](/guides/how-to-start-amazon-fba-uk)). At low volume that is one bad rating in a hundred orders. So feedback is not a vanity number; it is one of the three inputs to the metric that can deactivate your account.

## Why so few customers leave it

Most Amazon customers never rate a seller. They bought a branded product, Amazon delivered it, and to them the seller was invisible. That means two things. A new account will sit on a handful of ratings for months unless you ask. And a single bad one has an outsized effect on a small total: five ratings and one negative is 80% positive, which looks far worse than the same negative among fifty.

The practical consequence is that you should ask, every time, in the way Amazon allows, from the first order.

## Asking for feedback within the rules

Amazon's [Communication Guidelines](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/gp/help/external/G1791) govern every message a seller sends to a buyer. Read them once, properly. The parts that matter for feedback:

- You may ask a buyer to leave seller feedback or a product review. You may not ask for a positive one, ask only customers you think were happy, or ask them to change or remove one.
- You may not offer anything in return: no discount, refund, free product, or entry into anything.
- You may not include marketing, links away from Amazon, or a request to contact you outside Amazon.
- Messages must be sent through Buyer-Seller Messaging and must be about that order.
- Proactive messages (ones the buyer did not start) have to go within 30 days of the order completing.
- Buyers can opt out of non-essential messages, and a request for feedback is non-essential. If a buyer has opted out, no request reaches them.

Breaking these is a policy violation on your account health page, not a slap on the wrist. The safest way to ask is the one Amazon built.

### The Request a Review button

On every order in Seller Central (Orders, Manage Orders, open the order) there is a button labelled Request a Review. Pressing it sends one message, written by Amazon, translated into the buyer's language, asking for both a product review and seller feedback. You cannot edit it, the buyer cannot reply to it, and it respects opt-outs automatically. It works once per order, and only inside a window after delivery; at the time of writing that window opens five days after delivery and closes at thirty, and the button is greyed out outside it. Amazon's help page on the button is inside Seller Central; search Help for "Request a Review" for the current rules.

Our recommendation is to press it on every order, about a week after delivery. That is long enough for the customer to have used the product and short enough that they remember it arrived on time. It is boring, it takes a few seconds per order, and it is the entire feedback strategy for most small sellers.

### Automated request tools

Once you are doing more than a few dozen orders a week, clicking a button per order stops being sensible. Two kinds of tool exist:

- Tools that press the Request a Review button for you on a schedule, through Amazon's API. These send Amazon's own message and cannot break the wording rules, because there is no wording to write. They can still send at a bad time or to the wrong orders (refunded ones, for instance) if you set them up badly.
- Tools that send your own templated messages through Buyer-Seller Messaging. These can be written in breach of the guidelines. If you use one, use only Amazon's own permitted templates and never add an incentive, a link or a "if you had any problem contact us first" line, which Amazon treats as an attempt to filter out negatives.

We would use the first kind, set to fire once per order a few days after delivery, and skip the second unless you have a real reason to write your own words. Tools are described generically on purpose; do not choose one because a forum says so, choose one because it does the first thing above and nothing else.

## What to do with negative feedback

Read the comment before doing anything. Most negative seller feedback falls into one of these groups, and the response is different for each.

| What the feedback says | Whose fault Amazon considers it | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Late, damaged in transit, lost, wrong item picked, poor packaging on an FBA order | Amazon's | Request removal: it relates to fulfilment Amazon controlled |
| "Product broke after a week", "not as good as expected", anything only about the product | Nobody's, as far as your record goes | Request removal: it is entirely a product review |
| Contains swearing or the buyer's own personal details | Nobody's | Request removal: obscene or identifying content |
| Item not as described because you sent it opened, stickered or wrong | Yours | Contact the buyer, fix it, learn from it. Removal is unlikely |
| Slow reply to a message, rude reply | Yours | Same |

Amazon states the grounds it will act on in the Feedback Manager help (Performance, Feedback Manager, then the help link on that page). Feedback that is about the product or contains obscenity or personal information is removed. Feedback about a fulfilment problem on an FBA order is not deleted but struck through, with a note that the fulfilment issue was not the seller's fault, and it stops counting against your rating and your order defect rate.

Three rules for the process:

1. Request removal only through Feedback Manager. Next to the order in the feedback table there is a Request removal action. Do not raise a support case or email asking for feedback to be removed; Amazon warns that requesting removal by other routes can be treated as a policy breach.
2. Do it within 90 days of the feedback being left. After that the option is gone.
3. If the automated answer is no and you believe it plainly meets one of the grounds, the reasoning on the page is usually the only appeal you get. Write one clear sentence matching the ground ("The comment refers only to the product; the order was fulfilled by Amazon on time") when the form asks for it.

### Should you reply publicly

Amazon lets you post a public response under feedback. In practice it does almost nothing for sales and gives you a way to look defensive in public. Reply only where a future customer reading the storefront would be misled without it, keep it to two sentences, factual, and never argue. Otherwise message the buyer privately through the order to fix the problem, and ask nothing about the feedback itself.

Contacting a buyer to ask them to remove or change feedback is not allowed. If you fix a problem and they choose to remove it, that is theirs to do from their own order page, within the time limit Amazon gives buyers; you cannot ask them to.

## The Buy Box

On a listing with several sellers, the "Add to basket" and "Buy now" buttons belong to one offer at a time. Amazon calls it the featured offer. Everything else is behind "Other sellers" or "New and used from", which most customers never open, and on a phone screen the gap is even wider. If you are not in the box you are not selling much.

### What Amazon says decides it

Amazon publishes eligibility criteria and a list of factors but not the weighting, and it says openly that eligibility does not guarantee selection. The [featured offer eligibility page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/gp/help/external/G200418100) is the one to read; the factors it names are:

- **A Professional selling plan.** Individual plan offers are not eligible.
- **Price.** Amazon uses the landed price, meaning item price plus delivery, and compares it with similar offers and with the same item elsewhere. An offer priced well above the rest is flagged as not eligible because of a high price.
- **Fulfilment and delivery.** Speed, reliability and cost of delivery. FBA offers meet this automatically; a seller fulfilled offer has to compete on handling time, delivery promise and free delivery.
- **Seller performance.** Order defect rate, cancellation rate, late dispatch rate, and seller feedback history.
- **Availability.** Stock in hand; an offer that keeps running out is passed over.
- **Time and volume on the platform.** New accounts are told that it takes a while to become eligible and that it comes with sales and good service.

Notice what is not on the list: your storefront name, how long your description is, or how many times you have edited the price. And notice that feedback appears. That is the link between the two halves of this guide.

### It rotates

The featured offer is not a permanent award to the best offer. Among offers that are close on price and performance, Amazon rotates the box between them, and the share each gets is roughly proportional to how close they are. Two FBA sellers at the same price with clean metrics will typically split the sales; a third who is 2% higher will still get some. It moves during the day, and you can watch it move by refreshing a listing.

This is why "the lowest price wins" is wrong in both directions. Being lowest by a penny does not give you the box all day if a competitor is close and has a better record, and being a little above the lowest does not shut you out. What shuts you out is being far above the lowest, having a metric in the red, or being on the Individual plan.

### Pricing to hold a share

The aim is not to win the box; it is to get a share of it large enough to sell your stock at a margin before the price falls. A way of working we recommend:

1. Find the current featured offer price, not the lowest price on the listing. Those are often different, because the lowest may be a seller fulfilled offer with slow delivery that Amazon is not featuring.
2. Set your price at or fractionally under the featured price. Matching it is usually enough to enter the rotation if your metrics are clean and your offer is FBA.
3. Set a floor below which you will not go, worked out from your cost, fees and the VAT on fees if you are not registered. See [the manual's section on repricers](/guides/how-to-start-amazon-fba-uk) for the reasoning.
4. Do not chase. If a competitor undercuts you by a few pence, wait. Most undercutting sellers have a few units and are gone in a week. Racing them down takes the whole listing to a price nobody makes money at, including you, and Amazon remembers the low price when it flags "high" prices later.
5. Hold a share, not a position. If you are getting a third of the box across the day and selling through at your rate, that is a good outcome. Check a price history tool for the last ninety days to see what the price did the last time this many sellers were on it.

A repricer does all of this automatically once you tell it the floor and the strategy. Set it to match the featured offer rather than to beat the lowest price, and it will stop starting price wars on your behalf.

### When Amazon itself is on the listing

Sourcing tools warn that "Amazon does not share the Buy Box" on listings where Amazon is a seller. That is misleading. Amazon's own retail offer is treated like any other offer for eligibility, with the same weight on price, and in practice sellers we know share the box with Amazon on most such listings. What the warning usually means is that the third party sellers are priced above Amazon, so Amazon is winning on price, not on privilege.

The pattern is easy to see on a listing: Amazon at one price, a row of FBA and seller fulfilled offers a few pounds higher, all complaining they never get the box. Match Amazon's price and the rotation usually includes you. Undercut by a little and you often take most of it.

Two real cautions:

- Amazon's price is often below what you can buy the product for, because Amazon buys direct from the brand. Sharing the box at a loss is worse than not sharing it. Check your floor first.
- A listing with only Amazon selling, no history of third party sellers, and a brand that sells direct is a different case. There you are not fighting the algorithm, you are exposing yourself to the brand: a listing no reseller has ever been on is one where an intellectual property complaint is likely. Prefer listings with a visible history of other sellers.

Amazon does occasionally hold a listing (dangerous goods classification, a brand's gating, a price hold) so that no third party offer is featured. That shows as "no featured offer" or as your offer sitting under "other sellers" at the same price as Amazon for days. When that happens, move on; there is no pricing answer to a lock.

### The sales figure on the listing

Since 2023 Amazon has shown a sales figure on many listings, near the star rating: "500+ bought in past month" and similar. Amazon's own FAQ for sellers, called Sales Trend, says it is Amazon's own transaction data for the listing, refreshed at least daily, excluding returns and cancellations, and shown in bands rather than exact numbers ([Sales Trend FAQ](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/gp/help/external/G2CRYWPU52D7TGAK)). Bands have become finer over time; listings that once showed nothing under a certain level now show lower figures too, though the smallest sellers still show none.

How to use it:

- It is total sales across every seller on the listing, not what you will get. Divide by the number of sellers sharing the box, and then again for Amazon if Amazon is on it and priced below you.
- It is last month. Seasonal products show a month that will not repeat. Compare it with the sales rank history in a price history tool before trusting it.
- "Bought" is the bottom of the band. "500+" can be 500 or 900. Treat it as a floor.
- Its real value is as a sanity check on the estimates from tools, which are modelled from rank and often wrong for products that sell in bursts.

A listing showing 100+ sold last month with four FBA sellers all in the rotation is roughly 25 sales for you next month if nothing changes; a listing showing 50+ with ten sellers is a shelf. Do this sum before every buy, not after.

## What goes wrong

- **Asking for "positive feedback".** The single most common wording violation. Ask for feedback; the adjective is the breach.
- **Filtering.** "If there was any problem, contact us before leaving feedback" reads as harmless and Amazon treats it as steering. Leave it out.
- **Leaving product feedback to stand.** Sellers accept a one star rating that says "the batteries died" because they assume nothing can be done. That one is removable in two clicks and it was costing you the box.
- **Missing the 90 days.** Check Feedback Manager weekly, not when your rating drops.
- **Using support cases to get feedback removed.** The wrong route, and Amazon says it can count against you.
- **Repricing to beat the lowest offer instead of the featured one.** A seller fulfilled offer nobody is featuring drags you down to its price for no gain.
- **No floor price.** A repricer without a floor will sell at a loss and report success.
- **Reading "Amazon on the listing" as "no chance".** Usually it is "match the price".
- **Treating the sales figure as yours.** It is the listing's, shared with everyone on it.
- **Ignoring the Individual plan rule.** If you are still on the per item plan, none of the above will work; switch.
