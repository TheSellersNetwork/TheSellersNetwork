This is for UK resellers who want to sell to their own followers in an Instagram or Facebook live, where there is no checkout button. After reading it you will know what Meta does and does not offer for live selling now, how to run a claim-in-comments sale that does not collapse into arguments, how to take payment so that neither you nor the buyer gets scammed, and the distance selling rules that apply when you sell as a trader. Rules link to Meta's help centres, GOV.UK and legislation.gov.uk. Meta changes its commerce features often, so check the page before relying on it. None of this is legal or financial advice.

## What Meta offers now

For a few years Facebook and Instagram let sellers tag products in a live video and take payment inside the app. That is no longer how it works.

- **Checkout has left the apps.** Meta's [changes to Shops page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1314349509894768) says that as of September 2025, Shops on Facebook and Instagram use website checkout: customers browse in the app and are sent to your own website to pay.
- **Facebook has live shopping tools, for businesses with a catalogue.** Meta's [live shopping tools page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/975194481036128) describes pinning products from a catalogue in Live Producer, with viewers ordering through Messenger, your website or a messaging partner's site. It needs a catalogue in Commerce Manager or a synced third-party platform, and the page says the feature may not be available to you yet.
- **Instagram's help centre lists no live product tagging.** The [Shopping on Instagram](https://help.instagram.com/337910740093030) section covers tagging products in posts, stories and Reels, not in lives.

For a reseller selling one-offs from a phone, with no website and no catalogue, none of that helps. What you have is a live video, a comment box and your own payment arrangements. That is what the rest of this guide is about.

If you would rather have a checkout, protection and shipping labels built in, a dedicated live platform may suit you better. Our [live selling fees comparison](/guides/live-selling-fees-compared) works through the numbers, and the [first TikTok Shop LIVE guide](/guides/tiktok-shop-first-live) covers one of them.

## The claim-in-comments method

The idea is simple. You show an item, say its number and price, and the first viewer to comment the agreed word claims it. You invoice them after the live. The problems are all in the detail, so write your rules down and post them before every live, pinned as a comment and in the caption.

### Rules to publish before you start

1. **How to claim.** "Comment SOLD and the item number." One word, one format. Anything else does not count.
2. **First valid comment wins.** The comment timestamp decides, not who messages you afterwards.
3. **Postage.** One postage charge per buyer per live, whatever they claim. Say the price and the service (tracked or not).
4. **Payment deadline.** For example, within 24 hours of the invoice. Unpaid claims go to the next person who commented.
5. **Payment methods.** The methods you accept, and that you post only once payment has arrived.
6. **Returns.** What your returns policy is, which as a trader must meet the legal minimum (see below).
7. **No claims by private message.** It keeps the record in one place.

Pick a deadline you will enforce. The single most common complaint in live selling groups is the non-paying claimer, and the cure is a deadline that everyone saw in advance.

### During the live

- **Number every item** and say the number, price and condition out loud, then write it in a comment yourself. That comment becomes your record.
- **Show the flaws.** Hold them to the camera. It is your evidence if a buyer later says the item was not as described.
- **Confirm each claim out loud**: "Number 14 to the first SOLD." Viewers then know it has gone.
- **Have a helper if you can.** One person presents, the other watches comments and keeps the claim list. Check the app's current settings for adding a moderator to your live.
- **Keep the video** afterwards, by saving or downloading it where the app allows. Along with the comments, it is your proof of what was shown and claimed.

### After the live

1. Build the claim list from the comments: buyer, items, prices, postage.
2. Send each buyer one invoice with everything on it, from your payment provider, within an hour or two while they are still keen.
3. Chase once at the halfway point of the deadline.
4. When the deadline passes, offer unpaid items to the next valid claimer or relist.
5. Post only paid orders, tracked where the value justifies it, and keep proof of postage.

Keep the claim list and invoices: they are your sales records. Our [bookkeeping and tax guide](/guides/bookkeeping-and-tax-for-resellers) covers what to keep.

## Taking payment safely

Because Meta's Purchase Protection only covers eligible purchases made with checkout on Facebook, and not person-to-person payments ([About scams on Facebook Marketplace](https://www.facebook.com/help/2374002556073992)), protection in a live sale comes only from the payment method you choose.

### For your buyers

Use a payment method that gives the buyer a protected route if something goes wrong: a card payment through a payment processor, or a payment service's option for buying goods. Buyers who know they are protected pay faster and come back. Check your provider's terms to see which option it treats as a payment for goods and use that one. Do not ask buyers to pay using an option meant for sending money to friends and family to save a fee; it removes their protection and tells a careful buyer to be suspicious of you.

### For you

A protected method also brings the risk of a buyer disputing a payment. Your defence is evidence: the saved video showing the item and its flaws, the claim comment, the invoice, and tracked proof of delivery to the address on the payment. Post to that address only.

Meta's [scams page](https://www.facebook.com/help/2374002556073992) lists payment and contact tricks that target sellers. These, plus one more that sellers we know have met, turn up after lives:

| Scam | What happens | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payment screenshot | The buyer sends a picture of a payment and asks you to post | Check your payment account; never trust a screenshot |
| Overpayment | The buyer "accidentally" pays too much and asks for the difference back | Refund only through the same method, after the original payment has fully cleared, or cancel it entirely |
| Refund without payment | The buyer asks for a refund they never paid | Check the account before any refund |
| Fake payment emails | An email claims you must upgrade or pay a fee to receive the money | Log in to the payment service directly; ignore links |
| Moving off platform | The buyer pushes you to WhatsApp or text straight away | Keep the conversation on Messenger or Instagram messages |
| Different delivery address | The buyer asks you to post somewhere other than the address on the payment | Decline, or cancel and refund |

Never share login details, verification codes or bank passwords with anyone, however the request is dressed up.

## Your duties to buyers

If you sell as a trader, and most people running regular sale lives are trading, a sale agreed in a live and paid for afterwards is a distance contract. The [Consumer Contracts Regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/5) define a distance contract as one concluded without the trader and consumer being physically together, using means of distance communication. A live video, comments and an online invoice fit that description. Buyers do not lose their rights because the sale happened in a comment.

### Information before the sale

GOV.UK's [distance selling guidance](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses) says you must give buyers, among other things, your business details, a description of the goods, the price including taxes, delivery costs and arrangements, payment methods and the conditions for cancelling. In a live sale, the practical way is a pinned post or a page linked from your profile with your business name, contact address, postage terms and returns policy, and an invoice that repeats the key points.

### The 14 day right to cancel

For most goods sold at a distance, buyers can cancel within 14 days of delivery without giving a reason, then have another 14 days to send the item back. You must refund within 14 days of getting it back, including the standard delivery charge they originally paid ([accepting returns and giving refunds](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds)). If you do not tell buyers about their right to cancel, the cancellation period is extended to up to 12 months ([distance selling guidance](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses)).

The buyer pays the cost of sending it back only if you told them in advance that they would, or you agreed to pay it yourself ([regulation 35](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/35)). Put that in your published rules.

Some goods are excluded, such as personalised items and sealed hygiene products that have been unsealed. You can deduct from the refund only if the item has been used or damaged beyond what was needed to inspect it.

### Faulty and misdescribed goods

Separately from cancellation, goods must match the description you gave, including what you said out loud in the live. If they do not, the buyer is entitled to a remedy, and saying "sold as seen" does not remove that. Our [returns guide](/guides/returns-without-losing-money) covers the Consumer Rights Act in more detail. This is one more reason to show every flaw on camera.

### If you are a private seller

Clearing your own wardrobe occasionally is not trading, and the cancellation rules apply to traders. If you run sale lives regularly with stock bought to resell, assume you are a trader for consumer law and for tax. The [bookkeeping and tax guide](/guides/bookkeeping-and-tax-for-resellers) discusses how to tell.

## What you can sell

Meta's [Commerce Policies](https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/commerce) apply to commerce content, and Facebook's Community Standards and Instagram's Community Guidelines apply to every live. Breaking them can get content removed and access to commerce features suspended. The policies prohibit adult products, weapons, drugs and other categories you would expect; read the list before a themed live. The same page notes that Facebook Marketplace is intended for consumer to consumer sales, so do not assume a live audience can be moved to Marketplace listings.

## Our recommendation

Lives on Facebook and Instagram work best as a way to sell to people who already follow you: a regular slot, a clear set of rules, and a protected payment method. They do not bring new buyers the way a platform with a shop and a discovery feed does. If most of your viewers are new, or you want checkout and protection handled for you, use a dedicated live platform and keep Facebook and Instagram for announcing when you are live.

## What goes wrong

- **No published rules.** Disputes over who claimed first and whether postage combines.
- **No payment deadline, or one you do not enforce.** Stock held for people who never pay.
- **Posting on a screenshot.** The payment never existed.
- **Taking payment by an unprotected method.** Buyers are exposed, and the careful ones leave.
- **No saved video.** Nothing to show when a buyer says a flaw was not mentioned.
- **No returns information.** As a trader, the cancellation period stretches to up to 12 months.
- **Treating a live sale as sold as seen.** Consumer rights still apply to traders.
- **Posting to a different address.** The classic setup for a dispute you cannot win.
