This is for Vinted sellers who relist often, use or are thinking about using tools, or sell enough that Vinted might see them as a business. After reading it you will know what Vinted's terms and Catalogue Rules actually say, what Vinted can do to an account that breaks them, how appeals work, and a set of habits that keep your listings fresh without risking the account. The rules here come only from Vinted's own [terms](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions), [Catalogue Rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules) and Help Centre. Where we mention what sellers report, it is reported experience, not a rule. None of this is legal advice.

## Which rules apply in 2026

Vinted's current UK terms took effect on 30 April 2026, and new terms apply from 8 October 2026 ([our summary](/blog/vinted-terms-october-2026)). We compared the relisting and software clauses in both versions: the wording is the same. The Catalogue Rules form part of the terms, and where they conflict with the terms, the Catalogue Rules win ([terms, section 2](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions)).

## What the rules say

### Relisting

Section 6 of the [terms](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions) lists things you agree not to do. One of them is to "delete and re-list the same Item multiple times or multiple Items in bulk".

That covers two behaviours:

1. Deleting one listing and uploading it again, repeatedly, so it looks new.
2. Deleting and re-uploading many listings at once.

The terms do not give a number or a time period, so there is no safe count. Treat any pattern of delete-and-reupload done to get back to the top of search as the thing the clause describes.

What Vinted does allow:

- **Re-upload after a cancellation.** If an order is cancelled before it was sent, a Re-upload item button restores the listing with its original photos and details ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/102-re-uploading-an-item)).
- **A new listing for a returned item.** If you sold an item and it came back to you, Vinted says to upload a new listing for it, once you have it in hand.
- **Reselling something you bought on Vinted,** with new photos, because you cannot use other members' photos.
- **Editing a listing.** Changing the price, photos or description is normal use. Vinted notifies your followers when you lower a price ([selling faster](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/57-selling-your-items-faster)).
- **Paying for a Bump.** This is Vinted's approved way to push a listing up; see [bumps and Showcase](/guides/vinted-bumps-and-spotlight).

### Duplicate listings

The same item cannot be listed twice. Vinted's [hidden or removed listings page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/62-why-your-listing-was-hidden-or-deleted) lists "listed multiple times and is a duplicate" as a reason for removal, and says that if you have several of the same item you should make one listing and say in the description how many you have, with photos of each size's label or each colour. Listing the same item in a set and on its own also counts ([selling a set](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/257-selling-a-bundle)).

### Bots and outside software

Section 6 of the terms also says you must not:

- use any external software tools, including bots, scraping programs, crawling programs and spiders, when registering or using the site or services, including to promote items or add items to favourites, "unless such a use is authorised, offered or in any other way allowed by us";
- use any external tool that could disrupt the site;
- data mine, screen scrape or crawl any part of the site;
- share information from the site with third parties except through the site's Share button.

Section 12 bans unsolicited or mass private messages sent to 5 or more users.

We could not find any page on Vinted's UK Help Centre that lists outside tools Vinted has authorised for standard accounts. So the practical reading is: any tool that logs into your account and lists, relists, favourites, follows, messages or sends offers for you is an external software tool under section 6, unless Vinted has said otherwise. If a tool's makers claim Vinted allows it, ask them for Vinted's written confirmation, and ask Vinted directly through the [Help Centre](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help).

Sellers report accounts being restricted after using auto-relisting and auto-offer tools. We are not repeating any figures or timings from those reports, because Vinted does not publish them and they vary.

### Commercial selling

This is the rule that matters most for resellers. Vinted's [Catalogue Rules, section 4](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules) say standard accounts are for private, non-commercial use only, and that as a standard seller you cannot use Vinted to list items you have specifically bought or sourced to resell, earn a regular income through reselling, or run a reselling business, even if you have a registered business. Vinted's [commercial selling page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/1120-commercial-selling) puts it more plainly: if you sell on Vinted to make a profit, you need to sign up as a Pro seller.

Two things are banned for everyone, Pro or not:

- selling new items bought in bulk or sourced from a third-party supplier (commercial stock inventory);
- listing new commercial stock as pre-owned or personal to get around the rules.

The same page says you cannot sell new items bought specifically to resell, including items bought from retail shops to resell for a profit. The terms also bar listing items you bought in bulk or as dropship products with a low resale value ([terms, section 14](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions)). If you buy to resell, read [Vinted Pro and selling as a business](/guides/vinted-pro-and-selling-as-a-business).

## What Vinted can do to an account

Section 7 of the [terms](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions) sets out corrective actions, which Vinted says will be proportional to the breach:

| Action | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Warning | A message telling you what is wrong. |
| Hiding a listing | You fix it and it comes back. No appeal needed ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/62-why-your-listing-was-hidden-or-deleted)). |
| Removing a listing | It is gone. Paid Bumps or Showcase on it are not refunded. |
| Blocking a feature | For example, messages. |
| Temporary block | Vinted's [banned account page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/92-why-was-my-account-blocked) says temporary bans lift automatically after 7 to 30 days depending on the reason. |
| Permanent block | You can still contact support and usually withdraw your balance, unless the ban involves fraud risk or a compromised account. |

When an account is blocked, your items are delisted, paid extras are not refunded, and pending orders and payouts can usually be completed, though Vinted can cancel them and restrict payouts where there are security or fraud concerns ([terms, section 7](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions)). Vinted says it may use automated tools to detect breaches.

The reasons Vinted lists for bans that relate to this guide:

- **Temporary:** selling items for commercial purposes on a standard account; promoting selling outside Vinted's payment system.
- **Permanent:** repeatedly selling for commercial purposes on a standard account (Vinted then requires you to convert to Pro, and you can only convert within 6 months of the ban notice); having multiple accounts, except one standard and one Pro with different email addresses and phone numbers; repeatedly ignoring Vinted's messages and not taking the required action.

The Catalogue Rules add that repeated uploads of items that look commercial can lead to a suspension, giving 7 days as an example, or a permanent block.

### Appeals

Vinted must tell you in writing when it takes corrective action, and the notice includes your right to appeal, except where telling you would break the law or disrupt a fraud investigation ([terms, section 7](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions)). Use the appeal link in the moderation message. You can appeal once, within 6 months of the notice, and should explain why the decision was wrong with evidence ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/92-why-was-my-account-blocked)). You can also go to a certified out-of-court dispute settlement body or the courts.

A good appeal is short, factual and specific: the listing or action concerned, what you did and why it complies with the named rule, and evidence (receipts, original photos, screenshots). An appeal that argues the rule is unfair gets nowhere.

## Safe habits

**Freshen listings without deleting them.**

1. Edit rather than delete. Retake the first photo, tighten the title, add measurements. A better listing does more than a newer one.
2. Lower the price in real steps when the item has sat. Followers are notified of price drops.
3. Send offers to members who favourited the item, using Vinted's own offer button.
4. If you want reach, pay for a Bump on items with the margin to justify it.
5. Delete only items that are gone for good (sold elsewhere, damaged, given away).

**One item, one listing.** If you have multiples, one listing with the quantity in the description. Never list the same item twice, or as part of a set and on its own.

**No tools that act inside your account** unless Vinted has confirmed in writing that the tool is allowed. Spreadsheets, photo editors and your own notes are fine; they do not touch Vinted. See [when to automate](/guides/when-to-automate) for where tools do make sense.

**Cross-list by hand, and end quickly.** If the same item is on eBay or elsewhere, keep it on Vinted as one listing and remove it everywhere the moment it sells. See [multi-channel selling](/guides/multi-channel-selling). Do not put links to other sites in your Vinted listings or profile; the Catalogue Rules forbid references or links to external websites or platforms, and a listing that promotes another marketplace is hidden.

**Keep messages one to one.** No copy-paste messages to lists of favouriters, and no messages steering buyers off Vinted.

**Be honest about what you are.** If you buy stock to resell for profit, Vinted's rules say you need a Pro account. Staying on a standard account and hoping is the single biggest account risk in this guide.

**Answer Vinted's messages.** Repeatedly ignoring them is itself a listed ban reason.

## What goes wrong

- **Daily delete-and-reupload.** It is the clause word for word.
- **Auto-relisters and auto-offer bots.** External software tools under section 6 unless Vinted has authorised them.
- **Duplicates of multiples.** Ten identical listings for ten identical items get removed; one listing with a quantity does not.
- **Reselling on a standard account.** Vinted's rules say buying to resell needs Pro.
- **New bulk stock on any account.** Not allowed, Pro or not.
- **A second standard account after a ban.** Multiple accounts are a permanent ban reason.
- **Appealing late or twice.** One appeal, within 6 months.
- **Buying Bumps on listings that then get removed.** No refund.
