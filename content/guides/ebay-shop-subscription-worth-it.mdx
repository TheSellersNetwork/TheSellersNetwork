This is for UK eBay sellers wondering whether to pay for a Shop, or whether the Shop they already pay for still earns its keep. After reading it you will know what each tier includes, the number of listings at which each one pays for itself, and what else you get beyond cheaper listings. Figures come from eBay's own fee pages, which are updated often, so check them before you subscribe.

## The numbers you will need

All business seller figures below are from [Fees for business sellers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-business-sellers-activated-managed-payments?id=4809), last updated 4 August 2026. That page says all fees on it are exclusive of VAT.

| | No Shop | Basic | Featured | Anchor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly price | none | £27 | £77 | £437 |
| Free fixed price listings a month | none | 250 | 1,500 | Unlimited |
| Free 7-day auction listings a month | none | 100 | 600 | 1,000 |
| Each extra fixed price listing | 30p | 10p | 5p | Free |
| Each extra auction listing | 30p | 15p | 15p | 15p |
| Seller Hub Promotions (sale events, coupons, order discounts) | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Shopfront and link to Shop on listings | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Daily Deals eligibility | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| eBay packaging voucher | No | No | £10 a month | £20 a month |
| Free listings in 13 countries | No | No | Yes | Yes |
| Invitation to eBay Concierge | No | No | No | Yes |

Final value fees on that page are set by category, with one table for all business sellers; there are no separate final value fee columns by Shop tier. The per-order fee (30p on orders of £10 or less, 40p above) and the 0.35% regulatory operating fee are the same with or without a Shop. eBay's [Shops overview](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/ebay-stores/ebay-stores?id=4071) mentions "differing discounts on listing and final value fees", but the fee page is the one with numbers on it, and it shows listing fees as the difference. If you are in a category where you think a tier changes your final value fee, check the fee table for your category before you rely on it.

## What you are really paying for

A Shop is mainly a bulk deal on listing fees. Everything else is secondary.

### Listing fees are charged more often than you think

eBay's [free listings page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/listing-tips/free-listings?id=4163) says that for business sellers, **automatic renewals and relists count against your allocation**. The business fee page adds that listing fees "apply each month for Good 'Til Cancelled listings". So a business seller with no Shop and 200 fixed price listings sitting on Good 'Til Cancelled pays 30p on each of them every month they renew, not just once when they were created: £60 a month, before anything sells.

That is the number most sellers miss. Count your *active* listings, not your *new* listings. For a business seller, the monthly listing bill is roughly the number of live fixed price listings, plus new auctions, plus manual relists.

### Private sellers are different

Private sellers get [300 free listings a month](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-private-sellers-activated-managed-payments?id=4822), pay no final value fee on most items, and automatic renewals do not use up the allowance. The private seller Shop costs £19.99 a month and adds 100 free listings, taking you to 400, with 35p per listing after that. It pays for itself only once you would otherwise list about 57 extra items a month beyond the 300. A private seller creating 357 or more new listings a month should be asking a different question: whether they are actually trading, in which case they should be on a business account anyway. See [who counts as a private seller](/blog/ebay-free-selling-private-sellers).

The rest of this guide is about business sellers.

## The break-even maths

These are listing fees only, fixed price listings only, and ignore VAT. If you are not VAT registered, add 20% to the Shop price and the per-listing fees to get what you actually pay; the break-even points stay the same, because both sides go up by the same proportion.

### No Shop against Basic

Without a Shop every listing costs 30p. Basic costs £27 and covers 250.

£27 divided by 30p is **90 listings a month**. Above 90 active fixed price listings (counting monthly renewals), Basic is cheaper than no Shop on listing fees alone. At 250 listings, no Shop costs £75 against Basic's £27.

### Basic against Featured

Basic costs £27 plus 10p for every fixed price listing over 250. Featured costs £77 for up to 1,500.

The gap is £50. At 10p each, that is 500 listings over Basic's 250, so **750 listings a month** is the crossover. Above 750, Featured is cheaper. At 1,000 listings, Basic costs £102 and Featured £77.

### Featured against Anchor

Featured costs £77 plus 5p for every fixed price listing over 1,500. Anchor costs £437 with unlimited fixed price listings.

The gap is £360. At 5p each, that is 7,200 listings over 1,500, so **8,700 listings a month** before Anchor wins on listing fees alone. Very few resellers get there. Anchor is bought for Concierge, for the extra auction allowance, or by businesses whose listing count is that high, not as a fee saving at ordinary volumes.

Auctions change this a little. Each tier has a separate free auction allowance, and extra auctions cost 15p with any Shop against 30p without one. If you run a lot of 7-day auctions, add them in: Basic's 100 free auctions are worth up to £30 a month to a seller who would otherwise pay 30p each.

## What else a Shop gets you

Cheaper listings are the maths. These are the reasons that do not show up in the maths but can matter more.

- **Seller Hub Promotions.** Sale events with strikethrough prices, order discounts, coupons and similar campaigns set up in the Marketing tab need a Shop, according to the fee page. Multi-buy itself is available without one. If you use markdowns to move [stale stock](/guides/stale-stock), a sale event is often worth more than the subscription.
- **A shopfront.** A branded page and a URL you can put on social media, packing slips and cards. Useful if you have repeat buyers in a niche; close to worthless for one-off general resale.
- **Daily Deals eligibility.** Only relevant if you can supply a deal at the volume and price eBay wants.
- **Sourcing Insights.** Listed on the fee page for all three tiers.
- **Packaging voucher on Featured and Anchor.** Worth £10 or £20 a month only if you would buy eBay-branded packaging anyway. See [packaging cheaply](/guides/packaging-cheaply) before you count it.
- **Free international listings on Featured and Anchor.** An allowance of free Good 'Til Cancelled listings on eBay's US, German and Australian sites for UK-registered sellers, and free listings in 13 countries. Worth something if your stock sells abroad.

## Who should subscribe, and who should not

| You are | Recommendation | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private seller | No | 300 free listings, no final value fee in most categories. The £19.99 Shop only pays above about 357 listings a month. |
| Business seller under about 90 active listings | No Shop | You pay less per listing than the subscription. Revisit monthly. |
| Business seller, 90 to 750 active listings | Basic | Cheapest on listing fees, and it adds promotions and a shopfront. |
| Business seller who relies on sale events or coupons at any volume | Basic at least | The promotions tools are Shop-only. |
| Business seller, 750 to several thousand listings | Featured | Cheapest on fees above 750, plus international free listings. |
| Business seller in the high thousands, or wanting Concierge | Look at Anchor | Fee saving only above about 8,700 listings; otherwise you are paying for service. |

Our recommendation for most growing business resellers: go without a Shop until you are past about 90 active listings, then take Basic, then move to Featured when you are consistently over 750. Check your tier against your actual listing count every quarter.

## Changing, pausing and cancelling

eBay's [Shop terms and conditions](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behaviour-policies/ebay-shop-terms-conditions?id=4746) say subscriptions are monthly and renew automatically. Free listings are allocated per calendar month and do not roll over.

On cancelling, the two help pages read slightly differently. The terms say you pay for the whole calendar month in which you cancel and keep the benefits until the end of it. The [closing a Shop page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/ebay-stores/closing-ebay-store?id=4091) says subscriptions have a minimum 30-day period, you are billed for the full 30 days if you cancel or downgrade within it, and fees are prorated after that. Either way, **cancel or downgrade near the start of a month, not the end**, and do not upgrade for a single busy week.

When you close a Shop, current listings stay live until they end or sell, but fixed price listings may move onto standard fees, which for a business seller means 30p a listing at the next renewal. If you only need a break, put the Shop on holiday instead; eBay saves your Shop settings for 30 days after closing in case you reopen.

To open one, go to [Opening an eBay Shop](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/ebay-stores/opening-ebay-shop?id=4092). Opening a Shop does not raise your selling limits.

## What goes wrong

- **Counting new listings instead of active ones.** Monthly renewals are the bulk of a business seller's listing bill.
- **Buying Featured for the voucher.** £10 of eBay packaging is not worth £50 of extra subscription unless the fee maths already favours Featured.
- **Assuming a Shop cuts the final value fee.** Check the fee table for your category.
- **Cancelling on the 28th.** You pay for that month anyway.
- **Keeping Anchor out of habit.** Below about 8,700 listings it is a service purchase, not a saving.
- **A private seller buying a Shop to list more.** At that volume the real question is whether you should be on a business account.
