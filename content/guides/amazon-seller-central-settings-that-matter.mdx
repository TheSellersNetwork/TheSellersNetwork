This is for anyone who has just opened a UK Seller Central account, or has had one for a while and never looked past the dashboard. Amazon's defaults are set for Amazon's benefit: they make your stock available in as many countries as possible and copy your listings everywhere. After reading this you will have gone through the settings once, in order, and know why each one is set the way it is.

Menu names move around, and Amazon tests different layouts on different accounts, so if a path here does not match your screen use the search box at the top of Seller Central with the setting's name. For everything else about getting started, see [the working manual](/guides/how-to-start-amazon-fba-uk); this guide only covers the settings. The gear icon at the top right is what we mean by Settings.

## The order to do them in

Do these before your first shipment. Each takes a few minutes; the whole list is an evening.

| Order | Setting | Why it comes first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Two step verification | Everything else is behind this login |
| 2 | Overseas shipping and export | Stops your first stock going abroad |
| 3 | Build International Listings | Stops your first listings going abroad |
| 4 | Holiday settings on other marketplaces | Belt and braces for 2 and 3 |
| 5 | Dangerous goods programme | Approval takes weeks; apply now |
| 6 | Deposit and charge methods | So you get paid and the fees get paid |
| 7 | Tax settings | Gets VAT right from the first invoice |
| 8 | Business information and display | Decide what the public sees before it is public |
| 9 | Return and removal settings | Where unsellable stock goes |
| 10 | Notification preferences | So you see the emails that matter |
| 11 | A second user | So a locked login is not a locked business |
| 12 | A separate buying account | Not a setting, but a rule |

## 1. Two step verification

**Path.** Settings, Login Settings (or Advanced Security Settings), Two-Step Verification.

**What to do.** Turn it on with an authenticator app as the primary method and a phone number as backup. Amazon requires two step verification for seller accounts and will prompt you, but check it is actually on and that the backup number is one you control. Save the backup codes somewhere that is not the same phone.

**Why.** A Seller Central login is the business. The people who lose accounts to a phished password lose everything in them: payouts, stock they cannot remove, and the history that brand approvals were built on.

## 2. Overseas shipping and export settings

**Path.** Settings, Fulfilment by Amazon, then scroll to Shipping programmes and export settings, and Edit next to amazon.co.uk.

**What to do.** For a new UK seller, disable FBA Export, or restrict it to the UK only. If Amazon shows separate switches for individual countries or regions, turn off every one you have not chosen.

**Why.** FBA Export lets Amazon sell your UK stock to customers in other countries and ship it from the UK warehouse at Amazon's export rates; Amazon describes the programme in its [FBA Export policy](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/02/rainier/help/legal/FBA_Export_Program_Policy_EN_161220.pdf). It sounds like free reach. For arbitrage it usually is not: the fulfilment fee and the returns are different from what your calculator assumed, an international customer's return can take weeks to come back and arrive unsellable, and intellectual property complaints from brands in countries you never intended to sell in count against a UK account just the same. Leave it off until you have a reason, a calculator set up for it and a stomach for the returns.

## 3. Build International Listings

**Path.** Inventory, Sell Globally (or Settings, Account Info, then the international listings link), Build International Listings.

**What to do.** Check that no connections exist between your UK marketplace and any other. If a connection is there (Amazon sometimes creates one during registration), remove it and remove the listings it created on the other marketplaces.

**Why.** A UK Seller Central account is a European unified account: the same login can list on the German, French, Italian, Spanish and other European stores. Build International Listings copies your UK offers to those stores automatically and sets a price from a rule. Copied offers on marketplaces you are not watching mean orders you did not want, in languages you do not read, with VAT obligations in countries you have not registered in. Turn it off before you list your first product, because once listings exist elsewhere they have to be closed one marketplace at a time.

## 4. Holiday settings on other marketplaces

**Path.** Settings, Account Info, Listings status, then Going on holiday? and set each non UK marketplace to Inactive.

**What to do.** Leave the UK Active. Set every other marketplace the account shows to Inactive.

**Why.** Even with export off and international listings off, an offer can appear on another store by a route you did not expect. Setting those marketplaces inactive means nothing sells there whatever happens. It costs nothing and you can switch a marketplace on in a minute when you decide to expand deliberately.

## 5. The dangerous goods programme

**Path.** Settings, Fulfilment by Amazon, then the Dangerous goods (hazmat) programme section, and Apply. Amazon's [programme page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/gp/help/external/GZLZBQ7W6QZRKWWK) describes it.

**What to do.** Apply now, before you need it. Once enrolled, products that are classified as dangerous goods can be sent to FBA under the programme's conditions instead of being blocked.

**Why.** Aerosols, perfume, nail polish, some cleaning products, anything with a lithium battery and a surprising number of ordinary items are classified as dangerous goods for storage and transport. A product flagged as one cannot be shipped in until it is classified, which needs the information on Amazon's [required information page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/gp/help/external/G201371860) (a safety data sheet or an exemption sheet) and takes weeks. Most sellers meet this the first time they try to send a bottle of something and have stock stranded at home for a month. Apply while you have nothing waiting.

## 6. Deposit and charge methods

**Path.** Settings, Account Info, Payment Information, Deposit Methods and Charge Methods. Each has an Assign option for marketplaces.

**What to do.** Add a UK business bank account as the deposit method and assign it to the UK marketplace. Add a debit or credit card as the charge method and assign it to the UK marketplace too. If the account shows other marketplaces, assign the same methods there as well so nothing is left with no method at all.

**Why.** Payouts go to the deposit method; fees are charged to the deposit method when there is a balance and to the charge method when there is not. A marketplace with no valid method can stop payouts across the account. Use a business account, not a personal one, because the name on it must match the name on the account exactly and because separating the money makes bookkeeping and any future verification much simpler. Amazon can re-verify bank details from time to time; keep a statement to hand.

## 7. Tax settings and VAT number

**Path.** Settings, Account Info, Tax Information (VAT information), and the VAT Calculation Service settings under the same section.

**What to do.** If you are not VAT registered, leave the VAT number blank and do not sign up for VAT calculation. If you are registered, add your VAT number as soon as you have it and consider enabling the VAT Calculation Service so Amazon issues VAT invoices to customers for you.

**Why.** With a VAT number on file, Amazon's fee invoices show your number, Amazon can generate the invoices that Amazon Business customers are entitled to, and your accountant can reclaim the 20% VAT Amazon has charged on every fee since August 2024. Without one, and if you are not registered, that VAT is a cost and you must produce business invoices yourself within 48 hours of payment. Whether and when to register is a decision to take with an accountant against the current [VAT threshold](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat); none of this is tax advice. Do not enter a VAT number that is not yours, or one that has not been issued yet.

## 8. Business information and what the public sees

**Path.** Settings, Account Info, Business Information, then Display Name and Business Address. Your public storefront is at the link Amazon shows next to your display name.

**What to do.** Set a display name you are happy to trade under (it can differ from your legal name and appears on every offer). Then look at your storefront as a customer would and read the business address shown there.

**Why.** Amazon publishes a business name and address on your storefront and on the seller information panel of each listing. For a limited company that is the registered office; for a sole trader it is the address you gave. If that is your home and you would rather it were not on the internet, arrange a virtual office or a registered office address now, because changing the business address later triggers re-verification and can pause selling while it runs. Do not change the legal name or address in this section casually for the same reason; everything (bank, invoices, Companies House) has to match it.

## 9. Return settings and the removal address

**Path.** Settings, Return Settings for the customer facing side (this mostly matters if you also fulfil orders yourself). For FBA, Settings, Fulfilment by Amazon, then Automated unfulfillable settings, and the default address shown when you create a removal order.

**What to do.** Check the return address Amazon holds for you is an address where you can receive parcels. Decide on automated unfulfillable settings: Amazon can automatically return unsellable stock to you or dispose of it on a schedule; for a small seller, automatic return to you is the safer default, because disposal is final and returned stock can often be recovered (see [where the money leaks](/guides/amazon-where-the-money-leaks)). Set the removal address to wherever you actually open boxes, which may not be the business address.

**Why.** Unfulfillable stock sits in the warehouse being charged storage until something happens to it. Sellers who never set this discover a year of accumulated returns when the storage bill arrives. Sellers who set it to a prep centre they stopped using discover their stock went somewhere else.

## 10. Notification preferences

**Path.** Settings, Notification Preferences.

**What to do.** Turn on, and route to an inbox you read daily: account health and performance notifications, listing notifications (including removed or suppressed listings), order and return notifications for FBA, claims and chargebacks, and reports on shipments (received, closed, discrepancies). Turn off the marketing and promotional categories so the important ones are not buried. Consider a separate email address or a filtered folder for Amazon so a deactivation notice cannot hide behind a newsletter.

**Why.** Amazon gives deadlines (for a compliance document, for an appeal, for an invoice) that start from the notification, not from when you read it. The sellers who lose accounts over an unanswered notice were almost always sent the notice.

## 11. User permissions and a second user

**Path.** Settings, User Permissions, Add a new user. Amazon emails an invitation; the new user creates their own login and completes Amazon's verification for secondary users.

**What to do.** Add one trusted person (a partner, family member or business partner) with their own email address and phone number, and give them enough permission to act if you cannot: at minimum, view and manage orders, inventory and account settings. Store their details where you can find them. Review the user list twice a year and remove anyone who no longer needs access.

**Why.** If your primary login is locked (a lost phone, a security review, a mistaken flag on the primary account holder), a verified secondary user can still log in, ship, answer messages and withdraw money. Without one, the business stops until Amazon's identity process finishes, which can take weeks. Secondary users also matter the other way: never share your own login with an assistant or a service. Give them a user with limited permissions, so that when they leave, you remove the user rather than change your password.

## 12. A separate buying account

**Path.** There is no setting. Open a normal Amazon customer account on a different email address and a different phone number from your Seller Central login, and buy everything for the business (stock, packaging, supplies) on that one.

**What to do.** Never buy on the customer side of the account that is linked to Seller Central. Never let the buying account's email or phone match anything on the seller account.

**Why.** Amazon links accounts by shared details. A problem on a buying account (returns it considers excessive, a payment dispute, a suspected abuse flag) can be applied to a linked seller account, and a problem on the seller account can lock you out of buying. Keeping them apart means one cannot take the other down. It also keeps business purchases in one place for the invoice folder, and it is the account to use for Amazon to Amazon buys where a seller account buying from its own listing's competitors looks like exactly what it is.

## Settings to look at once you are trading

- **Business insurance.** Settings, Account Info, Business Insurance. Amazon's UK agreement requires product liability cover once your gross sales pass a threshold for three consecutive months; the figures are in [the manual](/guides/how-to-start-amazon-fba-uk). Upload the certificate here when you have it.
- **Selling plan.** Settings, Account Info, Your Services. Professional for almost everyone; switch here.
- **FBA settings for inbound.** Settings, Fulfilment by Amazon: the barcode preference (Amazon barcodes, since commingling ended), the inbound placement and shipment options, and who labels (you, unless you have priced Amazon's labelling into the margin).
- **Your Info and Policies.** Settings, Your Info and Policies, for the seller profile customers see: a short description, and nothing that promises more than Amazon's own returns policy.
- **Login history and app access.** Under login settings, review connected apps and devices when you cancel a tool. A repricer or profit tracker you stopped paying for should not still have access.

## What goes wrong

- **Leaving export on and finding out from a French return.** Setting 2, before the first shipment.
- **Listings appearing in Germany.** Build International Listings created a connection during setup. Remove it and close the offers.
- **Stock stranded for a month over a bottle of perfume.** Apply to the dangerous goods programme before you need it.
- **A payout held because a marketplace has no charge method.** Assign methods to every marketplace the account shows, not just the UK.
- **Your home address on the storefront.** Look at the storefront before the first sale, not after.
- **A deactivation notice in the promotions folder.** Notification preferences, and a filtered inbox you check daily.
- **A locked login with no second user.** Add one in week one, and verify them.
- **A shared password with an assistant who left.** Users get their own login; you remove it.
- **Changing the legal name or address to "tidy up".** It triggers re-verification. Change it only when it has actually changed, with matching documents ready.

## Questions

**I already have offers on other marketplaces. Do I have to close them?** Not necessarily, but decide deliberately. If you are not watching those stores daily and have not thought about VAT there, close the offers and set the marketplaces inactive until you do.

**Can I use one email for the seller account and the buying account if the phone numbers differ?** No. Different email and different phone, and ideally different payment cards.

**Amazon is asking me to re-verify after I changed my address. Is that normal?** Yes. Address and name changes trigger it. Have a document with the new address, dated within the last 90 days, before you make the change.

**Which of these can I skip if I am only ever selling in the UK?** None. The overseas settings are exactly for people who only want to sell in the UK.
