This is for anyone selling physical goods to UK buyers on eBay, Vinted, their own site or anywhere else, who wants to stop guessing at the post office counter. After reading it you will be able to size and weigh an item, choose between a large letter and a parcel, decide when tracking is worth paying for, know when a courier beats Royal Mail, and post in a way that eBay will actually back you on if a buyer says nothing arrived.

Prices in this guide were checked on 25 September 2026 and link to the official page. Royal Mail has announced a further change from 5 October 2026 on its [prices page](https://www.royalmail.com/prices2026), so treat every figure here as a marker for the size of the decision, and take the current number from the page before you build it into a listing. None of this is legal advice.

## The sizes decide the price

Royal Mail prices by format first and weight second. The format is set by the outer dimensions of the packed item, and a few millimetres either side of a line can change the price by pounds. The official limits are on Royal Mail's [size and weight guide](https://help.royalmail.com/personal/s/article/Size-and-weight-guide).

| Format | Maximum size | Maximum weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Letter | 24cm x 16.5cm x 0.5cm | 100g |
| Large letter | 35.3cm x 25cm x 2.5cm | 750g (1kg for Tracked 24 and 48 bought online) |
| Small parcel | 45cm x 35cm x 16cm | 2kg |
| Medium parcel | 61cm x 46cm x 46cm | 20kg |
| Tubes and rolls | Length plus twice the diameter no more than 104cm, longest side no more than 90cm | As the parcel band |
| Parcelforce | 1.5m long, 3m length and girth combined | 30kg |

Two of these lines matter more than the rest for a reseller.

**The large letter line.** Anything that packs to 2.5cm or thinner and under 750g (1kg on tracked services bought online) goes as a large letter. Clothing folded flat, books, trading cards, DVDs, cables, cosmetics, jewellery, small parts and most things sold under £20 can be made to fit. The price gap between a large letter and a small parcel is the biggest single saving available to a small seller, and it comes purely from packing. Buy a 2.5cm slot gauge or cut one from card and check every item before you choose the service.

**The 2kg line.** Small parcel stops at 2kg. Above it you are into medium parcel prices, and at that point a courier is often cheaper than Royal Mail.

Weigh the packed item, not the bare item, and write the packed weight and size into your listing notes so you never re-measure a product you sell repeatedly.

## Royal Mail's services, in the order you will use them

Royal Mail lists its UK services with starting prices on the [UK services page](https://www.royalmail.com/sending/uk), and a summary of current online prices on its [prices page](https://www.royalmail.com/current-postage-prices). What follows is what each one is for.

### 2nd Class and 1st Class

The basic services. No tracking. Delivery aim within three working days for 2nd Class and next working day for 1st Class. Both include compensation up to £20 for loss or damage, and only if you have proof of posting (more on that below). Online prices on 25 September 2026 started at 91p for a 2nd Class letter, £1.80 for 1st Class, and £3.95 for a 2nd Class small parcel bought online.

Since Royal Mail moved 2nd Class parcels onto barcoded labels, buying online gives you a "delivery confirmation" scan on many items: the buyer and you can see it was delivered, but it is not full tracking and it does not, on its own, meet eBay's proof of delivery test if the scan is missing. Treat it as a bonus, not a protection.

Use 2nd Class for cheap, light, easily replaced items where the occasional loss is a cost of doing business.

### Tracked 48 and Tracked 24

Tracked to the delivery point, with a photo on delivery and notifications to the buyer. Compensation up to £75 is included (see the [Tracked 24 page](https://www.royalmail.com/sending/uk/tracked-24)). Delivery aims of two working days and next working day. On 25 September 2026 a Tracked 48 small parcel started at £3.65 online and Tracked 24 at £4.65. The large letter format on Tracked services is priced lower again (the UK services page showed Tracked 48 "from £2.85") and carries the 1kg limit rather than 750g.

Tracked services are bought online (Royal Mail's own site or app, or through eBay's label tool), and dropped at a Post Office, Royal Mail customer service point, parcel postbox or locker, or collected from your door for a small fee when bought online. Signature and age verification are paid extras.

This is the default service for a business seller. The difference between Tracked 48 and 2nd Class is small on a small parcel and it buys you a delivery scan that eBay accepts, a photo the buyer can see, and a higher compensation limit. Sellers we know put almost everything over about £10 on Tracked 48 and reserve Tracked 24 for buyers who paid for express.

### Signed For (1st and 2nd Class)

An add-on to the basic services, not a tracked service. You get a signature at the door and online delivery confirmation, but no scans in transit. Compensation stays at £20 (see [Signed For 1st Class](https://www.royalmail.com/sending/uk/signed-for-1st-class)). Prices started at £2.91 for 2nd Class Signed For and £3.80 for 1st Class Signed For on 25 September 2026.

Worth using only when you specifically want a signature on something cheap. Where the value matters, Tracked with the signature option or Special Delivery does the job better.

### Special Delivery Guaranteed by 1pm

Full tracking, signature and photo on delivery, a next working day guarantee with a refund if missed, and compensation up to £750 included, with cover up to £2,500 available for a fee (see the [Special Delivery page](https://www.royalmail.com/sending/uk/special-delivery-guaranteed-1pm)). Maximum 20kg. Starting price on 25 September 2026 was £9.45. It must be handed over at a Post Office or a Royal Mail customer service point; it cannot go in a postbox, and if you book a collection Royal Mail adds a working day to the guarantee.

Use it for anything worth more than the Tracked compensation limit, for anything eBay requires a signature on (orders of £450 or more, see below), and for anything you could not afford to lose. Price it into the listing rather than absorbing it. There is also a 9am version, limited to 2kg and priced from £53.95, which is for the rare buyer who needs it.

### Parcelforce

Royal Mail's parcel arm handles items over 20kg or over the medium parcel size, up to 30kg; express48 started at £11.95 on Royal Mail's prices page. A courier will usually undercut it.

## When a courier beats Royal Mail

The honest answer for a small seller in 2026: couriers are often cheaper on parcels, and Royal Mail still wins on large letters and on the value of a Post Office network.

| Situation | Better choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fits a large letter | Royal Mail | Nobody else has a format this cheap for flat items |
| Small parcel, under £20 item | Either; compare | Locker and shop couriers often undercut Tracked 48 by pennies to a pound |
| 2kg to 15kg | Courier | Royal Mail's medium parcel prices climb fast above 2kg |
| Over 15kg or oversize | Parcelforce or a business courier account | Most consumer courier services stop at 15kg |
| High value, needs guarantee | Royal Mail Special Delivery | Guaranteed next day with £750 cover included |
| Buyer wants a locker | InPost or Evri | Locker to locker is the cheapest tracked option going |

Every courier's own page has the current price and limits, and they change through the year, so compare on the day:

- [Evri](https://www.evri.com/send): shop, locker and collection options, tracking and £20 cover included as standard, parcels up to 15kg. On 25 September 2026 the page showed standard delivery from £2.62 during a sale ending 30 September 2026, and its own comparison table against Royal Mail's tracked prices.
- [InPost](https://inpost.co.uk/send): locker to locker labels priced by compartment size, starting at £1.99 for a small compartment on 25 September 2026, no printer needed. The largest compartment takes 41cm x 38cm x 64cm and 15kg according to InPost's [locker size page](https://inpost.co.uk/help/how-big-are-inpost-lockers). Check InPost's help pages for the cover included and the paid upgrade before sending anything valuable.
- [DPD](https://www.dpd.co.uk/content/products_services/uk_services.jsp): one hour delivery windows and strong tracking, sold mainly through business accounts and through resellers' label platforms rather than at a walk-in price. Worth it for fragile or high value parcels where you want the buyer to be in.
- [Parcelforce](https://www.parcelforce.com/): the heavy and large option, with compensation included on all services.

Cheap courier prices assume a shop or locker drop and a standard size; add a collection or a "large" band and the gap closes. The cheapest services also tend to have the tightest claims process, so weigh the cover level, not just the price.

## Buying postage through eBay

For eBay orders, buy the label inside eBay rather than on the carrier's site. eBay's [postage labels page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/posting-items/labels-packaging-tips/buying-printing-postage-labels?id=4157) explains the options: Royal Mail labels paid from your eBay funds, Packlink labels for Evri, Yodel, UPS, InPost and DPD, Parcelforce, and QR codes so you can drop off without a printer. Tracking is uploaded to the order automatically for tracked services, which is the single most useful thing about it: no copying tracking numbers, no forgotten uploads, no lost protection. The [eBay Delivery by Packlink help centre](https://support-ebay.packlink.com/hc/en-gb) covers bulk buying non Royal Mail labels.

**Simple Delivery** is eBay's managed postage. The buyer pays a rate eBay sets at checkout (or you offer it free and eBay deducts the cost), eBay issues a prepaid label or QR code with tracking already attached, and eBay covers loss or damage between the first carrier scan and delivery. Under eBay's [Simple Delivery page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/posting-items/setting-postage-options/simple-delivery?id=5575) it is the only method for private sellers on eligible items; business sellers can choose whether to use it. The trade is control for convenience: you lose the ability to pick a specific service and to bake your own postage margin in, and you gain eBay handling the delivery dispute. For a business seller with a routine that already works, keep your own postage. For someone starting out, or shifting a lot of low value stock, it is a reasonable way to avoid the whole proof of delivery problem.

## Tracking, proof of posting and what each one protects

These are three different things and sellers confuse them constantly.

**Proof of posting** is evidence that you handed an item to Royal Mail on a date. It is free at a Post Office counter (ask for it), and Royal Mail's app now gives a digital proof of posting once an item dropped in a postbox is scanned; details on Royal Mail's [proof of posting page](https://help.royalmail.com/personal/s/article/Help-with-proof-of-posting-and-delivery). Proof of posting is what unlocks a Royal Mail compensation claim. It does nothing for you on eBay. eBay does not care that you posted it; eBay cares whether it was delivered.

**Tracking** is a scan history ending in a delivery scan. This is what protects you on eBay. eBay's [seller protections](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-protections?id=4345) say that for an item not received claim you are protected if the tracking number can be validated on the carrier's site, was uploaded to eBay before the latest estimated delivery date, shows "delivered" or "attempted delivery" with a date, and shows an address matching the order at least to town or postcode. On orders with a total of £450 or more (item, postage and any tax together), you also need a signature; the threshold is in eBay's [signature confirmation policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behaviour-policies/signature-confirmation-policy?id=5154).

**A signature** proves a person accepted it. It does not prove which person. It only matters on eBay above £450, and for your own peace of mind on anything expensive.

So the rules for a seller are simple. Untracked post protects you against nothing on eBay. Tracked post protects you against "it never arrived" if the scan says delivered. Nothing protects you against "it arrived but it is not what I ordered"; that is a returns problem, covered in our returns guide.

## How postage choice changes what happens on an item not received claim

When a buyer opens an item not received request, eBay's [Money Back Guarantee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy?id=4210) gives you three business days to respond with tracking showing delivery or a refund. What you posted with decides how that goes.

| You sent it by | Tracking shows | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2nd or 1st Class, no proof | Nothing | You refund. Then nothing to claim from Royal Mail either |
| 2nd or 1st Class with proof of posting | Nothing | You refund the buyer, then claim up to £20 plus postage from Royal Mail after the waiting period |
| Tracked 24 or 48 | Delivered, correct postcode | You are protected; eBay closes in your favour if the buyer escalates |
| Tracked 24 or 48 | Still in transit after the estimated date | You refund or wait; if it never scans as delivered, claim from Royal Mail up to £75 |
| Special Delivery | Delivered and signed | Protected, and the signature covers you above £450 |
| Any service, order £450 or more, no signature | Delivered | Not protected; eBay treats it as undelivered |

A refund you give on an eBay item not received request, before eBay steps in, does not count as a defect. A case eBay has to decide against you does. That asymmetry is why sellers who post untracked should refund quickly and claim from Royal Mail, rather than argue.

## Decisions, with our recommendation

**Should I offer free postage?** Yes for anything where the postage is a small share of the price, with the cost built into the item price. eBay's Premium Service badge requires a free delivery option within three working days, and buyers sort by total price anyway. Charge separately on heavy or bulky items where a flat free rate would lose you money on far postcodes.

**Tracked on everything, or only above a threshold?** Set a threshold and stick to it. A common one is around £10 to £15: below it, 2nd Class with proof of posting and accept the odd loss; above it, Tracked 48. If you are aiming for eBay Premium Service, tracked is required on everything listed at £20 and over (see eBay's [Premium Service page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/protection/ebay-premium-service)), so set the threshold there.

**Royal Mail or a courier as the default parcel service?** Royal Mail for anything under 2kg unless a courier is clearly cheaper on the day, because a Post Office is easy, the claims process is documented, and buyers know the brand. Courier for anything over 2kg. If you post more than a few parcels a day, price both through eBay's label tool for a week and see where your money goes.

**Should I let the buyer choose?** Offer two options: your standard (free or cheap, tracked) and an express (Tracked 24 or Special Delivery at cost). Do not offer untracked as a paid option to the buyer; if it goes missing they still get a refund and you have no evidence.

**Collection or drop off?** Drop off while volume is low; Royal Mail charges a small per item fee for collection on Tracked bought online. Book collections once you have more parcels than you can carry, and remember that a collection adds a day to the Special Delivery guarantee.

## What goes wrong

- **Choosing the format by the bare item.** A book that is 2.4cm becomes a 2.8cm small parcel once it is in a padded bag. Measure packed.
- **Posting Special Delivery in a postbox.** It has to be handed over. A postbox drop voids the guarantee and the compensation.
- **Buying a label and posting it a week later.** Online labels must be posted within seven days of purchase, according to Royal Mail's [labels page](https://help.royalmail.com/personal/s/article/Labels-and-packaging-guidelines-tips). Late posting can mean no compensation.
- **Uploading tracking after the estimated delivery date.** The seller protection test says before. Use eBay's label tool or upload the number the day you post.
- **Sending a £500 order Tracked without a signature.** Delivered is not enough at £450 and over. Add the signature option or use Special Delivery.
- **Skipping proof of posting because it is tracked.** Tracking proves delivery; proof of posting is still what Royal Mail asks for in a claim if the item is lost in transit. Get both.
- **Underestimating a "from" price.** Every figure on Royal Mail's pages is the lightest, smallest band. Price the band you actually send.
- **Not re-checking after a price change.** Royal Mail changes prices more than once a year and has announced changes from 5 October 2026. A listing priced on old postage loses margin silently.

## Questions

**Is the cheapest option ever untracked 2nd Class?** For items under about £5 to £8, usually yes: a lost item costs you the item plus refunded postage, and it happens rarely. Above that, the tracked premium is cheaper than the losses.

**Does "delivery confirmation" on a 2nd Class barcoded label count as tracking on eBay?** Only if the scan happened and says delivered. It often does; if it does not, you have nothing.

**What about Northern Ireland?** Royal Mail prices are the same to all UK addresses. Couriers sometimes add a surcharge for Northern Ireland, the Highlands and islands; check the "location charge" wording on the carrier's page before offering a flat rate.

**Where do I ask?** The [Postage and packaging](/community/c/ebay-postage-and-packaging) forum. Post the size, weight, value and where it is going and someone will have sent the same thing last week.
