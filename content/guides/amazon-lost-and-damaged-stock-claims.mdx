This is for FBA sellers who suspect Amazon has lost or broken some of their stock and want to know exactly what they can get back, by when, and how to prove it. After reading you will know the four types of claim, the window on each, which report shows each kind of loss, and what evidence to have ready before you press the button. [Where the money leaks](/guides/amazon-where-the-money-leaks) covers the monthly audit in outline; this guide is the detail behind it.

Every window and rule here comes from Amazon's UK help pages, linked where used. Amazon changed this policy twice in 2025 and adjusts the wording often, so read the linked page before you rely on a date. Nothing here is legal or financial advice.

## What Amazon promises, and what it does not

Amazon's [FBA lost and damaged inventory reimbursement policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/200213130?locale=en-GB) says that if a unit you sent in is lost or damaged at a facility or by a carrier operated by or for Amazon, it will either replace it with a new unit of the same product or pay you for it.

A unit only qualifies if all of these are true:

- It was registered in FBA when it was lost or damaged.
- It complied with FBA product restrictions and inventory requirements.
- You sent the exact items and quantities stated in your shipping plan.
- It was not pending disposal, and was not disposed of at your request or under Amazon's own right to dispose.
- It was not defective and was not damaged by a customer.
- Your account is in normal status when you file.

Two exclusions catch resellers more than any other. Customer returns that come back with a damaged box, a missing factory seal, missing packaging, tape or a shipping label over the box, or in a different box are treated as the customer's doing, and Amazon does not pay for them. And a unit that arrived at Amazon in a quantity that did not match your plan weakens every claim on that shipment, which is why the count at the packing stage matters.

## What you get paid

### Losses before a customer ordered

For shipment, warehouse and removal claims, Amazon now pays what it calls the **sourcing cost**: your cost to buy the product from a manufacturer, wholesaler or reseller, not including shipping, handling, customs duty or anything else ([policy page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/200213130?locale=en-GB)). It used to pay a value closer to the selling price. The change was announced for 10 March 2025 and then [pushed back to 31 March 2025](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/ed8abc33-a1d5-417d-b2e2-03b789099833) so sellers had time to enter their costs.

If you do not tell Amazon your cost, it uses its own estimate, built from comparable products sold by Amazon, other sellers and wholesale channels. For a reseller who paid near retail in a clearance aisle, that estimate can be well below what you actually paid. The fix is the **Manage Your Sourcing Cost** page inside the Inventory Defect and Reimbursement portal, where you enter your cost per product and upload proof if asked.

Amazon may decline a cost you submit if it is an outlier against similar products or its own estimate, if the document looks forged or is illegible, if your account is under suspicion, or if you already had a decision on the same item in the last 30 days and have nothing new to add. If it declines, it pays on your last approved figure or its estimate.

Units that were already unsellable when lost or damaged are paid at a reduced estimated cost. The maximum for any single unit is £2,000; Amazon suggests third-party insurance above that.

### Losses after a customer ordered

For customer return claims the value is the refund, or the price of the replacement, on the original order, minus applicable fees and VAT.

### Disputing the amount

If you think a reimbursement is too low, the policy page says you can file on the Get support page within 60 days of the reimbursement being issued. Some older guidance, including forum posts, says 90 days. Work to 60.

## The four claim types and their windows

The windows were cut on [9 January 2025](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/d0fc997e-de50-4100-9803-76b7993b66df). Before that, most claims could be filed for 18 months. A seller who still audits once a year is now losing most of what they find.

| Where it went wrong | Earliest you can claim | Deadline | Help page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shipment to Amazon | When the shipment shows as eligible for investigation on the Contents tab | Six months after the verified delivery date at the fulfilment centre | [Shipment claims](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GEYPWTSQRTXXXMVB?locale=en-GB) |
| In the fulfilment centre | The day it is reported lost or damaged | 60 days from that date | [Fulfilment centre claims](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GGEV4254LJJ9BAEG?locale=en-GB) |
| Customer returns | 45 days after the refund or replacement | 105 days after the refund or replacement | [Customer return claims](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G84Z6B8BTKVNV53S?locale=en-GB) |
| Removal, lost in transit | 15 days after the last confirmed movement | 74 or 75 days after the removal shipment was created (the page gives both; use 74) | [Removal claims](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G9ZB3H4DP4H72U6R?locale=en-GB) |
| Removal, damaged or wrong item | The day it is delivered to you | 60 days after delivery | [Removal claims](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G9ZB3H4DP4H72U6R?locale=en-GB) |

One inconsistency to know about: Amazon's general [FBA customer returns page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200379860?locale=en-GB) still says a returnless refund can be claimed up to 18 months after the refund. The claims page and the January 2025 announcement both say 105 days. Work to 105.

Claims filed too early are also a problem. The customer return window opens at 45 days because Amazon wants the customer to have had time to send the item back, and the removal window opens at 15 days so the parcel has time to arrive. Filing before then gets the claim declined and counts towards the behaviour Amazon warns about below.

## What Amazon now pays without being asked

Since [15 January 2025](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/d0fc997e-de50-4100-9803-76b7993b66df) Amazon reimburses units lost in its fulfilment centres as soon as they are reported lost, and says almost all warehouse and customer return cases are now reimbursed automatically. Removal claims still have to be filed by you.

For customer returns, the [FBA customer returns reimbursement policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G9N934L7Y4SFWPJ4?locale=en-GB) lists the cases Amazon pays automatically:

- The customer was refunded but did not return the item within 45 days.
- The item was lost in transit, either to the customer or on its way back.
- The item came back and was not authentic, or was a completely different product.

"Almost all" is the phrase to notice. The automation is good and it is not complete. The job each month is to find what it missed.

## Where to look

### The Inventory Defect and Reimbursement portal

This is the first stop for warehouse and customer return losses. It has three tabs: **Eligible for claim** lists defects you can still claim, filterable by FNSKU or defect type; **In progress** shows what Amazon is already investigating; **Resolved** shows what was settled, with the reason and the amount. If you disagree with a resolution on the Resolved tab, you can claim from there.

### The Inventory Ledger report

Under Reports, Fulfilment. It shows starting balance, receipts, customer orders, returns, adjustments, removals and ending balance for each FNSKU. Lost and damaged adjustments appear here with a transaction item ID, which is what the damaged claim tool asks for.

### The Reimbursements report

Every payment Amazon has made, by FNSKU, with the reason. Match each lost or damaged line in the ledger against this report. A loss with no matching payment, still inside its window, is a claim.

### Shipment Contents tab

For inbound shipments. Open the shipment in the Shipping queue, then Contents. It shows units expected against units received and the status of any discrepancy. Amazon's [shipment claims page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GEYPWTSQRTXXXMVB?locale=en-GB) asks you to confirm the contents match your plan before you claim.

### Removal Order Detail and Removal Shipment Detail reports

For stock coming back to you. The first shows order status; the second shows tracking IDs per parcel.

## Evidence, by claim type

Amazon will decline a claim that does not include what the relevant page asks for, so gather it before you open the form.

### Shipment to Amazon

- The Amazon shipment ID.
- Proof you owned the stock: a supplier invoice or a receipt from another seller, showing the date of purchase, product names that match the missing items, and quantities.
- Proof of delivery. For small parcels, the active tracking ID for each box. If you used your own carrier and did not enter tracking when you created the shipment, you must add it before the shipment can be investigated. For pallets, a delivery document stamped by Amazon showing box count and weight.

You get **one claim per shipment**. A second claim on the same shipment is declined. So wait until you are sure what is missing, and claim everything at once.

This is where a supermarket receipt that says "GIFT SET 12.00" is a problem. The product name on the proof has to match what went missing. Keep a note, next to each receipt, of the ASINs it covers, and where a receipt is vague, keep a photo of the stock on the day you bought it.

### Warehouse losses

Use the transaction ID, FNSKU and quantity shown on the Eligible for claim tab. Amazon may ask for the date or the fulfilment centre ID of the loss.

### Customer returns

Use the order ID shown in the portal. If you disagree with how Amazon graded a return (it said customer damaged, you think the warehouse did it), there is a separate route: create a removal order for that unit, and once it reaches you, file a grading dispute within 60 days of delivery.

### Removals

The [removal claims page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G9ZB3H4DP4H72U6R?locale=en-GB) is the most demanding. Depending on the problem it asks for the removal order ID, FNSKU, shipment ID and quantity, then:

| Problem | What to photograph or supply |
| --- | --- |
| Damaged unit | Full images of the unit, close-ups of the damage, the tracking label or packing slip, damage to the outer box |
| Missing parts | As above, plus the licence plate number (LPN) sticker, or six-sided images of the item if there is no LPN or FNSKU |
| Wrong item | The unit received, the FNSKU (X00) label and LPN, the tracking label, and evidence of the mismatch: material, serial number or model number against the catalogue |
| Units short in a parcel | The full shipping label showing tracking and weight, the delivery document showing the shortage, the packing list with missing units marked, the outer box if tampered |
| Whole shipment not delivered | Order ID, FNSKU, quantity, shipment ID and a description |

The practical rule: **open every removal parcel on camera, or at least photograph it before and after opening.** You cannot go back and take the label photo after the box is in the recycling.

## Filing without hurting yourself

Amazon's policy page is blunt that submitting insufficiently researched or premature requests, or a large number in a short time, can lead to delayed support, monitoring, investigation and account action. That warning is aimed at the bulk filing some services do on commission. For a small seller it means:

- File after the earliest date, never before.
- Check the In progress and Resolved tabs first so you do not claim something already handled.
- One clean claim with all the evidence beats three thin ones.
- Keep a simple log: date filed, case ID, FNSKU, amount claimed, amount paid.

Two further points from the policy. Amazon can reverse a reimbursement if the unit is later found and put back in your stock, and it may sell a unit it has paid you for, including through its own used-goods channel. Neither is worth fighting.

## A monthly routine

Once a month, on the same day:

1. Open the portal's Eligible for claim tab. Claim anything older than a few days that is still unpaid.
2. Check every inbound shipment delivered in the last six months that still shows a discrepancy. Claim once, with everything.
3. Pull refunds from 45 to 105 days ago. Any without a matching return or reimbursement is a claim.
4. Open removal orders created 15 to 74 days ago. Anything not delivered is a lost claim; anything delivered damaged or short in the last 60 days is a damaged claim.
5. Compare each reimbursement with your sourcing cost. Anything paid on Amazon's estimate that is below your real cost: update Manage Your Sourcing Cost, and dispute within 60 days.
6. Before sending new products in, enter their sourcing cost.

An hour a month, and it only works monthly. At 60 day windows, a quarterly check misses a third of what it finds.

## What goes wrong

- **Never entering sourcing costs.** Every reimbursement is paid on Amazon's estimate, and nobody checks.
- **Auditing annually.** The 18 month window went in January 2025. Most warehouse claims are now dead after 60 days.
- **Filing a shipment claim too soon.** One claim per shipment. File before receiving is finished and you have used your only go.
- **Receipts that do not name the product.** The proof of ownership has to match the items. A total on a till roll is weak evidence.
- **Throwing away removal packaging.** No label photo, no claim.
- **Disputing on a hunch.** Repeated thin claims are exactly what Amazon's warning describes.
- **Mixing up grading disputes and return claims.** A return graded customer damaged is disputed by removing the unit and then claiming, not through the customer return claim.
