This is for eBay UK sellers who have had, or will have, a buyer say "it never arrived". After reading it you will know exactly what eBay will ask for, what your postage choice has already decided, what to do on each day of the three business days you are given, when to refund without a fight and when not to, how to get money back from Royal Mail for a genuinely lost item, and how to stop any of it becoming a defect on your seller record.

Some parcels get lost. Some buyers lie. The process is the same for both and it is short, so the work is in what you did before the claim, not what you write during it. Nothing here is legal advice; eBay's and Royal Mail's own pages are linked wherever a rule or figure matters.

## How the Money Back Guarantee works for a missing item

eBay's [Money Back Guarantee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy?id=4210) promises the buyer a full refund, including original postage, if the item does not arrive at the address they gave at checkout. The buyer can report it once the estimated delivery date has passed and up to 30 calendar days after that date (see eBay's [buyer page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/buying/returns-refunds/reporting-item-didnt-receive?id=4042)). You then have three business days to respond. If it is not resolved, either side can ask eBay to step in, up to 21 business days from when the request opened. eBay says it normally decides within 48 hours.

eBay decides on one question: can the seller show the item was delivered? Not posted. Delivered. The proof of delivery test is on the [seller protections](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-protections?id=4345) page and it has five parts:

1. A tracking number that can be validated on the carrier's website.
2. Uploaded to eBay before the latest estimated delivery date.
3. A status of "delivered" or "attempted delivery".
4. The date of delivery or attempted delivery.
5. The recipient's address, matching the order at least to town or postcode.

And on orders with a total (item, postage and any tax) of £450 or more, signature confirmation as well, under the [signature confirmation policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behaviour-policies/signature-confirmation-policy?id=5154).

If you meet all of that, eBay closes the case in your favour, and if it had to step in, removes any defect, adjusts your late delivery rate and removes negative feedback. If you cannot, the buyer gets a refund. Which brings us to what your postage choice already decided.

## What tracking and proof of posting do and do not protect you from

| You have | On eBay | With Royal Mail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nothing (untracked, no receipt) | No protection; you refund | No claim; Royal Mail needs evidence of posting |
| Proof of posting only | No protection; you refund | A claim for the item's cost to you up to £20, plus postage, if you can also prove value |
| Tracking showing "in transit" or no scans | No protection; you refund | A claim after the waiting period, up to the service's compensation limit |
| Tracking showing delivered to the right postcode | Protected on orders under £450 | No claim (it was delivered) |
| Tracking showing delivered plus signature | Protected at any value | No claim |
| Special Delivery delivered and signed | Protected at any value | No claim; if lost, up to £750 included |

The lesson sellers learn expensively: **proof of posting is for Royal Mail, tracking is for eBay.** A Post Office receipt does not help you in an eBay case at all. A tracking number that never gets a delivery scan does not help you either, however genuine it looks. Only a delivery scan, with the right postcode, uploaded on time, is protection.

And what none of it protects you from: a buyer who says the item was delivered but not to them, or that the box was empty, or that it was the wrong thing. Those become "not as described" claims or reports of a delivery problem, and the delivery scan is your evidence but not an automatic win. Photograph high value items before packing and use signature where the value justifies it.

## The timeline of a request and a case

**Before anything opens.** The estimated delivery date on the order is eBay's own calculation from your dispatch time and the service you chose. Buyers cannot report a missing item before it passes. If tracking already shows a delay, message the buyer first with the tracking link and a date to check back; most requests that would have opened do not.

**Day 0: the request opens.** You get an email and it appears under Requests and disputes in Seller Hub. eBay's [help a buyer with an item they did not receive](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/help-buyer-item-didnt-receive?id=4116) page lists your options: add tracking, refund, message the buyer, or send a replacement.

**Days 1 to 3 (business days): your window.** Do one of the following, in the request, not by message alone:

- If tracking shows delivered to the right postcode: add or confirm the tracking in the request and reply with the delivery date, the address level shown and a polite suggestion to check with neighbours or a safe place. Do not refund. If the buyer escalates, eBay will find for you.
- If tracking shows in transit or stalled: tell the buyer the position and the date you will refund if it has not arrived; then refund on that date, inside the request, if it has not. Do not let the three days lapse without a response.
- If you have no tracking: refund now, in full, in the request. Then start the Royal Mail claim.
- If you have a replacement and the buyer wants the item: send it tracked and upload the new tracking.

**Day 3 onwards: escalation.** If nothing is agreed either side can ask eBay to step in. Once eBay is in, you can no longer refund the buyer yourself and the decision is out of your hands. eBay usually answers within 48 hours, and may hold funds from the order while it does.

**After a decision against you.** eBay refunds the buyer and recovers the money from you. You get no final value fee credit. It counts as a case closed without seller resolution: a defect. You have 30 calendar days to [appeal](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/appeal-outcome-case-seller?id=4369) with new information, such as tracking that now shows delivery or a signature you did not upload.

**After a decision for you.** The case closes, any defect and negative feedback tied to it come off automatically, and the buyer is not refunded by you (eBay may still refund them at its own cost in some circumstances, which does not affect you).

## How to respond

Short, factual, inside the request. The person reading it, if it escalates, is not the buyer; it is a member of eBay's team scanning for whether the evidence meets the test.

For a delivered item:

> Tracking [number] shows this was delivered on [date] to [postcode] at [time], with a photo on the carrier's site. Please check with neighbours, any safe place, and the carrier's photo. If you still cannot find it, let me know and I will raise a delivery investigation with the carrier.

For an item in transit:

> Tracking [number] shows the parcel last scanned at [place] on [date]. Royal Mail treats an item as lost after ten working days past the due date. If it has not arrived by [date] I will refund you in full through this request.

For an untracked item:

> I am sorry it has not arrived. I have refunded you in full through this request today. If it turns up, please let me know.

Never accuse, never mention that you have "reported this to eBay", never ask the buyer to close the request in exchange for a refund (refund inside the request and it closes), and never move the conversation off eBay.

## When to refund early

Refund early, without waiting the three days, when any of these is true:

- You have no tracking. You will lose a case and pick up a defect; a refund now is the same money without the defect.
- Tracking shows no delivery scan and the item has passed Royal Mail's "lost" threshold. Refund, then claim.
- The order is under the value at which a case is worth your time. Most sellers set this somewhere between £10 and £25.
- The buyer's account is new and the item is cheap. It is not worth finding out.

Do not refund early when tracking shows delivery to the right postcode and the order is under £450, or with a signature above it. That is the case eBay's protection exists for, and refunding voluntarily teaches the buyer that it works.

A refund you issue yourself through the request is not a defect. A refund eBay issues on your behalf after stepping in is. That is the whole reason early refunds exist as a tactic.

## Claiming from Royal Mail for lost post

If you refunded the buyer and the item really was lost, Royal Mail owes you something on most services. The rules are on its [compensation policy for loss](https://www.royalmail.com/retail-compensation-policy-loss), and the claim itself starts at the [Royal Mail Claims Centre](https://help.royalmail.com/personal/s/article/Royal-Mail-Claims-Centre-process-and-options), which routes you to the right form depending on how you paid and where you posted.

**When you can claim.** Not until the item is deemed lost: 10 working days after the due date for most services (the due date is the next working day for 1st Class and three working days for 2nd Class), or 5 working days for Special Delivery. If it turns up after that you can claim for delay but not loss.

**The deadline.** 80 calendar days from the date of posting. Miss it and there is no claim.

**What you need.** Royal Mail asks for basic evidence (names and addresses, service used, postage paid, where and when posted, description of contents) plus, for anything beyond a postage refund, "additional evidence": evidence of posting and evidence of value. Evidence of posting is a certificate of posting from a Post Office or customer service point, the collection confirmation if you booked a collection, or the digital proof of posting in Royal Mail's app once an item is scanned (see its [proof of posting page](https://help.royalmail.com/personal/s/article/Help-with-proof-of-posting-and-delivery)). Evidence of value is what it cost you: a supplier invoice, a receipt, a bank statement. Have all of it as digital files before you start; the claim is assessed on what you upload.

**What you get.** Royal Mail pays "actual loss", meaning what the item cost you to acquire, not what you sold it for. Its own example is a retailer who bought jeans for £15 and sold them for £25 claiming £15. Limits by service: up to £20 on 1st Class, 2nd Class and Signed For; up to £75 on Tracked 24 and 48 (the figure on the [Tracked 24 page](https://www.royalmail.com/sending/uk/tracked-24)); up to £750 included on Special Delivery, more if you bought it. Plus the postage. Royal Mail says it will give an outcome within 30 calendar days.

**What voids it.** Wrong or incomplete address, inadequate packaging, a breakable not marked "FRAGILE HANDLE WITH CARE" above the address, items on the prohibited or excluded list, and posting on a business account (which has its own [claims process](https://help.royalmail.com/business/s/article/Business-Account-Claims-Centre)). Cash and certain valuables are excluded on standard services.

Other carriers have their own limits and deadlines, and they tend to be shorter. Evri's [sending page](https://www.evri.com/send) states £20 cover included as standard; InPost's help pages state the included cover and the paid upgrade and short claim windows. Check the carrier's page when you choose the service, not when the parcel is missing.

Set up a simple log: order number, date posted, service, tracking, proof of posting file, and claim status. Claims are worth it in aggregate and forgotten individually.

## Keeping defects off your account

eBay's [seller standards policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-standards-policy?id=4347) defines a transaction defect as either a cancellation you initiate (out of stock, sold elsewhere) or a case closed without seller resolution (the buyer reported a problem, eBay stepped in, and found you responsible). Below Standard starts at more than 2% defects or more than 0.3% (or two) cases closed without resolution; Top Rated needs no more than 0.5% defects from no more than three buyers, and the same 0.3% or two cases. At small volumes, two lost cases in a year is the whole allowance.

Separately, eBay's [service metrics](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/service-metrics-policy?id=4769) compare your item not received rate to sellers like you every month. Requests count whether or not you win them. A "very high" rating needs at least 10 requests from 10 buyers, and brings payment holds until tracking shows dispatch. A single missing parcel does not move it; a courier that loses 3% of your parcels does.

What keeps both clean:

- **Tracked on anything you would mind refunding.** The delivery scan is the only evidence eBay accepts.
- **Buy labels through eBay** so tracking uploads automatically and on time. A tracking number uploaded after the estimated delivery date fails the test.
- **Signature at £450 and over.** Or the delivery scan is worth nothing.
- **Respond inside three business days, every time.** Silence is how buyers escalate.
- **Refund early when you will lose.** Voluntary refunds are not defects.
- **Never cancel because you cannot find the item.** That is a defect by definition. Find it, or send a substitute the buyer agrees to, or refund through a request the buyer opens.
- **Report abusive buyers.** Under the [abusive buyer policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behaviour-policies/abusive-buyer-policy?id=4374), a buyer who claims non receipt against a delivery scan is in breach; when eBay confirms it, the case, the defect and any feedback come off.
- **Appeal within 30 days** when you have the evidence. A won appeal removes the defect.
- **Watch where the losses are.** If one carrier or one service accounts for most of your requests, change it. The metric measures requests, not outcomes.

## What goes wrong

- **Treating a Post Office receipt as evidence in an eBay case.** It is not. Refund and claim from Royal Mail.
- **Uploading tracking after the estimated delivery date.** Fails the test even if it shows delivered.
- **Arguing with a delivery scan on your side.** Not needed; state it and wait.
- **Arguing without one.** Guaranteed loss plus a defect. Refund on day one.
- **Letting the three days pass.** eBay steps in, refunds, and records a case against you.
- **Refunding by message or outside eBay.** The request stays open and can still be escalated.
- **Missing the Royal Mail waiting period or the 80 day deadline.** Claim too early and it is rejected; claim too late and it is gone.
- **Claiming the selling price.** Royal Mail pays your cost. Have the supplier invoice.
- **Cancelling an order you cannot fulfil to "avoid a case".** Cancellations are defects too.
- **Ignoring a pattern.** Ten requests from ten buyers in the evaluation window is the point at which eBay's service metrics start to cost money.

## Questions

**The tracking says delivered but the buyer insists it was not. Who wins?** On an order under £450 with a delivery scan to the right postcode, you do, if the buyer escalates. Point them to the carrier's delivery photo and suggest neighbours and safe places. If they open a police or carrier investigation, cooperate, but do not refund voluntarily.

**Do I get my final value fee back if I refund?** If you refund through the request yourself, you may be eligible for a fee credit. If eBay refunds on your behalf after stepping in, you get no credit and a defect.

**Can I ask the buyer to wait longer than the three days?** You can ask, inside the request, with a date. Many buyers agree if the tracking shows movement. If they do not, refund or let it escalate; do not go silent.

**Does Simple Delivery change any of this?** For orders sent with an eBay Simple Delivery label, eBay covers loss from the carrier's first scan to delivery and handles the buyer's delivery dispute itself, per its [Simple Delivery page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/posting-items/setting-postage-options/simple-delivery?id=5575). You still need to hand the parcel over in time to get that first scan.

**Where do I ask?** The [Buyers and disputes](/community/c/ebay-buyers-and-disputes) forum for cases, and [Postage and packaging](/community/c/ebay-postage-and-packaging) for carrier claims. Post the service, what the tracking shows, the order value and the dates.
