This is for sellers who move stock through Facebook Marketplace, Vinted and eBay and spend too much of the day in message threads. After reading you will have a reply for each of the common messages, a rule for offers on each platform that protects your margin, a short list of scam patterns you can spot in the first line, a collection routine that is safe and quick, and a clear idea of when a conversation is over.

Platform rules and numbers below link to the official help page they come from. The platforms change them; check before relying on any of them. None of this is legal advice.

## "Is this still available"

On Facebook Marketplace this is a button. The buyer tapped it; they did not type it. Treat it as a door opening, not a question, and answer with something that moves them to the next step. The reply that converts has three parts and fits in two lines:

> Yes, still available. Collection from [area] any evening this week, or I can post for £[x]. Would [day] suit?

Yes, then the logistics, then a question that needs a decision. The question is what does the work. "Yes" on its own gets silence, because the buyer has to think of the next message. "Would Thursday suit?" gets a yes, a no, or a counter, and each of those is a conversation.

On Vinted the equivalent is "Hi, is this still for sale?" and the same reply works, minus collection: yes, it is as described, happy to send extra photos or measurements, and a nudge that offers are welcome or that the price is firm, whichever is true. Buyers on Vinted move on quickly; answer within a few hours where you can.

On eBay the message tends to be a question about the item rather than availability, because availability is on the page. Answer the question, then point them at the button: "Yes, it is the 2019 model. It is ready to go and posts same day if ordered before 2pm."

If the item has sold, say so in one line and mark it sold. Leaving sold items live is the main reason sellers drown in this message.

## Offers and haggling, platform by platform

Set the rule before the first message, not during it. The rule is a floor price per item, written down, below which you do not go. Everything below is about getting to the floor gracefully, not about whether to cross it.

### Facebook Marketplace

Everything is negotiable on Marketplace, and buyers know it, so price with a margin for it: a little above what you will take, not a lot, because a price that is obviously padded attracts the wrong offers. Then:

- **Offer within 10% to 15% of asking:** accept if you want it gone, or meet halfway. "I can do £45 if you can collect this week."
- **Offer at roughly half or below:** one line, no argument. "Thanks, but I can't go that low. The lowest I'd take is £[floor]. Let me know if that works." Then stop. Either they come back or they do not.
- **"What's your lowest price?"** Never answer with a number below your asking. "It's priced to sell at £50, but make me an offer and I'll tell you yes or no." Sending your floor to someone who has not offered anything sets the floor as the new asking.
- **"Will you take £X, cash, today?"** If X is at or above the floor, take it and say collection has to be today.
- **Sob stories.** Reply to the offer, not the story.

### Vinted

Vinted has offers built in, and it caps them: buyers can send up to 25 a day and go up to 40% below your price, and the [offer page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/258-i-want-to-make-an-offer-or-suggest-a-different-price) says an accepted offer is not binding until the buyer presses Buy now. So a stream of 40% off offers is the platform working as designed, and an accepted offer that never becomes a sale is normal, not rude.

- Price with the 40% floor in mind: if you would take £12 for a £20 item, list at £20 and accept at £14 to £16.
- Counter rather than decline. A counter keeps the buyer in the thread; a decline ends it. Counter once at a number you would be glad of, then hold.
- Bundle discounts do the haggling for you on multi-item buyers. Set them if you are a standard seller (Pro sellers cannot, per the [bundle page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/5/260-bundles-policy)), then say "there's a bundle discount if you add anything else" instead of taking a lower price on one item.
- Ignore offers that are at the 40% floor on items listed that day. Vinted tells buyers when an offer is declined; you do not have to say anything.
- A seller can send offers to people who have favourited an item. Sellers we know use a small offer to a favouriter after a week rather than a public price drop, because it keeps the listed price where it was.

### eBay

Best Offer is a formal process with time limits. You have 24 hours to respond to an offer; a counter or an offer you send to a buyer lasts 96 hours; buyers get three or five offers per item depending on the category; and, per eBay's [Best Offer page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/buying/buy-now/making-best-offer?id=4019), an accepted offer leaves the item available to other buyers until the buyer pays.

- Set automatic accept and decline limits on every listing with Best Offer. The auto decline catches the lowballs without you reading them, and the auto accept closes the deal at 3am. The accept limit is your floor plus a little; the decline limit is what you find insulting.
- Counter every offer between the two. A counter is free, and eBay tells the buyer it is a counter, which most take.
- Offers to watchers are the cleanest tool on any platform: a private discount, for 96 hours, to people who already wanted it. Use it before a public price drop.
- Messages asking for a "cheaper price if I buy direct" or "your PayPal address" are not haggling. See scams below.

### The three offer scripts

Keep these as saved replies.

**Accept.** "Done, £[x]. I'll mark it as yours. Collection from [area]; when suits?" (Or on Vinted: "Offer sent at £[x], press Buy now and I'll post tomorrow.")

**Counter.** "Thanks for the offer. I can't do £[their number], but I can meet you at £[counter]. Let me know."

**Decline, door open.** "Thanks, but the price is firm at £[x]; it's already priced against what these are selling for. If it's still here in a week, message me."

No explanation of your costs, no lecture on market value, no "I have had other interest" unless you have.

## Scripts for the rest of the weekly inbox

| Message | Reply |
| --- | --- |
| "Can you hold it for me until Saturday?" | "I can't hold without a deposit, but it's first come first served and I'll let you know if it goes." On Vinted or eBay, a hold does not exist; the button is the hold. |
| "Can you deliver?" | "I can post for £[x], tracked, or it's collection from [area]." Never drive to a stranger's house. |
| "Can you send more photos?" | Send them, promptly, of the exact thing they asked about. This buyer is real. |
| "Is it genuine?" | "Yes. Bought from [retailer type], and I've photographed the label and the code. Happy for you to check it on collection." If you cannot say yes, do not sell it. |
| "I'll take it" then silence | One follow up after 24 hours: "Still yours if you want it; otherwise I'll relist tomorrow." Then relist. |
| "Can I pay on collection?" | On Marketplace, yes, cash or an instant bank transfer that you see arrive before the item leaves your hands. On Vinted or eBay, no; payment goes through the platform. |
| "Can you do it cheaper if I come today?" | Same rule as any offer: floor or no. |
| Buyer arrives and re-haggles on the doorstep | "The price we agreed was £[x]. If that doesn't work, no problem." Say it pleasantly and be prepared to let them leave. Most pay. |

## Scams you can spot in the first line

Facebook's own [scams page](https://www.facebook.com/help/2374002556073992) and Vinted's [phishing guidance](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/628-recognise-spoof-and-phishing-messages) describe the patterns. Read both once. The versions sellers here see most:

**"Send me a code to prove you're real."** A text arrives with a verification code and the buyer asks you to read it back. It is a code for an account being set up in your name or number. Never share a code with anyone, for any reason.

**"My courier will collect, I've paid, here's the confirmation."** A screenshot of a payment, or an email that looks like a payment service, saying money is held until you provide a tracking number or pay a release fee. There is no money. Real payments appear in your own account, not in a screenshot.

**"I've overpaid by mistake, please refund the difference."** The original payment reverses; the refund does not. Never refund a payment you cannot see cleared in your own bank.

**"Let's do this off the app."** On Vinted and eBay this is the whole scam. Vinted's [buying safely page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/82) says payment outside the Buy now button has no Buyer Protection and no support; the same logic protects you as a seller. eBay's [policy on offers outside eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/payment-policies/offers-buy-sell-outside-ebay-policy?id=4272) bans sharing contact details before a sale and makes the seller liable for the final value fee even if the item never sells on eBay. Anyone asking for your PayPal address, bank details or phone number on these two platforms is either a scammer or about to cost you the platform's protection.

**"Pay friends and family / send as a gift."** On Marketplace a buyer who insists you accept a payment method you do not recognise, or who sends a link to "receive" money, is not a buyer.

**Links.** A message with a link to confirm a payment, verify your account, arrange a courier or "complete the order" is a phishing attempt. The platforms do not need you to leave the app to be paid. Report it to the platform (Vinted asks for screenshots to phishing@vinted.com).

**Urgency.** Every scam has a clock: pay now, reply now, the code expires. Real buyers can wait an hour.

**Too easy.** No questions, no haggling, full price, "just send me your details." Real buyers ask something.

If you have already sent money or details, contact your bank immediately, then report it to [Action Fraud](https://www.actionfraud.police.uk) (in Scotland, Police Scotland) and to the platform.

## Collection: etiquette and safety

Facebook's [tips for meeting in person](https://www.facebook.com/help/2329750133711372) cover the basics: agree the time, place and item details in advance, tell someone where you are going, and take a phone. The routine sellers here settle on:

- **Small, valuable items:** meet in a public place with people and cameras. A supermarket car park in daylight, a station forecourt, the foyer of a shop. Some police stations offer safe exchange spots; check your local force's website.
- **Large items:** collection from your home is unavoidable. Have someone else in the house, bring the item to the door or the drive, and do not invite the buyer in. If the item has to be seen inside, keep the front door open and the buyer between you and it.
- **Payment before the item moves.** Cash counted in front of you, or a bank transfer you watch land in your own app on your own phone. No screenshots, no "it's pending". If a transfer has not arrived, the item stays with you and you can wait together.
- **Test it together.** Plug it in, turn it on, count the pieces, with the buyer watching. This is what stops the "it didn't work when I got home" message.
- **Sold as seen.** Say it in the listing and in the thread. For private sales between individuals this is the position; if you sell as a business, consumer law applies to you and "sold as seen" does not override it, so do not lean on it.
- **No address until the day.** Give the area in the thread and the full address once the time is confirmed, by message, so there is a record.
- **Confirm the morning of.** "Still on for 6pm at [place]? Cash on collection, £[x]." A no-show costs an evening; the confirmation message catches most of them.
- **Be on time and be brief.** Pleasant, quick, done.

If anything about a buyer's messages makes you uneasy before the meeting, cancel. There is always another buyer for a fairly priced item.

## When to stop replying

Every message costs a minute, and some conversations are never going to end in a sale. Stop when:

- The same buyer has made a third offer below your floor. You have answered twice. Silence is the third answer.
- The buyer has asked for a hold, a delivery, a discount and more photos and has not committed to anything. One line: "Let me know if you'd like it at £[x], collection from [area]." Then nothing until they do.
- Any scam sign above appears. Do not engage, do not explain, do not warn them. Block and report. Vinted's [blocking page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/91-blocking-another-member) explains what blocking does: the member can no longer message you or buy from you. Facebook and eBay have the same options on the buyer's profile.
- The tone turns. Rude, threatening, or personal: one polite close, then block. Nothing you say improves it.
- A buyer who agreed a price and a time did not show and did not message. Do not chase. Relist and move on; if they come back, the price is the listed price.
- On eBay, a buyer who messages repeatedly before purchase with unusual conditions or complaints is a buyer to block before they buy, not after. The block list is under your buyer requirements.

The test for any thread is: has this person moved closer to paying since their last message? If not, your next reply should be the last one, and it should contain the price, the collection area and nothing else.

## What goes wrong

- **Answering "yes" and nothing else.** No question, no next step, no sale.
- **Sending your floor price to someone who has not offered.** It becomes the asking price.
- **Holding items for people who have not paid.** Then losing the real buyer.
- **Refunding an overpayment.** The original payment vanishes. Yours does not.
- **Reading a code out.** That is your number, or your account, being taken.
- **Taking a screenshot as payment.** Money is in your account or it does not exist.
- **Letting a stranger into the house alone.** Bring the item to the door.
- **Arguing with a lowball.** One reply, then silence. They either come back or they do not.
- **Leaving sold items live.** Every message thereafter is a wasted minute.
- **Leaving Vinted or eBay to complete a deal.** No protection, and on eBay a policy breach.

## Questions

**Do I have to accept a Vinted offer I accepted if a higher one comes in?** An accepted offer is not a sale until the buyer presses Buy now, and the item stays live to everyone. Whoever buys first gets it.

**A buyer on Marketplace wants to pay by bank transfer before collection. Fine?** Only if you see it arrive in your own banking app before the item leaves. Better still, on collection.
