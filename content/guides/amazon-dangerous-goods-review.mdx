This is for FBA sellers who have tried to send in a product and been told it is under dangerous goods review, or who have had stock already in the warehouse flagged and stranded. After reading you will know why the flag happens, which document clears it, how to fill in the listing so it is not flagged in the first place, and what to do when stock is already stuck. It assumes you have read the short note on the programme in [Seller Central settings that matter](/guides/amazon-seller-central-settings-that-matter#5-the-dangerous-goods-programme).

Rules and timings come from Amazon's UK help pages, linked where used. Amazon reviews and reclassifies products continually, so check the live classification of each ASIN rather than relying on what it was last month. Nothing here is legal advice, and transport law is not simplified by anything we say.

## What Amazon means by dangerous goods

Amazon's [dangerous goods identification guide](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201003400?locale=en-GB) defines dangerous goods (also called hazmat) as substances or materials that might pose a risk to health, safety, property or the environment during storage, handling or transport because they are flammable, pressurised, corrosive or otherwise harmful.

The key sentence on that page for a reseller: the physical and chemical properties of the finished product decide whether it is a dangerous good, and there is **no pre-defined classification by product type**. Two perfumes, two phone chargers or two cleaning sprays can be classified differently, which is why Amazon asks for documents for the specific product.

### Everyday products that get flagged

Amazon's own [examples page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200525640?locale=en-GB) is a useful reality check. It includes:

- **Beauty:** spray deodorant, hairspray, hair dye, perfume, self-tan, nail polish, shaving foam.
- **Household:** laundry liquid and powder, washing-up liquid, hand sanitiser, cleaners.
- **Electronics:** power banks, phones, chargers, speakers, smart watches, wireless earbuds and their cases.
- **Office:** toner, glue, tape, markers, highlighters, pens.
- **Food and drink:** essential oils, spirits, high-alcohol products, whipped cream, cooking extracts.
- **Toys:** battery-operated toys, chemistry sets, craft and painting kits.
- **Books:** magazines with beauty samples, e-readers.
- **Shoes and apparel:** light-up shoes, shoe polish, heated clothing.

Speakers and headphones appear because of magnets, which are class 9 for transport. Many flagged products carry no warning label; Amazon says the absence of labels does not mean a product is not a dangerous good.

### Classes Amazon will not take

The identification guide lists classes that are **prohibited from FBA** entirely: explosives, toxic gases, substances liable to spontaneous combustion, substances dangerous when wet, infectious substances and radioactive material. Other classes (flammable gases and liquids, flammable solids, oxidisers, toxic and corrosive substances, and class 9 miscellaneous, which includes lithium batteries and magnets) are accepted **in limited quantities only**. Lithium batteries must meet the conditions on Amazon's lithium battery page.

## Why a listing gets flagged

Amazon's [review process page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201749580?locale=en-GB) says catalogue information is the main way it identifies a possible dangerous good: title, description, bullet points and images. Its examples of what triggers review:

- The product needs a battery but the listing has no detailed battery information.
- Batteries are mentioned on the detail page **or in customer reviews**.
- The product contains or comes with something possibly hazardous, such as a screen cleaner or an aerosol.

Two consequences for resellers. You can be flagged because of words on a listing you did not write, including reviews. And a product can be reclassified at any time if the catalogue changes, which is why stock that was fine last month can be stranded today.

## What Amazon needs from you

### When you create or convert the offer

Amazon's [required information page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201371860?locale=en-GB) says that when you list a product for FBA you must complete **Add dangerous goods information** in the workflow. Without it the listing cannot be created or converted.

For batteries it asks, among other things: whether batteries are required and included, whether any are button or coin cells, the cell chemistry, number of batteries and cells, battery weight, lithium energy content in watt-hours, and whether the battery is packed with, contained in, or separate from the equipment. For chemical products it asks whether the product is regulated, and if so the UN number, weight or volume, the hazard pictograms, and ideally the safety data sheet link and flash point.

Answer from the product and its packaging, not from guesswork. Amazon says that incomplete, inaccurate or conflicting information can get a product blocked from FBA.

### A safety data sheet

A safety data sheet (SDS) is a 16-section document from the manufacturer or importer describing a product's properties, hazards and handling. Amazon's [required information page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201371860?locale=en-GB) says an SDS is only accepted if it:

- was created or updated within the **past five years**;
- includes current GHS/CLP hazard information;
- matches the listing: the product name on the SDS is the same as the listing title, and the manufacturer or brand matches the detail page;
- has all 16 sections.

A bundle or kit with several chemical products needs an SDS for each one.

The matching rule is where resellers get stuck. The manufacturer's SDS will name the product the way the manufacturer does, and the Amazon listing may call it something else. If the names do not line up, the document can be rejected. Where the listing title is wrong or misleading, it may be worth reporting the listing; [product safety and listing accuracy](/guides/amazon-product-safety-and-listing-accuracy#report-it-rather-than-sell-on-it) covers how.

### An exemption sheet

For products that do not need an SDS, Amazon accepts an exemption sheet, but **only** for two categories:

1. Batteries and battery-powered products.
2. Products without harmful chemicals.

Anything else submitted on an exemption sheet is rejected. You download the template from the Manage dangerous goods classification page, choose your language, and fill every cell highlighted in red; more cells turn red depending on your answers. It must be saved and uploaded as an Excel file. The battery sheet needs details such as watt-hours and voltage, which are usually on the battery, the packaging or the manual.

### Where to get the documents

The identification guide says the manufacturer is required to provide an SDS for regulated products, and you must pass it to Amazon. In practice:

- **Look first.** Many brands publish SDS documents on their websites, often under a product safety or professional section.
- **Ask the brand's customer service** for the SDS for the exact product and size, quoting the barcode.
- **Ask your supplier** if you bought wholesale.
- **Check the label** before buying. Hazard pictograms (a flame, an exclamation mark, a gas cylinder) tell you an SDS will be needed.

If you cannot get a valid SDS for a product that needs one, you cannot sell it through FBA. That is worth knowing before you buy twenty.

## How long it takes

Amazon's [review process page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201749580?locale=en-GB) says products with complete and correct information are reviewed and classified within **two working days** (Monday to Friday, excluding public holidays). A rejected document, or a request for more information, adds another two working days for each resubmission. Uploads on a Saturday or Sunday start the clock on Monday.

Two working days is the best case. A product that needs two or three attempts to get the SDS accepted takes a week or more, and that is why the advice in the [FBA manual](/guides/how-to-start-amazon-fba-uk) is to deal with dangerous goods before the stock is in a box.

Use the **Look up an ASIN** tool on the Manage dangerous goods classification page to see the current status of any product.

## The dangerous goods programme

Classification and the programme are different things. The [FBA dangerous goods programme page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GZLZBQ7W6QZRKWWK?locale=en-GB) says every product must be correctly classified whether or not you are in the programme. The programme is what lets you **store and sell** products that have been classified as allowed dangerous goods.

- The UK and EU programme has a **waiting list**. You apply from the Manage dangerous goods classification page.
- If stock you already have at Amazon is reclassified as a dangerous good, you are added to the waiting list automatically.
- Classified dangerous goods are stored in fulfilment centres that handle them. Stock at a standard centre is transferred, which can take several weeks, and shows as reserved while it moves.
- Products may be ground transport only, and so eligible for Prime four to five day delivery rather than faster options.
- Dangerous goods have their own [monthly storage rates](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200612770?locale=en-GB) and use separate capacity.

Apply before you need it. Joining the waiting list costs nothing.

## Fixing stock that is already flagged

### The 14 working day clock

This is the part that costs sellers money. Both the review process page and the programme page say: if a product is identified as a possible dangerous good while stock is already in a fulfilment centre, Amazon emails you, and you have **14 working days** to provide the documents. If you do not, **the stock is disposed of at your expense**. If stock is reclassified as a dangerous good that is prohibited from FBA, it is disposed of at your expense regardless.

Read Amazon emails about dangerous goods the day they arrive.

### Step by step

1. **Open Look up an ASIN** and read the current status and what is being asked for.
2. **Check the listing information you supplied.** Many flags are cleared by correcting the battery questions or the dangerous goods questions in your offer: batteries not declared, wrong cell type, missing watt-hours.
3. **Decide which document is needed.** Chemical product: SDS. Battery or battery-powered product, or a product with no harmful chemicals: exemption sheet.
4. **Get the document and check it against Amazon's rules** before uploading: date within five years, all 16 sections, product name and brand matching the listing. For an exemption sheet, every red cell filled.
5. **Upload** through Manage dangerous goods classification, Upload document.
6. **Diary the review date.** Two working days. If it is rejected, read the reason, fix that thing only, and resubmit.
7. **Once classified**, check the listing is still set to FBA. The review page reminds you to convert it back if needed. If it has been classified as an allowed dangerous good and you are not in the programme, it may stay unsellable until you are.
8. **If you disagree** with a classification, upload a new SDS or exemption sheet that supports your case. That is the appeal route.

The stranded inventory page will show these units under reasons such as dangerous goods in review or awaiting documents. [Inventory health](/guides/amazon-inventory-health#stranded-inventory) covers the rest of that page.

### When to give up

If you cannot get a valid SDS within the 14 working days, or the product is classified as prohibited, the choice is between removal and disposal. Removal is often not possible: Amazon's [remove inventory overview](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/200280650?locale=en-GB) says only hazmat in transport classes 8 or 9 can be returned to you; other classes can only be disposed of. Some sellers sell these products merchant fulfilled instead, where they can post them legally; [FBM, when it makes sense](/guides/amazon-fbm-when-it-makes-sense) covers that route, and the carrier's own rules decide what can be posted.

## Avoiding the problem when sourcing

- **Check the ASIN before buying.** The Look up an ASIN tool shows the current classification of a product you can already see in Seller Central.
- **Look at the label.** Pictograms, "flammable", "keep away from heat", a battery symbol.
- **Check the reviews.** If customers mention batteries or leaking, Amazon's system may too.
- **Budget the time.** For anything likely to need an SDS, add a week to the time between buying and selling.
- **Apply for the programme** now, while nothing is waiting.
- **Keep the documents.** Save every accepted SDS and exemption sheet with the ASIN in the file name. Amazon can ask again after a catalogue change.

## What goes wrong

- **Ignoring the email.** Fourteen working days, then disposal at your cost.
- **An SDS older than five years.** Rejected, and two more working days lost.
- **An SDS whose product name does not match the listing.** Rejected.
- **Using an exemption sheet for a chemical product.** Only batteries and products without harmful chemicals qualify.
- **Guessing the battery questions.** Conflicting answers get the listing blocked.
- **Sending hazmat in the same box as everything else.** [Creating shipments](/guides/amazon-adding-products-and-creating-shipments) explains why that holds up the whole shipment.
- **Expecting to get prohibited stock back.** Only classes 8 and 9 can be returned.
- **Buying a pallet of aerosols before checking.** Classification, the programme waiting list and limited quantity rules all apply.
