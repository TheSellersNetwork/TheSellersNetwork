This is for people who resell alongside a job and are wondering whether it could replace the job. It sets out the numbers to know before deciding, how to count your time so the hourly rate is honest, what HMRC expects once you are trading, and how to spot and head off burnout, which ends more reselling businesses than bad stock does. It is general information, not tax, legal or financial advice; every figure links to its official page, and the decision is yours.

## Are you already trading

If you buy things to sell them for profit, HMRC treats that as trading, whether or not you think of it as a business. GOV.UK's [working for yourself](https://www.gov.uk/working-for-yourself) page says you are trading if you sell regularly to make a profit, or sell items on a regular basis online, at car boot sales or through classified adverts. HMRC's [online selling guide](https://taxhelpforhustles.campaign.gov.uk/buying-or-making-things-to-sell-and-online-selling-tax-rules) makes the same point: buying to resell is trading, and once trading income passes £1,000 in a tax year you must register for Self Assessment by 5 October, even if you have a job as well.

If you are reading a guide about going full-time, you are almost certainly past that point. If you have not registered, do it now; see [bookkeeping and tax for resellers](/guides/bookkeeping-and-tax-for-resellers) for how. Platforms now report sellers' sales to HMRC under the [digital platform reporting rules](/blog/hmrc-platform-reporting-rules).

## The numbers to know before you decide

Turnover is not income. The question is not "how much do I sell" but "how much do I keep, per hour, reliably".

### 1. Twelve months of real profit

Work out profit from at least a full year of records, because reselling is seasonal (see the [UK reselling calendar](/guides/selling-calendar-uk)). A good November does not predict a good February.

For each month:

- **Sales** including postage charged.
- **Minus cost of the stock that sold.** This is why recording cost per item matters; the [stock tracker spreadsheet](/tools/downloads) does it.
- **Minus platform fees, postage, packaging and advertising.**
- **Minus overheads:** subscriptions, storage, phone, equipment, mileage.
- **Equals profit** before tax.

The [reseller bookkeeping spreadsheet](/tools/downloads) is laid out for this. If your records do not let you work this out, that is the first job, before any decision.

### 2. What that profit is after tax

Profit is taxed through Self Assessment, with income tax and Class 4 National Insurance on top of the [Personal Allowance](https://www.gov.uk/income-tax-rates). Nobody deducts it for you as an employer does. Set aside a share of every month's profit in a separate account for tax, and remember that [payments on account](https://www.gov.uk/understand-self-assessment-bill/payments-on-account) can mean paying part of next year's bill in advance.

### 3. Your hourly rate

This is the number most people never calculate, and it is the one that matters most.

Count every hour for a month: sourcing trips including travel, sorting, cleaning, photographing, listing, messages, packing, post office runs, returns, bookkeeping. Use a timer or a notebook, not a guess. Then:

**Monthly profit before tax ÷ hours worked = hourly rate.**

Compare it with the [National Minimum Wage](https://www.gov.uk/national-minimum-wage-rates) as a floor, and with what your job pays per hour. If reselling pays less per hour than your job, going full-time means working more hours for the same money, not the same hours for more.

### 4. What you would lose

An employer usually provides things that you will now pay for yourself or go without:

- **Sick pay and holiday pay.** When you do not work, nothing is sold.
- **An employer pension contribution.**
- **A steady monthly income,** which lenders and landlords like to see.
- **Colleagues,** which sounds trivial until you have worked alone for six months.

Put a figure on the ones you can price and add it to what the business needs to earn.

### 5. What you need to live on

Your fixed monthly household costs, plus tax, plus the items in step 4. The business needs to make that reliably, in the quiet months as well as the busy ones.

### 6. A buffer

Before leaving a job, sellers we know who managed it without panic had savings covering several months of household costs, separate from the stock budget. A slow month with no buffer leads to selling stock below the floor to pay rent, which makes the next month worse.

### If you claim benefits

If you receive Universal Credit, self-employment changes how your payment is worked out, including a possible [minimum income floor](https://www.gov.uk/self-employment-and-universal-credit) that assumes a level of earnings. Read the GOV.UK guidance and speak to your work coach before changing your hours.

## Testing it before you jump

The safest route is to prove the numbers while you still have the job.

- **Run it as a business for twelve months** with full records, even part-time.
- **Scale up to near full-time hours for a trial period** if your job allows it (annual leave, reduced hours, a sabbatical). See whether the profit scales with the hours, or whether you run out of stock, space or energy first.
- **Test your sourcing.** Full-time needs several times the stock. Can your sources supply it? See [sourcing stock for resale](/guides/sourcing-for-resale).
- **Test your space.** Several times the stock needs several times the room; see [storage and stock numbers](/guides/storage-and-stock-numbers).
- **Reduce hours at work first,** if you can, rather than leaving outright.

## Hours and routine

Working for yourself makes it easy to work all the time. A reselling business has no natural end to the day: there is always another item to list and another message to answer.

### Set working hours

Decide the hours and stick to them most weeks. Reselling suits a weekly rhythm, for example:

| Day | Main job |
| --- | --- |
| Sourcing days | Car boots, charity shops, auctions, collections |
| Processing days | Sorting, cleaning, photographing, listing |
| Every weekday | Packing and posting orders, messages at set times |
| One slot a week | Bookkeeping, repricing stale stock, planning |
| At least one full day | Nothing to do with the business |

### Batch the work

Switching between photographing, listing, packing and answering messages all day is slow and tiring. Do one kind of task at a time. Set two or three times a day for messages rather than answering as they arrive; see [handling haggles and messages](/guides/handling-haggles-and-is-this-still-available).

### Automate the repetitive parts

Templates for listings and messages, postage presets, and bulk tools save time once volume is high enough to justify them. See [when to automate](/guides/when-to-automate).

### Take holidays

Most platforms have holiday settings or allow longer handling times. Use them. A business that cannot survive a week off is a job with worse terms.

## Burnout: the signs

Burnout builds slowly. The NHS page on [stress](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/feelings-and-symptoms/stress/) lists signs including difficulty concentrating, struggling to make decisions, feeling overwhelmed, constant worrying, sleep problems, irritability and withdrawing from people. In a reselling business it often shows up as:

- **Unlisted stock piling up** because listing feels impossible.
- **Dreading messages** and leaving them unanswered.
- **Buying to feel productive** rather than because the numbers work.
- **Working every day** and still feeling behind.
- **No longer checking the numbers** because you do not want to know.
- **Physical strain:** back pain from lifting, sore hands from packing, eye strain from screens.

### What helps

- **Fix the hours** and stop at the end of them.
- **Cut the backlog in a planned way.** Pick a number of items to list each day and stop when it is done. Bundle or liquidate old stock rather than letting it weigh on you; see [stale stock](/guides/stale-stock).
- **Stop sourcing for a week** if unlisted stock is piling up.
- **Look after your body.** Lifting technique from the Health and Safety Executive's [good handling technique](https://www.hse.gov.uk/msd/manual-handling/good-handling-technique.htm), a packing table at the right height, breaks from the screen.
- **Talk to other sellers.** Working alone is part of the problem; the forums are here for that.
- **Get help if it does not lift.** The NHS page says to see a GP if you are struggling to cope, and you can refer yourself to NHS talking therapies in England.

## Keeping records when it is your living

When the business pays the rent, records stop being a January chore and become how you steer.

- **Every sale, every purchase, every expense,** with receipts and invoices. See [what records to keep](https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-records/what-records-to-keep).
- **A separate bank account** for the business, so personal and business money do not mix.
- **Monthly profit and hourly rate,** calculated as above, every month.
- **Tax set-aside,** moved to a separate account monthly.
- **Stock value,** so you can see money tied up on shelves.
- **Mileage log** if you use the [flat rate](https://www.gov.uk/simpler-income-tax-simplified-expenses/vehicles).

As turnover grows, watch the [VAT threshold](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat) and the [Making Tax Digital](/blog/making-tax-digital-income-tax-2026) dates, which bring quarterly digital updates. Whether to stay a sole trader or form a limited company is covered in [bookkeeping and tax for resellers](/guides/bookkeeping-and-tax-for-resellers); an accountant's view is worth having before you change structure.

## What goes wrong

- **Deciding on turnover.** Sales figures look like income. They are not.
- **Deciding on one good month.** Seasonality catches people out in their first January.
- **Not counting hours.** A profit that works out below minimum wage per hour is a hobby that pays.
- **No tax set-aside.** The first January bill, plus a payment on account, arrives at once.
- **No buffer.** One slow month forces bad decisions.
- **Scaling hours without scaling sourcing.** Full-time hours with part-time stock.
- **Working every day.** It feels productive for a month and then it stops working.
- **Working alone and telling nobody.** Problems are easier to solve when someone else has seen them before.
