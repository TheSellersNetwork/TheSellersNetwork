This is for UK sellers who run, or want to run, giveaways in live shows. After reading it you will understand the difference in law between a free draw, a prize competition and a lottery, why "buy something to be entered" is the format that gets sellers into trouble, what the advertising rules expect of your terms and your draw, and what each platform allows. It is general information drawn from the legislation, the Gambling Commission and the ASA, not legal advice. If you are planning anything beyond a simple free giveaway, take advice from a solicitor who deals with promotions.

## The short version

- A giveaway that anyone can enter for free, with the winner picked at random, is a **free draw**. It is not gambling and needs no licence.
- A giveaway where people must pay, or buy something, to be entered, and the winner is picked by chance, is a **lottery**. Lotteries cannot be run for private or commercial gain. A seller running one is running an illegal lottery.
- Adding a free entry route can turn a paid draw back into a free draw, but only if the free route meets strict conditions. In a five-minute live giveaway, it is very hard to meet them.
- Whatever the format, the CAP Code requires clear terms, prizes as described, and a verifiably random or independently supervised draw.
- Each platform has its own rules on top, and they are stricter than the law in places. eBay Live does not allow giveaways without eBay's prior approval.

The safe recommendation for almost every reseller: run giveaways that are free to enter, through the platform's own giveaway tool, with the prize and terms stated on screen and in the listing.

## What the law says

### Lotteries

Under [section 14 of the Gambling Act 2005](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/19/section/14), an arrangement is a simple lottery if people pay to take part, one or more prizes are allocated to some of them, and the prizes are allocated by a process that relies wholly on chance. A draw at the end of a show where only buyers are entered fits that description.

"Paying" is wide. [Schedule 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/19/schedule/2), paragraph 2, includes paying money, transferring money's worth, and paying for goods or services at a price that reflects the chance to take part. Buying a £5 card to be entered into a draw for a £50 box is paying to enter, even though the buyer got a card.

The Gambling Commission is clear about who may run lotteries. Its guidance on [raffling big ticket items](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/raffling-big-ticket-items-prizes-pitfalls-and-potential-risks) says lotteries cannot be run for private or commercial gain, and are limited to charities, clubs and other not-for-profit causes. A reseller's giveaway is for commercial gain.

### Free draws

A draw that is free to enter is not a lottery, because nobody pays. Paragraph 5 of Schedule 2 says the normal cost of sending a letter by ordinary post, making a phone call or using another method of communication does not count as paying. The Gambling Commission's page on [free draws and prize competitions](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/public-and-players/guide/page/free-draws-and-prize-competitions) confirms that free draws need no licence, as long as they comply with the Act.

### Prize competitions

A competition decided by skill, knowledge or judgement is not a lottery. [Section 339](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/19/section/339) says a prize competition is not gambling unless it is gaming, a lottery or betting. But the skill has to be real. Under section 14(5), a skill question that cannot reasonably be expected to prevent a significant proportion of people from entering or winning does not count, and the draw is treated as relying wholly on chance. The Gambling Commission's page says multiple choice questions rarely meet that test. "Type the colour of the card in chat to enter" is not a competition.

## Why buy-to-enter is risky

The usual live show version is "everyone who buys tonight goes into the draw" or "spend £20 and you are entered". That is payment to enter a draw decided by chance, which makes it a lottery unless there is a free entry route that meets all four conditions in [Schedule 2, paragraph 8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/19/schedule/2/paragraph/8):

| Condition | What it means for a live show |
| --- | --- |
| Everyone eligible can choose to enter by paying or by sending a communication | A free way in must exist for every viewer, not just a chosen few |
| The free method is a letter by ordinary post, or another method neither more expensive nor less convenient than paying | Tapping "buy" takes a second. A postal entry that arrives after the draw is less convenient. A web form available before and during the show may be closer |
| The choice is publicised so it is likely to come to the attention of everyone who proposes to take part | Saying it once, or putting it in small print, is not enough. The ASA's advice on [free entry routes](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/promotional-marketing-free-entry-routes.html) says it should be in the initial advertising and as prominent as the paid route |
| The prize system does not differentiate between paid and free entries | Free entrants must have exactly the same chance. No extra entries for bigger spenders |

In a giveaway that opens and closes inside a few minutes, a free route that is genuinely as convenient as buying, publicised as prominently, is difficult to build and difficult to prove. If you get it wrong, you are the promoter of an illegal lottery. That is why the recommendation is not to tie giveaways to purchases at all.

There is a lawful purchase-linked format: a **guaranteed gift** that every qualifying buyer receives, such as a free sleeve with every card or a free item with every order over a set amount. There is no chance involved, so it is not a lottery. Describe it as a gift, not a prize.

## The advertising rules: CAP Code section 8

The [CAP Code, section 8](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/08.html) covers promotions with prizes, including giveaways run in streams and on social media. The rules most relevant to a live show:

| Rule | What it requires |
| --- | --- |
| 8.2 | Run the promotion fairly, promptly and efficiently |
| 8.15.1 | Award the prizes as described, normally within 30 days |
| 8.17 | State all significant conditions: how to enter and any cost, any free entry route, start and closing times, the prize, restrictions such as age or UK only, and the promoter's name and address |
| 8.18 | Where space is limited, give what you can and point to where the full terms are |
| 8.19 | Do not claim someone has won a prize if they have not |
| 8.24 | Draw winners at random, by a computer process that produces verifiably random results or by or under the supervision of an independent person |
| 8.28 | Say how and when winners will be told, and whether winners' details will be published |

The ASA's advice on [prize draws in social media](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/promotional-marketing-prize-draws-in-social-media.html) adds points that apply directly to streams. Staff picking winners by hand are not independent. Significant conditions are normally expected in the post itself, not only behind a link. Keep a record that the draw was random.

The platform's built-in giveaway tool deals with most of rule 8.24, because the platform draws the winner automatically. Spinning a wheel on screen, drawing names from a hat yourself or picking "the best comment" does not.

A simple set of on-screen and listing terms that covers rule 8.17 for a free giveaway:

- Free to enter, no purchase necessary.
- Prize: [exact item and condition].
- How to enter: [the platform's entry button, while the giveaway is open].
- Opens and closes: [time and date, or "when the giveaway timer ends"].
- Open to UK residents aged 18 or over [or whatever restriction applies].
- Winner drawn at random by [platform name]'s giveaway tool and announced in the stream. Posted within [number] working days at no cost to the winner.
- Promoter: [your trading name and address].

The Government also agreed a [voluntary code of good practice for prize draw operators](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/voluntary-code-of-good-practice-for-prize-draw-operators/voluntary-code-of-good-practice-for-prize-draw-operators), aimed at businesses running paid-and-free prize draws. It is voluntary and not written for occasional live show giveaways, but it shows the direction of travel: over-18s only, free entry shown before the point of purchase, and spending limits.

## Platform rules

### Whatnot

Whatnot has three giveaway types, set out in [About Giveaways on Whatnot](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/11329417780365-About-Giveaways-on-Whatnot).

| Type | Who can enter |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | Anyone in the show, optionally UK only |
| Follower-only | Your followers in the show, optionally UK only |
| Buyer Appreciation Giveaway | People who buy in your show after the giveaway is created |

Key rules from that page and [Giveaway Rules and Requirements](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/14349906179597-Giveaway-Rules-Requirements):

- Giveaways run for up to five minutes, cannot be paused or cancelled, and Whatnot draws the winner at random. Entrants must be in the show when the winner is drawn.
- You must say what the prize is before running it.
- You pay the postage, deducted from your balance when the giveaway runs, and ship within two business days.
- You cannot refuse, downgrade or change the prize depending on who wins, and cannot add your own entry conditions such as following you elsewhere.
- No wheels, gumball machines, raffle drums or other physical randomisers. No cash prizes except collectable currency. No redeeming prizes in a future stream.
- Requiring a purchase or a minimum spend to win is prohibited, and Buyer Appreciation Giveaways without an alternative method of entry are prohibited.
- You must post Official Rules, including "NO PURCHASE NECESSARY" in capitals each time you promote the giveaway.

Whatnot's pages are not consistent on Buyer Appreciation Giveaways in the UK. The same page that bans them without a free alternative entry says the free web-form entry is available to US and Canadian users. We could not find an official statement of a free entry route for UK buyers. Given that, and the lottery rules above, we would not run Buyer Appreciation Giveaways in the UK until Whatnot's UK pages show a free route that meets Schedule 2. Use Standard or Follower-only giveaways.

Whatnot's [Gambling and Purchase-Based Prize Policy](https://help.whatnot.com/hc/en-us/articles/4410443596813-Gambling-and-Purchase-Based-Prize-Policy) separately bans raffles, wheels, bounties and any sale where a purchase carries a chance at an extra prize. Follower-only giveaways and disclosed rewards guaranteed on purchase are allowed.

### eBay Live

The [eBay Live policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behaviour-policies/ebay-live-policy?id=5478) says sellers may not run promotions, giveaways, competitions or similar activities unless eBay has explicitly approved them in advance, and giveaways or sweepstakes are only allowed through an official eBay Live partnership. Contests, lotteries, raffles and random drawings are listed as prohibited chance-based sales. Bonuses are allowed if every buyer gets the same one. On eBay Live, the answer is: no giveaways unless eBay has approved you in writing.

### TikTok Shop UK

TikTok's [Gambling, Gifting and Surprise-based Product Guidelines](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=1514396635760386&default_language=en-GB) for the UK set out the rules.

- Gambling and gamification are prohibited, whether or not the buyer pays. The list includes lucky spins, lucky wheels, lucky draws and "giveaway entry".
- Free giveaways ("join to win" or "comment to win", with no purchase required) are allowed. Where TikTok's Gift Tool is available you must use it, and it draws winners automatically at the end of a countdown.
- Without the Gift Tool, the giveaway must be free, the prize worth £20 or less, awarded within 30 days, not cash or alcohol, drawn at random by a verifiable computer process or an independent person, and the terms (winners, prize, restrictions, your name and address, how to claim, how winners are chosen, dates) stated in the listing or content.
- Purchase-based incentives are allowed only as buy one get one free, or buy one get other items free, with no chance involved, through the Gift with Purchase tool where available, and never cash, gift cards or alcohol.
- If you do not dispatch giveaway prizes on time, you can lose access to the Gift Tool.

### Instagram, YouTube and other streams

If you run a giveaway on a stream that is not a selling platform, the law and the CAP Code still apply in full, and there is no built-in random draw. Use a verifiably random computer process or an independent person, keep a record, and publish the terms in the post.

## VAT on giveaway prizes

This only matters if you are VAT registered. HMRC's [VAT Notice 700/7](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/business-promotions-and-vat-notice-7007) treats prizes of goods in free lotteries and competitions as business gifts (paragraph 2.2). You do not account for VAT on business gifts to the same person as long as their total cost does not exceed £50, excluding VAT, in any 12-month period (paragraph 2.3). If gifts to one person go over £50 in 12 months, VAT is due on the full cost of all of them. For most live show giveaways, the prizes are well under £50 and the winners change, so no VAT is due, but keep a record of what went to whom. If you give a regular viewer several larger prizes in a year, count them. This is general information, not tax advice; the cost of prizes as a business expense is a question for whoever prepares your accounts.

## What goes wrong

- **"Buy tonight to be entered."** Payment plus chance equals a lottery.
- **Minimum spend thresholds.** Banned by Whatnot and a lottery in law.
- **Wheels and hats.** Banned on Whatnot and TikTok, and not verifiably random under the CAP Code.
- **Changing the prize when a non-buyer wins.** Against Whatnot's rules and CAP rule 8.15.1.
- **Forgetting the postage.** On Whatnot it comes out of your balance when the giveaway runs.
- **Running a giveaway on eBay Live without approval.** It breaks the eBay Live policy.
- **No written terms.** If a dispute reaches the ASA, "I said it on stream" is not enough.
