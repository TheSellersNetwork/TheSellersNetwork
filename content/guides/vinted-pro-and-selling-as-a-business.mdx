This is for anyone who buys things to resell on Vinted, or sells enough that it has stopped being a clear-out. After reading it you will know whether Vinted's rules require you to go Pro, what registering involves, exactly what changes on a Pro account, the consumer law duties you take on, and how all of it connects to HMRC's platform reporting. It describes Vinted's rules and the law in plain terms; it is not legal or tax advice, and Vinted updates its pages often, so check the links.

## Do you need Vinted Pro

Vinted's [Catalogue Rules, section 4](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules) are direct. Standard accounts are for private, non-commercial use. As a standard seller you cannot use Vinted to:

- list items you have specifically bought or sourced to resell;
- earn a regular income through reselling; or
- run a reselling business, even if you own a registered business.

Vinted's [commercial selling page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/1120-commercial-selling) adds that if you have legal status as a business, act for one, or sell on Vinted to make a profit, you need to sign up as a Pro seller. The [terms](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions) describe standard users as people using the service "for your personal benefit only and not for any professional purposes".

In plain terms:

| You | Account Vinted's rules point to |
| --- | --- |
| Selling your own clothes and things you no longer need | Standard |
| Buying from charity shops, car boots or clearance to sell on at a profit | Pro |
| Selling regularly as a side income from sourced stock | Pro |
| A shop or registered business selling second-hand stock | Pro |
| Selling new stock bought in bulk or from a supplier | Not allowed on any account |

The last row matters. Vinted does not allow commercial stock inventory (new items bought in bulk or from a third-party supplier) on any account, and does not allow new commercial stock to be passed off as pre-owned ([Catalogue Rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules)). Pro accounts are also barred from selling a large number of brand-new items, including items with tags ([what Pro sellers can sell](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/1437-what-you-can-sell-as-a-pro-seller)). Pro is for second-hand trading.

### What happens if you should be Pro and are not

Vinted's [Pro guide](https://www.vinted.co.uk/pro-guide) says a professional must declare themselves as a Pro seller, that failing to do so can lead to sanctions under the [Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/contents), and that Vinted can ask you to create a Pro account and block your access if you do not. Its [banned account page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/92-why-was-my-account-blocked) lists commercial selling on a standard account as a reason for a temporary ban, and repeating it as a reason for a permanent ban, after which you must convert to Pro within 6 months of the notice. See [relisting and automation rules](/guides/vinted-relisting-and-automation-rules) for how restrictions work.

Our recommendation: if you are buying to resell, register as Pro before Vinted asks you to. Being moved after a ban is worse on every count.

## Who can register, and how

Vinted says you can be a Pro seller if you are a sole trader, a charity or a registered company incorporated in the UK ([registering as a Pro seller](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/906-registering-as-a-pro-seller)). You either convert your existing account or sign up fresh from the [Vinted Pro page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/pro).

What you will be asked for, from Vinted's [registration](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/906-registering-as-a-pro-seller) and [mandatory information](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1269-mandatory-information-for-pro-sellers) pages:

| Sole trader | Company or charity |
| --- | --- |
| National Insurance number | Registered business name |
| VAT number, if you have one | Tax identification number and business registration number |
| Business representative details and business address | Business representative details and business address |
| Identity document and bank details | Identity document, bank details and registration documents |

You will also go through Know Your Customer or Know Your Business checks with the payment provider ([why you may be asked for ID](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/111)).

**Converting versus a second account.** You cannot convert a standard account while it has orders in progress; finish them and withdraw your balance first. Converting means agreeing to withdraw all earnings, after which Vinted creates a new balance for you. Alternatively, you may keep one standard account and one Pro account, provided they use different email addresses and phone numbers ([registering](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/906-registering-as-a-pro-seller)). If you keep both, keep them genuinely separate: personal things on the standard account, stock on Pro. Mixing them undoes the point.

### Things Pro sellers cannot sell

From Vinted's [Pro selling page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/1437-what-you-can-sell-as-a-pro-seller) and [commercial selling page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/1120-commercial-selling):

- items you made yourself, customised items or made-to-order items;
- any cosmetics, beauty products or beauty devices;
- a large number of brand-new items, including items with tags;
- items bought in bulk for resale, or low value dropshipped items;
- services, including rentals.

Pro listings are also removed for stock photos, reusing the same photos across listings, duplicate listings and misleading brand names.

### The cash flow difference

The biggest practical change is money timing. A standard sale usually completes 2 days after delivery. A Pro sale cannot complete until 14 days after delivery, then takes up to 2 days to reach your available balance, then up to 5 business days to reach your bank ([withdrawals](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/68-how-to-withdraw-earnings)). Plan for roughly two to three weeks from delivery to cash in the bank on every Pro sale. See [payments and balance](/guides/vinted-payments-and-balance).

## Your duties as a business seller

When you sell to consumers as a business, consumer law applies to you as the seller. Vinted's [Pro guide](https://www.vinted.co.uk/pro-guide) says Vinted is an intermediary and you are liable to buyers for your sales.

### Information you must give

Before a consumer buys, you must give them your business name and contact details, a description of the goods, the price including taxes, delivery costs and arrangements, and their cancellation rights ([GOV.UK: online and distance selling](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses)). On Vinted this is split between your listings, your profile, the Vinted Pro terms of sale and your own Additional Policies, which you write in your Pro settings. The Pro guide has a table of who provides what. Write your Additional Policies plainly: Vinted warns that unclear policies are read in the way most favourable to the consumer.

### The 14 day right to cancel

Consumers buying at a distance can cancel within 14 days of receiving the goods, without giving a reason ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds)). On Vinted, the buyer can use the I have an issue button, the standard withdrawal form, or any clear statement ([Pro guide](https://www.vinted.co.uk/pro-guide)).

- The buyer must send the item back within 14 days of telling you.
- You must refund within 14 days of getting the item back or receiving proof it was sent, whichever is first, including the original standard delivery cost ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds)).
- The buyer pays the direct cost of returning it unless your Additional Policies say you will.
- On Vinted, do not refund the buyer yourself. When the buyer withdraws through Vinted, the refund is issued through Vinted's payment system.

There are exceptions set out in the regulations, such as goods made to the buyer's specification and sealed hygiene items unsealed after delivery. Most second-hand clothing is not an exception.

### Goods must be as described, of satisfactory quality and fit for purpose

The [Consumer Rights Act 2015](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/contents) applies to business sales, second-hand included. Vinted's Pro guide summarises the remedies: a refund for faulty goods within 30 days; within six months, a repair or replacement, or a refund if that cannot be done; and some money back up to six years if goods do not last a reasonable time. GOV.UK says you must repair or replace within 6 months unless you can prove the item was not faulty when sold ([accepting returns](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds)). What counts as satisfactory for a used item depends on its age, price and how you described it, which is why honest, detailed descriptions of wear protect you.

### Fair practice

Vinted's Pro guide reminds you not to mislead buyers about an item's characteristics, your identity as a business, or the price, and not to pressure them. Do not present yourself as a private seller clearing out a wardrobe when you are a business.

## Vinted Pro, tax and HMRC

Registering as Pro and paying tax are separate questions, but they point the same way.

**Platform reporting.** Vinted, like other platforms, must collect seller details and report sellers to HMRC each year under the digital platform reporting rules. A Pro account gives Vinted your National Insurance number or business details at registration. Our breakdown of [the HMRC platform reporting rules](/blog/hmrc-platform-reporting-rules) covers the thresholds and what being reported does and does not mean.

**Trading.** HMRC's test is whether you are trading. Buying goods intending to sell them for a profit is likely to be trading, and if your trading income is over £1,000 in a tax year you need to tell HMRC ([GOV.UK: check if you need to tell HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-tell-hmrc-about-your-income-from-online-platforms)). That is broadly the same line as Vinted's commercial selling rule: if Vinted's rules say you need Pro, HMRC's guidance probably says you are trading.

Be careful with platform statements about tax. Some pages written for private sellers explain why selling personal items is rarely taxed. That is true for personal items and irrelevant to stock bought to resell.

**VAT.** If your taxable turnover across all channels goes over the VAT registration threshold, you must register; check the current figure on [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat). Second-hand goods may qualify for a [VAT margin scheme](https://www.gov.uk/vat-margin-schemes). Prices on Vinted Pro must include VAT if you are registered. Vinted sends Pro sellers invoices for its services, such as labels and visibility features, which belong in your records.

For records, deadlines and allowable costs, see [bookkeeping and tax for resellers](/guides/bookkeeping-and-tax-for-resellers) and the [tax dates tool](/tools/tax-dates). This section is general information, not tax advice.

## What goes wrong

- **Reselling on a standard account.** Vinted's rules say it needs Pro, and it is a listed ban reason.
- **Going Pro with orders in progress.** You cannot convert until they complete and the balance is withdrawn.
- **Mixing personal and stock on one account.** Keep one standard and one Pro, with separate email addresses and phone numbers, or everything on Pro.
- **Expecting two day payouts.** Pro orders complete 14 days after delivery.
- **No Additional Policies, or vague ones.** They are read in the buyer's favour.
- **Refusing a no-reason return within 14 days.** The buyer has that right.
- **Refunding outside Vinted.** Let the refund go through Vinted's system.
- **Listing new stock with tags in volume, or cosmetics.** Not allowed on Pro.
- **Relying on "selling on Vinted is not taxed".** That is about personal items.
