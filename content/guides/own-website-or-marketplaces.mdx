This is for resellers who sell on eBay, Etsy, Vinted or Amazon and keep being told they should "own their customers" with a website. After reading it you will know what a web shop costs to run on Shopify's current UK pricing, how that compares with marketplace fees on the same order, where a site's buyers have to come from, and a clear rule for when it is worth doing. Prices change, sometimes monthly; every figure links to the official page, so check it before you commit. None of this is financial advice.

## The short answer

For most resellers, a website is not the next step. Marketplaces charge more per sale, but they bring the buyers. A web shop charges less per sale and brings nobody. The fee saving only matters once you already have people who will come to you directly: repeat buyers, a following, a niche where people search for you by name.

A website earns its keep when at least one of these is true:

- You have repeat customers who ask whether you sell anywhere else.
- You have a social following or mailing list that already buys.
- You sell a clear niche (one category, one era, one type of collector) where buyers search for specialists.
- A marketplace's rules or fees have made a big part of your stock unviable there.

If none is true, spend the money and hours on better listings on the platforms you are on, and on a second marketplace. Our [multi-channel selling guide](/guides/multi-channel-selling) covers that.

## What Shopify costs in the UK

Shopify is the most common hosted shop builder, so it is the example here; other hosted builders have similar cost structures and publish their own prices. These figures are from [Shopify's UK pricing page](https://www.shopify.com/uk/pricing) in September 2026.

| Plan | Paid monthly | Paid yearly (per month) | Online card rate with Shopify Payments | Fee if you use another payment provider |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basic | £25 | £19 | 2% + 25p | 2% |
| Grow | £65 | £49 | 1.7% + 25p | 1% |
| Advanced | £344 | £259 | 1.5% + 25p | 0.6% |
| Plus | from £1,800 | | 1.3% + 25p | 0.2% |

Other rates on the same page, for the Basic plan: 3.1% + 25p for American Express and for international cards, 1.7% for in-person card payments, and 4.99% + 30p for Klarna. Syncing more than 50 marketplace orders a month into Shopify costs 1% of those orders, up to 99 USD a month.

At the time of writing Shopify offers a free trial followed by a reduced price for the first months. Treat that as a trial, not the cost: plan on the full monthly price.

The pricing page does not say whether its prices include VAT. Check the plan price on your first bill before relying on the figures above.

### The costs the pricing page does not show

| Cost | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Domain name | Shopify includes connecting a custom domain; buying the name itself is a separate yearly cost, from Shopify or any registrar |
| Theme | Free themes exist; paid themes are a one-off cost |
| Apps | Reviews, product feeds, email, shipping labels and so on. Many are monthly subscriptions, and they add up faster than the plan |
| Postage labels | You buy them as now; the buyer's postage charge is set by you |
| Payment disputes | With Shopify Payments, a chargeback costs a £10 fee, returned if you win ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/chargebacks/chargeback-process)) |
| Photography and copy | You already do this for marketplaces; a site needs the same again, in your own voice |
| Your time | Building, maintaining, answering the contact form, writing the legal pages |
| Advertising | The big one. See below |

## The same sale, three ways

A £44 order (£40 item plus £4 postage), to compare the fee on one sale.

| Where | What comes off | On £44 | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shopify Basic, Shopify Payments, UK card | 2% + 25p | £1.13 | [Shopify pricing](https://www.shopify.com/uk/pricing) |
| Etsy, found in Etsy search, seller without a VAT ID | 6.5% transaction, 4% + 20p processing, 0.48% regulatory, VAT on fees | about £5.64, plus the listing fee | [Every Etsy fee on one sale](/guides/etsy-fees-on-one-sale) |
| eBay business seller, Clothes, Shoes and Accessories | 11.9% final value fee, 40p per order over £10, 0.35% regulatory fee, all before VAT | about £5.79 before VAT | [eBay business seller fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-business-sellers?id=4809) |

On this order the website saves roughly £4.50 in fees. With the Basic plan at £25 a month paid monthly, that is about six orders a month just to pay for the plan, before any apps, domain or advertising. On £19 a month paid yearly it is about five. The real question is not the plan price; it is whether those orders would arrive at all.

## Where a website's buyers come from

A marketplace fee buys you a place in front of people who are already shopping. A website has no such crowd. Every visitor has to be brought there, and each source has a cost in money or time.

| Source | Cost | How it works for a reseller |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing customers | Low | Include a card in parcels from your own site orders and tell buyers you have a shop. Do not do this on marketplace orders; see the rules below |
| Social media | Time | Works if you post consistently and your stock photographs well. Slow to build |
| Mailing list | Time, and a small tool cost as it grows | The most reliable source once built. Needs consent and a privacy notice |
| Google search, unpaid | Time, months | Product pages and useful category pages can rank over time for niche searches. Not a plan for this quarter |
| Google Shopping free listings | Low | Google can show products for free on its Shopping tab if you submit a product feed and follow its policies, including showing your returns policy and delivery costs ([Google Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/9199328?hl=en-GB)) |
| Paid ads | Money | Works, but you pay per click whether or not anyone buys. Many small shops spend more than the marketplace fee they were trying to save |

A reasonable expectation for a new shop with no following is very little traffic for months. Plan for that by keeping the marketplaces running at full strength while the site grows.

## The rules on moving marketplace buyers to your site

Marketplaces forbid using their sales to send buyers elsewhere. Etsy's fees policy prohibits any action to move a transaction off Etsy, including encouraging buyers to purchase through another venue or directing them there with a QR code ([Etsy Fees and Payments Policy](https://www.etsy.com/uk/legal/fees)). Other marketplaces set their own rules on contact and sales outside the platform; read them before putting anything in a parcel. A card in a marketplace parcel pointing to your website can cost you the account that pays your bills.

The legitimate routes are to build your own audience in public (social, search, a mailing list people sign up to on your site) and to let marketplace buyers find you by name if they look. Etsy offers a partial fee refund when a buyer orders from your Etsy shop through a link you shared yourself ([Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/16981332744087)); that is the permitted way to use your own following on Etsy.

## What a website asks of you that a marketplace does not

- **Legal pages.** A marketplace provides much of the checkout information; on your own site you provide all of it: who you are, cancellation rights, returns, privacy, cookies, prices including VAT. See [what a UK web shop legally needs](/guides/web-shop-legal-pages-uk).
- **Payments and fraud.** You choose a payment provider and handle disputes yourself. See [taking card payments compared](/guides/taking-card-payments-compared).
- **Trust.** Buyers know eBay's and Etsy's protection. On an unknown site they look for reviews, a real address, clear returns and a familiar checkout.
- **Stock control.** If the same item is on the site and a marketplace, you must avoid selling it twice.
- **Customer service with no platform in the middle.** No case system and no marketplace to decide disputes.

## A decision in three steps

1. **Count your direct demand.** How many orders a month would come to you if you asked your existing buyers and followers? If you cannot name a number above a handful, the site will mostly sit empty.
2. **Do the fee maths on your own average order.** Work out the fee difference per order, as in the table above, and divide the monthly running cost (plan, apps, domain spread over the year) by it. That is your break-even order count before advertising.
3. **Choose the smallest step.** If you are not sure, start with the Basic plan paid monthly so you can stop, one sales channel, no paid apps you do not need, and a hard review date at three months. If the site is not covering its running cost by then from direct demand, close it or pause it and put the time back into marketplaces.

For a reseller whose stock is mostly one-offs, a website also means photographing and describing everything a second time. The fastest way round that is to use the site for the lines you have in quantity, or a niche you are known for, rather than the whole stock.

## What goes wrong

- **Building the site before the audience.** A shop nobody visits costs the plan every month.
- **Counting the trial price as the price.** Budget on the full rate.
- **App creep.** Five small monthly subscriptions cost more than the plan.
- **Paid ads to fix no traffic.** Cost per sale can exceed the marketplace fee you left to avoid.
- **Parcel cards in marketplace orders.** Against Etsy's rules; check every other platform's before you try it.
- **Missing legal pages.** A site with no trader details, returns terms or privacy notice breaks the rules and loses sales.
- **Using a payment provider other than Shopify Payments without noticing the extra fee.** On Basic that is another 2% on top of the provider's own charge.
