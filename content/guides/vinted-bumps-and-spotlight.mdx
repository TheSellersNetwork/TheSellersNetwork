This is for Vinted sellers deciding whether to pay for visibility. After reading it you will know what a Bump and Showcase (the feature Vinted used to call Wardrobe Spotlight) do, how Vinted sets the price, the rules that catch people out, and a two-line sum that tells you whether a boost is worth it for a given item. Vinted changes these features and their names; check the linked pages before you rely on a detail.

## What Vinted officially charges

Start with the honest answer: Vinted does not publish a fixed price for either feature. Its [price list](https://www.vinted.co.uk/pricelist) says the fee for bumping listed items varies with the duration (3 or 7 calendar days) and the item's price, and that Showcase has a variable fee and duration, with the exact amount shown at checkout. Prices include VAT.

| Feature | What it boosts | How long | How the price is set | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Item Bump | One listing | 3 or 7 consecutive days, or until it sells | Depends on the item's price and the duration you choose; shown at payment | [What is an item Bump](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/340-what-are-item-bumps), [price list](https://www.vinted.co.uk/pricelist) |
| Showcase (formerly Wardrobe Spotlight) | Your whole profile, highlighting up to 15 listings to each viewer | 7 consecutive days from purchase | Depends on how many listings you have and their value; shown at checkout | [Showcase](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/452) |

So the only way to know the price is to go to the checkout for that item or for your profile. Do that before deciding, write the number down, and use the sum below. Any figure you see quoted elsewhere is someone else's item on someone else's day.

## What a Bump does

A bumped item appears higher in members' newsfeeds and search results for the chosen period, and is shown to members Vinted thinks are most likely to be interested, based on their preferences ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/340-what-are-item-bumps)). You can see how many people viewed and interacted with a bumped item from your profile.

### The rules that matter

From Vinted's [Bump](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/340-what-are-item-bumps) and [buying a Bump](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/450) pages:

- **You can lower the price during a Bump, but not raise it.** And the Bump price does not change if you lower the item price; there is no partial refund. Set the price you want before bumping.
- **One Bump per item at a time.** You cannot stack them.
- **Not transferable.** A Bump stays on that item. You cannot move it to another listing or another member.
- **Cancelled sale:** if an order is cancelled before it is shipped, you can use Re-upload item and the Bump continues until its original end date.
- **Items in review:** new listings sometimes go through an automated check, usually around 30 minutes but sometimes up to 2 days. If you bump an item that goes straight into review, the Bump is not processed and you are not charged. Bump it again when it is live.
- **Removed listings:** if the item is removed for breaking the Catalogue Rules, the Bump is not refunded. The same applies if your account is banned ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/62-why-your-listing-was-hidden-or-deleted)).
- **You can bump any item,** including ones eligible for Item Verification or Electronics Verification.
- **Where it shows:** Bumps increase visibility in your registered country.

You can buy a Bump from the Listings section of your profile (press Bump under the item, or Boost your visibility), or tick the Bump box while creating a listing, which also shows an estimated cost.

## What Showcase does

Showcase (previously Wardrobe Spotlight) promotes your profile rather than one item. When a member sees your Showcase in their feed or search results, it highlights up to 15 of your listings chosen for what that member is most likely to buy, and they can follow you or open the rest of your listings in one tap ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/452)).

- You need at least 5 items listed to buy it, and Vinted advises keeping at least 5 listed while it runs.
- It starts immediately and lasts 7 consecutive days.
- You will not necessarily see it in your own searches; it is shown to members whose preferences match your items.
- How often it appears depends on how often people search for the kind of items, sizes and brands you have ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/503)).
- It is not refunded if a listing is removed for breaking the rules.

The main extra benefit over a Bump is followers. Vinted says followers are notified when you list a new item and see your items in their feed after the Showcase ends. That is a longer tail than a Bump, which stops when it stops.

## The maths: is a boost worth it

Vinted does not take a selling fee from standard sellers; the buyer pays the Vinted fee and shipping ([terms, section 9](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions)). So your profit on a sale is simply the item price less what the item cost you and your packaging. That makes the sum easy.

**Break-even for a Bump:**

Extra profit needed = Bump price at checkout.

Break-even sale = one sale that would not have happened, or would have happened much later, whose profit is at least the Bump price.

Put another way: if the item's profit is less than the Bump price, the Bump loses money even if it works. If the profit is several times the Bump price, the Bump only has to make a modest difference to be worth it.

A worked example with made-up numbers, to show the arithmetic only (not Vinted's prices). An item listed at £20 that cost you £6 makes £14 profit. If the checkout showed a 7 day Bump at £2, the Bump pays for itself if it is responsible for the sale one time in seven (£14 divided by £2). If the same checkout showed £2 for an item that makes £3 profit, it needs to cause the sale two times in three, which it will not.

**Break-even for Showcase:**

Showcase price at checkout divided by your average profit per sale gives the number of extra sales across the whole profile in 7 days that you need. Compare that with your normal weekly sales. If you usually sell 10 items a week and the Showcase needs 1 extra sale to break even, it is a reasonable test. If it needs 5, it is a hope.

### How to tell whether it worked

Vinted shows you views and interactions for boosted items and for Showcase. Views are not sales. Keep a simple record for each boost:

| Date | Item or Showcase | Price paid | Views before (week) | Views during | Sold within the period | Profit on sale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

After ten or so boosts, the pattern is clear: which categories respond, which do not, and whether you are buying sales or buying views. Most sellers we know stop bumping their cheap items within a month of keeping this table.

## When to pay, and when not to

**A Bump is worth considering when:**

- The item has a good profit and is priced right against sold comparisons, and the listing is already strong (photos, title, measurements). A Bump puts a listing in front of more people; if the listing is weak, you pay to show more people a weak listing.
- It is seasonal and the season is now: coats in October, not in May.
- It is a popular brand or size where there is a lot of competition in search and being higher up matters.

**Showcase is worth considering when:**

- You have a deep, coherent profile: plenty of listings in similar sizes and styles, so a viewer who follows you will find more.
- You have just listed a batch of in-season stock.
- You want followers, not just this week's sales.

**Do not pay when:**

- The item's profit is below or close to the likely boost price.
- The price is above the market. Fix the price first; see [pricing from sold comps](/guides/pricing-from-sold-comps).
- The listing is new and may still go into automated review.
- There is any chance the listing breaks the Catalogue Rules (brand wording, stock photos, duplicate items). A removed listing loses the boost with no refund.
- You are about to raise the price. You cannot during a Bump.

**Free things to do first.** Vinted's own [selling faster](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/57-selling-your-items-faster) page lists the free levers: bundle discounts, sending offers to members who favourite or ask about your item, uploading regularly (followers are notified of new items and price drops), better photos and descriptions, and a verified profile with an About me section. Do those before paying. See [bundles and discounts](/guides/vinted-bundles-and-discounts), [photos on a phone](/guides/photos-on-a-phone) and [descriptions that sell](/guides/descriptions-that-sell).

## Boosts and the relisting rules

Paying for a Bump is the approved way to push a listing up. Deleting and relisting items to push them up is not: Vinted's terms say you must not "delete and re-list the same Item multiple times" ([terms, section 6](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions)), and the same section bans external software tools used to promote items. If you are tempted by a tool that promises to keep your listings at the top without paying, read [Vinted relisting and automation rules](/guides/vinted-relisting-and-automation-rules) first.

## What goes wrong

- **Bumping low-profit items.** The Bump costs more than the item makes.
- **Bumping before fixing the price.** You can lower it during the Bump but pay the same.
- **Wanting to raise the price mid-Bump.** You cannot.
- **Bumping a listing that then gets removed.** No refund. Check brand wording, photos and duplicates first.
- **Buying Showcase with a thin profile.** Fewer than 5 listings and you cannot buy it; only a few more and there is little for a new follower to see.
- **Judging by views.** Record sales and profit per boost, not views.
- **Assuming a quoted price is current.** The only real price is the one at your checkout for that item.

A note on account type. Vinted's [Catalogue Rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules) say standard accounts are for private, non-commercial selling; if you are buying stock to resell, see [Vinted Pro and selling as a business](/guides/vinted-pro-and-selling-as-a-business). The October 2026 terms changes are covered in [Vinted's new UK terms](/blog/vinted-terms-october-2026).
