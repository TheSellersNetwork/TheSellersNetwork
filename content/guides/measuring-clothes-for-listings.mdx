This is for anyone selling second-hand clothing online in the UK. Size labels are unreliable on used clothes: brands size differently, garments shrink and stretch, and labels get cut out. Measurements are what buyers actually use to decide whether something will fit, and a listing without them gets messages, hesitation and returns. This guide covers the kit, the method for each measurement, how to present them, and a copyable block for your listings.

## Why measure

- **Fewer questions.** "What is the pit to pit?" is one of the most common buyer messages on clothing listings. Answering it before it is asked saves time and keeps the buyer moving.
- **Fewer fit returns.** A buyer who compared your measurements with a garment they already own is much less likely to say it does not fit. Where a platform's own return rules treat fit as a change of mind, clear measurements also help your case if a buyer claims the item was not as described. See [returns without losing money](/guides/returns-without-losing-money).
- **Better search.** Some buyers search for measurements directly, and some platforms have item specifics or fields for them. Fill in the fields where they exist.
- **Trust.** Measurements tell a buyer you have handled the item properly.

## Kit

| Item | Why |
| --- | --- |
| A soft tailor's tape measure, in centimetres and inches | Flexible for curves, and both units for buyers who think in either |
| A flat, hard surface longer than a pair of trousers | A table or clean floor; beds and sofas give wrong readings |
| A steel ruler or retractable tape (optional) | Easier for straight measurements on long items |
| Your phone | Photograph the tape across the garment for key measurements |

Keep the tape with your photography kit so measuring becomes part of the same routine; see [photos on a phone](/guides/photos-on-a-phone).

## The rules for every measurement

1. **Lay the garment flat, face up, buttoned or zipped closed.** Smooth it without stretching.
2. **Measure flat, not around.** A flat measurement across the front is half the circumference. Say that it is flat in the listing, so buyers can double it.
3. **Do not stretch.** Knitwear and stretch fabrics especially. Measure relaxed; if you want, note "stretchy" separately.
4. **Measure in a straight line** from point to point, not following the curve of a creased fabric.
5. **Use the same method every time.** Your buyers learn to trust your numbers.
6. **Round to the nearest half centimetre or quarter inch.** More precision than that is false precision on fabric.
7. **Give both centimetres and inches** if you can. Many UK buyers still think in inches for waist and inside leg, and many think in centimetres for everything else.

## Tops, shirts, jackets and knitwear

### Pit to pit (chest)

The most asked-for measurement. Lay the garment face up. Measure straight across the front from the seam at one armpit to the seam at the other, about 2.5cm (1 inch) below the armpit where the sleeve joins the body. Keep the tape parallel to the hem.

This is half the chest circumference of the garment, not of the wearer. A buyer compares it with a garment they own that fits well.

### Length

For tops and jackets, measure from the highest point of the shoulder, where the shoulder seam meets the collar, straight down to the bottom hem. Some sellers measure from the base of the collar at the back (back length) instead. Either is fine; say which.

### Shoulder

Straight across the back from one shoulder seam to the other. Useful for tailored jackets, shirts and coats, where shoulder fit matters more than chest.

### Sleeve

Two common methods; say which you used.

- **From the shoulder seam:** from where the sleeve joins the body at the shoulder to the end of the cuff. Standard for set-in sleeves.
- **From the centre back of the collar:** across the shoulder and down the sleeve to the cuff. Used for shirts sized by sleeve length, and for raglan or dropped shoulders where there is no clear shoulder seam.

### Hem width

Straight across the bottom hem, flat. Useful for cropped tops, jumpers with a tight rib, and boxy fits.

## Trousers, jeans and shorts

### Waist

Button or zip the waistband closed, lay it flat, and measure straight across the top edge from one side to the other. This is half the waist; double it for the circumference. Label sizes on trousers are often different from the actual measurement, especially on jeans and on older garments, so buyers value this one.

### Inseam (inside leg)

From the crotch seam, where the leg seams meet, straight down the inside leg seam to the bottom of the hem. Lay one leg flat and straight to do this.

### Outseam (outside leg)

From the top of the waistband straight down the outside of the leg to the hem. Optional, but helpful for high-rise and cropped styles.

### Rise

The rise tells a buyer where the trousers sit on the body.

- **Front rise:** from the crotch seam straight up to the top of the front waistband.
- **Back rise:** from the crotch seam up to the top of the back waistband. Optional.

Front rise is the one most buyers ask for.

### Thigh

Straight across one leg, just below the crotch, from the inside seam to the outside edge. Useful for slim and skinny fits.

### Leg opening

Straight across the hem of one leg, flat. Useful for flared, bootcut, slim and tapered styles.

### Hip

For trousers without much stretch, measure straight across at the widest part, usually level with the bottom of the fly.

## Dresses and skirts

| Measurement | How |
| --- | --- |
| Pit to pit | As for tops |
| Waist | Straight across the narrowest part, or across the waist seam if there is one |
| Hip | Straight across the widest part below the waist; say how far below the waist you measured |
| Length | Dresses: from the highest point of the shoulder to the hem. Skirts: from the top of the waistband to the hem |
| Slit | From the hem to the top of the slit, if there is one |

For wrap and stretch styles, give the measurement relaxed and say it will stretch or adjust.

## Shoes and accessories

Shoes are usually sold by size, not measurement, but an insole length from heel to toe, measured inside the shoe, helps buyers where sizes vary by brand or the size label has worn off. For belts, measure from the buckle end (where the leather meets the buckle) to the hole you consider the middle, and state how many holes there are. For bags, give width, height and depth, and the strap drop (the distance from the top of the strap to the top of the bag).

## UK, EU and US sizes

Size systems differ, and they differ between brands within the same system. The general points:

- **Women's clothing.** UK, EU and US use different number scales for the same garment size. US women's sizes are numerically lower than UK sizes, and EU sizes are numerically higher. The exact conversion varies by brand.
- **Men's clothing.** Tops are often sold as S, M, L or by chest in inches; trousers by waist and leg in inches in the UK and US. EU sizes use a different scale.
- **Shoes.** UK, EU and US shoe sizes all use different scales, and US sizes differ between men's and women's.
- **Children's clothing.** Usually by age or height; brands differ widely.
- **Vintage.** Older garments are often cut smaller than the same label size today.

**Our recommendation:** list the size shown on the label exactly as it appears, add the size system if it is not obvious ("UK 12", "EU 40"), and let the measurements do the work. Do not convert sizes yourself in the title or the size field. If a buyer asks for a conversion, point them to the measurements and the brand's own size chart. A conversion you got wrong is a fit return; a measurement is a fact.

Where the label is missing, say so, and list by measurements alone with an honest estimate ("fits like a UK 10 to 12, please check the measurements").

## Presenting measurements

- **Put them in the description in the same order every time.** Buyers scanning many listings look for the block.
- **Photograph the tape across the garment** for pit to pit and waist at least. It proves the measurement and answers doubts before they are raised.
- **Say "flat" or "laid flat"** for width measurements, so nobody reads pit to pit as full chest.
- **Fill in the platform's own size and measurement fields** as well as the description. On Vinted and Depop, the size field is what search filters use; on eBay, fill in the item specifics.
- **Note stretch and fit.** "No stretch", "some stretch", "oversized fit", "cropped".

### A copyable measurements block

Copy this into your listing template and delete the lines that do not apply. The code layout here is only so it copies cleanly; paste it as plain text.

```
Measurements (laid flat, not stretched):
Pit to pit: __ cm / __ in
Length (shoulder to hem): __ cm / __ in
Shoulder to shoulder: __ cm / __ in
Sleeve (shoulder seam to cuff): __ cm / __ in
Waist (flat, double for full): __ cm / __ in
Hip: __ cm / __ in
Inside leg: __ cm / __ in
Front rise: __ cm / __ in
Leg opening: __ cm / __ in
Label size: __ (UK / EU / US)
Stretch: none / some / lots
Please compare with a similar item you own before buying.
```

## Speeding it up

- **Measure during intake,** before photographing, and write the numbers on the tag with the stock number. See [storage and stock numbers](/guides/storage-and-stock-numbers).
- **Measure in batches.** All tops, then all trousers. The same movements over and over are faster than switching.
- **Keep a garment of known measurements** next to your table for sense checks. If your "pit to pit" for a medium jumper is wildly different from your reference, measure again.
- **Use text replacement** on your phone or computer to paste the block in a few keystrokes.

## What goes wrong

- **Measuring around instead of flat,** or not saying which. The buyer doubles a number that was already doubled.
- **Stretching knitwear.** The garment arrives smaller than the listing said.
- **Measuring on a bed.** Soft surfaces add centimetres.
- **Mixing methods.** Sleeve from the shoulder on one listing and from the collar on the next.
- **Converting sizes yourself.** Brand charts differ; your conversion becomes the buyer's complaint.
- **Leaving out the measurement buyers ask for most.** Pit to pit for tops, waist and inside leg for trousers.
- **No photo of the tape.** A number without proof invites a "not as described" claim.
- **Measurements only in the description,** with the platform's size field blank. The item does not appear in filtered searches.
