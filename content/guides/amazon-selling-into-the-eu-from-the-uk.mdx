For UK Amazon sellers who already run a profitable UK account and are wondering whether the German, French, Italian and Spanish stores are worth the trouble. After reading you will know the three routes open to a UK account, what each one asks of you in VAT, customs, product safety and packaging rules, and which one, if any, fits a reseller. It assumes you have already switched off the overseas settings that catch new sellers out, as set out in [the Seller Central settings guide](/guides/amazon-seller-central-settings-that-matter).

Every rule here links to Amazon, GOV.UK or the European Commission. The rules on low value parcels into the EU changed in July 2026 and more are coming, so read the linked page before relying on anything. This is general information, not tax, legal or financial advice; an accountant who does cross border VAT for Amazon sellers is worth paying before you list abroad.

## What a UK account can already do

A UK Seller Central account is a European unified account: the same login can list on the other European stores, and the Professional subscription is paid once. Amazon's [Remote Fulfilment handbook](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/01/rainier/help/Handbook_RF_UK-EU_EN_2023.pdf) says you pay subscription fees only for the country where you first registered, while referral fees follow the store the item sold on.

So the question is never whether you can list in Germany. It is how the parcel gets there, who pays the VAT, and whether the product is legal to sell there. There are three answers.

| Route | Where the stock sits | Who ships | EU VAT registration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FBM to EU buyers | With you in the UK | You | Usually not, if the order is €150 or less and sold through Amazon |
| Remote Fulfilment with FBA | Amazon's UK warehouses | Amazon, across the border | Generally not |
| EFN or Pan-European FBA | Amazon's EU warehouses | Amazon, inside the EU | Yes, in every country that holds your stock |

## Route one: FBM to EU buyers

You list on an EU store, the buyer orders, and you post the item from the UK yourself. It is the cheapest route to try and the most work per order.

### VAT on the sale

Since the EU's e-commerce VAT rules came in, an online marketplace that facilitates the sale of imported goods worth €150 or less is treated as the supplier for VAT. The European Commission's [One Stop Shop page](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/taxation/vat/one-stop-shop/) says platforms may be deemed the seller for VAT in that case, and HMRC's guidance on the [Import One Stop Shop](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-can-register-for-the-vat-import-one-stop-shop-scheme) says that if you sell through an online marketplace, the marketplace is responsible for reporting and paying the VAT due. In practice Amazon charges EU VAT at checkout on those orders and accounts for it. You do not need your own IOSS registration for Amazon orders.

Above €150 the marketplace is no longer the deemed supplier. The buyer is the importer and faces import VAT, any duty and a carrier's handling charge on delivery. Buyers who were not expecting a bill refuse parcels, and a refused parcel is a return you pay for twice. For a reseller, keep FBM sales into the EU under that line.

### Customs and duty

Every parcel leaving Great Britain for the EU needs a customs declaration, as GOV.UK's page on [sending post or a parcel abroad](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/sending-post-or-a-parcel-abroad) says. The [export step by step guide](https://www.gov.uk/export-goods) adds that you need an EORI number starting with GB ([apply here](https://www.gov.uk/eori)) and the right commodity code for the goods. Keep the code and value on your listing record.

The duty side has just changed. From 1 July 2026 the EU removed the duty exemption for parcels worth €150 or less, and GOV.UK's business support site says a [€3 customs duty charge per item](https://www.business.gov.uk/campaign/europe/european-union-eu-regulations/eu-ucc-reforms/) now applies to parcels from businesses outside the EU to EU consumers, with an EU wide handling fee on e-commerce goods expected from November 2026. The page advises asking your carrier what it needs from you. Check with the carrier and Amazon who pays that charge on an FBM order before you price: €3 on a €15 item is a fifth of the sale.

Goods that originate in the UK can enter the EU at a zero tariff if they meet the rules of origin; GOV.UK explains [claiming preferential rates of duty between the UK and EU](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/claiming-preferential-rates-of-duty-between-the-uk-and-eu). Goods made in China and bought in a UK shop do not become UK origin by passing through your garage.

### UK VAT

If you are VAT registered in the UK, exports can be zero rated only if you keep the evidence HMRC's [Notice 703](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-on-goods-exported-from-the-uk-notice-703) describes, usually within three months. Keep the postage and tracking records. The wider UK side is in [VAT, invoices and business accounts](/guides/amazon-vat-invoices-and-business-accounts).

## Route two: Remote Fulfilment with FBA

### How it works

Your stock stays in Amazon's UK warehouses. An EU buyer orders from, say, Amazon.de, and Amazon picks the unit in the UK, ships it across the border and clears customs. Amazon's page on [fulfilment between the UK and EU](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/fulfilment-by-amazon/fulfilment-between-uk-eu) says you do not need to store stock in the destination and that a VAT registration number is not required, with Amazon handling customs clearance and collecting and remitting VAT. It lists the arcs between the UK and Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Sweden and the Netherlands.

This used to be called the European Fulfilment Network between the UK and EU, and some older help pages still use that name.

### The limits

From the same Amazon page and the [Remote Fulfilment handbook](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/01/rainier/help/Handbook_RF_UK-EU_EN_2023.pdf):

- **A price cap.** An eligible item must not be priced above €135 in the EU or £122 in the UK.
- **Excluded programmes.** The handbook lists dangerous goods and heavy and bulky items as excluded.
- **Country of origin is mandatory** on the listing, in both the source and destination stores, or the offer is not eligible.
- **The same SKU** must be used in the UK and EU stores. The handbook warns that a merchant fulfilled offer sharing an SKU with an FBA offer has its quantity set to zero, so give any FBM offer its own SKU.
- **Returns come back to the UK** warehouse, under the normal FBA returns policy and fees.
- **Some ASINs are ineligible** because of local rules or import requirements. The ASIN eligibility report in the Remote Fulfilment section of Seller Central shows the reason.

The handbook is dated 2023 and lists fewer stores than Amazon's current page, so treat the Seller Central enrolment page as the final word.

### VAT, fees and pricing

The handbook says you are generally not required to register for VAT in the destination store. On consumer sales of €150 or less Amazon accounts for the VAT paid at checkout; above that, and on business sales, the customer is the importer and Amazon collects an estimate of import VAT and duties at checkout.

You pay the referral fee of the store the item sold on and a Remote Fulfilment fee set by size and weight, higher than the local UK fee. Amazon's [Europe rate card effective 1 February 2026](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/02/sell/images/260114-FBA-Rate-Card-EN.pdf) has a section for EFN and Remote Fulfilment UK-EU fees, and the FBA revenue calculator in Seller Central compares the options for a given ASIN.

Enrolment offers to price your EU offers through Build International Listings with a rule that converts your UK price and adds destination VAT and fees. The handbook calls this an estimate, not a VAT calculation. Check a few prices by hand before you trust it.

## Route three: stock held in the EU

### EFN and Pan-European FBA

With the European Fulfilment Network you send stock to one EU country and Amazon serves the other EU stores from there. Amazon's [FBA Europe page](https://sell.amazon.fr/en/expedie-par-amazon/europe) describes Amazon storing products locally, ready for local delivery or to dispatch to other countries, and charging cross border EFN fees on orders from other stores.

Pan-European FBA goes further: Amazon moves your stock between its EU warehouses to sit near demand. Amazon's [Pan-European FBA page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/fulfilment-by-amazon/fba-europe) says:

- storage can be enabled in France, Germany, Italy, Spain or Poland, and you must enable at least two;
- eligible products must be listed with the same SKU in all the required stores, which it lists as France, Germany, Italy, Spain and the Netherlands;
- from 3 September 2026 every Pan-European product needs an active Netherlands offer, and from 26 February 2027 an active Belgium offer at enrolment;
- you pay local fulfilment fees in countries enabled for storage and EFN fees elsewhere;
- you can start with two VAT numbers, and storing goods in countries other than your home store brings additional VAT reporting requirements.

Pan-European FBA also carries its own charges, including a low inventory fee covered in [the 2024 FBA fee changes](/blog/amazon-2024-fba-fee-changes); the current figures are in the rate card above.

### What holding stock in the EU means

This is where selling into the EU stops being a listing decision and becomes a second business.

- **You import into the EU.** Each shipment from the UK to an EU warehouse needs an export from the UK and an import declaration in the EU. The European Commission's [EORI page](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/customs-4/customs-procedures-import-and-export/customs-procedures/economic-operators-registration-and-identification-number-eori_en) says a business outside the EU gets an EU EORI from the member state where it first lodges a customs declaration. Import VAT and any duty are due at that border, before a single unit sells.
- **You register for VAT where the stock sits.** Amazon says so itself on the Pan-European page. That means a VAT registration and periodic returns in each storage country, and each country's own rules on how a business from outside the EU registers. The fees for doing that properly are a fixed cost every quarter whether you sell or not.
- **Amazon may still collect some of the VAT.** The Commission's [One Stop Shop page](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/taxation/vat/one-stop-shop/) says platforms may be deemed the supplier where sellers from outside the EU sell to EU customers. How that fits with your own registrations is a question for the accountant.

For a reseller buying clearance stock in small lots, the fixed cost rarely pays. This route is for brands and wholesale sellers with repeat lines and volume.

## Rules that apply whichever route you choose

### Product safety and the responsible person

Since 13 December 2024 the EU General Product Safety Regulation applies to consumer products sold into the EU, including through marketplaces. Listings must show the manufacturer's details and there must be a responsible person established in the EU. GOV.UK's [GPSR factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/general-product-safety-regulations-northern-ireland/eu-regulation-2023988-on-general-product-safety-factsheet) sets out who can fill that role, and our breakdown of [GPSR for Northern Ireland and EU sales](/blog/gpsr-product-safety-ni-eu) covers what a reseller has to check. A UK brand with no EU address, or a product with no manufacturer details on the box, is a product you cannot offer to EU buyers, however good the price.

### Labelling and language

GPSR requires safety warnings and information in a language buyers in that country can easily understand. Warnings only in English are a problem on Amazon.fr. Amazon's [UK and EU product marking guidance](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G3NRFVS4YUYRQ85J) covers CE and UKCA marking; in the EU it is CE that counts.

### Packaging and producer responsibility (EPR)

Several EU countries make whoever puts packaging or certain products on their market pay towards recycling it. Amazon's [EPR compliance page](https://sell.amazon.de/en/einhaltung-der-erweiterten-herstellerverantwortung) says Germany enforces EPR for packaging, with a 13 digit LUCID registration number, and for electrical and electronic equipment, while France has 19 categories including electrical goods, batteries, packaged goods, furniture, textiles and tyres. Sellers must give Amazon their registration numbers or risk deactivated listings, and the page says the rules apply if you sell goods in the country, wherever you are based. That catches FBM and Remote Fulfilment sellers too. Check each store's EPR requirements in Seller Central before you enrol it.

### Northern Ireland

Sellers based in Northern Ireland are under different rules for goods moving to and from the EU, including an EORI starting with XI. Start with the [EORI page](https://www.gov.uk/eori) and HMRC's Northern Ireland guidance, not this guide.

## The decision

| Your situation | Route | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arbitrage seller, mixed one off stock | Stay UK only | Compliance work per ASIN outweighs the extra sales |
| Steady FBA lines under €135, EU compliant, not dangerous goods | Remote Fulfilment on a short list of ASINs | No EU VAT number, Amazon clears customs, easy to switch off |
| A few higher value items you already post abroad | FBM, under €150 per order | Amazon handles VAT; you control the parcel |
| Own brand or wholesale lines with EU demand and volume | EFN or Pan-European FBA, with an accountant | Local fees and delivery speed only pay back at volume |
| Anything without EU manufacturer or responsible person details | None | It cannot legally be offered to EU buyers |

### Our recommendation

For most resellers, stay UK only and put the time into sourcing. If you want to test Europe, use Remote Fulfilment, not FBM and not Pan-European. Pick five to ten ASINs that sell steadily in the UK, are under the price cap, have CE marking, an EU responsible person and multilingual warnings on the box, and are not in an EPR category you have not registered for. Enrol them in Germany and France only, set prices by hand, and give it three months. Look at net margin per unit after the Remote Fulfilment fee, the €3 duty line and the returns, not at the extra sales figure. If the numbers are not clearly better than selling the same unit in the UK, switch it off.

Only consider holding stock in the EU once Remote Fulfilment has proved sustained demand, and only after an accountant has costed the registrations.

## What goes wrong

- **Build International Listings left on.** Offers appear in stores you never chose, with VAT and EPR duties you have not met. See [the settings guide](/guides/amazon-seller-central-settings-that-matter).
- **Pricing from the UK figure.** The EU fee, destination VAT, currency and the new duty charge all come off the sale. An item that makes £3 in the UK can lose money in Spain.
- **Ignoring EPR.** Listings in Germany or France deactivated because no registration number was on file.
- **GPSR details missing.** No responsible person, or warnings only in English. The listing is blocked or, worse, sells and becomes your liability.
- **FBM orders over €150.** The buyer is asked for VAT and duty on the doorstep and refuses the parcel.
- **Stock sent to the EU before VAT registration.** Amazon's own page ties the VAT reporting to where goods are stored. Register first, then ship.
- **Assuming the old rules.** Low value parcels into the EU no longer come in duty free. Anything written before July 2026 on this topic is out of date.
