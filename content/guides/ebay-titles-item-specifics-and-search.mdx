This is for anyone selling on eBay UK who wants more of their listings seen by the people who would buy them. It covers the three things you control before a buyer ever clicks: the title, the item specifics and the category, and it explains what is publicly known about how eBay's search ranks listings so you can stop guessing. After reading it you will be able to write an 80 character title in a couple of minutes, fill specifics in a way that lifts search and filter visibility, and judge for yourself whether a claim about "the algorithm" is worth acting on.

## What eBay says about search, and what it does not

eBay's default sort order is called Best Match. The ranking system behind it is often called Cassini after the search platform eBay rolled out in 2013; eBay itself rarely uses that name any more and never publishes the formula. What it does publish is a short list of the things Best Match takes into account. The [Best Match help page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/listing-tips/optimising-listings-best-match?id=4166) says the ordering considers how closely a listing matches the buyer's search terms, how popular the item is, the price, the quality of the listing (description, photos, completeness), the terms of sale such as returns and dispatch time, and the seller's track record. The [Get found in search](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/listings/listings-in-search) page in Seller Centre adds that complete listings with relevant keywords and completed item specifics are the main lever, that competitive pricing, free or reasonable postage, fast dispatch and a good returns policy help, and that Top Rated Seller status gives improved visibility across all listings.

That is the whole official position. Everything beyond it, including the confident percentages you will see in blog posts ("40% relevance, 30% seller performance"), is inference from people watching their own results. Some of that inference is sound and repeatable. Some is folklore. The table below separates them so you know how much weight to put on each.

| Claim | Status | Our reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Titles are matched against the search words | Official | Stated by eBay. The title is the first thing search reads. |
| Item specifics are matched and drive filters | Official | Stated by eBay; specifics feed the left-hand filters and external search engines. |
| Price, postage, dispatch time and returns affect rank | Official | Stated on the Best Match page. |
| Seller performance affects rank | Official | Stated. Top Rated status is described as improving visibility. |
| Sales and views relative to impressions matter | Official in outline | eBay says "popularity" counts. That listings which convert get shown more is the working assumption of every experienced seller and fits what eBay says. |
| The first few words of a title carry more weight | Inference | Plausible, widely repeated, not stated by eBay. Front loading the main keyword costs nothing, so do it anyway. |
| Repeating a keyword helps | Inference, probably wrong | No evidence, and it wastes characters. |
| A relisted item "resets" and gets a boost | Inference | Mixed reports. See the [relisting guide](/guides/relisting-and-sell-similar). |
| Descriptions are searched by default | Partly official | eBay's own advice says to include keywords in the description, but the default search box searches titles; buyers must tick "search including description". |
| Exact weightings (percentages) | Unsupported | Nobody outside eBay knows them. Treat any percentage as decoration. |

A practical way to hold all this: search first finds the listings that match the words, then orders them by how likely each is to end in a sale the buyer is happy with. Your title and specifics decide whether you are in the pool. Your price, postage, photos, terms and history decide where in the pool you sit. eBay's own [Listing Quality Report](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/selling/how-to-sell/listing-quality-report), available to business sellers from the performance area of Seller Hub, is the closest thing to a look inside: it benchmarks your listings against the best in each category and shows impressions, views and conversion for each.

## Titles

### The limits

A title can be up to 80 characters, per the [Best Match help page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/listing-tips/optimising-listings-best-match?id=4166), which also asks for correct spelling and no filler. A subtitle is a paid upgrade: £2 per listing for private sellers on the [selling fees page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4364) and £2 for business sellers on the [business fees page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-business-sellers?id=4809), and it is not searched by the default search. In practice a subtitle is only worth paying for on high value items where a second line of reassurance changes the click.

The [item description policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/listing-policies/item-description-policy?id=4372) forbids symbols and special characters in titles (write "and" rather than an ampersand where you can, and never use stars or arrows), and forbids "confusing or unclear" titles. Keyword spamming, which means putting brand names or terms in the title that do not describe the item ("like Nike"), is a separate policy breach that gets listings removed.

### How a buyer searches

Buyers type what they would say out loud: brand, what it is, the size or model, and sometimes the colour or a feature. "levis 501 jeans 34 32", "dyson v8 battery", "lego 75192". They almost never type adjectives. Nobody searches "stunning", "rare", "look" or "wow", so those words are dead weight in a title. Also, the mobile app cuts long titles in the results grid, so whatever you put after about the fortieth character is often not seen until the buyer opens the listing, though it is still searched.

### A recipe that works

Build the title from the parts a buyer might type, most important first, and stop when you run out of useful words rather than when you hit 80.

1. Brand (or maker, designer, publisher)
2. Product name or model number
3. What it is, in the words buyers use (jacket, trainers, lens, board game)
4. The key variable: size, capacity, colour, edition, year
5. One or two distinguishing facts: material, gender, "boxed", "complete", "sealed", pattern, fit
6. Condition only where it is a search term ("new", "BNWT", "vintage")

Example, built the same way each time:

- Barbour Ashby Wax Jacket Mens Medium Olive Green Cotton Corduroy Collar
- Canon EF 50mm f1.8 STM Lens Boxed with Caps Tested Working
- LEGO Star Wars 75192 Millennium Falcon UCS Sealed New Retired Set
- Nike Air Max 90 Trainers Womens UK 5 White Pink Leather

Three rules on top of the recipe. Include both forms of a word where buyers use both (trainers and sneakers, jumper and sweater, hoodie and hoody) if you have room, but never at the expense of a real attribute. Write sizes the way the item specific writes them (UK 10, Size M, 34W 32L). Spell brand names correctly and check the model number twice; a wrong digit puts you in the wrong pool entirely.

### What not to put in a title

- Anything that is not searched: "look", "wow", "stunning", "rare" (unless it genuinely is and is described as such by collectors), "free postage" (that is a filter and a badge, not a search term)
- Your shop name or username
- Punctuation used as decoration
- Words that duplicate the category ("book" in the Books category is often wasted, though "hardback" is not)
- Anything untrue. A title is part of the description in law and in eBay's policy

## Item specifics

### What they are and why they matter more than the title

Item specifics are the structured fields under the title: brand, size, colour, type, material, model, and dozens more depending on category. Seller Centre's [item specifics page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/listings/item-specifics) describes three tiers. Required specifics must be filled or the listing cannot be saved or revised. Recommended ones are the fields buyers filter on most in that category, ranked from the last 30 days of buyer search data. Additional ones are optional.

Why they outrank the title in importance: filters. A buyer who searches "mens jacket" and then ticks Size M, Colour Green, Brand Barbour never sees any listing that has those facts only in the title. The filter reads the specifics and nothing else. The same fields are what eBay sends to external search and shopping results, and what the app uses to build its own suggestions. A perfect title with empty specifics is a listing that is invisible to everyone who filters, and on eBay most serious buyers filter.

### How to fill them

- Fill every required and recommended field, and as many additional ones as apply. Leave nothing you know blank.
- Use eBay's suggested values rather than typing your own. A typed "Med" is not the same as the selectable "M" and may not match the filter at all.
- Never enter "Does not apply" or "N/A" for a product identifier (EAN, ISBN, MPN) when the manufacturer's number exists. The [item description policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/listing-policies/item-description-policy?id=4372) names this as a breach. If you really cannot find it, "Does not apply" is the honest answer for unbranded or handmade goods.
- Add the product identifier where you have one. Scanning a barcode into the app usually fills half the specifics from eBay's catalogue and puts you on the product page other buyers land on.
- Keep specifics consistent with the title and the condition. Policy requires the condition to match across title, specifics and description.

### Clothing and footwear: the size rule

eBay UK has moved fashion categories to standardised sizes. The [create a listing](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/create-listing?id=4105) help page states that from August 2026 apparel and footwear listings with missing or unclear size or condition may be hidden until updated, and the description policy lists the supported formats (alpha sizes from 2XS to XXL, numeric sizes, and regional options). "See description", "One size fits most" and "N/A" in the size field are exactly what gets a listing hidden. Pick the value that matches the manufacturer's label, then put measurements in the description for buyers who want them.

### Bulk fixing

If you have hundreds of active listings, Seller Hub's Active listings page has an "Add item specifics" action for selected listings and a download and upload tool that gives you a spreadsheet of your listings with the missing fields highlighted. Do a category at a time. Seller Centre notes that the required fields change over time and shows a "required soon" filter so you can fix listings before they are blocked.

## Category

Put the item in the category a buyer would browse to, not the one that sounds most impressive. The Best Match page is blunt: choose the most relevant category. The app's suggested category is usually right for branded goods with a barcode and often wrong for vintage, collectables and parts, so check it. A second category is available in some cases for a fee; it is rarely worth it now that filters do the work. Wrong category costs you twice: the buyer filtering within the right category never sees you, and the specifics offered in the wrong category are the wrong ones, so you fill in fields nobody searches.

## The other half: what makes eBay show you higher

Once you are in the pool, the official list says price, postage, dispatch time, returns, listing quality and track record decide your place. In practice that means:

- **Price against the market.** Search the sold listings (tick "Sold items" in the filters) for your exact item and price against what actually sold in the last 90 days, not against what is listed.
- **Postage.** Free or cheap postage is called out on both official pages as helping placement. Whether you build it into the price is your choice; the point is that the total looks reasonable next to the competition.
- **Dispatch time.** One working day if you can honestly deliver it. Slow handling is a stated negative.
- **Returns.** Business sellers must accept returns anyway (see [Descriptions that sell](/guides/descriptions-that-sell)). For private sellers, a returns policy is a choice, and the official page says a favourable one helps.
- **Photos and completeness.** Photos are part of listing quality. First photo on a plain background, then every angle. The [photos guide](/guides/photos-on-a-phone) covers it.
- **Track record.** Late dispatch, cases closed without seller resolution and low ratings drag every listing down, not just the one that went wrong. Seller standards are set out on [Seller Centre](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/protection/seller-standards).
- **Conversion.** A listing that gets clicked and not bought tells eBay something. If a listing has plenty of views and no sales, the price or the photos are the problem, not the title.

## How to tell whether a change worked

Do not judge a title change by feel. Seller Hub's Traffic report (under Performance) shows impressions, page views and click-through for each listing over a chosen period. Change one thing on a listing, wait a week or two, and compare. Impressions up with clicks flat means you are now in more searches but the result card is not winning the click (photo or price). Impressions flat means the words did not add reach. Clicks up with sales flat is a price or description problem. This is slower than reading a blog post and it is the only method that produces results you can rely on for your own stock.

## Worked example: fixing a weak listing

Before: "Lovely warm mens coat, great condition, look" in Men's Clothing, brand blank, size in the description only, colour blank, two photos on a bed.

Why it fails: no brand or type to match, no size or colour in the specifics so every filter excludes it, three words that nobody searches, and photos that make the buyer work.

After: title "Barbour Bedale Wax Jacket Mens Size 42 Navy Blue Classic Tartan Lining C42". Category Men's Coats, Jackets and Waistcoats. Specifics: Brand Barbour, Size 42, Colour Navy, Type Wax Jacket, Outer Shell Material Cotton, Style Casual, Features Pockets, Department Men, Size Type Regular, Model Bedale. Condition Used with a note on the worn cuffs. Six photos on a white sheet in daylight, including the label and the cuffs. Same coat, and now it appears in every filtered search a Barbour buyer runs.

## Mistakes we see most

1. Filling the title with adjectives and leaving the size and colour in the description.
2. Typing custom values into specifics instead of choosing eBay's, so filters miss them.
3. "Does not apply" on a product that has a barcode on the box.
4. Trusting the app's category suggestion for vintage and collectable items.
5. Changing five things at once and crediting the title when the price was what moved.
6. Rewriting titles from a "top keywords" list without checking what buyers of that item actually type: search for the item yourself on the app and see which suggestions autocomplete.
7. Leaving old fashion listings with "See description" in the size field and wondering why they vanished.
8. Believing a percentage weighting because it was in a confident article.
