This is for FBA sellers planning what to send in for October, November and December 2026. After reading you will know this year's official inbound dates, exactly how the festive peak fee and Q4 storage change your per unit numbers, and how to decide how many units of each product to send. It is written on 28 September, which leaves one month before the Black Friday inbound date.

[The Amazon selling year](/guides/amazon-selling-year#q4) covers what sells in Q4, Black Friday buying and pricing towards Christmas. This guide is the numbers and the logistics for this year specifically. Every date and fee comes from Amazon's own 2026 pages, linked where used. Nothing here is financial advice.

## The 2026 dates

From Amazon's [peak season 2026 announcement](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/d8c34b0b-86ed-4aec-9b0a-8be6eabb8fb0) and the [Q4 2026 peak readiness playbook](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G4QH4XCRWUXRJBLY?locale=en-GB):

| Date | What |
| --- | --- |
| 16 September | Inbound date for Prime Big Deal Days stock (UK). Passed. |
| 1 October | Monthly storage moves to the October to December rate. |
| 15 October | Festive peak fulfilment fee starts. |
| 28 October | Stock must arrive at UK fulfilment centres by this date to be ready for Black Friday Week. |
| 1 November | Orders from here to 31 December can mostly be returned until 31 January. |
| 18 November | Deal submissions for Black Friday Week close. |
| 27 November | Black Friday. |
| 1 January 2027 | Monthly storage returns to the January to September rate. |
| 14 January 2027 | Last day of the festive peak fulfilment fee. |
| 31 January 2027 | End of the extended festive returns window. |

Two notes. First, the Black Friday inbound date has moved between years. Earlier years used the end of October, and [an older announcement extended it to 31 October](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/74522160-ba2b-41b2-abd0-cfb9583e3875). For 2026 it is **28 October for the UK** (21 October for EU stores). Work to 28 October.

Second, "arrive by" means arrive, not dispatch. Allow for the carrier, and for receiving, which Amazon says gets slower. From the announcement: fulfilment centres focus on receiving in September and October, then shift to customer orders in November and December, and inbound delivery slots will be limited. Stock that arrives after 28 October is still received and still sells. It just may not be checked in for the week that matters.

The extended returns window comes from Amazon's [FBA customer returns page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200379860?locale=en-GB). It means December sales are not final until February.

## The festive peak fulfilment fee

### Who pays it

From Amazon's [2026 festive peak fee page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GJ7N4DN3ZZBK4UC7?locale=en-GB): the fee applies to UK orders delivered between **15 October 2026 and 14 January 2027**, whenever the stock arrived and whenever the order was placed. In the UK it covers small and standard parcels and large and extra-large envelopes. It does not apply to light or standard envelopes, oversize items, or items on low-price FBA rates. Amazon's announcement puts the average at **£0.12 per parcel item and £0.07 per large or extra-large envelope item**, and the 1.5% [fuel and logistics surcharge](/blog/amazon-fba-fuel-logistics-surcharge) applies on top of the peak rate.

### What it looks like per unit

A selection of UK rates from the same page, for items outside the categories with their own parcel tiers:

| Size tier and weight | Non-peak fee | Peak fee (15 Oct to 14 Jan) | Difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Light envelope, up to 100 g | £2.08 | £2.08 | None |
| Standard envelope, up to 460 g | £2.16 | £2.16 | None |
| Large envelope, up to 960 g | £2.72 | £2.79 | £0.07 |
| Extra-large envelope, up to 960 g | £2.94 | £3.01 | £0.07 |
| Small parcel, up to 400 g | £3.00 | £3.11 | £0.11 |
| Standard parcel, up to 400 g | £3.01 | £3.12 | £0.11 |
| Standard parcel, up to 900 g | £3.06 | £3.18 | £0.12 |
| Standard parcel, up to 1.9 kg | £3.48 | £3.61 | £0.13 |
| Standard parcel, up to 11.9 kg | £3.58 | £3.71 | £0.13 |
| Small oversize, up to 760 g | £3.49 | £3.49 | None |

Parcel-size items in some categories use a separate set of parcel tiers, also on that page, with peak increases of £0.11 to £0.15 in the UK. Amazon says the Revenue Calculator and the FBA Fee Preview report already show the peak fee.

On its own, 11p to 13p a unit is small. On a product that makes £1 a unit it is more than a tenth of your profit, and it lands in the same months as the storage rise below.

## Q4 storage

### The rates

From Amazon's [monthly storage fee page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200612770?locale=en-GB), per cubic foot per month in the UK, rates effective from 1 February 2026:

| Product type | January to September | October to December |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard size, most categories | £0.76 | £1.51 |
| Standard size clothing, accessories, eyewear, footwear, rucksacks, handbags | £0.62 | £0.82 |
| Oversize | £0.55 | £0.87 |
| Dangerous goods, standard size | £0.74 | £1.30 |
| Dangerous goods, oversize | £0.70 | £1.11 |

Storage is charged on your daily average volume, so the October rate applies to every day a unit sits there in October, not to a snapshot. Sellers subject to the storage utilisation surcharge pay that on top; [inventory health](/guides/amazon-inventory-health#monthly-storage) explains who that is.

### What it means per unit

Multiply the unit's volume in cubic feet by the rate. The fee page's own example uses a unit of 0.05 cubic feet, roughly a paperback in a bag: about 4p a month from January to September and about 8p from October. A boxed gift set of 0.3 cubic feet costs about 23p a month in September and about 45p a month in October to December. A board game at 0.6 cubic feet goes from about 46p to about 91p a month.

The per month number is not the point. The point is how many months a unit sits. A board game that arrives on 1 October and sells on 20 December costs nearly £2.50 in storage alone. If it sells on 20 November, it costs about £1.50. If it does not sell until March, you have paid Q4 rates for three months to hold stock that sold at January prices.

## What Amazon's warehouses do differently

Three things change in Q4, all from Amazon's own pages:

- **Receiving slows.** As above, inbound slots are limited from November. A shipment sent in mid November may take noticeably longer to go live than the same shipment in September.
- **Removals slow.** Amazon's [remove inventory overview](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/200280650?locale=en-GB) says removal orders can take up to 30 working days or more in October, November and December, against 10 to 14 normally. Clear dead stock in September, not during peak. [Removal, disposal and liquidation](/guides/amazon-removal-disposal-liquidation) covers the options.
- **Capacity is tighter.** The playbook says estimated capacity limits for October were visible in the Capacity Monitor from 23 July and for November from 20 August. Check yours now, and cancel any open shipment plans you will not send, because they count against the limit until cancelled.

## How much to send

### Start from what Amazon will tolerate

Amazon's playbook recommends holding at least **28 days of inventory** based on your historical demand, and its inventory score treats more than **90 days of supply** as excess ([inventory health](/guides/amazon-inventory-health#what-feeds-it) explains the score). So for a product you intend to keep in stock, the target sits between four weeks and three months of expected sales, and the Q4 storage rate pushes you towards the lower end.

The playbook also mentions a low-inventory-level fee on products held below the recommended level over the past 90 days. Amazon says it does not apply to new Professional sellers for their first 365 days after first receiving stock. If you are past that, check the "exempted from low-inventory-level fee" column in the [FBA Inventory report](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GKT9YKCHXMSDVJKB?locale=en-GB) for your replenishable lines.

### Then work from evidence

For each product:

1. **Look at last year's Q4.** Use the rank history on a price history chart for October to January last year. A product whose rank improved sharply from mid November and fell away in the first week of January is seasonal; one that barely moved is not. [Reading price history charts](/guides/amazon-reading-price-history-charts) explains what the rank line can and cannot tell you.
2. **Estimate the listing's sales, then your share.** Use the "bought in past month" figure and the rank history together, divide by the number of FBA offers competing at the featured price, and discount further if Amazon itself sells it.
3. **Decide the selling window.** Stock that arrives by 28 October sells from Black Friday Week to the Christmas delivery cut-off. Stock arriving in late November sells for roughly three weeks before Christmas.
4. **Send enough for that window, not more.** Expected weekly sales times weeks in the window, then take off a margin for the chance you are wrong. A seller who expects to sell 20 units over six weeks and sends 20 has no room for error. Sending 15 and topping up is almost always cheaper than holding the unsold five until March.
5. **Hold back the rest of your budget.** The [selling year guide](/guides/amazon-selling-year#how-deep-to-buy) recommends buying Q4 stock in two rounds, and the evidence from round one is what makes round two profitable.

### A worked example

A boxed toy, 0.3 cubic feet, standard parcel up to 900 g, bought at £9, selling at £22. Your estimate is eight sales a week from Black Friday Week to mid December, so four weeks: 32 units.

- Send 24 by 28 October, not 32. If the first 24 are gone by early December, a top-up of 10 sent the same week will probably arrive in time for the last fortnight.
- Each unit costs about 45p a month to store from October, plus the peak fee, around 12p more per sale than in September.
- If eight of the 24 do not sell before Christmas, they will likely sell in January at a lower price, with three months of Q4 storage already paid and a chance of festive returns on the ones that did sell.

The numbers in this example are illustrative. Use your own fee preview and your own sales estimate.

## The last useful shipment

Stock has to be received to sell, and receiving is slowest in December. For most sellers, anything that leaves after the first week of December is January stock. Only send it if it will sell in January at January prices, or if it is a product that does not depend on Christmas at all.

## What goes wrong

- **Working to 31 October.** That was an earlier year. The 2026 UK date is 28 October, and it means arrived.
- **Forgetting the peak fee in the calculator.** It runs to 14 January, well past Christmas.
- **Sending in September's dead stock with October's good stock.** Everything sitting there from 1 October pays the higher storage rate.
- **Removing stock in November.** Thirty working days or more, at peak storage rates while you wait.
- **Sending the whole Q4 budget in one go.** The second round, bought with evidence, is where the margin is.
- **Counting December sales as final.** Festive orders can come back until 31 January. Keep cash back for the refunds.
- **Leaving open shipment plans.** They use capacity you might need for a top-up.
