This is for UK eBay sellers who have never sold abroad, or who have just been moved from the Global Shipping Programme and want to know how the new scheme works in practice. After reading it you will know what you post and where, which items and parcels are excluded, how returns and lost parcels are handled, what it costs you, and how to control where you sell. For the change itself, dates and enrolment, read our breakdown of [eBay International Shipping replacing GSP](/blog/ebay-international-shipping-replaces-gsp). The programme is being rolled out in phases, so check the linked pages before relying on any detail.

## How it works

eBay International Shipping (eIS) lets an overseas buyer purchase your item as if it were a UK sale for you. When an item sells abroad:

1. eBay tells you the order is international and gives you the address of its **UK shipping hub**.
2. You post the item to the hub, inside the UK, with tracking and the reference number on the label.
3. The hub takes it from there: international postage, customs paperwork, import charges and international tracking.

According to eBay's [help page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/shipping-items/setting-shipping-options/ebay-international-shipping-program?id=5348), once the hub accepts your parcel you are protected from item not received claims and chargebacks, and from refunding the buyer if the item is lost or damaged on the international leg. You pay only what it costs to get the parcel to the hub.

| Opting back in after opting out | 24 hours later | [eIS help](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/shipping-items/setting-shipping-options/ebay-international-shipping-program?id=5348) |

On the £900 limit: the help page says the order cost must not exceed £900 including shipping, while the Seller Centre FAQ says £900 excluding duties, buyer protection fee and delivery fees. Until eBay settles it, treat anything near £900 as possibly outside the programme.

## Who is eligible

The account needs, per the help page:

- Above Standard or Top Rated seller performance
- Eligibility for seller protections
- Low or Average service metrics
- Compliance with eBay's rules and policies

The item needs to be physically in the UK, listed on ebay.co.uk, and eligible for export. eBay decides listing eligibility for you and, according to the Seller Centre FAQ, does not offer a tool to check it listing by listing.

If you are enrolled, eligible listings are added automatically and eIS becomes your default international option where you do not offer your own.

## What you post, and where

### Day to day

- **Post to the hub address on the order, not the buyer's address.** The hub is in the UK. Its address changed from the old GSP hub, so never reuse an old label.
- **Always use tracking and put the reference number on the label.** eBay labels and Simple Delivery do this for you. Your protection starts when the hub scans the parcel; before that, the parcel is your responsibility.
- **Your handling time still applies.** eBay manages the delivery estimate from the hub onwards, but you must still meet your domestic dispatch commitments. Late dispatch to the hub still counts against you.
- **One order, one package.** Each eIS order should fit in a single parcel within the size and weight limits.
- **Pack for an international journey.** The parcel is going further than the hub. [Packaging cheaply and safely](/guides/packaging-cheaply) covers what holds up.

For the domestic leg, choose a tracked service you would use for a UK sale of the same value. Our [postage guide](/guides/postage-and-royal-mail-choices) and the [parcel size checker](/tools/postage?tab=size) help you pick.

### Combined orders

Multiple items to one buyer are combined automatically when eligible. The help page lists cases where they are not: the order is over £900, the listing is an auction, the item was bought through Best Offer or a seller offer, the items exceed the package limits, or the buyer is paying import fees at checkout for some items and on delivery for others. If postage is not combined, each item is its own parcel to the hub.

## What is excluded

### Items

The help page has a long list of restricted items. Among the ones resellers actually run into:

- Alcohol, of any strength
- Animals and animal parts, including fur
- Damaged, recalled or used batteries. Working batteries are allowed only if properly installed in the device they power and secured
- E-bikes
- Aerosols and other pressurised containers
- Flammable items, including nail varnish, some paints and solvents
- Hair dyes containing peroxide
- Matches
- Plants and seeds for growing
- Supplements, vitamins and medicines
- Tobacco and e-cigarettes
- Weapons and ammunition, including replicas and decorative or deactivated weapons
- Counterfeit and infringing items
- Currency, gift vouchers and bullion
- Stamps still valid for postage

Anything under eBay's hazardous materials policy or its international trading policy is also out. The list is not exhaustive and eBay may update it. If you sell cosmetics, electronics with batteries, or collectables near any of those lines, read the full list on the help page.

### Places and people

You can still exclude countries. In Account Settings, go to Postage Preferences and open the excluded shipping locations setting. The Seller Centre FAQ says eBay automatically works out which countries your items can reach through eIS, and you can turn off the setting that expands your reach, exclude specific countries, or exclude a country on a single listing. If account-level and listing-level exclusions differ, the most recent change wins.

## Returns: what actually happens

This is the part that surprises people. According to the help page and the [Seller Centre FAQ](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/postage/ebay-international-shipping):

- Buyers can return eIS items up to **30 days from delivery**. eBay applies that return window automatically, whatever your own policy says.
- When a buyer opens a return, **eBay refunds them and you keep the sale**.
- The returned item goes to the eIS hub or a third-party provider, is inspected, and the buyer is refunded, usually within 5 to 7 business days of it arriving.
- **You do not get the item back.** Returned items, items that could not be delivered, and items that fail inspection at the hub are not returned to sellers. The FAQ says they may go to a third party or be liquidated.
- Negative or neutral feedback about handling during international shipping is removed.

For most resellers that is a good trade: an overseas return that used to cost you the item plus two lots of international postage now costs you nothing but the item, and you keep the money. The exception is one-off or sentimental stock where you would rather have the item back than the payout. For those, do not let them go through eIS.

If an overseas buyer messages you asking for a refund, eBay's FAQ says to tell them to use the return option in their order history, not to refund them yourself.

## What it costs you

| Cost | Who pays |
| --- | --- |
| Postage from you to the UK hub | You, or the buyer through your domestic postage charge |
| International postage from the hub | The buyer, shown at checkout |
| Import duties and taxes | The buyer, at checkout where possible |
| International selling fee | Waived on eIS orders |
| Final value fee | You, as normal; the Seller Centre FAQ says it stays the same as under GSP |

For scale, the international fee business sellers pay on orders they post abroad themselves is 1.05% for the Eurozone and Northern Europe, 1.8% for the US and Canada, and 2.0% for other countries, on the total sale, according to [Fees for business sellers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-business-sellers-activated-managed-payments?id=4809). Private sellers pay [3% on items delivered outside the UK](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-private-sellers-activated-managed-payments?id=4822). eBay's help page frames the eIS fee saving as a benefit for business sellers. If you are a private seller, check the fees on your first eIS sale to see whether the 3% was charged.

### VAT

The help page says eIS items always leave the UK and are treated as exports. For sales to the EU, eBay adjusts the price to remove any UK VAT you included, and the buyer pays import charges on the adjusted price. The Seller Centre FAQ adds that eIS provides proof-of-export data for your VAT records, and that if the hub decides an item cannot be exported, you are told and may need to consider UK VAT on that sale. If you are VAT registered, talk to whoever does your VAT return about how you record these. This is not tax advice.

## Should you stay in or opt out

**Stay in** if you sell general resale stock under £900 that is not on the restricted list. You get overseas buyers with almost no extra work or risk, and returns stop being your problem.

**Opt out, or exclude the item,** if:

- You sell items where getting a return back matters more than keeping the payout
- You already post abroad yourself at rates your buyers accept, and you want returns to come to you
- Most of your stock is over £900 or restricted, and you sell abroad yourself anyway

To opt out, go to My eBay, Account, Postage Preferences, and select Opt Out next to eBay International Shipping. You will be asked why. You can opt back in 24 hours later. Remember that once you have been moved to eIS, GSP is no longer available to you, even if you opt out, so the only alternative is posting abroad yourself.

You can also run both. If your own international postage is cheaper than eIS for a buyer, the buyer is shown the cheapest option, and the Seller Centre FAQ says your own rates show first when they are lower. If you only want eIS, remove your own international services from the listing.

## What goes wrong

- **Using an old GSP hub label.** The hub address changed.
- **Posting untracked to the hub.** Protection starts at the hub scan.
- **Late dispatch.** eIS does not stretch your handling time.
- **Expecting a return to come back.** It will not.
- **Refunding an overseas buyer yourself.** Send them to the return option in their order.
- **Items near £900.** The two eBay pages define the limit differently.
- **Sending a restricted item.** Batteries and aerosols are the ones resellers hit most.
- **Opting out without a plan.** GSP is not coming back for you.
