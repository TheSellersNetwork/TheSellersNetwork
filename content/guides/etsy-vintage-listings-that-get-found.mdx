This is for resellers listing vintage on Etsy who have the stock right but not the views. After reading it you will have a routine for each listing: a title a buyer can scan, tags that cover the searches the title cannot, attributes filled in so filters find you, a description that answers the age and condition questions before they are asked, and a photo set that meets Etsy's rules and sells the item. It assumes your stock already qualifies; if you are not sure, read [Etsy's vintage rules](/guides/etsy-vintage-rules) first.

## How Etsy decides what to show

Etsy has said that its search now looks at the whole listing rather than leaning on the title: title, tags, attributes, description, first photo and even reviews ([Etsy Seller Handbook](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/1399426136697)). Its search visibility page also names things a listing needs for a chance at priority placement, including at least one image, a sharp and well-lit first photo that shows a single finished item rather than a collage, the most specific category available, and a return policy, even if that policy is no returns ([Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/25869947521175)). Shop-level factors count too: a complete shop with a logo, prompt replies, on-time dispatch with tracking, good reviews and few cases.

Two consequences for vintage sellers. First, stuffing the title with every keyword now works against you, because the other fields carry them. Second, the listing basics (category, return policy, first photo) are search factors, not admin. Get them right on every listing.

A note on the return policy for business sellers: Etsy accepts "no returns" as a setting, but UK consumer law gives buyers from a business a 14-day right to cancel most distance purchases whatever your listing says. See [what a UK web shop legally needs](/guides/web-shop-legal-pages-uk).

## The fields and their limits

| Field | Limit | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title | Up to 140 characters; Etsy suggests fewer than 15 words | [How to create a listing](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/115015628707) |
| Tags | Up to 13, each up to 20 characters; letters, numbers and spaces, with apostrophes and hyphens inside words | [How to use tags](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360000336307) |
| Photos and video | Up to 20 photos and 2 videos of 5 to 15 seconds | [How to create a listing](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/115015628707) |
| Photo size | At least 2000 pixels wide and high recommended; first photo at least 635 pixels to avoid ranking lower | [Image requirements](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/115015663347) |
| Attributes | Depend on the category you choose | [How to use attributes](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/115014502508) |
| "When was it made?" | Set in the listing form; for vintage, choose the era | [How to create a listing](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/115015628707) |

One more thing from the listing help: the listing's web address is fixed from the title you publish with ([Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/115015628707)). Write the title properly before you publish, not after.

## Titles

A vintage buyer searches for a thing, then filters by what makes it the right one: maker, era, material, colour, size, pattern. The title should say what the item is once, then give the few traits that decide the purchase, in the order a buyer would check them.

A working order for vintage:

1. **Era or date**, as precise as your evidence allows: "1970s", "1960s to 1970s", "circa 1950".
2. **Maker or designer**, if known and genuine.
3. **The item**, in the plain word buyers use: "jug", "dress", "table lamp".
4. **The one to three traits that matter most for the category**: material, colour, pattern name, size.
5. **Anything that makes this one different**: a named range, an unusual colourway, original box.

Examples of the shape, not text to copy:

| Weak | Better |
| --- | --- |
| Beautiful Retro Vintage Jug Perfect Gift Mid Century Kitsch Kitchen Decor Vintage Jug | 1970s Stoneware Jug, Brown Drip Glaze, 1 Pint |
| VINTAGE Dress 70s boho hippie festival maxi dress vintage clothing | 1970s Cotton Maxi Dress, Floral Print, UK 10 |
| Vintage Book Old Book Antique Book Collectible First Edition Rare | 1962 First Edition Hardback, Original Dust Jacket |

What to leave out, in line with Etsy's own guidance on titles ([Etsy Seller Handbook](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/1399426136697)): words that are opinion ("beautiful", "stunning", "perfect"), words about the sale ("free postage", "sale"), gift phrases unless the item is a gift by nature, and the same word twice. "Vintage" belongs once at most; the era does the same job more precisely, and the "when was it made" field already tells Etsy the item is vintage.

Never put a brand in the title that is not on the item. "In the style of" a famous maker, or a maker's name followed by "style", is both misleading to buyers and an intellectual property risk.

## Tags

Tags are where the searches go that the title cannot hold. Etsy says tags are most useful where there is no matching attribute, and for descriptive phrases buyers search for ([Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360000336307)). You have 13; use all of them, and do not spend any on words the title or attributes already cover exactly.

A way to fill 13 tags for a vintage item:

| Tags | What they cover | Example for the jug above |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 or 3 | Other names for the item | milk jug, pitcher, cream jug |
| 2 | The era in other forms | seventies kitchen, 70s pottery |
| 2 | Style words buyers use | mid century, retro kitchen |
| 2 | Material and technique | stoneware, drip glaze |
| 2 | Use or setting | farmhouse kitchen, flower vase |
| 1 or 2 | Maker, range or place of manufacture, if known | the maker or pottery name |
| 1 | British and American spellings or terms, if they differ | pitcher covers the US word |

Rules that save tags from being wasted:

- **Phrases beat single words.** "stoneware jug" says more than "jug" and "stoneware" as two tags.
- **Stay within 20 characters.** Longer phrases will not fit; rewrite rather than truncate into nonsense.
- **Do not repeat the category or attribute values** word for word.
- **Only describe what is true.** A tag for a material the item is not made of will bring the wrong buyer, and Etsy treats material claims as your responsibility ([Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/115014502508)).
- **Review after 60 days.** Your shop stats show which searches brought views. Swap tags that brought nothing for the words buyers actually used.

## Attributes

Attributes are the fields that change with the category: colour, material, dimensions, style, occasion and so on. They feed Etsy's filters, so a buyer filtering by colour or material only sees your item if you set it ([Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/115014502508)). Fill every attribute that honestly applies.

Three points for vintage.

**Choose the most specific category first.** The attributes available depend on it, and the category is itself a search visibility factor. "Jugs" under kitchen and dining beats a general "home decor".

**Pick the nearest value, then tag the exact one.** If the colour you would call "mustard" is not offered, choose yellow and put "mustard" in a tag. Etsy recommends this itself.

**Material claims are claims.** Etsy says that selecting a material or material feature is a claim that it is accurate and supported, and that you should keep records supporting it ([Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/115014502508)). If you cannot tell whether a jug is stoneware or earthenware, do not choose either; describe what you can see and let the buyer decide.

## The description

Vintage buyers want four things answered: what it is, how old, what condition, and will it fit or work. Put them in that order, near the top, in short lines.

1. **One or two sentences** saying what the item is, in plain words.
2. **Age and how you know.** "Made in the 1970s, dated from the backstamp, which this pottery used in that decade." This is the line that sells vintage, and it is the same reasoning you keep in your dating record.
3. **Condition, flaws first.** Chips, crazing, fading, stains, repairs, missing parts, smells. Point to the photo that shows each one. A buyer who knew about the chip does not open a case over it.
4. **Measurements** in centimetres, with the method (for clothes, laid flat, pit to pit, shoulder to hem). Vintage sizes do not match modern labels; say so.
5. **Care or use notes** if they matter. If you cannot confirm that a piece is safe for food, or that an old electrical item is safe to use, say so plainly and sell it as decorative.
6. **Postage and packing**, briefly.

Keep your own words. Do not paste a maker's history from a website or another seller's description, and do not copy the phrasing of Etsy's help pages into your listings. Short, specific and honest reads as expertise.

## Photos

Vintage must be shown with photos of the actual item ([Listing Image Requirements](https://www.etsy.com/uk/legal/policy/listing-image-requirements/253962679005)). Beyond the rule, the photos do most of the selling, and the first one decides whether anyone clicks.

**The first photo.** A single item, square or landscape so it crops cleanly in search, sharp, evenly lit, on a plain background, filling most of the frame ([Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/115015663347)). No collages, no text, no borders. Shoot it at least 2000 pixels on each side.

**The set.** Use more of the 20 slots than you think you need. A useful sequence for vintage:

1. The hero shot, front on.
2. Back and sides.
3. Top and underneath, including the base.
4. The maker's mark, label, backstamp or printed date: the evidence of age.
5. Every flaw, close up, one per photo if needed.
6. Scale: next to a ruler or tape, or in a hand.
7. In use or in a simple setting, if it helps the buyer imagine it. Keep it the same item, and do not let props look included.
8. Detail of pattern, texture or glaze.

**Video.** A short clip turning the item in daylight shows glaze, sheen and condition better than any still. You can add two per listing.

**Keep the shape consistent.** The first photo sets the shape for the rest of the listing; matching shapes look tidier.

**Alt text.** Etsy lets you add a text description to each image for buyers using screen readers. Describe what is in the photo in a short sentence.

The full phone setup (light, background, steadiness) is in [product photos on a phone](/guides/photos-on-a-phone).

## A routine per listing

| Step | Check |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Item qualifies and is dated, with a record kept |
| 2 | Most specific category chosen; "when was it made" set |
| 3 | Title: era, maker, item, key traits, under 15 words, nothing repeated |
| 4 | All relevant attributes filled; nearest values chosen |
| 5 | 13 tags, phrases, none duplicating title or attributes exactly |
| 6 | Description: what, age and how you know, flaws, measurements |
| 7 | 8 or more photos, hero shot clean, marks and flaws shown, alt text added |
| 8 | Return policy set; postage profile correct |

## What goes wrong

- **Keyword-stuffed titles.** They are harder to read and Etsy has moved weight to other fields.
- **Empty attributes.** The item never appears when buyers filter.
- **Half the tags unused, or used on duplicates.** Thirteen chances, taken as five.
- **A collage or text-heavy first photo.** Etsy names a single, clear item as a requirement for priority placement.
- **Flaws in the description but not in the photos, or the other way round.** Show and say both.
- **"In the style of" a famous maker.** Misleading and an intellectual property risk.
- **Copying other listings.** It reads as copied, and it is not yours to use.
- **Editing the title after publishing to fix a typo.** The web address stays with the first version; get it right first.
