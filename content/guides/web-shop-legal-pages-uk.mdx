This is for resellers setting up their own web shop, or selling through a social shop or messages as a business, who want to know what the law expects before the first order. After reading it you will have a checklist of what must be on the site, what each page needs to say, and where the official wording lives. It covers England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, which share these rules for online consumer sales. This is general information from official sources, not legal advice; if your business is unusual (you sell services, digital downloads, age-restricted goods, or to other countries), read the linked sources in full or take advice.

## Who this applies to

These rules apply to traders selling to consumers: anyone selling in the course of a business, whether a sole trader or a limited company. Someone clearing out their own belongings is not a trader. A reseller who buys stock to sell at a profit is. If you are unsure which you are, our [bookkeeping and tax guide](/guides/bookkeeping-and-tax-for-resellers) covers how HMRC looks at trading.

The main laws, all linked below:

| Law | What it covers for a web shop |
| --- | --- |
| [Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/contents) | Information before the order, confirmation, the 14-day right to cancel, refunds |
| [Electronic Commerce (EC Directive) Regulations 2002](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/2013/contents) | Who you are, how to contact you, clear prices online |
| [Consumer Rights Act 2015](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/contents) | Goods must be as described, of satisfactory quality and fit for purpose; remedies for faults |
| UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, guidance from the [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/privacy-notices-and-cookies/) | Privacy notice, handling customer data, data protection fee |
| Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR), guidance from the [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/) | Cookies and similar technologies; marketing emails |
| [Price Marking Order 2004](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/price-marking-order-2004-government-guidance/price-marking-order-2004-government-guidance) and the CMA's [price transparency guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/691b10065a253e2c40d705d9/Price_transparency_-_CMA209_.pdf) | Prices shown to consumers must include VAT and unavoidable charges |

## 1. Who you are: trader details

The site must say who is selling and how to reach you. Under the Electronic Commerce Regulations, an online seller must make available its name, the geographic address where it is established, and contact details including an email address that allow quick, direct contact, plus a VAT number if registered and any trade register or professional body details ([regulation 6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/2013/regulation/6)). GOV.UK's [online selling guidance](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses/online-selling) lists the same points for web shops.

| You are | Show on the site |
| --- | --- |
| Sole trader | Your own name and any business name ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/become-sole-trader/choose-your-business-name)), a geographic address, an email address, VAT number if registered |
| Limited company | The full company name, registered number, registered office address and where it is registered, for example England and Wales ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/running-a-limited-company/signs-stationery-and-promotional-material)), plus an email address and VAT number if registered |

**The home address question.** The regulation asks for the geographic address where you are established, which a PO box on its own does not give. Many sole traders do not want their home address on a public page. The lawful options are to use a business address you are entitled to use (a rented office, a registered address service that gives you a real address, or premises you trade from). Choosing to leave the address off is not one of them.

Put these details on a "Contact" or "About us" page and in the site footer, and repeat them in your terms.

## 2. Information before the order

Before the buyer is bound, you must give certain information in a clear way. GOV.UK summarises the list for distance sales ([online and distance selling](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses)), and the full list is in [Schedule 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/schedule/2) of the regulations. For a reseller web shop, the practical checklist:

- Your business name, address and contact details (as above).
- A description of the goods, including condition for second-hand items.
- The total price including all taxes, and delivery costs or how they are calculated.
- How the buyer can pay and when payment is taken.
- Delivery arrangements and how long goods take to arrive.
- The right to cancel, the conditions and how to do it, and the model cancellation form.
- Who pays for return postage if the buyer cancels.
- Your returns and complaints process, and a reminder that the buyer has legal rights if goods are faulty.

Online sellers must also list the steps to place an order, let buyers correct errors before they buy, make it clear that placing the order means paying (a button that says "order and pay" or similar), and let buyers save or print the terms ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses/online-selling)).

After the order, confirm it as soon as possible and no later than delivery, in a form the buyer can keep, usually an email. Deliver within 30 days unless you agreed a different time.

One figure people misread: GOV.UK mentions that the rules do not apply to goods worth £42 or less. In the regulations themselves that exemption is for off-premises contracts, such as doorstep sales ([regulation 7(4)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/7)), not online sales. Treat every online order as covered.

## 3. The 14-day right to cancel

This is the rule that surprises marketplace sellers used to "no returns".

| Rule | Detail | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cancellation window | Up to 14 days after the goods are delivered, for any reason | [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses) |
| If you do not tell buyers about the right | The window extends, up to 12 months | [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses) |
| Time to send goods back | 14 days after telling you they are cancelling | [Regulation 35](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/35) |
| Who pays return postage | The buyer, if you told them before the order; otherwise you, or if you offered to pay or collect | [Regulation 35](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/35) |
| Refund deadline | 14 days after you get the goods back, or evidence they were sent, whichever is first | [Regulation 34](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/34) |
| What to refund | The price and the original delivery charge, up to the cost of your cheapest standard delivery | [Regulation 34](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/34) |
| How to refund | The same payment method, with no fee, unless the buyer expressly agrees otherwise | [Regulation 34](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/34) |
| Deductions | Only for loss of value from handling beyond what was needed to check the goods, and only if you gave the required cancellation information | [Regulation 34](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/34) |

**Exceptions.** Items made to the buyer's specification or clearly personalised, goods that go off quickly, and sealed goods not suitable for return for health or hygiene reasons once unsealed are among the exceptions ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds)). Second-hand or vintage goods are not an exception. Nor is a low price.

**The model cancellation form.** The regulations include a [model form](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/schedule/3) buyers can use. Put it on your returns page as text they can copy or download. Buyers do not have to use it; any clear statement that they are cancelling counts.

**Our recommendation.** Tell buyers clearly, before they order, that they pay return postage when they change their mind. It is lawful, it is normal, and it keeps change-of-mind returns rare. Our [returns guide](/guides/returns-without-losing-money) covers the process and the costs.

## 4. Faulty and not-as-described goods

Separate from the right to cancel, goods must match their description, be of satisfactory quality and fit for purpose under the [Consumer Rights Act 2015](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/contents). For second-hand goods, satisfactory quality takes account of age, price and the description you gave, which is why describing and photographing flaws matters so much.

If goods are faulty, the buyer can reject them for a full refund within 30 days ([section 22](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/22)). After that, GOV.UK explains the repair or replacement route and that, within the first six months, the fault is assumed to have been there at delivery unless you can prove otherwise ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds)). When a buyer rejects faulty goods, you bear the reasonable cost of getting them back ([section 20](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/20)).

A line in your terms saying "sold as seen" or "no refunds on faulty items" does not remove these rights for consumers; terms that try to are not binding on them ([section 31](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/31)). GOV.UK covers the wider topic under [unfair terms](https://www.gov.uk/unfair-terms-in-sales-contracts).

## 5. The returns page

One page, in plain words, that covers:

1. How long buyers have to cancel (14 days from delivery) and how to tell you.
2. The model cancellation form, or a link to it.
3. Where to send the item, and that the buyer pays return postage for change-of-mind returns.
4. How and when you refund (within 14 days of getting the item back, to the original payment method).
5. The exceptions that apply to your stock, if any.
6. What happens if an item is faulty or not as described, and that you pay the return in that case.
7. Your contact details for returns.

## 6. The privacy notice

Almost every web shop collects personal data (names, addresses, emails, order history), so it needs a privacy notice. The ICO describes it as information about why you need people's data, what you do with it, how long you keep it and who you share it with ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/privacy-notices-and-cookies/how-to-write-a-privacy-notice-and-what-goes-in-it/)).

For a small shop, the ICO's free [privacy notice generator](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/privacy-notices-and-cookies/) is designed for small businesses and sole traders. It does not cover cookies, so do that separately. Make sure the notice names the services that handle your customers' data for you: your shop platform, payment provider, courier and email tool.

**The data protection fee.** Organisations, including sole traders, that use personal information must pay a data protection fee to the ICO unless exempt. The lowest tier is £52 a year, with £5 off by direct debit, for a turnover up to £632,000 or no more than 10 staff ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/data-protection-fee/)). Whether you are exempt depends on what you do with data; the ICO has a self-assessment on its [fee pages](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/). Do it before you launch.

## 7. Cookies

If your site stores or reads anything on a visitor's device (cookies, pixels, local storage), PECR applies. The ICO's position in short:

- Tell visitors what you use and why, in clear terms.
- Strictly necessary storage, such as the basket and checkout, does not need consent.
- Since the Data (Use and Access) Act, two further exceptions exist: storage used only to produce statistics to improve your own site, and storage used only to adapt how the site looks or works to a visitor's preferences. Both still require clear information and a simple, free way to object. Analytics shared with a provider fits only if the provider acts purely for you and does not combine the data with other sources ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/what-are-the-exceptions/)).
- Everything else, including advertising and tracking pixels, needs consent before it is set.

In practice: a cookie banner or settings panel that sets nothing optional until the visitor chooses, with refusing as easy as accepting, and a cookies page listing what the site uses. Shop platforms and themes load third-party scripts you may not know about; check what your site actually sets before writing the page. The ICO notes its guidance is being updated for the new law, so check the current version.

## 8. Prices including VAT and all unavoidable charges

Prices shown to consumers must be the final price including VAT and other taxes ([Price Marking Order guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/price-marking-order-2004-government-guidance/price-marking-order-2004-government-guidance)), and delivery costs must be shown or explained before the order ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses/online-selling)). Online prices must also say clearly whether they include tax and delivery ([E-Commerce Regulations, regulation 6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/2013/regulation/6)).

Mandatory fees must be in the headline price rather than added at checkout. The CMA sets out what that means in its [price transparency guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/691b10065a253e2c40d705d9/Price_transparency_-_CMA209_.pdf) under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act. For a reseller the practical rule: no "handling fee", "card fee" or "packaging charge" that appears only at checkout. Build it into the price, or into the delivery charge shown from the start.

If you are not VAT registered, you do not add VAT, and you should not describe prices as "including VAT". If you are registered, show VAT-inclusive prices to consumers and your VAT number on the site. The GOV.UK price marking guidance notes that sales of antiques and works of art, and sales by auction, are outside the Price Marking Order; the other pricing rules still apply to them.

Price reductions and urgency claims ("was £30", "only 2 left", countdown timers) must be true. GOV.UK links the government's guidance on [urgency and price reduction claims](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/using-urgency-and-price-reduction-claims-online).

## Pages checklist

| Page | Must include |
| --- | --- |
| Contact or About | Name, business name, geographic address, email, company details if a company, VAT number if registered |
| Terms of sale | Who you are, how the contract is formed, prices and payment, delivery times and costs, cancellation right, returns, faults, complaints |
| Returns and cancellations | The 14-day right, the model form, return postage, refund timing, exceptions, faulty goods |
| Delivery | Services, costs, timescales, where you post to |
| Privacy notice | What data, why, how long, who it is shared with, people's rights, your contact details |
| Cookies | What is used, why, how to change choices |
| Checkout | Total price including VAT and delivery, order steps, error correction, a clear pay button, link to terms |
| Order confirmation email | Order details, price, delivery, cancellation information and form |

Hosted shop builders provide templates for some of these. A template is a starting point, not compliance: read it, fix anything that does not match what you actually do (for example a 30-day returns promise you do not intend to keep), and add what is missing.

## What goes wrong

- **"No returns" on a business web shop.** Consumers have 14 days to cancel regardless.
- **Not saying who pays return postage.** If you do not tell them, you pay.
- **Late refunds, or refunds minus postage out.** The original standard delivery charge is refundable, within 14 days.
- **No address, or a PO box only.** A geographic address is required.
- **A privacy notice copied from another shop.** It describes their suppliers and practices, not yours.
- **Tracking cookies that fire before the banner is answered.** Consent comes first.
- **Checkout fees that appear at the last step.** Mandatory charges belong in the headline price.
- **Leaving the templates untouched.** A promise in your own terms binds you.
