This is for eBay sellers who want descriptions that do two jobs at once: convince the buyer who is nearly there, and leave no room for a "not as described" return from the buyer who was not paying attention. It covers what a description should contain, eBay's condition grades and what each one commits you to, how to lay it out so it reads on a phone, and the legal side of describing goods when you sell as a business. After reading it you will have a template you can fill in a few minutes for any item, and a clear idea of where a cheerful sentence becomes a misleading one. None of this is legal advice.

## What a description is for

The title and photos got the buyer here. The description exists to answer the questions they have before pressing buy, and to fix what the item is so that nobody can later claim it was something else. On a phone, which is where most eBay buying now happens, the description is collapsed behind a "See full description" link under the specifics and photos. Many buyers never open it. So the description is not where your key facts live; those go in the title, the photos, the specifics and the condition note. The description is where the detail lives for the buyer who wants it and the record lives for the case you hope never to have.

That leads to a rule that saves a lot of grief: anything that would change a buyer's decision goes in more than one place. A cracked screen is in a photo, in the condition note and in the description. A missing charger is in the specifics if there is a field, in the photos (of what is included) and in the first line of the description.

## What to include

A working order for a description, top to bottom:

1. **One line that repeats what it is.** Brand, model, size, colour. The buyer may have landed here from a search for something slightly different; confirm they are in the right place.
2. **Condition, plainly, in the first two lines.** Grade, then the specific flaws, then what is not included. "Used, good condition. Small scuff on the left toe, shown in photo 6. No box." If there are no flaws, say you looked: "No marks, holes or bobbling found."
3. **What is included.** List every item in the sale. What is in the contents photo is what the buyer will expect. If the box is not the original, or the manual is a printout, say so.
4. **Measurements and specifications.** For clothing: pit to pit, length, sleeve, waist, inside leg, laid flat, in centimetres or inches, stated. For hard goods: dimensions and weight. For electricals: model number, capacity, connections, region, what it was tested with.
5. **Provenance where it helps.** "From a smoke free home", "one owner", "bought new in 2021", "vintage, dated by the label". Only if true.
6. **Anything the buyer needs to know to use it.** Requires a particular cable; needs batteries; UK plug; PAL region; runs on a specific system; sold as a pair.
7. **Your terms, briefly.** Dispatch time, postage method, returns. On eBay these are also set in the listing fields, and the fields win; the description is for a short restatement.

That is enough. Long descriptions do not sell more; complete ones do.

## What to leave out

- **Adjectives without facts.** "Stunning", "gorgeous", "amazing" tell a buyer nothing and make a listing read like every other listing. "Unworn, with the swing tag" tells them something.
- **Words that promise.** "Perfect", "mint", "immaculate", "flawless", "as new". For a used item each one is a promise you will be held to; a buyer who finds one mark has a claim. Use the eBay grade and describe.
- **Padding aimed at search.** Lists of unrelated brands or keywords. eBay's [item description policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/listing-policies/item-description-policy?id=4372) requires everything in a listing to be directly related to the item or your terms, and keyword spamming is a breach.
- **Disclaimers that try to remove rights.** "No returns", "sold as seen", "not responsible for damage in post". For a business seller these are unlawful attempts to exclude statutory rights; for anyone they are a signal that something is wrong, and they do not stop an eBay case.
- **Contact details, links to other sites, and requests to buy elsewhere.** Policy breaches.
- **Instructions to "read the description" in capitals.** If the fact matters, put it in the first line, the condition note and a photo. Shouting does not make it seen.
- **Symbols and special characters.** The policy names subscript, superscript and symbols such as the copyright sign as not allowed; write the word.
- **Anything in another language.** Descriptions on eBay UK must be in English.

## eBay's condition grades

eBay defines every condition it offers on the [item conditions by category](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/creating-managing-listings/item-conditions-category?id=4765) page, and the description policy requires the condition you pick to match the item and to be consistent across title, description and specifics. Choose the grade from the definition, not from how you feel about the item. The ones most resellers use, in eBay's words paraphrased:

| Grade | What eBay's definition commits you to |
| --- | --- |
| New | Brand new, unused, unopened, undamaged, in original packaging where packaging applies. An opened box is not New. |
| New other (see details) | New and unused with no signs of wear, but missing its packaging or with the packaging opened. |
| New with tags (clothing) | Brand new, never worn, original tags or packaging still attached. |
| New without tags (clothing) | Brand new, never worn, but tags or packaging missing. |
| New with imperfections (clothing) | Brand new, never worn, with a defect. The defect must be described. |
| Seller refurbished | Restored to working order by you or a third party: inspected, cleaned and repaired to full working order. |
| Used | Previously used; may show cosmetic wear but fully operational and functions as intended. |
| Pre-owned, Excellent / Good / Fair (clothing) | Worn, graded by the amount of visible wear; eBay's definitions say signs of wear should be shown and described, and for Fair that all imperfections should be shown and described. |
| For parts or not working | Does not function as intended, needs repair, or is missing essential components. |

Two grades need care. "Used" commits you to "fully operational and functions as intended"; if it has a fault, however small, it is not Used, it is For parts or not working, with the fault described, or it is Used with the fault clearly stated and the buyer told what still works. And the refurbished grades with warranties (Certified, Excellent, Very Good, Good Refurbished) are for eligible sellers in specific categories and carry warranty and inspection requirements; do not select them for something you have wiped down.

Since June 2026 eBay has been standardising fashion listings, and the [create a listing](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/create-listing?id=4105) help page says that from August 2026 apparel and footwear listings with missing or unclear size or condition may be hidden. Clothing conditions must be one of the supported values; a custom condition is a hidden listing.

## The condition note

Below the grade is a free text condition note. It shows near the top of the listing on a phone, above the fold, before the collapsed description. This is the most valuable text box on eBay and most sellers waste it or leave it empty.

Write it as the flaws, in order of seriousness, in plain words, each tied to a photo. Then what is missing. Then stop.

- "Light wear to the sole, small pull on the left cuff (photo 7). No box."
- "Tested and working. Screen has a hairline scratch, not visible when on (photo 4). Battery holds a charge but has not been tested for duration. Charger included, not original."
- "Spine creased, pages clean, no writing. Previous owner's name inside cover."

If there is nothing wrong, say what you checked: "Checked for marks, holes and bobbling; none found."

## Formatting for a phone

The description is rendered inside eBay's app and mobile site, and anything you paste from a word processor or an old template can break it. eBay's [Get found in search](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/listings/listings-in-search) page recommends short paragraphs, bold subheadings, a readable 12 to 14 point font and no active content such as scripts, which mobile does not render and which can hurt indexing.

- Plain text or the simplest formatting: bold headings, short lines, one fact per line.
- First 200 characters carry the condition and what is included. That is roughly what shows before the "See full description" link on the app.
- No tables built in HTML, no images in the description, no coloured backgrounds, no centred text, no fonts. All of it renders unpredictably on a phone.
- No copied template from a previous listing with a different item's details left in. Sellers we know have refunded items because a pasted paragraph said "boxed" when the new item was not.
- Read it on your own phone before you publish. Open the listing in the app and tap the description. If you have to zoom, rewrite it.

## The legal side for business sellers

If you buy things to sell them, eBay's [business seller policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/business-seller-policy?id=4710) says you must register as a business on eBay, give your contact details, state your prices including postage, and set out a returns policy that includes the buyer's right to withdraw within 14 days under the Consumer Contracts Regulations and the acceptance of faulty item returns within 30 days with a full refund. The law behind that is what makes accurate description matter beyond an eBay case.

**Consumer Rights Act 2015.** Applies to any trader selling to a consumer, including second-hand goods. Goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described. If they are not, the consumer has a short term right to reject within 30 days for a full refund, then a right to repair or replacement, then a final right to reject or a price reduction; and for the first six months a fault is presumed to have been present at sale unless the trader shows otherwise. GOV.UK's [Consumer Rights Act summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/consumer-rights-act-2015/consumer-rights-act-2015) covers the outline; the Trading Standards [sale and supply of goods guide](https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/goods/sale-and-supply-of-goods) covers the detail. "As described" is the one that turns on your text: if the description says "working" and it is not, or "complete" and it is not, the buyer has a statutory right whatever your returns policy says. For second-hand goods, satisfactory quality takes account of age, price and the described condition, which is exactly why describing wear accurately protects you: a mark you described is not a fault.

**Distance selling.** For online sales, GOV.UK's [online and distance selling](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses) page sets out what you must tell buyers before they buy, including a description of the goods, the total price, delivery costs and their right to cancel within 14 days of receiving the goods for any reason. If you fail to tell them about the cancellation right, the period extends to a year. Refunds after a cancellation are due within 14 days of getting the goods back, per GOV.UK's [accepting returns and giving refunds](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds), which also states that it is illegal to mislead customers about their rights, for instance with a "no refunds" line.

**Misleading descriptions.** Since April 2025 the rules on unfair commercial practices sit in Part 4 of the [Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/part/4/chapter/1). A description that contains false or misleading information, or that is true but presented in a way likely to deceive, is a misleading action; leaving out material information a buyer needs, or hiding it where they are unlikely to see it, is a misleading omission. Both can be offences enforced by Trading Standards, and the omission offence for an invitation to purchase no longer depends on showing that the buyer would have decided differently. In plain terms: the flaw you left out of the listing because it might put people off is the thing the law is aimed at.

**Private sellers.** The Consumer Rights Act does not apply to a private individual clearing their own belongings; the older rule that goods must match their description still does, eBay's Money Back Guarantee applies to every sale, and a private seller who is really trading (buying to resell, making to sell) is a trader in law whatever the account type says. eBay's policy is explicit that business sellers cannot present themselves as private individuals.

Northern Ireland follows the same consumer law as the rest of the UK for these purposes; Scotland's limitation period for claims is five years rather than six, which does not change how you describe anything.

## A template

Copy this and fill it in. Delete lines that do not apply rather than leaving them blank.

```
[Brand] [Model or name] [Size] [Colour]

Condition: [eBay grade]. [Flaws, each with photo number]. [What is missing.]
Includes: [everything in the sale].

Measurements (laid flat): pit to pit [ ]cm, length [ ]cm, sleeve [ ]cm.
Details: [material, capacity, connections, region, year, anything needed to use it].
Tested: [what you tested and how, for anything electrical].
From: [smoke free home, one owner, bought new in year].

Dispatch within [ ] working day(s) by [service]. Returns: [as set in the listing].
```

Everything on the first two lines also goes in the condition note and the specifics. That is the whole discipline.

## What goes wrong

1. **The flaw was in the description only.** The buyer did not open it. Put flaws in the condition note and a photo.
2. **"Perfect condition" on a used item.** One mark and it is not as described. Grade it and describe it.
3. **Condition grade and text disagree.** "New" with "worn once" in the note. Policy breach and a return. Pick the grade from the definition.
4. **Template debris.** A line from the last listing about the box or the charger. Read every description once before publishing.
5. **"No returns" or "sold as seen" on a business account.** Unlawful and useless. State your real returns policy.
6. **Measurements missing on clothing.** The commonest cause of "doesn't fit" returns. Two numbers prevent most of them.
7. **Untested electricals described as working.** If you did not test it, say "untested", grade accordingly and price accordingly.
8. **Description formatted on a desktop and never read on a phone.** Open it in the app.
9. **A private account running a business.** Once you buy to resell, you are a trader with the obligations above; register as a business before eBay does it for you.
