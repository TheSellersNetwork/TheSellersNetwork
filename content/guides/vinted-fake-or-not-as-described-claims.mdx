This is for Vinted sellers who have had, or want to avoid, a buyer saying "this is fake" or "this is not what was described". After reading it you will know what counts as significantly not as described, the exact timeline once a claim is opened, what each of your options costs you, how counterfeit claims are decided under the terms that apply from 8 October 2026, and the evidence habits that win cases. It describes Vinted's rules as published; it is not legal advice.

## The terms you need

Vinted calls "not as described" SNAD, short for significantly not as described. The [terms, section 11](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions) define it as a significant difference between the item received and its description or photos, such as a different size, colour or severe damage (stains, smells or holes), or an item missing from a bundle. A counterfeit is treated as its own kind of SNAD claim.

Vinted's [SNAD page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1090-significantly-not-as-described-items-at-vinted) lists what does and does not count:

| Counts as SNAD | Does not count |
| --- | --- |
| Wrong colour or size | The buyer does not like it or it does not fit |
| Missing parts or accessories not mentioned in the listing | Minor differences that do not affect use or look, such as slight colour differences from lighting |
| Missing items from a bundle | Differences in minor materials beyond the main three listed |
| Damage (stains, rips, breaks) not shown or described | Normal wear on electronics that was clearly shown and described |
| Main materials different from the listing | |
| Counterfeit | |
| Functionality not as described (for example a device that does not charge) | |

The right-hand column is your defence. But notice how much depends on "not shown or described". Most seller losses come from a flaw that was real but not in the photos.

## The timeline once a buyer complains

For standard sellers, from Vinted's [refund policy](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/465), [SNAD claim page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/463/1356-item-is-significantly-not-as-described) and [terms, section 11](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions):

| Step | Deadline |
| --- | --- |
| Buyer reports the issue with I have an issue | Within 2 calendar days of Vinted's notice that the item was delivered |
| Order is suspended; money stays held | Until resolved |
| You respond: full refund, partial refund, or request a return | If you do nothing within 14 days of the report, Vinted's support team decides |
| Buyer sends the return | Within 5 business days of your request, or the claim closes and you are paid |
| You check the returned item | 2 days from Vinted's notice that it should have arrived; press Everything is OK or report an issue |
| Either side escalates | Vinted says it replies within 48 hours, then makes a final decision |

If the buyer does not report within the 2 days, or presses Everything is OK, the order completes and the buyer cannot use Vinted's refund policy afterwards.

Pro sellers work to different rules: buyers have 14 days and a legal right to withdraw for any reason. See [Vinted Pro and selling as a business](/guides/vinted-pro-and-selling-as-a-business).

## Your options as the seller

When a claim arrives, you can ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/272-returning-an-item)):

1. **Offer a full refund and let them keep the item.** Fast, and costs you the item.
2. **Offer a partial refund and let them keep the item.** Single item orders only; not available on bundles. The buyer can accept or decline.
3. **Ask for the item back.** The buyer pays the return postage unless you offer to cover it by providing a Vinted label. You refund once you have checked it.

How to choose:

| Situation | Our recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| The claim is fair and the flaw was your miss | Say so. Offer a partial refund if the item is still usable and the buyer is reasonable; otherwise take it back. |
| The claim is fair but minor | A modest partial refund usually settles it faster than a return. |
| The item is worth more than the hassle and you think the buyer is wrong | Ask for it back. In practice, a buyer who is trying it on often drops the claim when they have to pay return postage. |
| Low value item, clearly your error | Full refund, let them keep it. The return postage and your time can exceed the item. |
| You believe the buyer has swapped or damaged the item | Ask for it back, photograph it on arrival, report an issue within your 2 days, and escalate with your original photos. |

Keep every message on Vinted, polite and factual. Support reads the conversation.

### When the item comes back

When the return arrives, open it on camera if you can. Compare it with your listing photos: labels, flaws, serials. If it is fine, press Everything is OK. If it is not the same item, is damaged, has been worn or washed, or has missing parts, report an issue within the 2 days. The terms say a buyer does not get a refund if they used, washed or changed the item before returning it ([section 11](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions)).

## Counterfeit claims

These are the claims that cost sellers most, and the rules changed on 8 October 2026 ([our summary](/blog/vinted-terms-october-2026)).

### How a counterfeit claim is decided

When a buyer claims an item is fake, they submit photos of the whole item, the suspicious parts, the care label and any serial number ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1471)). Vinted may suspend the order and asks both sides for information. Under the [terms, section 11](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions) there are three outcomes:

| Vinted's finding | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Authenticity is not in doubt | The order completes and you are paid. |
| Not enough information to decide (ambiguous) | The order is cancelled, the buyer is refunded, and the buyer must return the item to you, paying return postage unless agreed otherwise. |
| Strong evidence it is counterfeit | You have 48 hours to provide evidence that it is authentic (under the terms that applied before 8 October 2026 this was 24 hours). If you cannot, the order is cancelled, the buyer is refunded and does not have to return the item. |

Read the last row twice. With strong evidence against you and no proof within 48 hours, you lose the sale and the item. That is why proof has to exist before the claim, not be gathered after it.

Vinted's [authenticity policy](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/307) also says it will remove counterfeit listings and suspend the account, and permanently block accounts that repeat it. You cannot list something with "not sure if authentic" as a disclaimer; if you cannot verify it, do not list it.

### Item Verification

Some designer items are eligible for Vinted's Item Verification: currently items priced at £85 or more, in listed brands, in clothing, bags, accessories, jewellery and shoes. The buyer pays £10 per item; sellers cannot opt out on an eligible listing ([Item Verification](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1147-item-verification-for-designer-items)). The item goes to Vinted's hub first, and you send it with any original authenticity documents ([selling with Item Verification](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/1153-selling-with-the-item-verification-service)).

If it fails verification, the order is cancelled and it is sent back to you ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1185)). Under the October 2026 terms ([section 17](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions)):

- an item that fails for authenticity or Catalogue Rules reasons can never be listed on Vinted again;
- an item verified as counterfeit is held for six weeks from Vinted's notice so you can challenge it; if you do not provide proof of authenticity or a formal objection in that time, Vinted can dispose of or destroy it without compensation.

If an item of yours fails and you believe it is genuine, act inside the six weeks, with purchase proof.

## The evidence that wins cases

Build this into listing, not into disputes.

**At purchase**

- Keep receipts, order confirmations and any authenticity cards for branded items. Vinted's authenticity policy says to keep proof such as invoices and certificates because it may ask for them.
- For charity shop or car boot finds with no paperwork, photograph the item as you found it, with a note of where and when. It is weaker than a receipt, but better than nothing.

**At listing**

- Photograph the brand label, care label (all sides), size label, and any serial or date code, in natural light with text readable. These are exactly the photos Vinted asks buyers to send against you.
- Photograph every flaw, close up, and name it in the description. A flaw in the photos is not SNAD.
- Give measurements, not just the size label. Size claims are common, and measurements settle them.
- State the main materials from the care label. Different main materials counts as SNAD.
- For electronics, show it working, and show the serial number where you can.
- Use only your own photos. Stock or brand website photos get listings removed and hurt you in a dispute.

**At packing**

- Photograph the item next to the open parcel, then the sealed parcel, inside and outside, as Vinted's [packing page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/65) recommends.
- For bundles, photograph every item going in. Missing items are a SNAD reason.
- For higher value items, a short video of packing is worth the minute it takes.

**In the claim**

- Reply within the deadlines. The 48 hour counterfeit window is short.
- Send the proof, not an argument: receipt, listing photos showing the label or flaw, packing photos.
- Refer to the listing: "the stain is shown in photo 4 and described in line 2".

## Buyers who abuse the process

Most claims are honest. Some are not: a worn item returned, a different item sent back, a "fake" claim on a genuine item to get a free refund. Vinted's terms say a buyer does not get a refund if the item is found not to be SNAD, if they used or washed it, if they missed the deadlines, or if they are under investigation for abusing the refund policy. Your protection is the evidence above, plus reporting the issue within your 2 days of the return arriving. Escalate calmly with photos; do not argue in the chat.

## What goes wrong

- **Flaws you knew about but did not photograph.** The single biggest cause of lost claims.
- **No label photos on branded items.** You have 48 hours to produce proof you should already have.
- **Listing branded items you cannot verify.** Not allowed, and a counterfeit finding can cost you the item.
- **Missing the 2 day window to check a return.** Report issues before it ends.
- **Offering a partial refund on a bundle.** Not available; plan for a full refund of that item or a return.
- **Ignoring a failed verification.** After six weeks the item can be destroyed.
- **Relisting an item that failed verification.** The October 2026 terms bar it.
- **Arguing instead of evidencing.** Support decides on proof.
