This is for business sellers on eBay UK who get return requests and want to handle them without giving money away or picking up defects. After reading it you will know the legal minimum you must offer, which eBay returns setting to pick and why, who pays the postage each way, exactly what to do in the three business days after a request opens, when a partial refund is the right answer, and what to change in your listings so fewer requests arrive in the first place.

Returns are a cost of selling at a distance. The aim is not zero returns; it is a returns process that costs you the least per return, protects your seller standing, and does not turn ordinary buyers into angry ones. Nothing here is legal advice; where the law is involved the official page is linked.

## The legal minimum for a business seller

If you are registered as a business seller you are selling at a distance to consumers, and the Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013 apply. GOV.UK's [accepting returns and giving refunds](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds) page and its [online and distance selling](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses) page set out what that means, and the [regulations themselves](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/contents) are the source.

- A consumer can cancel for any reason up to 14 days after receiving the goods. No reason needed, and "no returns" does not remove the right.
- They then have a further 14 days to send the goods back.
- You must refund within 14 days of getting the goods back (or of proof they were sent), including the cost of your standard delivery. You do not have to refund the extra for an express option they chose.
- You can deduct for loss of value if they have handled the goods beyond what a shop would allow, but not a restocking fee.
- The consumer pays the cost of returning the goods only if you told them so before they bought. If you did not, you pay.
- If you did not tell the consumer about the right to cancel, the cancellation period extends to up to 12 months.
- Some goods are exempt: personalised or made to order items, perishables, sealed items that cannot be returned for health or hygiene reasons once opened, sealed audio, video and software once unsealed.

Separately, the Consumer Rights Act 2015 gives a consumer a short term right to reject faulty goods within 30 days for a full refund ([section 22](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/22)), and rights to repair, replacement or a refund after that. That applies regardless of your returns policy and regardless of whether the item was new or used, as long as the fault was not described.

eBay builds the 14 day rule into its business seller settings: you cannot offer less. What you choose above that is a commercial decision.

## eBay's returns policy options and what each does

You set the policy in your business policies. eBay's [setting up your returns policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/handling-return-requests/setting-return-policy?id=4368) page and its [buyer friendly returns](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/postage/buyer-friendly-returns) page describe the choices. In practice, for a UK business seller, the options are:

| Policy | What the buyer sees | Who pays remorse return postage | Effect on eBay standing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 14 day returns, buyer pays | The legal minimum (only available in some categories) | Buyer | Meets the minimum; no Premium Service eligibility |
| 30 day returns, buyer pays | Common default | Buyer | Meets the returns condition for Premium Service |
| 30 day free returns | "Free returns" label in search and a filter buyers can use | You | Meets Premium Service; extra visibility; eligible for partial refund deductions |
| 60 day returns, buyer pays or free | Longer window | As chosen | As 30 day |

Two things are true regardless of which you pick. First, a buyer whose item is damaged, faulty or not as described can return it under eBay's [Money Back Guarantee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy?id=4210) whatever your policy says, and you pay the return postage. Second, the returns window in your policy only governs remorse returns; the Money Back Guarantee gives buyers 30 days from delivery for not as described claims whatever you chose.

### What it does to visibility and Top Rated status

eBay's own seller centre page says listings with free returns get a "free returns" label in search and are included when buyers filter for it, and quotes its own study of sellers who moved from 14 to 30 day returns seeing a lift in conversion. Treat the exact figure with caution; the direction is right and it is consistent with what sellers we know report.

Top Rated status itself does not depend on your returns policy. eBay's [seller standards policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-standards-policy?id=4347) sets Top Rated on defect rate (no more than 0.5%, from no more than three buyers), cases closed without seller resolution (no more than 0.3% or two cases), late delivery rate (no more than 3% or five), 100 transactions and £1,000 of sales with UK and Irish buyers in the past 12 months, and being registered as a business. A return handled properly is not a defect.

What the returns policy does gate is **eBay Premium Service**, the badge that goes on individual listings and carries a 10% discount on final value fees. eBay's [Premium Service page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/protection/ebay-premium-service) lists the conditions: Top Rated status, a free delivery option within three working days, an express option within two working days for no more than £10, tracked services on everything listed at £20 and over, a UK return address, same or one day dispatch, and a 30 day returns policy. Free returns are not required for the badge; 30 days are.

### Which to choose

**Our recommendation: 30 day returns, buyer pays return postage, as the default for a business seller.** It meets the law with room to spare, it qualifies for Premium Service, and it does not commit you to paying postage on every change of mind. Move to 30 day free returns on categories where your competitors offer it and your return rate is low (branded clothing in known sizes, sealed media, small consumables): the visibility gain is real and the cost is small when returns are rare. Stay on buyer pays for categories where remorse returns are common and heavy: shoes, bulky items, anything where the return postage is a large share of the price.

Do not pick 14 days where 30 is available. The saving is illusory (the law already gives 14 plus 14 to return) and it costs you the Premium Service badge.

## Who pays the return postage

The single most misunderstood point, and the source of most arguments. eBay's [return postage for sellers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/return-postage-sellers?id=4703) page is the reference.

| Reason for return | Who pays return postage | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not as described, damaged, faulty, wrong item | You | Whatever your policy says, including "no returns" |
| Changed mind, ordered wrong size, found it cheaper | Buyer, unless you offer free returns | Your policy must say the buyer pays, and eBay's settings do that for you |
| Buyer says not as described but it is | You, in the first instance | See handling below; the remedy is the deduction, the report and the appeal, not refusing the label |

When you owe the return postage you provide it by uploading a label to the request, sending the buyer the money for one, or using a label you included in the parcel. On orders of £450 or more, the return needs signature confirmation, the same threshold as outbound (see the [signature confirmation policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behaviour-policies/signature-confirmation-policy?id=5154)). If you do nothing, eBay may accept the return and issue a label on your behalf, and if you ignore the request entirely eBay may refund the buyer without requiring the item back.

Two practical rules. Never ask a buyer to pay return postage on a not as described return "and I will refund it later"; it looks like a refusal and eBay will treat it as one. And when the buyer pays, do not argue about the price of their label; they choose the service, you refund the item.

## Handling a return request, step by step

eBay's [handling return requests](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/handling-return-requests?id=4115) page gives you three business days to respond. What to do with them:

**Day one: read the reason and the photos.** A remorse return with a policy that says buyer pays: accept it, the buyer gets a label at their cost, you refund on receipt. A not as described return: accept it and provide the label, or offer an alternative, the same day. Do not message the buyer for a "discussion" before accepting; the clock is running and a stalled request is how buyers end up asking eBay to step in.

**Offer an alternative where it fits.** eBay lets you offer a partial refund with the buyer keeping the item, a replacement or exchange, or a full refund without return. A partial refund is right when the issue is small and the return postage would cost more than the discount (a scuff on a £15 item, a missing accessory you can send). A replacement is right when you have another and the buyer wanted the item. A refund without return is right when the item is worth less than the return postage; write it off and keep the buyer.

**Decline only when you can.** You can decline a remorse return only if your policy is no returns, which as a business seller it is not. Everything else is accept or negotiate.

**On receipt: inspect and refund within two business days.** eBay will refund on your behalf if you do not, and you will have lost the chance to deduct. Refund the item and the original postage on a not as described return; on a remorse return you refund the item and your standard delivery cost, and you may keep the difference if the buyer chose an express service.

**Deduct only when you are allowed to and it is true.** Under eBay's [seller protections](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-protections?id=4345), eligible sellers can deduct up to 50% from the refund where the item comes back used, altered or damaged. eBay's handling page gives bands: no deduction for excellent condition, 5% to 10% for good, 15% to 30% for fair, 35% to 50% for poor. Deductions are for loss of value, not for market moves or restocking. You need to report the buyer and refund within two business days of receiving the item, and you lose the right to deduct once eBay has stepped in. Deducting also costs you the final value fee credit on the refund.

**Never let it go to eBay if you can help it.** If you and the buyer cannot agree, either side can ask eBay to step in after three business days. If eBay decides against you, that is a case closed without seller resolution: a defect, counted against the 0.3% limit, with no fee credit. A refund you gave voluntarily is not a defect. That asymmetry decides most returns: a £20 refund you disagree with is cheaper than a defect on an account doing 300 transactions a year.

## Not as described claims that are wrong

Some are. The buyer ordered the wrong size and knows the "not as described" button gets free postage. The item was fine and they changed their mind. A few are outright abuse: a different item comes back, or a broken one. The playbook:

1. **Accept the return and provide the label anyway.** Refusing does not work; it becomes an eBay case and you lose it, with a defect.
2. **Document.** Photograph the item before dispatch as a habit for anything over a threshold you choose. Photograph what comes back, on the packaging, before you open it fully.
3. **Refund with a deduction if the item is not as sent**, within two business days, and report the buyer through the same flow. eBay's [abusive buyer policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behaviour-policies/abusive-buyer-policy?id=4374) covers false not as described claims, returning a different item, and using or damaging an item then returning it. When eBay confirms the abuse, the case and any feedback come off your record and the return does not count in your item not as described rate.
4. **If a different item came back, or nothing did**, refund only what the seller protections require and report with photographs; this is the situation eBay's protections were written for.
5. **Appeal within 30 days** if eBay decides against you and you have evidence, using the [appeal process](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/appeal-outcome-case-seller?id=4369).

Then watch the pattern, not the incident. eBay's [service metrics](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/service-metrics-policy?id=4769) compare your item not as described rate to peers each month; a "very high" rating (which needs at least 10 requests from 10 buyers) raises your final value fees and removes your right to deduct. One bad buyer does not move it. A product that generates a return every ten sales does.

## Partial refunds done properly

A partial refund is a tool, not a concession. It is right when the buyer is broadly happy, the problem is real but minor, and the return would cost more than the discount. It is wrong when the buyer is fishing (a demand for money off with no photo and no wish to return) or when the item is genuinely faulty and the buyer is entitled to a full refund.

Offer it through the request, so eBay records it and the buyer's acceptance closes the request. Never by message alone and never outside eBay. Size it to the fault: the cost of the missing part, a proportion for a cosmetic mark, the difference between the size described and the size sent. If the buyer refuses, accept the return; do not raise the offer twice.

## Reducing returns at listing time

Every return starts in a listing. The cheapest return is the one that never opens.

- **Measure, do not just size.** For clothing, give the flat measurements (pit to pit, length, waist, inside leg) as well as the label size. Sizing is the largest cause of remorse returns and the easiest to reduce.
- **Photograph every flaw** and write it in the description. A buyer who saw the mark before buying cannot claim not as described for it.
- **Photograph what is included.** If there is no charger, show the item without one and say so. "Does not include" lines prevent claims.
- **Use item specifics honestly.** Condition, colour, model number, compatibility. Mismatched specifics are the second largest source of not as described claims after condition.
- **Say what "used" means for this item.** Cosmetic wear, tested and working, hours of use, battery health where relevant.
- **Test before you list.** Electronics that were "working when I bought them" are the classic faulty return.
- **Pack to arrive.** Damage in transit is your not as described claim. See our packaging guide.
- **Dispatch when you said.** Late arrival is not a return reason, but it is the mood in which returns get opened.
- **Watch your return reasons.** Seller Hub shows them. Two returns for the same reason on the same product is a listing to fix or a product to stop selling.

## What goes wrong

- **Choosing "no returns" as a business seller.** It is not available in a way that means anything, it forfeits Premium Service, and buyers read it as a warning.
- **Arguing in the three days instead of accepting.** The buyer asks eBay to step in, eBay refunds, you get a defect and no fee credit.
- **Refusing a not as described label because "it is as described".** Same result. Accept, inspect, deduct, report.
- **Missing the two business day refund window after receipt.** eBay refunds for you, in full, and you lose the deduction.
- **Deducting for the wrong reason.** Restocking, a fall in the market price, or your time are not allowed. Misusing deductions loses you the protection.
- **Refunding outside eBay.** No record, no fee credit, and the request stays open.
- **Ignoring the legal side because eBay handles it.** The 14 day right and the 30 day faulty goods right exist whether or not the buyer uses eBay's tools, and a buyer who writes to you a year after purchase because you never told them about cancellation is a real, if rare, problem.
- **Treating one abusive buyer as a reason to tighten the policy for everyone.** The policy is a marketing setting. Handle the buyer; keep the policy.

## Questions

**Do I have to accept a return on a used item?** Yes. The 14 day right applies to used goods sold by a business, and eBay's Money Back Guarantee covers them for not as described. Condition, described honestly, is your protection.

**Can I charge a restocking fee?** No. Not under the regulations and not on eBay UK. A deduction for actual loss of value is the only reduction permitted.

**The buyer says faulty but it was working. What now?** Accept the return with your label, test it when it arrives, and if it works refund with the appropriate deduction (if any), report the buyer, and keep the test evidence. If it really is faulty, refund in full and check whether the rest of that stock has the same problem.

**Does Northern Ireland differ?** The Consumer Contracts Regulations and the Consumer Rights Act apply across the UK. eBay's policies are the same across eBay.co.uk.
