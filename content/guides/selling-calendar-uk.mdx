This is for UK resellers on eBay, Vinted, Depop, Facebook Marketplace and similar platforms who want to stop buying coats in March and swimwear in October. It sets out, month by month, what tends to sell, what to source for the months ahead, and the fixed dates that move demand and postage. The patterns are described in general terms from how the trade works in practice; your own sales history is the only source of numbers you should trust, so the last section shows how to build it.

## How to use this calendar

The rule underneath every month is the same: **list about a month before buyers want the item, and source about two months before.** A winter coat bought in September is listed in October and sold in November. A winter coat bought in November is competing with every other seller at the peak and still on your rail in February.

Three things shift that timing.

- **Weather.** The first properly cold week sells more knitwear than any date on the calendar. A late warm autumn holds it back. Have the stock ready and let the weather pull the trigger.
- **Pay day.** Many buyers are paid at the end of the month. In practice, sellers we know see the last few days of a month and the first few of the next busier than the middle. Time price drops and new listings for that window rather than the week before it.
- **Platform events.** Promotions, fee changes and site-wide sales run on each platform's own schedule. Watch the seller news pages and our [change breakdowns](/tools/policy-changes).

If you sell on Amazon, the [Amazon selling year](/guides/amazon-selling-year) covers Prime Day, the Q4 shipment deadlines and storage in more detail than this guide does.

## The fixed dates

These dates matter every year. Most are set by someone else and published on an official page; check the page each year rather than relying on last year's.

| Date or period | Why it matters to resellers | Where to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| School summer holidays end | Uniform, school shoes, bags, stationery, calculators, sports kit | Term dates vary by council and by nation; Scottish schools go back earlier than English and Welsh ones. Find yours through [GOV.UK school term dates](https://www.gov.uk/school-term-holiday-dates). |
| Bank holidays | Postal collections and deliveries change around them; buyers browse more on long weekends | [GOV.UK bank holidays](https://www.gov.uk/bank-holidays), which lists England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland separately |
| Royal Mail price changes | In 2026 prices changed in April and again in October, which changes your postage charges | [April 2026 changes](/blog/royal-mail-prices-april-2026), [October 2026 changes](/blog/royal-mail-prices-october-2026) |
| Halloween (31 October) | Costumes, props, decorations; demand ends abruptly on the day | Fixed date |
| Black Friday | The Friday after US Thanksgiving, which in 2026 is 27 November. Retail discounting runs for most of the month either side. | Fixed by the US calendar |
| Christmas last posting dates | The last day you can post and expect delivery before Christmas, by service | [Royal Mail last posting dates](https://www.royalmail.com/christmas/last-posting-dates). Royal Mail publishes these each autumn; if they are not up when you look, check back. Couriers publish their own. |
| 31 January | Online Self Assessment deadline and payment date | [GOV.UK deadlines](https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/deadlines), [tax dates tool](/tools/tax) |
| Mothering Sunday | Three weeks before Easter Sunday, so the date moves every year | Check a calendar for the year |
| 5 April | End of the tax year | [Tax dates tool](/tools/tax) |
| 5 October | Deadline to register for Self Assessment if you started trading in the previous tax year | [GOV.UK: register for Self Assessment](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-self-assessment) |

Second class letters and large letters now move on alternate weekdays; see [Royal Mail second class alternate days](/blog/royal-mail-second-class-alternate-days) before you promise a delivery time in December.

## Month by month

Each month has three parts: what tends to sell, what to source now for later, and the jobs worth doing.

### January

**Selling.** Gym and fitness kit, running gear, home organisation, diaries and planners early in the month, and anything bought as a present that the recipient did not want. Winter coats and boots still sell while it is cold. Many buyers have less money after Christmas, so price-sensitive items move better than luxury ones.

**Sourcing.** Post-Christmas retail clearance and unwanted gifts appear everywhere: charity shops, car boots that are still running, local selling groups. Buy Christmas decorations and gift sets only if you have space to hold them for eleven months and the price leaves margin after that wait.

**Jobs.** Returns from December sales arrive. Deal with them promptly (see [returns without losing money](/guides/returns-without-losing-money)). If you file Self Assessment, the online deadline is 31 January.

### February

**Selling.** Valentine's gifts in the first half of the month: jewellery, fragrance, small gifts, anything red. Still winter clothing. Ski and snow gear around the February half term.

**Sourcing.** Start buying spring and lighter layers: lightweight jackets, transitional knitwear, garden tools, outdoor furniture if you have space. Car boots are few, so charity shops and online auctions carry most of the load.

**Jobs.** A quiet month for many sellers, which makes it the right time to fix old listings, photograph a backlog and work through [stale stock](/guides/stale-stock).

### March

**Selling.** Mothering Sunday gifts in the weeks before it. Spring jackets, gardening, bikes and outdoor toys start to move as the clocks go forward. Easter crafts and decorations if Easter falls early.

**Sourcing.** Car boot season starts on many sites as the ground dries; check the organiser's page for opening dates. Buy summer clothing, festival wear and outdoor gear now.

**Jobs.** Plan the tax year end. Count your stock if you use traditional accounting; see [bookkeeping and tax for resellers](/guides/bookkeeping-and-tax-for-resellers).

### April

**Selling.** Garden and outdoor, bikes, camping, lighter clothing. Easter often falls in April; Easter holiday trips drive luggage and travel accessories.

**Sourcing.** Car boots are busy and cheap. Buy summer holiday stock (luggage, swimwear, sandals, sunglasses) for May and June listing.

**Jobs.** New tax year from 6 April. Royal Mail has changed prices in April in recent years, so check your postage settings.

### May

**Selling.** Summer clothing, garden furniture, barbecues, camping, festival gear, wedding guest outfits. Two bank holiday weekends are good for Facebook Marketplace collections.

**Sourcing.** Keep buying summer, but start watching for back-to-school and autumn items at car boots, where households clear winter clothing they no longer need.

**Jobs.** Photograph in good daylight while it lasts; see [photos on a phone](/guides/photos-on-a-phone).

### June

**Selling.** Summer peak for clothing, holiday items and outdoor sport. Father's Day (the third Sunday) moves tools, gadgets and men's accessories in the week or two before. Tournament years move football shirts and memorabilia.

**Sourcing.** Car boots are at their busiest. Buy autumn and winter clothing now, while sellers are clearing it and nobody else wants it. This is the cheapest time of year for coats and knitwear.

**Jobs.** Check storage space for the autumn build-up (see [storage and stock numbers](/guides/storage-and-stock-numbers)).

### July

**Selling.** Holiday and festival items continue. School leavers' and prom items early in the month. Uniform starts to be searched later in the month in Scotland.

**Sourcing.** Back-to-school stock: uniform in common colours, school shoes, bags, scientific calculators, lunch boxes. Keep buying winter clothing cheaply.

**Jobs.** The second [payment on account](https://www.gov.uk/understand-self-assessment-bill/payments-on-account) is due on 31 July if you make them. The [tax dates tool](/tools/tax) lists the rest.

### August

**Selling.** Back to school is the main event: uniform, shoes, bags, stationery, sports kit, student kitchen and bedding for university starters later in the month. Summer clothing starts to slow.

**Sourcing.** Autumn clothing, Halloween costumes and props, and the first Christmas stock (toys, games, gift sets) if you have space. Summer clothing can be bought cheaply now for next year if you can hold it.

**Jobs.** List autumn and winter stock at the end of the month, ahead of the first cold week.

### September

**Selling.** Back to school continues in England and Wales in the first week or so. Autumn coats, boots and knitwear start to move. University starters buy household basics.

**Sourcing.** Halloween stock must be in hand now. Buy Christmas stock: toys, games, gift sets, collectables, party wear. Stock bought after mid-November is late.

**Jobs.** Register for Self Assessment by 5 October if you started trading in the last tax year. Clear summer stock out of the way before the autumn build-up.

### October

**Selling.** Winter clothing, Halloween until the 31st then nothing, early Christmas shoppers. Buyers who plan ahead start looking for gifts.

**Sourcing.** Last main month for Christmas stock. Car boots begin to close for the season on many sites.

**Jobs.** Royal Mail changed parcel prices on 5 October 2026; update your postage. Check the Christmas last posting dates when they appear and plan your cut-off.

### November

**Selling.** The busiest month for gifts. Retail discounting around Black Friday pulls some buyers to new goods, so pre-owned items do best where they are clearly cheaper than the new price or where the item is no longer sold new. Party wear for December events.

**Sourcing.** Stop buying Christmas stock by the middle of the month unless it will sell in days. Buy January items (fitness, organisation) in retail discounting if the numbers work.

**Jobs.** Set dispatch times you can keep. Stock up on packaging before suppliers get busy (see [packaging cheaply](/guides/packaging-cheaply)).

### December

**Selling.** Gifts until the last posting dates, then local collection only. After Christmas, buyers with gift money and vouchers browse from Boxing Day.

**Sourcing.** Very little, except post-Christmas clearance from Boxing Day onwards.

**Jobs.** Put a clear last-posting note in your listings and shop pages a few days before Royal Mail's [last posting dates](https://www.royalmail.com/christmas/last-posting-dates) for the service you use, and switch to longer handling times or holiday settings if you are away. Start the January returns list.

## A season at a glance

| Season | List | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Winter (Dec to Feb) | Coats, boots, knitwear, gifts, fitness in January | Spring and summer items, post-Christmas clearance |
| Spring (Mar to May) | Lighter layers, garden, bikes, outdoor, Mothering Sunday and Easter | Summer clothing, holiday and festival items |
| Summer (Jun to Aug) | Summer clothing, holiday, outdoor sport, back to school | Autumn and winter clothing, Halloween, early Christmas |
| Autumn (Sep to Nov) | Coats, knitwear, Halloween, Christmas gifts | Christmas until mid-November, January items |

## Build your own calendar

The pattern above is general. Your categories, platforms and buyers will differ, and the only calendar worth trusting is one built from your own sales.

1. **Record the sale date and category for every sale.** The [stock tracker spreadsheet](/tools/downloads) records the date bought, date listed and date sold for each item; add a category column if you do not already start each item name with one.
2. **At the end of each month, count sales by category.** After a year you have twelve counts per category, and the seasonal shape is visible.
3. **Mark the buying date too.** The gap between buying and selling, by category and month, tells you how far ahead to source.
4. **Note the one-offs.** A cold snap, a television programme, a platform promotion. Without a note you will mistake a one-off for a season.

For comparison across the market, the sold search on eBay and the Research tab in Seller Hub show what sold and when; see [pricing from sold comps](/guides/pricing-from-sold-comps).

## What goes wrong

- **Sourcing in season.** You pay peak prices and list into the most crowded month.
- **Holding seasonal stock past its date.** A Halloween costume unsold on 1 November is a Halloween costume next October. Mark it down in the second half of October or plan to store it.
- **Ignoring posting dates.** An order that arrives after Christmas is often a return, and sometimes a claim. Stop promising pre-Christmas delivery a day or two before the official last posting date.
- **Assuming school dates.** Scotland, Northern Ireland, England and Wales run different terms, and councils differ.
- **Buying too much Christmas stock too late.** After mid-November, anything that has not sold by the posting dates waits a year.
- **No storage plan for off-season stock.** Buying winter coats in June only works if you have somewhere dry to keep them until October.
