This is for business sellers on eBay UK who want more feedback, fewer negatives, and a clear idea of what feedback does and does not do for the account. After reading you will know the current rules on asking for it, what eBay now leaves on your behalf, how to reply to a bad one, when a revision request is worth using, and which numbers to watch instead if what you actually want is Top Rated.

The short version: feedback matters to buyers and barely matters to eBay's seller evaluation. Most of the effort sellers put into chasing it would be better spent on dispatch times and tracking. Policies change; the figures below link to the page they come from, and you should check that page before relying on any of them.

## How feedback works now

A buyer can leave a positive, neutral or negative rating with a comment and up to five photos. They can also rate four aspects of the transaction with one to five stars: whether the item arrived on time, how accurate the description was, the postage cost, and your communication. Those star ratings are the detailed seller ratings, and they show as averages on your feedback profile. Buyers see the percentage positive figure and the count, and a lot of them read the last few comments before they buy. That is the whole audience for feedback: other buyers.

Your feedback score is one number for the account, not per listing, and it never resets. The [feedback profile](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/account/changing-account-settings/feedback-profiles?id=4204) shows the overall score and the percentage positive; the percentage is a recent measure and eBay does not publish the exact calculation, so do not quote a formula to a buyer.

Three things have changed in the last couple of years that older advice does not reflect.

**eBay leaves positive feedback for you.** Since spring 2025, on any order sent with a tracked service that arrives on time and has no reported problem, eBay adds a positive rating seven days after the delivery scan if the buyer has not already left one. The comment reads as an automated note that the order completed successfully, tracked and on time. It counts towards both your score and your percentage. If the buyer later leaves their own feedback, positive or negative, it replaces the automated one, and if they open a return, a not received claim or a cancellation, it does not appear. The details are on [eBay's feedback update](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/news/2025-april/feedback-update). In practice this has quietly doubled the feedback rate for sellers who post everything tracked, and it has made untracked postage a feedback decision as well as a claims decision.

**Buyers cannot downgrade a positive.** Once a buyer has left positive feedback they cannot change it to neutral or negative. They can add one follow-up comment. So a positive is safe once it lands.

**Sellers can only leave positive feedback for buyers.** That has been the case for years, but it still surprises people. You have 90 days from the transaction to leave it ([leaving feedback for buyers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/leaving-feedback-buyers/leaving-feedback-buyers?id=4078)). Feedback for buyers is a courtesy, not a lever, and there is no way to rate a bad buyer through it. A buyer who behaves badly is reported through the order instead; if eBay agrees the behaviour was abusive, its [seller protections](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-protections?id=4345) say it removes the related negative or neutral feedback and any defects.

Feedback does have one direct effect on the account that is worth knowing: eBay's [selling limits](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/selling-limits?id=4107) page says monthly limit reviews take into account your sales volume and the feedback you have received. For a newer account, a healthy run of positives is part of how the limit goes up.

## What you can and cannot do to ask for it

The rules live in eBay's [feedback policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/feedback-policies/feedback-policy?id=4208), which links to the feedback manipulation and feedback extortion policies. The principle is simple: feedback must reflect the buyer's real experience, and you must not buy, trade, pressure or engineer it. What that means in practice:

**Allowed.**

- A polite, once-only message after delivery saying you hope they are happy with the item and that feedback is appreciated, with a clear invitation to contact you first if anything is wrong.
- A packing slip or card in the parcel that says the same.
- Leaving feedback for the buyer promptly. Many buyers reciprocate; eBay's [automatic feedback settings](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/leaving-feedback-buyers/automatic-feedback-settings?id=4101) let you leave it on payment, on dispatch or when the buyer leaves positive feedback for you.
- Solving the problem before the buyer reaches the feedback page. This is the only reliable way to prevent a negative.

**Not allowed.**

- Offering a discount, a refund, a free item, a voucher or anything else in exchange for positive feedback, or for changing feedback.
- Making a refund or a replacement conditional on feedback being revised.
- Terms in a listing that restrict or discourage feedback ("negative feedback will be reported", "contact me before leaving feedback or I will not refund").
- Referring to your feedback score in a listing title or description to draw buyers.
- Buying from yourself, swapping feedback with other accounts, or running sales solely to build a score. The [multiple accounts policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/identity-policies/multiple-accounts-policy?id=4232) links all your accounts and you cannot interact between them.
- Any message that reads as pressure. Repeated reminders, "please leave five stars", or anything mentioning what a negative would do to you.

The consequences run from removal of the feedback to warnings, restrictions and suspension. eBay reads messages when it investigates, so write every one as if it will be read out to a stranger.

## Our recommendation on asking

Ask once, ask late, and make the message about the buyer, not about you. The message that works is short: the item was sent tracked on this date, here is how to reach you if anything is not right, and a single line that feedback helps a small business. Send it a couple of days after delivery, not on dispatch, because the automated positive lands at seven days and a buyer who has just had a good delivery experience is the one you want writing.

Do not send it to buyers who have opened a return, a case or a message about a problem. Fix the problem, then say nothing about feedback. A buyer who has been well looked after leaves a positive on their own more often than one who has been asked.

Sellers we know who post everything tracked and ask nobody get feedback on roughly the same share of orders as sellers who ask everybody, because the automated feedback does the work. The asking mainly changes how many comments are written by people rather than by eBay, and written comments are what other buyers read. That is worth something, but it is not worth a policy risk.

## Responding to negative feedback

You get one reply per comment and it cannot be edited or removed, so write it once and write it cold. The audience is the next hundred buyers who read it, not the person who left it. A reply that argues loses the argument for everyone reading.

The shape that works:

1. Acknowledge the specific problem in plain words.
2. Say what you did about it (refund, replacement, return accepted).
3. Say, if true, that the buyer is welcome to contact you.

Nothing about the buyer's behaviour, nothing about how unfair it is, no capitals. If the buyer has already been refunded in full, say so; that is the single most reassuring thing a future buyer can read under a negative. If the feedback is about something eBay controls, such as a courier delay that tracking shows was not yours, say that briefly and then request removal rather than debate it in public.

Wait a day before replying. Then reply to every negative and neutral, because an unanswered negative reads as a seller who does not care, and a calm answer reads as a seller who does.

## Revision and removal: which one, and when

There are two separate routes and sellers mix them up.

**Feedback revision** asks the buyer to change their rating. You can request it on feedback under 30 days old, you get five requests a calendar year (plus five more for every 1,000 ratings you receive), and the buyer has 10 days to accept or decline. Full rules on [viewing and responding to feedback](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/leaving-feedback-buyers/viewing-responding-feedback-buyers?id=4102). Use it only after you have already resolved the problem and the buyer has told you they are happy. Sending it cold wastes one of five, and sending it with any hint that the refund depends on it is extortion under eBay's policy. The message you attach should say the problem was resolved on such a date and, if they feel the resolution deserves it, they can update the rating; no pressure either way.

**Feedback removal** asks eBay to take the feedback down because it breaks the [feedback policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/feedback-policies/feedback-policy?id=4208). You have 90 days from the transaction and you request it through Seller Help. eBay removes feedback that, among other grounds:

- Was left after the buyer asked to cancel or did not pay.
- Complains about something clearly disclosed in the listing.
- Is a change of mind return complaint where returns were not offered.
- Complains about late delivery when tracking shows it was sent on time and delivered by the estimate, or the delay was outside your control.
- Complains about a return you handled through eBay's process.
- Contains threats, personal information, profanity, links, or content unrelated to the transaction.
- Has a negative comment on a positive rating, or the reverse.
- Was used to extort: "refund me or I leave a negative" in a message is a removal ground, so keep the message.

Removal is free, unlimited and does not need the buyer's agreement. Try it first on anything that plausibly fits a ground. Keep the request short: the order number, the ground, and the one fact that proves it (a tracking number, the listing text, the message). If it is refused and the buyer is reasonable, then spend a revision request.

## What actually affects Top Rated Seller status

Here is the part most sellers get wrong. Your feedback score, your percentage and your detailed seller ratings are not inputs to eBay's seller level. The [seller standards policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-standards-policy?id=4347) evaluates you on the 20th of each month on three things:

| Measure | Top Rated | Minimum (Above Standard) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transaction defect rate | 0.5% or less, from no more than 3 buyers | 2% or less, from more than 4 buyers before you are Below Standard |
| Cases closed without seller resolution | 2 or fewer, or 0.3% | 2 or fewer, or 0.3% |
| Late delivery rate | 5 or fewer, or 3% | No Top Rated style limit, but late delivery feeds defects and buyer claims |

A defect is an order you cancelled because you could not fulfil it, or a buyer problem you did not resolve. A late delivery, with tracking, is an item whose delivery scan is after the latest estimated date and which had no carrier scan within your dispatch time; without tracking, eBay asks the buyer at feedback time whether it arrived on time. If you had more than 400 transactions in the last three months you are judged on those; otherwise on the last 12 months.

For Top Rated you also need to be a business seller, have an account at least 90 days old, and have at least 100 transactions and £1,000 of sales in the last 12 months. Top Rated on its own gives you enhanced protections and eligibility for fee discounts. The discount itself, 10% off final value fees on eBay.co.uk, comes from listing with eBay Premium Service, which needs a free domestic delivery option within 3 working days, an express option within 2 working days for no more than £10, 30 day returns, and tracked services with tracking uploaded within the dispatch time on items over £20. The requirements are on the [Seller Centre seller standards page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/protection/seller-standards).

Notice what is missing. A negative comment does not change your level unless the order behind it also became an unresolved case or a cancellation. A run of neutrals costs you buyers, not status. The things that do cost you status are: cancelling orders because you oversold, ignoring a return request until eBay steps in, and dispatch scans after your stated handling time.

So the priority order for a seller who wants Top Rated is:

1. Tracked postage on everything, scanned within your handling time. This also feeds the automated feedback.
2. Never let a case be decided by eBay. Refund or accept the return yourself, every time, even when you are right.
3. Stock control so you never cancel.
4. Then, and only then, feedback.

Below Standard is the other end of the same scale and it is expensive: the [business seller fees page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-business-sellers?id=4809) sets out an additional 6 percentage points on final value fees, rising to 7 after four consecutive months, plus lower search placement, a block on Promoted Listings, and possible holds and limits.

## Detailed seller ratings in practice

The four stars matter less than they did. They are shown on your profile and buyers glance at them, but they are not part of the seller level evaluation and eBay no longer publishes thresholds for them. Two of the four are entirely in your hands: postage cost (free postage removes the complaint) and dispatch time (post within your handling time). Communication is mostly about answering messages the same day. Item description is the one that bites: every "not as described" in a rating is a warning that the listing over promised, and the same problem will show up in returns. Treat a low description star as a listing fault, not a buyer fault, and reread the listing.

## What goes wrong

- **Chasing feedback with reminders.** Two messages is a pattern; three is a complaint. One message, once.
- **Refund "if you change the feedback".** This is extortion under eBay's policy even when you mean it kindly. Refund first, unconditionally, then ask.
- **Replying angry.** The reply is permanent. Draft it, sleep on it, cut it in half.
- **Spending revision requests before removal.** Removal is free. Revision requests run out.
- **Untracked postage.** No automated positive, no defence against a late delivery rating, no defence in a not received case.
- **Treating feedback as account health.** The seller dashboard is account health. Feedback is marketing.
- **Feedback in listing text.** "100% positive feedback, buy with confidence" in a description breaches the manipulation policy and can get the listing pulled.
- **Ignoring the neutral.** A neutral with a complaint reads like a negative to buyers. Reply to it.

## Questions

**Can I leave a buyer negative feedback?** No. You can report an abusive buyer through the order and block them from buying again; feedback for buyers is positive only, and a successful abusive buyer report removes their feedback for you.

**Does a negative from a buyer who never paid stay?** Request removal; feedback after non payment or a buyer cancellation is a removal ground.

**Will one negative stop me being Top Rated?** Not by itself. Check the seller dashboard: if the order behind it is not a defect or an unresolved case, your level is unaffected.

**Where do I ask when a case is unclear?** Post the order details, with names removed, in [Buyers and disputes](/community/c/ebay-buyers-and-disputes) or [Account health and policy](/community/c/ebay-account-health-and-policy).
