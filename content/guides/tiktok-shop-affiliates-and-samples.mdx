This is for UK TikTok Shop sellers who want creators to sell for them, and for resellers who post their own content and wonder whether it needs an ad label. After reading it you will know how the affiliate system works, how to set a commission rate from your margin rather than from hope, how to run free samples without bleeding stock, and what the Advertising Standards Authority expects on disclosure. Rules link to TikTok's Seller Academy and the ASA. Both change their guidance, so check the page before relying on it.

## How affiliates work

A creator adds your product to their showcase, then sells it in a short video or a LIVE. The buyer checks out on TikTok Shop, you ship, and after delivery the creator's commission comes out of your settlement as a line called "Affiliate commission" ([Finance Report Guide](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=7753826522744578)). You are still the seller: you handle the order, the returns and the customer service.

There are two ways to offer products to creators ([Affiliate Collaborations](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=7753824408749825&lang=en-GB)).

| | Open collaboration | Target collaboration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who can promote | Any creator who finds it in the affiliate marketplace | Creators you invite |
| Commission | One rate for everyone | A rate per invitation |
| Control | Low; you can approve or decline sample requests | High; you choose who and for how long |
| Best for | Products with plenty of stock and a margin that survives any creator | Limited stock, or one or two creators whose audience fits |

Open collaboration also has to be switched on before creators can request free samples from it ([Free Sample User Guide](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6837861609473794&default_language=en-GB)).

### Is it worth it for a reseller

Affiliates suit stock you can repeat: multiple units of the same new item, a line you can buy again, a clearance lot of fifty. They rarely suit one-offs. A creator will not make a video for a single used jacket, and if they do, the jacket sells once and the video keeps sending viewers to a product that is gone. TikTok's [content policy](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=8913678280345345) treats content showing a product that is no longer available as a breach.

Our recommendation: use affiliates for your repeatable lines and sell one-offs yourself, in your own videos and LIVEs ([first LIVE guide](/guides/tiktok-shop-first-live)).

## Setting the commission rate

TikTok leaves the rate to you and shows suggested percentages. Work backwards from your numbers, not from the suggestion.

1. Start with the selling price and the shipping the buyer pays.
2. Take off TikTok's 9% commission, which is charged on price plus shipping ([commission policy](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=3337893683398432&lang=en-GB)).
3. Take off your stock cost, your postage cost and packaging.
4. Take off an allowance for returns.
5. What is left is your profit before the creator. Decide how much of it you will give up for a sale you would not otherwise have made.

The affiliate commission is calculated on the product price after discounts ([seller fees](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=7753826522154754&lang=en-GB)), so on the same sale you pay TikTok on price plus shipping and the creator on the price. Both come out before you see a penny.

To see what a higher affiliate rate costs you across a month, put your affiliate-driven sales (product price only) into the calculator.

A round example. An item sells for £20 with £3 buyer-paid shipping. TikTok's 9% on £23 is £2.07. A 15% affiliate rate on £20 is £3. If the item cost you £8 and postage and packaging are £3.50, you keep £3.43 before returns. At 25% affiliate you keep £1.43. The margin has to be there before you raise the rate.

Rules we suggest:

- **Never set a rate you would not accept on every sale.** On open collaboration, any creator can take it.
- **Use target collaboration for a higher rate.** Offer the higher rate only to the creator whose audience matches, and for a fixed period.
- **Change rates deliberately.** TikTok lets you edit rates on existing collaborations ([Affiliate Collaborations](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=7753824408749825&lang=en-GB)), but creators who made content at one rate and see it cut stop promoting you.
- **Check the statement.** The affiliate line on each order should match the rate you set. If it does not, raise it with support.

## Free samples

Samples are the currency of TikTok affiliates. A creator requests one, you approve and ship it, and they are expected to post.

TikTok's [Samples: Seller's Guide](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=2490998925051680&lang=en-GB) says the seller pays for the product, the shipping and any tax, and that the creator fulfils the sample by posting a short video and keeping it live for at least 3 days, or hosting a LIVE that drives at least 1 sale, within 14 days of delivery. If not, the sample is marked as failed. You do not get the item back.

### When samples pay back

A sample costs you the item, the postage and your time. It pays back only if the creator sells enough to cover it. Before approving, look at what TikTok shows you in the review tab: the creator's follower count, their fulfilment rate on past samples and their sales in your category. A creator with a large following and a poor fulfilment rate is a worse bet than a small one who posts every time.

A working rule: approve a sample only if the creator's recent sales in your category suggest they will sell at least enough units, at your commission, to cover the item and postage. Cap the number of samples each creator can request, which the sample settings let you do.

### Samples and your balance

TikTok's [Negative Balance page](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=5286902741042976) lists excessive creator samples as one of the common causes of a shop's balance going below zero. A negative balance brings restrictions and slower payment. Treat samples as marketing spend with a monthly budget, and stop when the budget is gone.

### Samples you cannot recover

Sellers we know have learned three things the hard way.

- **Do not sample one-offs.** You have given away the only unit.
- **Do not sample stock you cannot reorder.** If the video works, you have nothing to sell.
- **Do not sample high-value items to unknown creators.** The failed sample costs you the full item.

## The #ad rules

UK advertising rules apply to TikTok content like any other. The CAP Code, which the ASA enforces, says marketing communications must be obviously identifiable as such ([CAP Code rule 2.1](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/02.html)) and that marketers must make their commercial intent clear if it is not obvious from the context (rule 2.3).

### For creators

The ASA's [guidance on social media and influencer marketing](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recognising-ads-social-media.html) (last updated 6 August 2026) says a post needs an ad label when the brand pays or gives an incentive, controls the content, or when the creator earns commission through an affiliate link or code. A TikTok Shop affiliate video is all of these: the creator earns commission on each sale through the product link. It is an ad.

What the ASA accepts and does not accept:

| Labels the ASA accepts | Labels the ASA says are not clear enough |
| --- | --- |
| Ad, Advert, Advertising, Ad feature, Advertisement feature | Sponsored, Supported by, Funded by, In association with, #sp, #spon, aff, affiliate, #BrandAmbassador, a discount code alone |

The label must be upfront, visible before someone engages, and not buried in a pile of hashtags or behind "see more". A statement in the bio does not cover individual posts. Platform tools such as a paid partnership label may need an "Ad" label alongside ([ASA guidance](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recognising-ads-social-media.html)). On affiliate content specifically, the ASA has said "aff" on its own is not enough, because people are unlikely to understand it ([affiliate marketing rules](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/get-yourself-affiliated-with-the-rules-on-affiliate-marketing.html)).

Free samples count too. A gifted product that the creator is expected to post about, and that they then sell with an affiliate link, is advertising. The ASA's full [influencers' guide](https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/influencers-guide.html) has worked examples.

TikTok has its own layer. Its [Branded Content Policy](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/bc-policy/en) requires creators to turn on the commercial content disclosure setting when posting sponsored material, and TikTok Shop's [content policy](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=8913678280345345) says the commercial nature of e-commerce content must be obviously identifiable. The TikTok toggle and a clear "Ad" in the caption or on screen together are the safe approach.

### For sellers

The ASA says brands and creators are jointly responsible for ads, and both can be named in a ruling ([ASA guidance](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recognising-ads-social-media.html)). On affiliate content it has said this applies even where the brand did not control the content ([affiliate marketing rules](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/get-yourself-affiliated-with-the-rules-on-affiliate-marketing.html)). If a creator sells your product without a label, you share the problem.

What to do:

- Put a line in every target collaboration invitation asking the creator to label the content as an ad and switch on TikTok's disclosure setting.
- Check the first video from each new creator. If it is not labelled, ask them to fix it and stop working with creators who refuse.
- Your own content selling your own shop's products is marketing too, and CAP Code rule 2.3 says the commercial intent must be clear if it is not obvious from the context. On your own shop account selling your stock that is usually obvious from context, but a video that looks like a personal review and hides that you are the seller is not.

Creators must also stay within the claims rules. TikTok's [content policy](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=8913678280345345) requires claims to be genuine, accurate and verifiable. If a creator says your item does something it does not, the returns land with you.

## What goes wrong

- **Setting a rate from the suggestion, not the margin.** Every creator sale loses money.
- **Forgetting TikTok's commission includes shipping.** The two commissions together are bigger than they look.
- **Sampling one-offs.** The stock and the video are both wasted.
- **Approving every sample request.** Samples become the largest cost on the statement, and the balance goes negative.
- **Cutting rates suddenly.** Creators drop your product mid-campaign.
- **No ad label.** Creator and seller both at risk under the CAP Code, and the video may be held back by TikTok.
- **Letting creators make claims you cannot support.** The returns, complaints and violations are yours.
