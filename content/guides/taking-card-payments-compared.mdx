This is for resellers who take payment outside a marketplace checkout: on their own web shop, through social media or messages, or when a buyer collects in person. After reading it you will know the main types of payment method, how their costs are built, what protection each gives the buyer and you, and which to use in each situation. Where you already use a platform with published fees (Shopify Payments, PayPal), the figures link to its official page; for other providers, the guide describes the type and tells you what to check. Fees change often. None of this is financial advice.

## The types at a glance

| Type | How it works | Typical cost structure | Buyer protection | Seller risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Web shop card processor | Card payment inside your shop's checkout | Percentage plus a fixed amount per payment | Card chargeback rights | Chargebacks and their fees |
| Digital wallet (PayPal and similar) | Buyer pays from their wallet account, or by card through it | Percentage plus a fixed amount; extra for international | The wallet's own buyer protection plus card rights | Disputes decided by the wallet; seller protection only if you meet its rules |
| Payment link or invoice | You send a link; the buyer pays by card or wallet | As for the processor behind it | As for the method used | As for the method used |
| Card reader at collection | Buyer taps or inserts a card in person | Percentage, sometimes plus a fixed amount; the reader costs money to buy | Card chargeback rights | Low for items handed over in person, but not nil |
| QR code payment in person | Buyer scans a code and pays from their wallet | Percentage plus a fixed amount | The wallet's rules | As for the wallet |
| Bank transfer | Buyer sends money from their bank to yours | Usually nothing for personal accounts; business accounts vary | Little for a genuine sale that goes wrong; scam reimbursement rules for fraud | No chargebacks, but fake "payment sent" proof is common |
| Cash | Notes in hand | Nothing, apart from the trip to the bank | None beyond the law on the sale | Counterfeit notes; security |

## Card processors on your own site

If you run a web shop, the processor is usually built into the checkout. On Shopify, the built-in option is Shopify Payments. Its online card rate on the Basic plan is 2% + 25p for standard cards and 3.1% + 25p for American Express and international cards; the higher plans pay less ([Shopify UK pricing](https://www.shopify.com/uk/pricing)). If you use a different provider on Shopify instead, Shopify adds its own transaction fee on top of that provider's charge: 2% on Basic ([Shopify UK pricing](https://www.shopify.com/uk/pricing)).

For any other processor, the cost is built the same way: a percentage, a fixed amount per payment, and often a higher percentage for premium, business and non-UK cards. Look for these on the provider's own pricing page before you sign up:

- The domestic card rate and the fixed fee.
- The rate for international and business cards.
- The chargeback fee, and whether it is returned if you win.
- How long before money reaches your bank.
- Whether funds can be held back, and when.

**Our recommendation.** Use the processor built into your shop platform unless you have a strong reason not to. Switching to an outside provider on a hosted platform often adds a platform fee on top, and a single set of reports is easier to reconcile.

## Digital wallets: PayPal

Many buyers prefer to pay with PayPal because they trust its dispute process, and many resellers already have an account. PayPal's UK standard rate for receiving a domestic payment for goods is 2.9% plus a fixed fee of £0.30, with a surcharge of 1.29% for buyers in the EEA and 1.99% for other international buyers ([PayPal business fees](https://www.paypal.com/uk/business/paypal-business-fees)). A card chargeback costs £14 on the same page. That page was last updated on 15 July 2026 when we checked it.

**Goods and services, not friends and family.** PayPal does not cover personal ("friends and family") payments under its Buyer Protection ([PayPal Help](https://www.paypal.com/uk/cshelp/article/whats-the-difference-between-friends-and-family-or-goods-and-services-payments-help277)). A buyer who offers to pay that way "to save the fee" is giving up their protection, and a seller who asks for it is asking the buyer to trust them blindly. Always take payment for goods as a goods and services payment, and budget for the fee.

**Seller protection has rules.** PayPal Seller Protection covers eligible transactions against item-not-received claims and unauthorised payments, but not claims that the item was significantly not as described. For posted items you must send to the address on the Transaction Details page, provide valid proof of postage or delivery, and answer PayPal's requests for documents in time ([PayPal Help](https://www.paypal.com/uk/cshelp/article/what-is-paypal-seller-protection-help156)). If a buyer asks you to post to a different address from the one on the payment, do not; ask them to pay again with the right address.

PayPal's help page does not say how items handed over in person are treated. The safest assumption is that seller protection is built around proof of postage, and that a collected item gives you less to show.

## Card readers and QR codes at collection

For buyers collecting in person (bulky items, local sales, fairs), a card reader turns "I don't carry cash" into a sale. The costs are the reader itself, a percentage per payment, and sometimes a fixed fee.

If you already use Shopify, its in-person card rate on the Basic plan is 1.7% with no fixed fee ([Shopify UK pricing](https://www.shopify.com/uk/pricing)); the reader is bought separately. If you already use PayPal, its QR code payments are charged at 1.5% plus £0.10 on payments over £10, and 2% plus £0.05 on payments of £10 or less ([PayPal business fees](https://www.paypal.com/uk/business/paypal-business-fees)), and need no hardware at all: the buyer scans a code on your phone.

For standalone readers from other providers, compare:

- The price of the reader, and whether there is a monthly fee.
- The rate per tap, and any fixed fee.
- How fast payouts arrive, and whether the provider can hold funds.
- Whether it works on your phone's signal where you trade.

**Our recommendation.** If you do occasional collections, use the in-person option of a platform you already have (a QR code or your shop platform's reader) rather than adding another provider. If collections are a regular part of your business, a reader with no monthly fee and a low per-tap rate pays for itself quickly.

A card payment in person is safer than a posted sale, but the buyer can still dispute it with their card issuer. Keep a record of what was sold and when it was handed over, and a note or photo of the item as it left.

## Bank transfer

Bank transfer is free or nearly free and cannot be charged back like a card payment. That is both its appeal and its danger.

**For the buyer, it is the least protected way to pay a stranger.** The scam reimbursement rules for Faster Payments and CHAPS cover individuals and small businesses who are tricked into paying a fraudster, up to £85,000 per claim, reported within 13 months. They do not cover civil disputes, where a genuine seller supplies something faulty or does not deliver without intent to defraud ([Payment Systems Regulator](https://www.psr.org.uk/information-for-consumers/app-fraud-reimbursement-protections/)). A buyer who pays you by transfer and is unhappy has only the law on the sale, and you.

**For the seller, the risk is fake proof.** The commonest scam aimed at resellers is a buyer who sends a screenshot of a "payment sent" screen, or claims a transfer is "pending", and asks you to post or hand over the item. A screenshot is not money.

Rules for taking bank transfer safely:

1. Only release goods when the money shows as received in your own banking app. Not an email, not a screenshot, not a text.
2. Be wary of "overpayments" and requests to refund the difference.
3. Give your account details only in response to a real sale, and expect the buyer's bank to show a name check result for your account; a mismatch warning makes honest buyers nervous, so use an account in the name you trade under.
4. For collected items, ask the buyer to transfer while they are with you and wait for it to arrive before handing over.

**Our recommendation.** Bank transfer is fine for repeat customers and local collections where you watch the money arrive. For a first-time buyer on a posted order, use a method that gives them protection. It costs a little, and it removes the reason for a careful buyer to walk away.

## Buyer protection: what your buyers have

Understanding the buyer's rights explains why disputes happen and what they will cost.

| Buyer paid by | Their main protection |
| --- | --- |
| Debit or credit card | Chargeback through their card issuer, for fraud, non-delivery or goods not as described |
| Credit card, item over £100 and up to £30,000 | Also section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act: the card company is jointly liable with you for breach of contract or misrepresentation ([Consumer Credit Act 1974, section 75](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1974/39/section/75)) |
| PayPal goods and services | PayPal Buyer Protection, plus card rights if they funded it by card |
| PayPal friends and family | Not covered by PayPal Buyer Protection |
| Bank transfer | Scam reimbursement if defrauded; not for civil disputes |
| Cash | None beyond the law |

If you sell as a business to consumers, the buyer also has their legal rights on the sale itself, whatever the payment method: the right to cancel most distance purchases within 14 days and rights when goods are faulty. See [what a UK web shop legally needs](/guides/web-shop-legal-pages-uk).

## Seller protection: what you can do

You cannot stop a buyer opening a dispute. You can make sure you win the fair ones.

- **Describe and photograph honestly**, flaws included. "Not as described" is the dispute sellers lose most, and no payment method protects you from it.
- **Post with tracking to the address on the payment.** For higher-value items, use a service with a signature.
- **Keep records**: the listing, messages, proof of postage, delivery confirmation, and for collections a note of the handover.
- **Answer disputes quickly and completely.** Every provider sets a deadline; missing it loses the case. Shopify says responses are usually due within 7 to 21 days, and its £10 chargeback fee is returned if you win ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/chargebacks/chargeback-process)).
- **Refund when you are wrong.** A prompt refund costs less than a lost chargeback plus its fee.

## Which to use when

| Situation | Use | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your own web shop | The processor built into the shop, plus PayPal as a second option | Buyers expect cards; many prefer PayPal; one set of reports |
| Sale agreed in messages, posted | A payment link or PayPal goods and services | Buyer protection makes them willing; you get seller protection if you follow the rules |
| Local collection, occasional | QR code or the in-person option of a platform you already use, or cash | No new provider; money confirmed before handover |
| Local collection, regular | A card reader with no monthly fee | Low cost per payment, faster than cash |
| Repeat trade customer | Bank transfer | Cheap and quick once trust is established |
| First-time buyer, high value, posted | Card or PayPal goods and services, tracked and signed for | Protection on both sides |

## What goes wrong

- **Releasing goods on a screenshot.** Wait until the money is in your account.
- **Accepting friends and family payments for goods.** The buyer loses protection, and you look like someone avoiding it.
- **Posting to an address that is not on the payment.** It breaks PayPal's seller protection rules.
- **Choosing a processor on the headline rate alone.** The international rate, chargeback fee and payout timing matter as much.
- **Adding an outside processor to a hosted shop without checking the platform fee.** On Shopify Basic that is another 2%.
- **Ignoring a dispute notice.** Deadlines are short and missing one loses the case.
- **Mixing personal and business money.** Use one account for the business so disputes, refunds and tax records are easy to follow. See [bookkeeping and tax for resellers](/guides/bookkeeping-and-tax-for-resellers).
