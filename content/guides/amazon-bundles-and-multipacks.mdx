For Amazon sellers who have cheap items that barely cover their fees on their own, or who have found a multipack on the shelf and want to know which listing it belongs on. After reading you will know which kind of pack you are holding, what Amazon's UK policy pages say about it, how to match or create the listing without breaking a rule, and how to work out whether putting items together actually makes more money than selling them singly.

Every rule here links to the Amazon page it comes from. Bundling is an area where Amazon's rules are moving, so read the linked page before you act, and read the section on 2027 before you build anything new.

## Bundle, multipack or set

Amazon uses these words loosely, and so do sellers. The distinctions below are the ones that change what you are allowed to do.

| What you have | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Multipack | Several identical items packed together, or a large quantity of one item | Six tins of the same soup in a shrink wrapped tray |
| Variety multipack | Several different items of one product line packed together | A box of assorted flavour crisps from one maker |
| Bundle | Two or more different products sold as one unit under one ASIN | A game console with a second controller |
| Set | Amazon's warehouse word for any unit made of several pieces that must not be separated | Any of the above, once it is inside FBA |

Amazon's [multipack imaging standards](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G94GSFMC79RSLDBY?locale=en-GB) define the first two. Its [packaging requirements](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200141500?locale=en-GB) treat any listing with multiple products under one ASIN as a bundle, and any bundle or multipack as a set for prep.

The question that matters more than the label is **who made the pack**. A pack the manufacturer made, with its own barcode printed on the outer packaging, is a product like any other. A pack you made is a new product, and the rules below are almost all about packs sellers make themselves.

## What the UK rules say now

### Multipacks

Amazon's [ASIN creation policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201844590?locale=en-GB) is the clearest UK statement on multipacks. It says:

- A multipack that the manufacturer does not sell must be matched to an identical multipack detail page. If there is no identical page, it needs a new detail page with its own unique barcode.
- You must not add a multipack you made as a variation (a child) of an existing product family. Amazon's own example is bundling two three packs to create a six pack. Multipack children must be packaged by the manufacturer, and a customer who wants two can change the quantity at checkout.

So the single listing's variation drop down (1 pack, 2 pack, 4 pack) is reserved for packs the maker sells. Adding your own pack to that drop down is a listing violation, even if the pack is perfectly honest.

### Bundles

The UK [product detail page rules](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200390640?locale=en-GB) require one detail page per unique item, no duplicate pages, a new ASIN when you sell under a different brand, and barcodes that you own or are authorised to use, bought from GS1 or with the prefix owner's permission. The same page says a generic listing must not carry a brand's intellectual property or display trade marks.

That last rule is where most home made bundles go wrong. The route Amazon offers for a bundle without a barcode is a GTIN exemption, and its [GTIN exemption page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GZAY2L8NRY53DXD9?locale=en-GB) lists "a bundled pack of more than one product" as a valid reason and tells you to enter the brand as Generic for bundles. A bundle listed as Generic, whose title and images show two well known brands, is a generic listing carrying other people's trade marks. You can follow one Amazon page to the letter and break another.

### Who can create the listing at all

The same ASIN creation policy says Amazon limits new ASIN creation by sellers who do not have a selling role for a brand enrolled in Brand Registry. Most brands a reseller buys are enrolled. In practice this means a new detail page containing a registered brand's product is often blocked before you reach the pricing screen, whatever the bundle rules say.

### Fair pricing

The [fair pricing policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G5TUVJKZHUVMN77V?locale=en-GB) names selling multiple units for more per unit than a single unit of the same product as a practice that harms customer trust. Amazon can remove the Buy Box, remove the offer, or in serious or repeated cases suspend selling privileges. Before you price any multipack, divide by the pack count and compare with the cheapest current single.

### Virtual bundles

Amazon's [virtual product bundles](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G87HAE6PMKKM23Z7?locale=en-GB) page says the tool works in the UK store, groups two to five ASINs onto one detail page, and ships the components separately with no repackaging and no extra fee. It is only open to sellers with a Brand Representative or Authorised Reseller role in Brand Registry, the ASINs must have new FBA stock, and each component is charged its own fees. For most arbitrage sellers it is not available, and where it is, it does nothing for the fee problem described below.

## The January 2027 change: what is known

Sellers and several third party sites are reporting that Amazon will require most physical bundles listed under one ASIN to be packaged by the original manufacturer or brand from January 2027, with exceptions for gift baskets, cameras, virtual bundles and bundles backed by a letter of authorisation from every brand involved.

We could not confirm that on an Amazon UK page we can link. Here is what we did find:

- The [Amazon US bundling policy page](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G200442350?locale=en-US), which the UK packaging page links to, now describes that manufacturer packaged model. It does not give a date, and it is a US page.
- Amazon's October 2024 [Seller Forums announcement](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/44a087bd-f831-4635-9c46-21a153705af9) that tightened bundling for grocery, pet, baby, and health and beauty was stated to apply to the US store.
- The UK help pages we could read (ASIN creation, detail page rules, GTIN exemption, packaging) do not mention a 2027 date.

So treat it as likely but unconfirmed for the UK. Our recommendation: do not build new home made bundles for FBA now. Stock sent in during Q4 that becomes non compliant in January would need a removal order, and removal fees plus lost stock turn a small margin into a loss. If you already hold bundle listings, keep the stock low until the UK position is clear.

Where to watch: Seller Central News on your home page, the Seller Forums announcements channel, the packaging requirements page linked above (it links to the bundling policy), and any Account Health notice naming a bundle ASIN. When Amazon confirms it for the UK, the notice will come with a date and a list of affected listings.

## Matching an existing multipack

This is the version of bundling that works for resellers: you buy a pack the manufacturer or retailer made and sell it on the listing that already exists for it.

1. **Scan the outer barcode.** The barcode on the tray, sleeve or box, not on the item inside. Search Add products by that number. If it returns an ASIN, that is your listing.
2. **Check the count against the title and main image.** Amazon's imaging standards require the main image to show the total quantity delivered and the title to state it ("pack of 6" or "6 count"). If the title says 6 and the image shows one tin with a "6" sticker, read the detail page carefully; a listing that confuses buyers will confuse them about your stock too. [Listing accuracy](/guides/amazon-product-safety-and-listing-accuracy) covers the full check.
3. **No outer barcode, no match.** If the pack is a paper band round six singles with no barcode of its own, you have six singles. Sell them on the single listing, or leave them.
4. **Compare per unit prices.** The fair pricing rule applies to your offer, not to whoever created the page.
5. **Check approval.** A multipack of a gated brand is still gated. See [ungating](/guides/amazon-ungating-uk).

### Prep for multipacks and sets

The [packaging requirements](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200141500?locale=en-GB) say a set must be sealed so it cannot be separated, labelled clearly as a set (Amazon's examples are "Sold as set" and "This is a set. Do not separate"), and have the inner barcodes turned inwards or covered, so the warehouse scans the pack and not a single. A retailer multipack held by a cardboard sleeve usually needs taping or bagging. The practical how-to is in [prep equipment and prep centres](/guides/amazon-prep-equipment-and-prep-centres#sets-and-bundles). Get it wrong and a pack of six can be received as six singles on a different ASIN.

## Creating a bundle listing, if you still decide to

If you have a product where a bundle is genuinely allowed (for example your own brand, or a letter of authorisation from every brand in it), these are the fields Amazon's pages set rules for.

| Field | Rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barcode | A GS1 barcode you own or are authorised to use, or a GTIN exemption; never the barcode of one item inside | [Detail page rules](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200390640?locale=en-GB) |
| Brand | Generic when using a bundle GTIN exemption, which means no brand names or logos in title, bullets or images | [GTIN exemption](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GZAY2L8NRY53DXD9?locale=en-GB) |
| Title | 75 characters maximum including spaces, no word more than twice, total quantity stated for packs | [Title requirements](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GYTR6SYGFA5E3EQC?locale=en-GB) |
| Main image | Pure white background, product filling about 85% of the frame, only what is included, multipacks shown flat without a model | [Image requirements](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G1881?locale=en-GB) |
| Contents | Everything in the box described; nothing that is not | [Detail page rules](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200390640?locale=en-GB) |

The GTIN exemption page says approval comes by email within 48 hours, that you need two to nine real photographs of the product and packaging, and that a restricted category still needs category approval on top. Also note from the detail page rules that a product ID cannot be edited after the listing is created unless the listing was made with a GTIN exemption.

The US bundling policy adds that a bundle's title should include the word "Bundle", that the first bullet should say how many items it contains, and that the main image shows every item. We could not find those rules on a UK page, but following them costs nothing and makes the listing harder to misread.

## The margin maths

The reason sellers bundle low value items is fixed fees. Amazon's referral fee is a percentage of the price, but the FBA fulfilment fee is charged per unit shipped, set by size and weight ([FBA fulfilment fees](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200209150?locale=en-GB)). On a £4 item that fixed fee can be most of the margin. Three items in one pack pay one fulfilment fee instead of three.

That is the whole case, and it has three catches.

1. **The pack moves up a size tier.** One fulfilment fee on a heavier, bigger parcel is not the same as one small fee. Run both versions through the Revenue Calculator in Seller Central.
2. **Low-price FBA rates stop.** Amazon's [low-price FBA page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/low-price-fba-rates) says items at or below £20 including VAT get reduced fulfilment fees automatically (£10 in some categories, including beauty, health, grocery, office and books). A bundle that crosses the threshold loses the discount the singles had.
3. **Timing.** The fulfilment fee page says a 1.5% fuel and logistics surcharge has applied in the UK since 17 April 2026, and festive peak fees apply to some UK orders from 15 October 2026 to 14 January 2027. Price anything you send in now against the peak rate.

Work it out as a table before you buy:

| Line | Three singles | One pack of three |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Selling price | 3 x single price | Pack price, no more per unit than a single |
| Referral fee | Percentage of each | Percentage of the pack |
| Fulfilment fee | 3 x the single's fee, at low-price rate if eligible | 1 x the pack's fee, at its size tier |
| Stock cost | 3 x unit cost | 3 x unit cost |
| Prep | 3 labels | Bag or tape, set label, one Amazon barcode |
| Your time | Three listings already exist | A new listing, possibly a GTIN exemption |

If the pack column does not win by more than the cost of the extra prep and the risk below, sell the singles. For stock that has simply stopped selling, bundling is one option among several; see [stale stock](/guides/stale-stock) and [where the money leaks](/guides/amazon-where-the-money-leaks) first.

This is general information, not financial advice.

## What goes wrong

- **Other sellers join your listing.** Once your bundle ASIN exists, anyone can offer on it, and they may not send the same contents. Amazon's image page says it selects and arranges images from multiple sellers, so the page you built may not stay the page you built. Complaints about another seller's contents land on the ASIN you created.
- **Brand complaints.** A brand whose product appears in your bundle can file an intellectual property complaint, and a Generic listing showing its logo is an easy one to win. See [brand complaints](/guides/amazon-brand-complaints-and-inauthentic-claims) for what you can prove.
- **A home made multipack as a variation.** Named in the ASIN creation policy as a prohibited practice. It can cost you listing creation rights.
- **Per unit price above the single.** A fair pricing breach even if your cost per unit is higher.
- **Prep that comes apart.** An untaped sleeve arrives as loose units, gets scanned as singles, and your pack listing shows no stock while a single listing shows units you never sent.
- **Building for Q4 on a rule that may change in January.** If the reported change reaches the UK, stock built this autumn could need removing within weeks of the peak.
