This is for Amazon UK sellers who keep hitting "you need approval to sell this" and want a process that gets approvals rather than refusals. After reading you will be able to tell whether a product is gated before you pay for it, buy the right stock from the right supplier for an application, submit paperwork that passes, and handle a refusal without wasting the invoice. The [FBA manual](/guides/how-to-start-amazon-fba-uk) covers where gating sits among the other decisions; this is the detail. Nothing here is legal advice.

## How gating works

Amazon restricts some brands, some categories and some individual products so that only sellers it has checked can list them. Its [categories and products that require approval](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/gp/help/external/G200333160) page explains the principle: approval may involve documents, performance checks and other qualifications, and the requirements differ by what is being gated.

There are three layers, and they stack.

**Category gating.** Whole areas of the catalogue need approval before you can list anything in them. On Amazon UK the page lists them; grocery, beauty, health and personal care, baby, jewellery, watches and several others have appeared on it for years, and the list changes, so check it rather than memorising it.

**Brand gating.** Individual brands are restricted regardless of category. This is the layer resellers meet most, because well known consumer brands are precisely the ones Amazon and the brands themselves want to police. A brand can be open in one category and gated in another.

**ASIN gating.** A single product can be restricted on its own, usually because of a safety, regulatory or rights issue with that item. You can be approved for the brand and still blocked on one product.

Gating is per account, and it is not fixed. Amazon weighs your account's history, and the same application from a two year old account with clean metrics and a new account with none can get different answers. Some approvals arrive without applying once an account has been trading well for a while.

## Seeing it before you buy

Do not find out at the shipment stage. Two ways to check.

**Add a product.** Catalogue, Add products, search the product's barcode or ASIN. Next to the result Amazon shows either the option to sell, or a note that approval is needed with a button to apply. This is the authoritative check and it takes seconds. Sourcing tools show a gating flag too, but the tool reads an approximation and Add products reads your account.

**Selling applications.** Catalogue, Add products, then the selling applications view lists everything you have applied for, its status, and anything Amazon has asked for. Use it to see what you already hold.

The rule that follows: check on the account before paying, every product, every time. A product bought first and checked second is the most expensive kind of stock, because it cannot be listed and often cannot be returned.

## What Amazon requires from an invoice

When the application asks for documents, Amazon is specific about what passes. Its requirements, on the [approval documents page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201704440) and repeated in the application itself, come down to this.

| Requirement | What Amazon states | What it means for you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Age | Dated within the last 180 days | An old invoice fails even if everything else is right. Buy for the application, then apply promptly |
| Quantity | The application states a minimum number of units, and the invoice must show at least that many of the brand or category being applied for | The number is shown in the application; ten units is common but some brands and categories ask for more. Read the number on your application, not on a forum |
| Supplier details | Manufacturer or distributor name, address and contact details clearly shown | A supplier Amazon can verify. A marketplace order confirmation with no company address is not enough |
| Your details | Your name and address matching your Seller Central account exactly | Legal entity name if you are a company, the same address, character for character |
| Status | Shows the transaction is paid and fulfilled | Not a quote, a pro forma or an order acknowledgement |
| Format | PDF, DOC, PNG, GIF or JPG, up to 10MB | A clear scan or the supplier's own PDF; a phone photo of a crumpled receipt is not clear |

Some points that the requirements imply rather than state.

Retail till receipts are weak. They rarely carry your name and address and often do not name the manufacturer. Some retailers will issue a VAT invoice with your details on request, in store or online, and that document is what you want. Many arbitrage approvals have been obtained with retailer invoices that carry the seller's business name and billing address; it is the details that matter, not whether the supplier is a wholesaler.

Your billing address must be the one Amazon holds. If you deliver to a prep centre, the invoice still needs your address as the billing address. If Amazon has your registered office and the supplier has your home, one of them must change before you buy.

Amazon may combine invoices, and may verify the supplier. Applications have been approved on two invoices that together reach the quantity; they have also been refused because Amazon contacted the supplier and the supplier did not confirm the sale. Buy from businesses that answer the phone.

Amazon can also ask for a letter of authorisation from the brand instead of, or as well as, invoices. For a reseller buying from retailers that is not obtainable, and a brand that asks for it is, for you, hard gated; see below.

## Why applications are refused, and reapplying

Most refusals to new accounts are automated and arrive fast. Treat the first refusal as the normal path, not the end of it. The common causes, in order of how often we see them:

1. **Details do not match.** Trading name instead of legal name, a different address, a missing postcode, the supplier's address missing.
2. **Not enough units of the right thing.** The invoice shows ten units in total but six of them are another brand, or the product on the invoice is not in the category being applied for.
3. **Document type.** Order confirmation, screenshot, pro forma, packing slip, bank statement. Amazon wants an invoice.
4. **Unverifiable supplier.** No company address, no contact details, a supplier name that does not match any trading business, or a supplier who would not confirm.
5. **Account history.** New account, or an account with recent policy violations. The refusal may say nothing about the invoice at all.
6. **Legibility.** Blurred, cropped, key fields cut off.

When you are refused, do not open a new application. Go back to the same application in selling applications, read the reason given, fix exactly that, and resubmit on the same case. A new application for the same brand with the same invoice is treated as a repeat and tends to get the same automated answer. If the reason is a mismatch, fix your Seller Central details or get the supplier to reissue the invoice with the right ones, and only resubmit once the two agree. If the reason is quantity, buy the balance from the same supplier and submit both invoices together. Photographs of the units showing the barcode and packaging can support an application where Amazon suspects the product is not the one on the invoice; they do not replace the invoice.

If the reason is your account rather than your paperwork, wait. Sell cleanly for a month or two, then apply again with a fresh invoice. Time and clean metrics change the answer more than anything else you can do.

## Requesting a human review

Automated refusals say very little. After one careful resubmission that addressed the stated reason, ask for a person. Reply on the same application stating plainly that the documents meet each listed requirement, walking through them one by one (date, quantity, supplier details, your details, paid status), and asking for the application to be reviewed by a member of the team. Keep it factual and short. If the reply is the same template, open a case with seller support quoting the application ID and ask for it to be escalated to the approvals team, and use the call option if it is offered. Sellers we know have had applications approved on the third attempt with paperwork identical to the first; the difference was a person reading it.

What not to do: submit the same document five times in an afternoon, send an angry message, or alter a document. Altered invoices are detected, and the result is a violation on the Account Health page rather than a refusal.

## Category approvals and their extra requirements

Category gates ask for more than a brand gate does, because the categories are the ones with safety or regulatory rules. The [approval page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/gp/help/external/G200333160) sets out what each requires, and the application shows the specifics. Expect, depending on the category:

- **Invoices for the category** as above, often with a minimum unit count across the products you intend to list.
- **Product images** showing the item, its packaging and labelling, sometimes all sides.
- **Compliance documents** for regulated goods: a safety data sheet, a declaration of conformity, ingredients and labelling for food and cosmetics, and for some categories evidence of the responsible person in the UK. Manage Your Compliance handles these once you are listing; at application stage they are part of the form.
- **Account performance** checks, which you cannot influence except by trading cleanly.

Category approvals on a mature account can be automatic. On a new account they are the slowest approvals to get, and the sensible order is to build a few months of history in open categories before applying for the regulated ones. Note that category approval does not override brand gating: approval for beauty does not approve you for a gated skincare brand.

## Brands you cannot apply for

Some brands do not offer an application at all. Add products shows "not eligible" or the equivalent, with no apply button, or the application asks only for a letter of authorisation from the brand owner. Brands enrolled in Amazon's Transparency programme fall into a similar bucket in practice: you may be allowed to list, but you cannot send units in without a serialised code on each one, and retailer stock does not carry them. The [FBA manual](/guides/how-to-start-amazon-fba-uk) covers Transparency in more detail.

For a reseller these are not for now. Do not buy stock on the hope that the gate opens. Keep a list of them so you stop checking; revisit it every six months, because gates do change, and occasionally a brand that was closed appears with an apply button on an account that has aged well.

## How approvals change as the account ages

Amazon says on its approval page that performance is part of the decision, and in practice the pattern is consistent. In the first weeks, expect automated refusals even on good paperwork. From a few months of clean selling, applications with correct invoices start to pass, and some brands approve without documents. Beyond a year with good metrics, many brand gates open on request or are not there at all. Violations reset this: an authenticity complaint or a received IP complaint on the Account Health page makes approvals harder for months afterwards, and an account that is deactivated and reinstated starts near the bottom.

So the strategy is not to fight for the hardest brand first. Build history in the open catalogue, apply for the easy gates when you have a natural invoice for them, and let the account earn the difficult ones.

## Before buying stock for an ungating

Run this before paying for anything intended for an application.

1. **Is there an apply button?** Check in Add products on your account. No button means no application; do not buy.
2. **What does the application ask for?** Open it and read the requirements: unit count, documents, images. Screenshot it.
3. **Does the supplier issue a proper invoice?** With their name, address and contact details, and with your legal name and Seller Central billing address on it. Confirm this before ordering, not after.
4. **Will the invoice reach the quantity for this brand or category?** Count units of the brand or category being applied for, not the order total.
5. **Can you sell the units profitably or return them if the application fails?** The application is not guaranteed. Buy something you would be happy to hold, sell elsewhere, or send back.
6. **Are the units themselves listable?** Not a Transparency product, not a pack the brand does not sell, not restricted at ASIN level, not hazmat without approval, not dated stock that will be too short by the time it lands.
7. **Is the account clean?** An open violation on the Account Health page is a reason to wait a week rather than apply today.
8. **Will you apply within days?** The 180 day limit is generous but the clock starts on the invoice date, and the details are easiest to fix while the supplier still remembers the order.

Then buy, apply the day the invoice arrives, and keep the invoice in the same folder as every other invoice, because Amazon will ask for it again if a complaint ever lands on the same product.

## What goes wrong

**Buying first, checking second.** The whole problem in four words.

**Believing a tool over the account.** Gating is per account; only Add products on your login is definitive.

**Details that almost match.** "Ltd" missing, a trading name, an old address. Amazon compares text, not intent.

**Applying with a receipt.** Retail till receipts without your details are the most common refused document.

**Reapplying instead of resubmitting.** A fresh application with the same document gets the same automated answer and clutters your history.

**Buying deep for an application.** Ten units bought for approval and unsellable if it fails is a lesson; two hundred is a problem.

**Chasing hard gated brands.** Time spent on a brand with no apply button is time not spent on the many that have one.

## Questions

**Can I use one invoice for several brands?** If the invoice shows enough units of each brand and meets the other requirements, yes, one document can support several applications. Check each application's unit count separately.

**Do I need to be VAT registered to be approved?** No. Registration is a tax matter, not a gating requirement. Some wholesalers require it to open an account, which is a supplier decision rather than Amazon's.

**Does approval cover Europe?** Approvals are per marketplace. Being approved on Amazon UK does not approve you on other stores.

**I was approved and still cannot list. Why?** The product may be gated at ASIN level, restricted, or a Transparency item; or the listing may be in a category you are not approved for. Add products shows which.
