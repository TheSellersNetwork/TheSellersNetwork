This is for UK resellers who want to know, before a dispute rather than during one, what each selling platform will actually do for the **seller**. It compares eBay, Vinted, Depop, Etsy, Amazon and Facebook Marketplace on six problems: item not received despite tracking, not as described claims, returns abuse, chargebacks, lost parcels and damage in transit, plus the evidence each platform asks for. Everything comes from the platforms' own help pages and policies, read in September 2026. Protections come with conditions and can change, so check the linked page. This is general information, not legal advice.

## The short version

- Every platform's protection depends on conditions, and the most common one is using its own label or valid tracking, and dispatching on time.
- **eBay** publishes the most detailed seller protections: tracking rules for "not received" claims, a 50% deduction for returns sent back used or damaged, and payment dispute cover.
- **Vinted** and **Depop** compensate sellers for lost or damaged parcels only when you use their prepaid labels, up to £500 on Vinted and sales up to £250 on Depop.
- **Etsy** may cover buyer refunds up to $250 for non-delivery and listing accuracy disputes, but only one damaged-item case a year.
- **Amazon** FBM sellers rely on the A-to-z claims process and SAFE-T reimbursements; with FBA, Amazon handles customer returns and reimburses some losses under its own policy.
- **Facebook** says its Seller Protection Policy is only available in the US, and Purchase Protection does not cover local pick-ups, so UK Marketplace sellers have no platform cover.
- On the published terms, we would choose **eBay** for seller protection overall, with Vinted ahead for lost or damaged parcels worth up to £500.

## What matters most for resellers

- **Label and tracking.** Which postage method does the protection require?
- **Speed.** How quickly must you respond or dispatch to stay covered?
- **Limits.** Is there a value cap, and does it include postage?
- **Not as described.** Who decides, and who pays for the return?
- **Returns abuse.** Can you recover anything when an item comes back used, damaged or swapped?
- **Evidence.** What does the platform actually ask you for?

## eBay

eBay's [seller protections page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-protections?id=4345) is the most complete of any platform here.

**Item not received.** You are protected if you dispatch within your handling time and upload tracking before the latest estimated delivery date that shows: a tracking number that can be validated on the carrier's site, a status of delivered or attempted delivery, the date, an address matching the order to at least town, county or postcode, and signature confirmation on orders totalling £450 or more. If you used an eBay label and the item turns up after you refunded, eBay reimburses you automatically.

**Postage.** With [Simple Delivery](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/posting-items/setting-postage-options/simple-delivery?id=5575), which private sellers must use for eligible items, eBay says you are protected if the item is lost or damaged from the carrier's first scan until it is marked delivered, and eBay deals with the buyer.

**Not as described.** Buyers can return items that do not match the listing under the [Money Back Guarantee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy?id=4210), within 30 days of delivery or your returns window if longer, and you have 3 business days to respond before they can ask eBay to step in. Business sellers who are Above Standard or Top Rated and report a false claim may get up to £3.50 towards return postage, and the return will not count in their not as described rate. Private sellers have extra legal points to know; see [selling privately on eBay](/blog/ebay-private-sellers-returns-and-the-law).

**Returns abuse.** If an item you accepted back is returned used or damaged, you can deduct up to 50% of the refund, as long as you report the buyer and refund within 2 business days of getting it back. This applies to eligible business sellers and to any seller who is not Below Standard on listings with free returns. You lose the option if eBay steps in first.

**Chargebacks.** Buyers cannot use two routes: if they file a chargeback, eBay closes the eBay request. If the dispute is eligible under eBay's payment dispute protections, eBay covers the amount, waives the dispute fee and removes related feedback.

**Good**
- Precise, published tracking rules for "not received" claims.
- A route to recover part of the value on used or damaged returns.
- Payment dispute cover for eligible transactions.

**Bad**
- Signature confirmation is required on orders of £450 or more.
- The £3.50 postage help for false not as described claims is only for qualifying business sellers.
- You can lose protections, for example by posting with a slower service than the buyer chose.

## Vinted

**Lost or damaged in transit.** Standard and Pro sellers who send with a prepaid Vinted-generated label can get compensation if the carrier confirms the parcel lost or damaged, up to £500. If you used your own label, you claim from the carrier. Damaged parcels are only compensated if they were properly packed, and investigations can take several weeks ([compensation for sellers](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1018-compensation-for-sellers-when-shipping-with-integrated-labels)). If you accept compensation for a lost parcel, ownership of it passes to Vinted's shipping company. When a buyer reports damage, Vinted asks them for photos and videos of the item, all sides of the packaging and the label, and may contact the carrier ([parcel damaged in transit](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/729-parcel-was-damaged-in-transit)).

**Not received.** With an integrated label and tracking that looks stuck, the buyer waits up to 25 business days. If it still has not arrived, the buyer is refunded and, in Vinted's words, "the seller will be compensated" ([parcel didn't arrive](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/658-parcel-didn-t-arrive)).

**Not as described.** The buyer has 2 days after Vinted says the parcel should have arrived to report a problem with photos. You can offer a partial refund, a full refund, or ask for a return. The buyer pays return postage unless you choose to cover it, must post within 5 business days, and you then have 2 days to check the item. If you cannot agree, either side can ask Vinted's support team, which says it replies within 48 hours ([item significantly not as described](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/463/1356-item-is-significantly-not-as-described), [refund policy](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/465-the-vinted-refund-policy)). Vinted lists what counts as not as described, and what does not, including items the buyer does not like or that do not fit ([SNAD explained](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1090-significantly-not-as-described-items-at-vinted)).

**Returns abuse.** The refund policy says returned items must be in the same condition, with all tags and accessories, and that if these conditions are not met "the refund request may be disputed or dismissed". Buyers must send returns within 5 business days of your request ([returning an item](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/272-returning-an-item)). Vinted does not publish a separate seller process for swapped returns beyond that.

**Evidence.** Vinted recommends photos of the item and of the packed parcel, inside and outside, before shipping ([packing an item](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/65)). For more on what helps, see [packing videos and evidence](/blog/sealed-parcel-videos-and-evidence), and for how Vinted's rules look to consumer regulators, [what Trading Standards says about Vinted](/blog/what-trading-standards-says-about-vinted).

**Good**
- Compensation up to £500 for lost or damaged parcels with a Vinted label.
- Buyers pay return postage on not as described returns.
- A short 2 day window for buyers to report problems.

**Bad**
- No compensation through Vinted if you use your own label.
- Damage is only compensated if packing meets Vinted's rules.
- Nothing published on chargebacks or a dedicated route for swapped returns.

## Depop

**Lost or damaged in transit.** [Depop Protection for sellers](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001845367-Depop-Protection-for-sellers) applies when all of these are true: the sale went through the BUY button, you used the Depop Shipping label within 5 days of the sale, you sent it to the address on the sales receipt, and the transaction, including postage and taxes, is no more than £250. It also covers returns lost or damaged on the way back. Posting another way, or handing over in person, is not covered. Depop says the programme is not insurance and that it decides at its sole discretion.

**Not as described.** The buyer raises an issue in the Resolution Centre. You may need to provide photos showing the item matches your listing, and Depop Support decides. If it rules the item is not as described, it may arrange a return and then refund the buyer in full from your account once you have it back ([my buyer said the item is not as described](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001772188-My-buyer-said-the-item-is-not-as-described)). Depop asks you to respond to issues within 48 hours and reserves the right to refund a buyer on your behalf ([Resolution Centre as a seller](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/4417829104657-Resolution-Centre-as-a-seller)).

**Good**
- Clear eligibility rules for lost and damaged parcels.
- Covers returns lost on the way back as well.
- Refunding the buyer automatically refunds Depop's fees.

**Bad**
- Cover stops at £250 per transaction, bundles included.
- Nothing published on returns abuse or chargebacks for sellers.
- Depop's decision is discretionary and you have "no legal claim under the program".

## Etsy

Etsy's [Purchase Protection Programme for Sellers](https://www.etsy.com/uk/legal/policy/purchase-protection-programme-for/34509585385) (updated for 9 July 2026) says Etsy may refund buyers up to $250, including postage and taxes, so that you are not held responsible. It covers items that do not arrive or arrive late, and disputes where the item matches your listing but the buyer says it does not. Damaged items are generally not covered, except your first damaged-item case and then one per calendar year.

To qualify you must, among other things: meet Etsy's customer service standards and reply to a buyer's Help with Order message within 48 hours; use valid tracking or an Etsy postage label; use Etsy Payments; dispatch to the address on Etsy within your stated processing time; package carefully; and have shop policies filled in. If you have your own insurance or carrier cover, Etsy asks you to claim there first.

**Chargebacks.** Etsy asks you for information by email and responds to the card issuer or payment partner, but it says the bank or card company makes the decision ([chargebacks](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/115015729027)).

**Good**
- Covers non-delivery and false "not as described" claims when you meet the steps.
- Clear checklist of what keeps an order eligible.

**Bad**
- The cap is in US dollars, $250, including postage and taxes.
- Only one damaged-item case covered a year; Etsy recommends buying delivery insurance.
- Etsy's decision is discretionary.

## Amazon

**Seller-fulfilled (FBM).** The [A-to-z Guarantee](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G27951?locale=en-GB) covers delivery and the condition of items you fulfil. Customers must contact you first. You have 48 hours to respond to a delivery message or authorise a return request. A claim is granted automatically if, for example, you did not ship on time, did not use trackable shipping, or did not respond. Amazon says that where it is unclear whether an item was received, a claim is "more likely to be granted" to the customer. You have 30 calendar days to appeal a granted claim.

Using [Buy Shipping](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200202220?locale=en-GB) and dispatching on time protects you against delivery-related A-to-z claims affecting your Order Defect Rate, but Amazon says you are "still responsible for the financial loss".

For returns abuse, [SAFE-T](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G202175000?locale=en-GB) lets you claim within 60 days when Amazon refunded a customer on your behalf on a return made with an Amazon prepaid label, and the item came back used, damaged, materially different or incomplete, or the customer misused the returns policy. It excludes parcels lost or damaged in transit (claim with the carrier), chargebacks, A-to-z claims and FBA orders, and reimbursement excludes VAT and fees. Amazon asks for photos of the item and the returned parcel showing the return label, the invoice and tracking. More detail is in our [damaged returns and SAFE-T guide](/guides/amazon-damaged-returns-and-safe-t).

**Fulfilled by Amazon (FBA).** Amazon handles customer returns and refunds. Under its [FBA customer returns reimbursement policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G9N934L7Y4SFWPJ4?locale=en-GB) you are reimbursed automatically if the item is lost in transit, not returned within 45 days of a refund, or comes back as a completely different product. Amazon does not reimburse for items the customer damaged.

**Good**
- SAFE-T gives FBM sellers a published route for used, damaged or swapped returns.
- FBA sellers are reimbursed for items lost in transit without having to chase a carrier.
- A 30 day appeal window on A-to-z claims.

**Bad**
- Buy Shipping protects your metrics, not your money, on delivery claims.
- Unclear deliveries tend to be decided for the customer.
- Tight deadlines: 48 hours to respond, 7 days to supply extra SAFE-T information.

## Facebook Marketplace

Facebook's Purchase Protection covers eligible orders bought with delivery or checkout on Marketplace, and says local pick-ups, Messenger transactions and third-party sites do not qualify ([how Purchase Protection works](https://www.facebook.com/help/228307904608701)). Its [Seller Protection Policy](https://www.facebook.com/legal/merchant_policies) does protect sellers against lost parcels, false not as described claims, abusive returns and unauthorised chargebacks, but it states that seller protection "is currently only available in the US". Facebook's help pages told us, from the UK, that selling with delivery "isn't available in your location at the moment" ([sell with delivery](https://www.facebook.com/help/773379109714742)).

So for a UK seller, a Marketplace sale is a private deal between you and the buyer. Our guides on [collection safety](/guides/facebook-marketplace-collection-safety) and [Marketplace scams](/guides/facebook-marketplace-scams) cover protecting yourself.

**Good**
- Local cash or bank transfer on collection means no platform refunds to worry about.

**Bad**
- No seller protection from Facebook in the UK.
- Disputes are between you and the buyer.

## How to choose

- What do you sell? High value items hit caps quickly: £250 on Depop, $250 on Etsy, £500 on Vinted.
- Will you always use the platform's own label? Most protection depends on it.
- How quickly can you respond? Amazon, Depop and Etsy expect replies within 48 hours.
- How often do you get returns? eBay and Amazon FBM have published routes for used or swapped returns; the others publish much less.
- Are you willing to buy your own parcel insurance for items above the caps?

Our view: for resellers, the label rule matters most. On Vinted and Depop, choosing your own postage to save a little can remove your cover entirely.

## Our verdict

This is about protection only, not fees or buyers. If we had to choose one platform for a typical UK reseller on the published terms, we would choose **eBay**. By typical we mean someone posting tracked parcels to buyers across the UK, getting a steady trickle of returns and the occasional "not received" or "not as described" claim.

Why:

- **It covers more of the six problems than any other platform here.** eBay publishes seller protection for not received with tracking, used or damaged returns, payment disputes and loss or damage in transit.
- **The rules are specific.** It sets out exactly what tracking must show to defeat a "not received" claim, so you know before you post what keeps you covered.
- **A route for returns abuse.** You can deduct up to 50% from the refund on items returned used or damaged, if you report the buyer and refund within 2 business days.
- **Chargebacks are covered** for eligible transactions: eBay covers the amount, waives the dispute fee and removes related feedback.

Picks by situation:

- **If you sell mostly clothes and always use the platform's label:** Vinted, because its compensation for lost or damaged parcels goes up to £500, double Depop's £250 cap, and buyers pay return postage on not as described returns.
- **If you sell on Amazon:** FBA over seller-fulfilled, because Amazon reimburses items lost in transit automatically, while FBM sellers remain "responsible for the financial loss" on delivery claims even with Buy Shipping.
- **If you sell vintage on Etsy:** meet every step of the Purchase Protection checklist, because non-delivery and false not as described claims are covered up to $250 only when you do, and buy delivery insurance, because only one damaged-item case a year is covered.

What we would avoid, and why:

- **Posting Vinted or Depop sales with your own label**, because it removes the platform's parcel compensation.
- **Relying on Facebook Marketplace for anything you post**, because Facebook's seller protection is only available in the US.

*We are not paid by any platform named here and there are no affiliate links. This is our opinion based on the published terms when we checked them in September 2026; check each platform's current terms before you rely on them.*

## Common mistakes

- **Posting with your own label** on Vinted or Depop and expecting platform compensation.
- **Missing signature confirmation** on eBay orders of £450 or more.
- **Sending to a different address** because a buyer asked in messages. Depop and Etsy both exclude this.
- **Refunding before reporting** on eBay, which can cost you the 50% deduction.
- **Leaving messages unanswered** for more than 48 hours on Amazon, Depop or Etsy.
- **Assuming Facebook will step in** on a UK collection sale. It will not.
