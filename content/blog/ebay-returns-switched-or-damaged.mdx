This is for eBay UK sellers who have opened a return parcel and found something that is not what they sent: an older model, a fake, a broken version, a used item, or nothing at all. By the end you will know what eBay's own policies say about returned items, what you are allowed to deduct and when that option disappears, the evidence that gives you the best chance, and the order to do things in before the refund deadline. It is general information about eBay's published policies, not legal advice.

## The short version

- eBay's [condition of returned items policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behavior-policies/condition-returned-items-policy?id=4763) says buyers must return the item in the condition they received it, with everything that came in the original package.
- Returning an item other than the one received is listed as abusive behaviour in eBay's [abusive buyer policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/rules-policies-buyers/abusive-buyer-policy?id=4374), and the [Money Back Guarantee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy?id=4210) says a buyer who does it loses coverage.
- Once a return is delivered back to you, you normally have **2 business days** to refund, or eBay may refund on your behalf ([handling a return as a seller](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/handle-return-request-seller?id=4115)).
- For used or damaged returns, eligible sellers can deduct **up to 50%** from the refund. That option is lost once eBay steps in.
- A swapped or empty return is not a "deduction" problem. Do not refund; report it to eBay inside the deadline with your evidence.
- The evidence that matters is made **before you post**: photos, serial numbers, weights and packing records.
- eBay's pages do not promise a particular outcome in swapped item cases. Plan on the basis that your evidence has to carry it.

## What eBay's policies say about returned items

Three pages set the rules.

**The condition of returned items policy.** Buyers must return items "unused, unaltered, with no damage or signs of use" beyond the listed condition or how they were received, and must include everything that was in the original package ([policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behavior-policies/condition-returned-items-policy?id=4763)). The policy gives examples: parts must be intact and not previously installed, tags and labels must not be removed, fashion items must not appear washed or worn, factory seals on software and media must not be broken, and sealed collectibles must not be opened. Buyers with a history of abusing returns may face action under the abusive buyer policy.

**The Money Back Guarantee.** The buyer "must return the item in the same condition in which it was received", using tracked postage that can be independently validated. Returning an item other than the original, or using or damaging an item and then returning it, means the buyer is not covered ([Money Back Guarantee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy?id=4210)).

**The abusive buyer policy.** It lists "returning an item other than the original item received", "using or damaging an item and then returning it" and claiming an item was not as described when it was described accurately. eBay says it may respond with warnings, loss of Money Back Guarantee cover, blocking the buyer from returns and claims, or suspension ([abusive buyer policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/rules-policies-buyers/abusive-buyer-policy?id=4374)).

What those pages do **not** say is that eBay will automatically find in your favour, refund you, or reverse a refund you already sent. The policies set the rules; your evidence decides whether eBay agrees the rules were broken.

## Used or damaged: when you can deduct

If the right item comes back but in worse condition, eBay's [seller guide to returns](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/handle-return-request-seller?id=4115) gives suggested deductions:

| Condition when returned | Suggested deduction |
| --- | --- |
| Excellent: unused, undamaged | None |
| Good: missing packaging, seal opened | 5% to 10% |
| Fair: worn, missing parts | 15% to 30% |
| Poor: significant damage, defective | 35% to 50% |

The limits, from the same page and eBay's [seller protections](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-protections?id=4345):

- The maximum is **50%**, and only to cover the lost value of the item. You cannot use it to recover return postage or a drop in market price on an item returned in the same condition.
- It applies to sellers who are **not Below Standard**. For sellers who are not registered as businesses, eBay says it applies to listings that offer free returns. If you sell privately with "no returns", assume this option is not open to you (our post on [private sellers, returns and the law](/blog/ebay-private-sellers-returns-and-the-law) explains why).
- You must report the buyer and send the partial refund **within 2 business days** of getting the item back.
- **Once eBay steps in, you can no longer deduct.** This is the step people miss. If you want to deduct, do it before the case escalates.
- A very high "not as described" rating can also limit deductions ([service metrics policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/service-metrics-policy?id=4769)).

## Swapped, empty or a different item: do not refund yet

A deduction is for the same item in worse condition. If what came back is a different item, a fake, or an empty box, issuing even a partial refund can look like you accepted the return. The steps below are about keeping the case open until eBay has seen your evidence.

### Step 1: stop and record, before you open anything else

1. Photograph the parcel as delivered: all sides, the label, the tape and any damage.
2. Weigh the parcel on a kitchen or postal scale and photograph the reading. Compare it with the weight on your outbound postage label or receipt.
3. If you can, film the opening in one continuous take, with the label in shot at the start.
4. Photograph what is inside next to your original listing photos, and photograph any serial number, IMEI or batch code that does not match your records.
5. Keep the packaging, the label and the item exactly as they arrived.

A parcel video is not proof on its own, since it only shows what you chose to film. It is most useful alongside records you made before the sale. Our post on [packing videos and evidence](/blog/sealed-parcel-videos-and-evidence) looks at how much weight they carry.

### Step 2: gather the evidence from before the sale

This is what makes a swapped item case winnable:

- **Your listing photos**, especially close-ups of scratches, marks and anything unique.
- **Serial numbers, IMEI numbers or batch codes** you recorded before posting. If the listing showed them, better still.
- **Outbound weight.** Your postage label or the carrier receipt usually shows it. A 900g parcel out and a 250g parcel back tells its own story.
- **Packing photos or video** of the item going into the box.
- **Your messages with the buyer**, kept on eBay.

Our guide to [returns without losing money](/guides/returns-without-losing-money) covers building this into your packing routine so it is there when you need it.

### Step 3: contact eBay inside the 2 business days

Once the return is marked delivered, the refund clock is running. If you do nothing, eBay may refund the buyer on your behalf ([handling a return as a seller](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/handle-return-request-seller?id=4115)).

- Use the options in the return itself to report a problem and ask eBay to step in. eBay says it normally responds within 48 hours when it steps in, though it can take longer.
- State plainly what happened: "The item returned is not the item I sent. Serial number sent: X. Serial number received: Y." Short, factual, with your photos attached.
- Keep messages to the buyer on eBay and polite. Do not accuse; describe.

### Step 4: report the buyer

Report the buyer through eBay's reporting tools, which the [abusive buyer policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/rules-policies-buyers/abusive-buyer-policy?id=4374) links to. Even if the case does not go your way, the report puts the pattern on record, and it is the route to eBay removing related defects and feedback where it finds abuse ([seller protections](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-protections?id=4345)).

Then block the buyer so they cannot buy from you again ([blocking buyers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/resolving-buyer-issues/blocking-buyer-ebay?id=4082)).

### Step 5: report the fraud

Deliberately returning a different item to get a refund can be fraud. In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, report it to [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/reporting-a-fraud/), the national service that replaced Action Fraud. In Scotland, contact Police Scotland on 101. Keep the reference number: it is new information you can give eBay.

### Step 6: appeal if the decision goes against you

You can appeal a case outcome by giving new information within **30 calendar days** of the case closing. eBay's page lists documents showing the item matches the listing description among the evidence it accepts, and says it will come back with a final decision ([appeal as a seller](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/appeal-outcome-case-seller?id=4369)). New serial number evidence or a fraud reference are the kind of thing to add. Do not simply resend what eBay has already seen.

## If the parcel arrives damaged in transit

Not every damaged return is the buyer's fault. The [condition of returned items policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behavior-policies/condition-returned-items-policy?id=4763) says that where an item is damaged because it was not packaged properly for the return, the seller can deduct to cover the loss in value. If the packaging was fine and the carrier damaged it, that is a claim with the carrier on the return label, not with the buyer. Photograph the outer box before opening either way.

## How to make the next one less likely

- Put serial numbers or unique marks in your listing photos for anything worth swapping: phones, consoles, cameras, branded clothing, trainers.
- Note the outbound weight of every parcel above a value you would miss.
- Photograph or film the packing of higher-value items.
- Describe condition honestly so fewer "not as described" returns start at all. Our guide to [descriptions that sell](/guides/descriptions-that-sell) helps.
- Use tracked postage both ways; eBay's guarantee expects tracked returns.

## What goes wrong

- **Refunding first and complaining second.** We could not find anything on eBay's help pages that gives a seller a way to claw back a refund already sent. Report first.
- **Letting the case escalate when a deduction would have done.** For a scuffed but genuine item, a deduction inside 2 business days keeps control with you.
- **Throwing away the box.** The label, tape and weight are evidence.
- **Arguing with the buyer in messages.** eBay reads them. Stay factual.
- **Having no record of what you sent.** Without serials, photos or weights, it is your word against theirs.

For a second view on a return, post the facts (without the buyer's username) in [eBay buyers and disputes](/community/c/ebay-buyers-and-disputes).
