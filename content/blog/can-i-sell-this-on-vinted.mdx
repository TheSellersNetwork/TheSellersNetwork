This is for Vinted sellers clearing out a drawer or a loft who want to know, before they list, whether something is allowed. We have gone through Vinted's [Catalogue Rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules) category by category and set out what they allow, what they ban, and the conditions in between. Where the rules do not address something, we say so rather than guess. The rules form part of Vinted's terms, Vinted can change them without notice, and it says its prohibited list is not exhaustive, so check the page itself before you list anything borderline. This is general information, not legal advice.

## The short version

- Cosmetics and perfume are allowed only new, unused, in original packaging with any seal unbroken, and not expired. Pro sellers cannot sell cosmetics at all.
- Underwear must be new with tags or original packaging shown in the photos. Worn bras, bathing suits, tights and socks are allowed if properly washed and described as worn. Worn underwear bottoms are not.
- Food and drinks of any kind are banned. So are medicines, vitamins and food supplements.
- Most electronics are allowed, but not blocked, blacklisted, finance-blocked, jailbroken or rooted devices, SIM cards, swollen batteries or batteries over 100 watt-hours.
- Counterfeits are banned outright, and you cannot hedge with "not sure if genuine" or "inspired by".
- Children's clothes, toys and equipment are allowed, with specific bans on some sleep and feeding products, recalled items and handmade toys.

This covers the categories sellers ask about most. "Standard" means a normal Vinted account; "Pro" means a registered Vinted Pro seller. The rules are the same for both unless the table says otherwise.

## Perfume, make-up and skincare

The rule is short and strict. All cosmetics, which Vinted says includes perfume, make-up, creams, lotions and hair care, are banned if they have been used or are not in their original packaging. If the product or its box was sealed, the seal must not be broken or removed. Selling cosmetics that have been used or tried even once, or that are expired, is "strictly prohibited".

So a half-used bottle of perfume is out. So is a lipstick you swatched once. A boxed, sealed fragrance you never opened is in.

Three further points:

- **Samples and testers** that are not intended for sale are banned as their own item type. That catches a lot of perfume minis and department store freebies.
- **Pro sellers** are not allowed to sell anything in the cosmetic category, and the commercial selling section adds that Pro sellers cannot resell cosmetic, beauty or personal grooming items "regardless of their condition".
- **Perfume is a common counterfeit.** Vinted's IP section says a fake can copy a product's notable features, and gives the shape of a perfume bottle as an example.

Vinted recommends photos showing the packaging information, ingredients, barcode and expiry date. In our view, treat those photos as required: they are your evidence that the item meets the rule.

## Underwear and swimwear

For hygiene reasons, only new underwear can be sold, and the photos must clearly show the attached tags or original packaging. Vinted says the brand and composition labels sewn inside do not count as tags.

Worn bras, bathing suits, tights and socks are allowed as long as they are properly washed and the description clearly says they have been worn. Worn underwear bottoms, handmade underwear bottoms, shorts worn as underwear, body shapers and shapewear with bottom parts, and protective underwear bottoms for sports are all on the unsafe items list.

Swimwear sits between the two. The rules allow worn bathing suits if washed and described. They do not name bikini bottoms specifically, but they do ban worn underwear bottoms, so our reading is that worn swim bottoms are not worth the risk. New swim nappies, and new reusable swim nappies, are allowed only with tags or packaging shown.

## Food, drink, medicines and supplements

"Food and drinks of any kind" are banned, which includes alcohol. So are medicines, vitamins, food supplements "and any other health-related products", and veterinary medicines such as flea shampoo. Sealed does not help here: these are banned whatever their condition.

## Electronics

Electronics are a large allowed category, from phones and consoles to smart speakers and chargers. The conditions are about safety and whether the device can actually be used by the buyer:

- **Not allowed:** jailbroken or rooted devices; greylisted or finance-blocked devices; devices blacklisted by a carrier as lost, stolen or not paid off; phones locked to an account you cannot access; SIM cards; devices with swollen batteries; batteries over 100 watt-hours.
- **New only:** batteries, power banks, memory cards and blank media such as USB sticks, unless they are used rechargeable batteries under 100 watt-hours, which are allowed.
- **Restricted outright:** washing machines, dishwashers, pet grooming electronics, pet cameras, class 2 or higher laser pointers, software keys, e-bikes, e-scooters and hoverboards.
- **Before you ship:** clear personal data, log out of accounts, factory reset, switch it off, and remove your SIM card. Plug-in products and heat-producing battery products must not have frayed or loose cables.

If a buyer adds Vinted's Electronics Verification, the device goes to a hub to be checked first; see [Vinted item verification explained](/blog/vinted-item-verification-explained).

## Counterfeits

Counterfeits are banned, and so are fake spare parts, handmade items that look like a brand, and anything that infringes someone's intellectual property. Vinted notes that selling counterfeits "could expose sellers to civil and criminal proceedings".

The rules close the usual loopholes. You may only list items you are sure are authentic, you cannot add a disclaimer saying you are not sure, and "inspired by" followed by a brand name is generally prohibited. You must also upload photos showing the item's authenticity as clearly as possible.

For what happens when a buyer says your item is fake, see [Vinted fake and not as described claims](/guides/vinted-fake-or-not-as-described-claims). ## Children's items and safety

Children's clothing, toys, furniture and childcare equipment are all allowed categories. The specific bans are:

- unsafe sleeping bags, with examples given as those without arm holes, those with hoods, and ill-fitting ones;
- feeding and self-feeding pillows, and any self-feeding products;
- crib cushions;
- handmade kids' toys, and handmade items in pushchairs and carriers, bathing and changing, childproofing and safety, and nursing and feeding;
- recalled products, meaning anything recalled or flagged as unsafe by manufacturers or regulators in the UK, the EU or nationally;
- used helmets and other head protection, which are allowed only new, and protective equipment that no longer works or meets safety standards;
- mattress toppers, except new kids' toppers with the original tag visible.

If an item came with instructions or safety warnings, the rules say to include them when you send it.

**What the rules do not address.** The Catalogue Rules do not mention used car seats, cots or high chairs by name, and Vinted's catalogue has a car seats category. So they are not banned as such. The general rules still apply: recalled or unsafe products are banned, and Vinted says it may remove items that pose a risk even if they are not listed. Before listing any child safety product, search the government's [product safety alerts and recalls](https://www.gov.uk/product-safety-alerts-reports-recalls) for the make and model, and describe its history honestly.

## Other bans that catch people out

A few items on the list are easy to miss:

- **All knives,** including kitchen knives and cutlery knives in a set, plus fabric and craft scissors, rotary cutters, letter openers and manual razors.
- **Advent calendars, surprise packages and mystery bundles.**
- **Tickets, gift cards, discount coupons and subscriptions,** plus digital games, keys, e-books and audiobooks.
- **Autographed items and graded cards** without a trusted certificate of authenticity.
- **Mattresses and duvets,** outside the kids' categories.
- **Official work uniforms,** including delivery company uniforms, unless vintage and no longer in use.

Vinted also says items must already be lawfully on the market in Great Britain before they can be listed. Vinted is not a route for bringing goods into the country for the first time.

## What happens if you get it wrong

The rules set out the penalties. A listing that needs changing is hidden and you get 24 hours to fix it; if you do not, it stays hidden. A listing with a prohibited item is removed. Repeated prohibited listings can lead to a suspension, for example for 7 days, or a permanent block. If you had bumped a listing that is then removed, Vinted will not refund the bump. See [banned on Vinted: how to appeal](/blog/banned-on-vinted-how-to-appeal) if it has gone that far.

Standard accounts are also for selling your own things. Vinted says resellers need Vinted Pro, and Pro has its own extra bans, including cosmetics and seller-made handmade items. See [Vinted Pro and selling as a business](/guides/vinted-pro-and-selling-as-a-business).

## What goes wrong

- **Listing opened perfume or make-up because it is "nearly full".** Used means used.
- **Relying on the sewn-in label as the "tag" on new underwear.** Vinted says it does not count.
- **Forgetting to factory reset a phone,** or listing one still locked to your account.
- **Selling vitamins or protein powder** because they are sealed. Supplements are banned regardless.
- **Adding "not sure if real" to a branded item.** It is not allowed, and it tells Vinted you have not checked.
- **Listing a used child safety product without checking recalls.**

If you are unsure about a specific item, ask in the [Vinted forum](/community/c/vinted) before you list it.
