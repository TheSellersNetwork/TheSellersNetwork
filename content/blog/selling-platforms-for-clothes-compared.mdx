This is for UK resellers who sell clothes, or want to, and are deciding where to list. It compares Vinted, Depop, eBay, Vestiaire Collective, Facebook Marketplace and Etsy (for vintage only) on what you pay, what your buyer pays, how postage works, what protection you get, which account type a reseller needs, and how much work each takes. It goes wider than our [eBay or Vinted for your own clothes](/blog/ebay-or-vinted-for-your-own-clothes), which is for clearing a personal wardrobe. Figures come from each platform's own pages and from our [checked fee table](/tools/calculator), read in September 2026. Fees change, so check the linked page before relying on a figure. This is general information, not tax, legal or financial advice.

## The short version

- **Vinted, Depop and eBay (private)** charge UK sellers no selling fee on clothes. The buyer pays a fee instead, and Depop takes a payment processing fee from you.
- **Reselling changes the rules.** Vinted says standard accounts cannot be used to resell for profit; resellers need Vinted Pro. eBay has separate business seller fees.
- **eBay business sellers** pay 11.9% on clothes plus a per order fee and a 0.35% regulatory fee, before VAT.
- **Vestiaire Collective** charges a £10 minimum selling fee under £83, plus 3% processing with a £3 minimum, so it suits higher priced items.
- **Etsy** only allows clothes as vintage, which it defines as 20 or more years old.
- **Facebook Marketplace** in the UK is local only, with no delivery checkout and no seller protection from Facebook.
- Our pick for a typical clothes reseller is **Vinted Pro**: no selling fee, free to join, and prepaid labels with up to £500 of parcel compensation.

## What matters most for resellers

- **Account type.** Are you allowed to resell on a personal account, and what does a business account cost you?
- **Total fees.** What you pay and what the buyer pays, because a high buyer fee can cost you sales.
- **Postage.** Does the platform provide the label, and does the protection depend on it?
- **Protection.** What happens when a buyer says the item is lost or not as described?
- **Price range.** Some fee structures punish cheap items and others punish expensive ones.
- **Effort.** Listing, answering offers, and posting on time.

## Vinted

Vinted charges sellers nothing; its How it works page gives the buyer's fee as 5% of the item price plus £0.70, while its Help Centre says it usually ranges from 3% to 8% plus £0.30 to £0.80 depending on the item and order ([How it works](https://www.vinted.co.uk/how_it_works), [Vinted fee](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1654)). The buyer chooses and pays for a prepaid label from carriers including Evri, InPost, DPD and Royal Mail, and you must use that label or the order is cancelled ([shipping on Vinted](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/234-shipping-methods)).

The account rule matters most for resellers. Vinted says standard accounts "can't be used to sell items professionally", including buying items and reselling them at a profit, and that nobody, Pro sellers included, may sell new items bought specifically to resell ([commercial selling](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/1120-commercial-selling)). Vinted Pro is free, but you must be a registered sole trader, charity or company, your business details show on your profile unless you are a sole trader who opts to hide them, and buyers get a 14 day return right ([Vinted Pro](https://www.vinted.co.uk/pro), [registering as a Pro seller](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/906-registering-as-a-pro-seller)). Our [Vinted Pro guide](/guides/vinted-pro-and-selling-as-a-business) covers the switch.

**Good**
- No selling fee on standard or Pro accounts.
- Prepaid labels with compensation up to £500 for lost or damaged parcels.
- Buyers pay return postage on not as described returns from standard accounts ([refund policy](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/465-the-vinted-refund-policy)).

**Bad**
- Reselling on a standard account breaks Vinted's rules.
- Pro orders do not complete for 14 days, which slows payment.
- New stock bought to resell is not allowed on any account.

## Depop

Depop charges UK sellers no selling fee, but takes a 2.9% plus £0.30 payment processing fee on the item price plus shipping. Boosted listings cost 12% ([seller fees](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001791127-Seller-fees-and-charges)). Buyers pay a Marketplace fee of up to 5% of the item price plus up to £1 ([Marketplace fee](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/21752555753361-What-is-the-Marketplace-fee)). Depop Shipping uses Evri, and Depop Protection for lost or damaged parcels only applies when you use its label and the sale is no more than £250 ([Depop Protection for sellers](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001845367-Depop-Protection-for-sellers)).

On audience, Depop describes itself to business sellers as reaching "a younger, fashion-forward, and sustainability-conscious audience". Its business set-up asks for a company name, address and company registration number, and a personal Depop Payments account cannot be switched to a business one in the app ([selling as a charity or business](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/4411154329233-Selling-as-a-charity-or-business)).

**Good**
- No selling fee, and fees are reversed automatically on refunds.
- Depop Shipping caps the wait for payment at 10 business days after the sale.

**Bad**
- The processing fee applies to postage too.
- Protection stops at £250.
- Moving from a personal to a business account needs support to do it.

## eBay

**Private sellers** pay no final value fee on UK sales, get 300 free listings a month and then pay 35p each, and pay 3% if the buyer's address is abroad ([selling fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822)). The buyer pays a Buyer Protection fee, already included in the price they see: £0.10 plus 7% of the price up to £20, 4% from £20 to £300 and 2% from £300 to £4,000 ([Buyer Protection fee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/buying/paying-items/buyer-protection-fee?id=5594)). Simple Delivery is the only postage method for private sellers on eligible items priced £750 or less, with a prepaid label or QR code for Evri, Royal Mail, DHL or InPost ([Simple Delivery](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/posting-items/setting-postage-options/simple-delivery?id=5575)).

**Business sellers** pay a final value fee of 11.9% on clothes, shoes and accessories, 7% on trainers of £100 or more, and 12.9% on women's bags up to £800 then 7% above. On top there is £0.30 per order of £10 or less, £0.40 above, and a 0.35% regulatory operating fee. All are exclusive of VAT, and a shop subscription is optional ([business selling fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/business-selling-fees?id=4809)). If you buy to resell, read [when eBay says you are a business seller](/blog/when-ebay-says-you-are-a-business-seller).

**Good**
- Detailed published seller protections, including tracking rules and cover for used or damaged returns; see [seller protection compared](/blog/seller-protection-compared).
- Auctions and a wide range of categories beyond clothes.
- Business sellers' buyers pay no Buyer Protection fee.

**Bad**
- Business sellers pay a percentage on every sale plus a per order fee, with VAT added on top.
- More listing fields to complete than the clothing apps.
- Private sellers must use Simple Delivery for eligible items.

## Vestiaire Collective

Listing is free. For UK sellers listing in pounds, the selling fee is 12% on items priced from £83 to £16,667, a fixed £10 below £83, and a fixed £2,000 above £16,667, plus a 3% payment processing fee with a £3 minimum. Items cannot be listed below £14 ([selling fees](https://faq.vestiairecollective.com/hc/en-gb/articles/24659638721425-Seller-Selling-Fees)). Buyers pay a service fee that "typically ranges between 5% to 28%" of the price, with a £5 minimum, which Vestiaire says pays for delivery guarantees, online inspection and physical authentication where Direct Shipping is not available ([buyer service fee](https://faq.vestiairecollective.com/hc/en-gb/articles/18378951869585-Buyer-What-is-the-Buyer-Service-Fee)).

Professional sellers get their own commission rates, with a reduced rate for those whose monthly cancellation rate stays at 10% or below; cancellations for authenticity, items not matching the description, or not shipping within 7 days count against you ([professional seller commission](https://faq.vestiairecollective.com/hc/en-gb/articles/11538017492241-Professional-seller-Commission)). To join, you need proof of a registered business and a valid VAT number ([becoming a Professional Seller](https://faq.vestiairecollective.com/hc/en-gb/articles/4408896527633-Becoming-a-Professional-Seller)).

**Good**
- Percentage fee falls to a fixed amount on very expensive items.
- Authentication and inspection are part of the buyer's service.
- Prepaid shipping labels.

**Bad**
- Fixed minimum fees make cheap items expensive to sell.
- Buyer fees can be high, up to 28% on some items.
- The professional programme needs a VAT number, which many small resellers do not have.

## Facebook Marketplace

Facebook's listing steps for a local sale show no selling fee ([sell something on Marketplace](https://www.facebook.com/help/561376580709359)). From the UK, its help page on selling with delivery says the feature "isn't available in your location at the moment" ([sell with delivery](https://www.facebook.com/help/773379109714742)), Purchase Protection does not cover local pick-ups, and its Seller Protection Policy is "currently only available in the US" ([Seller Protection Policy](https://www.facebook.com/legal/merchant_policies)). So Marketplace here means local buyers, collection and payment arranged between you.

**Good**
- No platform fee shown, and payment on the spot.
- Suits bulky or bundled items that are awkward to post.

**Bad**
- No delivery checkout and no seller protection from Facebook in the UK.
- Meet-ups take time and need care; see [collection safety](/guides/facebook-marketplace-collection-safety).

## Etsy (vintage only)

Etsy only accepts clothes as vintage, meaning items 20 or more years old that you personally curated. Resold commercial items newer than 20 years are not allowed, even if they look vintage ([Creativity Standards](https://www.etsy.com/uk/legal/creativity)). Fees are a 0.20 USD listing fee per item and 6.5% transaction fee on the item and postage ([fees policy](https://www.etsy.com/uk/legal/fees/)), plus the 4% plus £0.20 payment processing and 0.48% regulatory operating fees in our checked fee table. If Etsy's Offsite Ads bring the sale, you pay 15% on that order, or 12% where Etsy's policy says the lower rate applies; shops with under 10,000 USD of sales in the past year can opt out. Etsy's fees page shows an update dated 5 October 2026, so recheck after then. Our guides on [Etsy vintage rules](/guides/etsy-vintage-rules) and [Etsy fees on one sale](/guides/etsy-fees-on-one-sale) go further.

**Good**
- Built for vintage buyers, with its own definition of vintage.
- Purchase Protection for sellers covers up to $250 on eligible cases.

**Bad**
- Most second-hand clothes are too new to qualify.
- The most separate fees of any platform here, and Offsite Ads can add 12% to 15%.
- New sellers usually wait 14 days after a sale for their money ([Etsy deposits](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360002080688)).

Managed resale services, where you post a bag of clothes and the company sells them, also exist. Sellpy lists the countries its sales service is live in and the UK is not one of them ([Sellpy](https://intercom.help/sellpy/en/articles/408062-how-does-sellpy-s-sales-service-work)), so we have left it out.

## A worked example: a £20 jacket

Say you sell a jacket for £20, and ignore postage for simplicity. These use the published fee figures above; your real fees depend on postage, category and account. The [fee calculator](/tools/calculator) does the sums for your own prices.

| Platform | Fees you pay | What you keep | What the buyer pays on top |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vinted (standard or Pro) | £0 | £20.00 | £1.70 using How it works' 5% plus £0.70 |
| Depop | £0.88 (2.9% plus £0.30) | £19.12 | Up to £2.00 |
| eBay (private) | £0 | £20.00 | £1.50 (£0.10 plus 7%) |
| eBay (business) | £2.85 before VAT (£2.38 plus £0.40 plus £0.07), £3.42 with 20% VAT | £16.58 to £17.15 | Nothing |
| Vestiaire Collective | £13.00 (£10 fixed plus the £3 minimum processing fee) | £7.00 | At least £5 |
| Etsy (vintage) | About £2.40 (£1.30 plus £1.00 plus £0.10) plus the 0.20 USD listing fee | About £17.60, less the listing fee | Not published |

The Vestiaire row shows why its fixed minimums make it a platform for higher priced items. At £200 the same fees would be 12% (£24) plus 3% processing (£6).

## How to choose

- Are you reselling for profit? If so, which platforms' account rules can you meet, and do you have a VAT number?
- What is your typical sale price? Fixed minimum fees hurt cheap items; percentage fees hurt dear ones.
- Do your items suit the platform? Only vintage belongs on Etsy.
- How much time do you have for listing, offers and posting?
- Do you want to post, or sell locally for collection?
- Would you rather the buyer paid the fee (Vinted, eBay private, Depop's Marketplace fee) or keep your price clean and pay it yourself (eBay business)?

Our view: for resellers selling everyday clothes, the account rules decide more than the fees. A reseller on a standard Vinted account is breaking Vinted's rules, whatever the fee saving.

## Our verdict

If we had to choose one for a typical UK clothes reseller, we would choose **Vinted Pro**. By typical we mean a registered sole trader buying second-hand clothes at car boots and charity shops and selling everyday pieces at around £20 each.

Why:

- **You keep the full price.** No selling fee on Pro, and Pro itself is free, so our £20 jacket leaves you £20, against £16.58 to £17.15 on eBay as a business seller.
- **It fits the account rules.** Vinted says resellers need Pro, and a registered sole trader can join, with the option to hide business details from the profile.
- **Postage and protection come together.** Prepaid labels, with compensation up to £500 for lost or damaged parcels.
- **Low effort**, on our own rating in the table.

The costs of Pro are real: buyers get a 14 day return right and orders do not complete for 14 days, so money arrives later than on a standard account.

Picks by situation:

- **If you sell new or deadstock clothes bought to resell:** eBay as a business seller, because Vinted does not allow new items bought specifically to resell on any account.
- **If you are VAT registered and sell designer pieces priced £83 or more:** Vestiaire Collective, because its fee is 12% in that range, buyers pay for authentication and inspection, and its professional programme needs a VAT number.
- **If you sell genuine vintage, 20 or more years old:** Etsy, because it is built for vintage buyers, though it has the most separate fees here.
- **If you clear bulky bundles locally:** Facebook Marketplace, because there is no fee shown and you are paid on collection.

What we would avoid, and why:

- **A standard Vinted account for reselling**, because Vinted's rules do not allow it.
- **Vestiaire Collective for items under £83**, because the £10 fixed fee and £3 minimum processing fee took £13 of our £20 jacket.

*We are not paid by any platform named here and there are no affiliate links. This is our opinion based on the published prices and terms when we checked them in September 2026; check each platform's current terms before you sign up.*

## Common mistakes

- **Reselling on a personal account** where the platform says you need a business one.
- **Ignoring VAT** on eBay's business fees, which are quoted without it.
- **Listing cheap items on Vestiaire Collective**, where the minimum fees can take most of the price.
- **Listing clothes under 20 years old on Etsy**, which its Creativity Standards do not allow.
- **Using your own postage** on Vinted or Depop and losing the parcel protection.
- **Expecting Facebook to deliver**, or to protect you, on a UK sale.
