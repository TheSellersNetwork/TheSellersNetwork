This is for anyone in the UK who has bought, or is about to buy, an online course, membership or coaching programme and wants to know when they can get their money back. It covers the 14-day cancellation right, how you can lose it once you start the course, your rights if the course was not what you were sold, and the card protections that sit on top. The rules are more specific than most course sellers' refund pages suggest, so it is worth knowing them before you pay. This is general information, not legal or financial advice.

## The short version

- Online courses bought at a distance usually come with a **14-day cancellation right** under the [Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/30).
- For recorded content, you only lose that right early if you **expressly agreed** to start straight away **and acknowledged** you would lose it, and the content then started.
- For live coaching or other services, asking to start early means you **pay for what you used** if you cancel, rather than losing the right altogether.
- If the seller did not tell you about your cancellation rights, the period can be **extended by up to 12 months**.
- If the course was not as described, the [Consumer Rights Act 2015](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/42) gives you remedies that can go up to a full refund.
- Card payments may be recoverable by **chargeback**; credit card purchases with a cash price over £100 and up to £30,000 may also be covered by **Section 75**.

## First: do these rules apply to you?

The cancellation rules protect consumers. The regulations define a consumer as "an individual acting for purposes which are wholly or mainly outside that individual's trade, business, craft or profession" ([regulation 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/4)). The Consumer Rights Act uses a similar definition.

For resellers this is not always clear cut. If you bought the course as an individual who had not started a business, you are more likely to be a consumer. If you were already trading and bought it for the business, for example through your limited company or as a business expense, you may not be. If the seller disputes this, get advice from the [Citizens Advice consumer service](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/about-us/information/contact-the-consumer-service/) before you rely on consumer rights.

## What did you actually buy?

The rules differ depending on what you paid for, and many courses are a mix.

| Part of the course | How the law is likely to treat it | Key rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recorded videos, downloadable guides, templates | Digital content not on a tangible medium | [Regulation 37](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/37); Consumer Rights Act sections 34 to 45 |
| Live calls, one-to-one coaching, feedback on your work | A service | [Regulation 36](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/36); Consumer Rights Act sections 49 to 57 |
| A "done-for-you" store or account set-up | A service | As above |
| A printed workbook or physical kit | Goods | Different cancellation rules, starting from delivery |

Look at what the sales page and contract said you would get. That is the starting point for any claim.

## The 14-day cancellation right

For a service or for digital content not on a physical medium, the cancellation period ends "at the end of 14 days after the day on which the contract is entered into" ([regulation 30](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/30)). You do not need a reason. Tell the seller clearly, in writing, that you are cancelling, and keep a copy.

### If you were not told about the right to cancel

The seller has to give you information about your right to cancel before you buy. If it does not, the period is extended. If the seller gives you the information later, within 12 months, you have 14 days from receiving it. If it never does, the period ends 12 months after it would normally have ended ([regulation 31](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/31)).

## Losing the right once access starts

This is the part most course sellers rely on, and the conditions matter.

### Recorded content

Under [regulation 37](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/37), the seller must not start supplying digital content during the 14 days unless you have:

1. given **express consent** to it starting, and
2. **acknowledged** that you will lose your right to cancel.

If you did both and the content then started, you lose the right to cancel. In practice, this is the tick box at checkout saying something like "I want access now and understand I lose my right to cancel".

If the seller started supplying without that consent, or you consented but were not asked to acknowledge losing the right, or the seller did not send you the confirmation the regulations require, you keep the right to cancel and "bear no cost" for the content you received during the cancellation period ([regulation 37(4)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/37)).

**What to check:** your order confirmation email and a screenshot of the checkout page if you have one. Look for the consent wording and the acknowledgement.

### Coaching and other services

Services work differently under [regulation 36](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/36):

- If you **expressly asked** for the service to start within the 14 days, you can still cancel, but you must pay for what was supplied up to the point you cancelled, in proportion to the whole contract.
- That amount is based on the total price, or on the market value of what you received if the total price is excessive.
- You only lose the right to cancel altogether once the service has been **fully performed**, and only if you acknowledged when you asked for it to start that you would lose the right at that point.
- You pay **nothing** for the service in the cancellation period if the seller did not give you the required information on cancellation and costs, or if the service was not started at your express request.

### Mixed courses

Where a course combines recorded modules with live coaching, the two parts may be treated differently. A seller cannot simply say "you logged in, so no refund" for the whole package if part of it is a service you asked to start early. Set out each part in your cancellation letter.

## When the course was not as described

Cancellation rights are about changing your mind. If the course was not what you were sold, you have separate rights that do not depend on the 14 days.

### Digital content

Digital content must be of satisfactory quality, fit for a purpose you made known, and as described. If it is not, [section 42 of the Consumer Rights Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/42) gives you the right to repair or replacement, and then a price reduction. Under [section 44](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/44), the reduction can be the full price in appropriate cases, and must be paid within 14 days of the seller agreeing to it, without a fee. [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/phone-internet-downloads-or-tv/problem-with-an-app-software-or-download/) notes that the law does not give an automatic right to a full refund for faulty digital content in the way it does for goods.

### Services

A service must be performed with reasonable care and skill. Just as importantly for courses, anything the seller said or wrote to you about the service is treated as a term of the contract if you took it into account when deciding to buy ([section 50](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/50)). If the sales page promised weekly one-to-one calls and you got a monthly group call, that is a breach.

Your remedies are repeat performance and then a price reduction. Under [section 56](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/56), the reduction "may, where appropriate, be the full amount of the price", paid within 14 days of the seller agreeing, by the same payment method and without a fee.

### If you were misled or pressured

If you bought because of misleading claims or high-pressure selling, [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/changed-your-mind/if-you-were-misled-or-pressured-into-buying-something-you-didnt-want/) explains that you may be entitled to a refund, and possibly compensation. It says you may get a full refund if you bought or started the service within the last 90 days and have not fully used it, and a partial refund in other cases. Read their page for the details and the wording to use.

Income claims that were never true, false countdowns and "free" offers that were not free are the common examples in course sales; see [how to spot a reselling course scam](/blog/how-to-spot-a-reselling-course-scam).

## Chargebacks and Section 75

If the seller will not refund you, your card provider may be able to help. The [Financial Ombudsman Service](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/consumers/complaints-can-help/credit-borrowing-money/goods-services-bought-credit) explains both routes.

| | Chargeback | Section 75 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | A process under the card scheme's rules to reverse a card payment | A legal right under the [Consumer Credit Act 1974](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1974/39/section/75) to claim against the lender for the seller's misrepresentation or breach of contract |
| Cards and credit covered | Debit and credit cards | Credit cards and some other credit, such as point-of-sale loans. Not debit cards |
| Amount | No fixed range | Cash price of the item over £100 and up to £30,000 |
| Time limit | Set by the card scheme; the Ombudsman says you usually have around 120 days | Not the same as chargeback; ask your lender and act promptly |
| Does the bank have to do it? | No, but it should raise one where there is a valid reason | Yes, if the conditions are met |

A few points from the Ombudsman's guidance that often matter for courses:

- **Section 75 depends on the cash price, not what you put on the card.** It can apply even if only part of the payment was made on credit.
- **There needs to be a direct link** between you, the seller and the lender. Paying through some third-party payment services can break that link.
- **Complain to the bank first.** If you are unhappy with its answer, or it has not replied within eight weeks, you can take it to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

If you paid by instalments through a finance agreement arranged by the course seller, ask the lender about Section 75 and check the lender on the [FCA Financial Services Register](https://register.fca.org.uk/s/).

## A practical order of steps

1. **Gather your evidence.** Sales page, ads, checkout screenshots, order confirmation, contract, messages and a note of what you actually received.
2. **Write to the seller.** Say whether you are cancelling within the cancellation period, claiming because it was not as described, or both. Quote the regulation or section. Ask for a refund by a set date, such as 14 days.
3. **If you paid by card, contact your card provider early.** Do not wait for the seller to run the clock down on a chargeback.
4. **Get advice** from the [Citizens Advice consumer service](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/about-us/information/contact-the-consumer-service/), which can also pass a report to Trading Standards.
5. **Report misleading ads** to the [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/make-a-complaint.html). It will not get you a refund but can stop the ad; see [what the ASA says about earnings claims](/blog/what-the-asa-says-about-earnings-claims).

## What goes wrong

- **Accepting "no refunds once you log in" at face value.** It is only true if the consent and acknowledgement conditions were met, and only for the digital content part.
- **Cancelling by phone only.** Put it in writing so there is a date on record.
- **Waiting to see if it gets better.** The 14 days and the chargeback window both run while you wait.
- **Asking for a refund without saying why.** Name the right you are relying on.
- **Signing a new agreement to settle.** Some sellers offer an "upgrade" or a partial credit in return for dropping a claim. Read it before you agree.
