This is for Vinted sellers of designer clothing, bags, shoes and accessories, and of phones, tablets and consoles, who have seen the diamond badge on a listing or had a sale routed through a verification hub. By the end you will know what Vinted's two verification services check, who pays and how much, how long they take, what you have to do differently when sending, and what happens if your item fails, including the tougher counterfeit terms that apply from 8 October 2026. Everything comes from Vinted's Help Centre, price list and terms as published in September 2026. It is general information, not legal advice.

## The short version

- There are two services: **Item Verification** for selected designer items, and **Electronics Verification** for selected phones, tablets, consoles and accessories.
- The **buyer** pays: £10 per item for Item Verification and £5 per item for Electronics Verification, which Vinted says may be included at no extra cost on some orders. Sellers pay nothing ([Vinted price list](https://www.vinted.co.uk/pricelist)).
- You cannot remove the option from an eligible listing.
- You still have 5 business days to send, but to Vinted's hub rather than the buyer. Checks take 1 to 2 business days; the whole journey usually takes up to 10 business days.
- If the item fails, the order is cancelled, the buyer gets a full refund and you cannot list that item on Vinted again.
- From 8 October 2026, an item verified as counterfeit can be held for six weeks for you to challenge, then disposed of without compensation.

## The two services at a glance

| | Item Verification | Electronics Verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is eligible | Items priced at £85 or more, from brands on Vinted's eligible list, in clothing, bags, accessories, jewellery and shoes | Mobile phones, tablets and games consoles, plus listed accessories such as controllers, styluses, keyboards, power supplies and VR headsets |
| What is checked | Authenticity | Hardware and software, authenticity, that the condition matches the listing, and that the listing details match the device |
| Fee | £10 per item | £5 per item, or included at no extra cost on some eligible orders |
| Who pays | The buyer | The buyer |
| Can the seller opt out on an eligible listing | No | No |
| Check time at the hub | 1 to 2 business days | 1 to 2 business days |
| Typical delivery to the buyer | Up to 10 business days after you send it | Up to 10 business days after you send it |
| Buyer receives | The item with a hangtag and a digital verification report | The device, factory reset, with a verification report both sides can see |

Sources: [Item Verification](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1147-item-verification-for-designer-items), [Electronics Verification for buyers](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1360-electronics-verification-service-for-buyers), [selling with Electronics Verification](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1361-selling-with-the-electronics-verification-service) and the [price list](https://www.vinted.co.uk/pricelist). Vinted says the fee covers the check and the shipping from you to the hub.

## Who pays, and what it means for your price

Neither service costs the seller anything. Vinted says selling with Item Verification "is free for you" ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/1153-selling-with-the-item-verification-service)), and that for Electronics Verification buyers pay, so you are not charged.

The buyer's cost is on top of the item price, shipping and the Vinted fee (called the Buyer Protection fee until 8 October 2026). Say a buyer is choosing between two similar designer bags at £90. On yours they can add Item Verification for £10. That is a cost to them, but for many buyers of designer items it is the reason to buy from you rather than a listing without the badge. You receive the same item price either way.

## What you do differently as the seller

The sale looks normal until the buyer chooses verification. Then ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/1153-selling-with-the-item-verification-service)):

1. Vinted tells you the buyer has bought the service.
2. You get a prepaid label to Vinted's verification hub, not to the buyer. Only Vinted's integrated shipping is available for these orders ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/482-shipping-an-item)).
3. You send it within 5 business days, the same deadline as any other order. See [Vinted's shipping deadline](/blog/vinted-shipping-deadline-and-cancellations) for what happens if you miss it.
4. The hub checks it and, if it passes, sends it on to the buyer.
5. The buyer has the usual window to raise an issue or confirm the order. You are paid when they confirm, or when that window ends without a claim.

**For designer items,** Vinted asks you to put any original authenticity documents in the parcel, especially any you showed in the listing. Keep copies, and check pockets for personal belongings before you seal it.

**For electronics,** factory reset the device and remove all personal data and accounts first. Vinted recommends clear photos of the device, its accessories and any damage. Pack batteries carefully; Vinted has a page on [preparing electronics for shipping](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1334).

**A warning on sealed electronics.** Vinted says that if a sealed item goes through Electronics Verification, any existing warranty "may be affected" when it is opened for the check, and Vinted does not compensate for voided warranties or packaging damage ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1360-electronics-verification-service-for-buyers)). If you are selling something sealed, say so clearly in the listing so the buyer understands before they add the service.

## What happens if it fails

Vinted's page on [items that fail verification](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1185) gives three reasons an item can fail: it appears not to be authentic, it breaks the Catalogue Rules, or the parcel contains the wrong item. Vinted's electronics pages add that the check covers condition and listing details as well as authenticity, and that both sides are told the reason for any failure.

When an item fails, Vinted's help pages say:

- both of you are told, and the order is cancelled;
- the item is sent back to you in 1 to 2 business days and should arrive in 3 to 5 business days;
- the buyer gets a full refund of the price and all fees, within 1 to 2 business days of you receiving the item back;
- you cannot re-upload that item.

Vinted says it has a zero-tolerance policy on items that are not genuine, and that sellers who repeatedly list them will have their accounts blocked for good.

### The October 2026 terms on counterfeits

Vinted's new UK terms apply from 8 October 2026 and change what happens when verification finds a counterfeit. We cover the whole update in [Vinted's new terms](/blog/vinted-terms-october-2026). For verification, the [terms](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms_and_conditions) now say:

- an item that fails verification cannot be listed on Vinted again;
- an item verified as counterfeit can be held by Vinted for six weeks from its notice, so you can challenge the finding;
- if you do not provide proof of authenticity or a formal objection within those six weeks, Vinted can dispose of, recycle or destroy the item without further notice or compensation.

The help pages above still describe failed items being posted back to you. From 8 October, for items found to be counterfeit, expect the terms to take priority: the item may be held rather than returned. If you get a failure notice, read it carefully for which outcome applies, and act inside the six weeks.

## If you think the result is wrong

Verification is carried out by Vinted's own experts, and there is no published figure for how often it gets things wrong. If an item you are confident is genuine fails:

1. **Gather your proof straight away.** Receipt or order confirmation, the authenticity card or certificate, photos of the labels, serial numbers and hardware you took at listing.
2. **Challenge within six weeks** of Vinted's notice if the item is being held as counterfeit. Send proof or a formal objection, not an argument.
3. **Keep every message on Vinted,** and note dates.
4. **Do not relist it,** whatever the outcome. The terms say an item that failed cannot be listed again.

If you bought the item from a shop or another seller and it turns out not to be genuine, your complaint is with them, and you have the same consumer routes as any buyer. We cover what Trading Standards tells Vinted users, including chargebacks and small claims, in [what Trading Standards says about Vinted](/blog/what-trading-standards-says-about-vinted). Selling a counterfeit can also be a criminal matter for the seller, which is one more reason not to relist a failed item anywhere.

## A second check after delivery

Verification does not end the buyer's rights. Vinted says Buyer Protection still applies after verification for lost or damaged parcels and items not as described ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1360-electronics-verification-service-for-buyers)). If a buyer disputes authenticity after a designer item has passed, Vinted says it can go through a second verification at the hub, and if it fails again, the order is cancelled and the buyer refunded ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1185)).

## Should you want the badge?

The badge is not something you choose, so this is about how you list. In our view the service works in the honest seller's favour: an item that passes arrives with a report and a hangtag, which makes a "this is fake" claim much harder to sustain later. The risk sits entirely with items you cannot prove are genuine, and the answer to that is not to list them. Vinted's rules already say you may only list items you are sure are authentic ([Catalogue Rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules)).

## What goes wrong

- **Leaving the authenticity card out of the parcel.** Vinted asks for original documents to go to the hub.
- **Sending a phone still logged in to your account.** It must be reset and signed out.
- **Selling sealed electronics without warning the buyer** that verification opens the box.
- **Relisting an item that failed.** It is not allowed, before or after 8 October 2026.
- **Ignoring a counterfeit finding.** After six weeks, the item can be destroyed.
- **Sending with anything other than the hub label.** Only integrated shipping works for these orders.
