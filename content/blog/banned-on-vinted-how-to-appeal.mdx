This is for Vinted sellers who have logged in to find their account suspended or permanently banned, and for anyone who wants to know the route before it happens. By the end you will know how Vinted's appeal works, what to put in it, what Vinted's own pages say about your balance and open orders, and how to use two UK data protection rights: getting a copy of your data, and asking for a human to review a decision made by a system. It is based on Vinted's published terms and Help Centre and on guidance from the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO). It is general information, not legal advice.

## The short version

- Vinted's ban notice, in your Vinted inbox or by email, contains an appeal link. Vinted's help says you can appeal **once**, within **6 months** of the notice ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/92)).
- Temporary bans last 7 to 30 days depending on the reason, and lift on their own. Permanent bans need an appeal.
- If you are permanently banned, Vinted says you can still log in to withdraw your balance, unless the withdrawal poses a risk or the ban is for suspected fraud.
- Blocked accounts have their listings taken down, and pending orders may be cancelled and refunded to buyers.
- You can ask Vinted for a copy of the personal data it holds on you (a subject access request). Organisations usually have one month to reply ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/getting-copies-of-your-information-subject-access-request/)).
- If a decision was made by a system with no meaningful human involvement, UK data protection law gives you safeguards, including the right to obtain human intervention and to contest it.
- Do not open a new account while you wait. Vinted's terms allow you only one account (plus a separate Pro account where available).

## First, work out which kind of ban it is

Log in. Vinted's [ban and appeal help page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/92) says that for a temporary ban you will see the end date when you log in, and that temporary bans last 7 to 30 days depending on the reason. The account is unbanned automatically at the end. You can ask for an earlier unban only with evidence that the ban was a mistake.

A permanent ban has no end date. You can still access the account to do a few things, including contacting Vinted and withdrawing your balance, but you cannot delete it. Vinted says a permanently banned account cannot be deleted "due to safety reasons".

Then find the notice itself. Under section 7 of Vinted's [terms](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms-and-conditions), Vinted says it will tell you in writing when it takes a corrective action, and that the notice will tell you about your right to appeal. The terms allow Vinted to hold back that notice in some cases, for example where giving it would break the law or disrupt a fraud investigation. Screenshot it now.

## What happens to your balance, listings and orders

This part comes only from Vinted's own pages. We do not know how any individual case is handled.

| What | What Vinted says |
| --- | --- |
| Listings | Items are delisted and taken off the catalogue while the account is blocked ([terms, section 7](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms-and-conditions)) |
| Pending orders | "Might be cancelled", with fees refunded to the buyer ([terms, section 7](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms-and-conditions)) |
| Payouts | Your ability to make a payout "might be restricted" ([terms, section 7](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms-and-conditions)) |
| Balance after a permanent ban | You can log in and withdraw from your Vinted Balance, but withdrawals may not be possible if they pose a risk to other members, involve a compromised account, or the ban is due to a fraud risk as determined by the payment provider ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/92)) |

The version of the terms that applies until 8 October 2026 ([previous terms](https://www.vinted.co.uk/old-terms-and-conditions)) says a blocked member can generally complete pending transactions and payouts, and that cancellation and payout restrictions apply where the block is for security or fraud concerns. The new terms, which apply from 8 October 2026, state the consequences more broadly. Read the version in force on the date of your ban.

**What to do:** if you have a balance and the account allows it, withdraw it. If you have parcels already posted, keep proof of postage and tracking; if an order is cancelled after you sent it, you will need that evidence when you contact support.

## How to appeal

1. **Find the appeal link** in the Vinted inbox message or email that contains the moderation decision ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/92)).
2. **Check the deadline.** Six months from the notice. You get one appeal, so do not send a two-line reply in anger and waste it.
3. **Explain why the decision is wrong**, and attach evidence. Vinted's help asks you to explain why you think the decision was incorrect and to provide supporting evidence.
4. **Wait for the outcome.** Vinted's terms say appeals are reviewed "under the supervision of qualified staff". The terms also say that if you have a dispute with Vinted you can bring a claim in the courts where you live.

### What to put in it

Stick to facts Vinted can check, and keep it calm. A good appeal usually covers:

- **Your username, the email on the account and the date of the notice.**
- **The reason given**, quoted from the notice, and why it does not fit what happened.
- **Evidence for each point.** Receipts or proof of purchase for branded items. Screenshots of listings and messages. Proof of postage for disputed orders.
- **An honest account of anything you did that may have triggered it.** If you used a relisting tool, say so and say you have stopped. A believable explanation helps more than a flat denial.
- **What you are asking for**: the ban lifted, or at least a clear explanation of the reason.

### A common trigger: relisting and automation

Heavy delete-and-relist habits and outside automation tools are a common reason for restrictions. Vinted's terms say you must not "delete and re-list the same Item multiple times or multiple Items in bulk", and restrict bots and outside software. Our guide to [Vinted relisting and automation rules](/guides/vinted-relisting-and-automation-rules) sets out exactly what the terms say. If you sell regularly for profit, Vinted also expects you to use Vinted Pro rather than a standard account; see [Vinted Pro and selling as a business](/guides/vinted-pro-and-selling-as-a-business).

In August 2026 the BBC reported that sellers said they had been wrongly suspended or banned, with some suspecting automated systems were making incorrect fraud and counterfeit decisions. Vinted told the BBC its moderation combines trained teams, member reports and detection tools, and that human review teams assess appeals ([BBC News](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cx2kwnrn5zpo)). We cannot say how many bans are wrong, and neither can anyone outside Vinted.

## Your data rights

Vinted's terms say it may use automated tools to detect and act on problems. If you think a machine made the call, two UK data protection rights are worth knowing. They sit alongside the appeal, not instead of it.

### Getting a copy of your data

A subject access request (SAR) lets you ask any organisation whether it holds personal information about you, and for a copy. The [ICO's guidance for the public](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/getting-copies-of-your-information-subject-access-request/) says:

- you can make a request online, by email, by post, by phone or in person;
- it helps to put "subject access request" in the subject line and include your name, contact details and account identifiers;
- organisations usually have one month to respond.

For a ban, the useful parts are anything recording the moderation decision: flags, risk scores, the reason codes, and any notes from staff. The organisation may withhold some information, for example where it would affect a fraud investigation, so you may not get everything.

If Vinted does not reply, the ICO says to complain to the organisation first, then to the ICO. You should raise a complaint with the ICO within three months of your last meaningful contact with the organisation ([ICO: if you do not get a response](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/getting-copies-of-your-information-subject-access-request/what-to-do-if-you-dont-get-a-response-or-youre-unhappy-with-it/)). The ICO cannot award compensation.

Send the request to the privacy or data protection contact given in Vinted's [privacy policy](https://www.vinted.co.uk/privacy-policy), or through Vinted's [Help Centre contact form](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/615). Keep a copy and the date you sent it.

### A template request

Adapt this. Remove anything that does not apply.

> Subject: Subject access request
>
> Date: [date]
>
> I am making a subject access request under Article 15 of the UK GDPR.
>
> Name: [your full name]
> Vinted username: [username]
> Email address on the account: [email]
>
> Please provide a copy of all personal data you hold about me, including in particular:
>
> 1. All records relating to the suspension or ban of my account notified on [date], including the reasons, any flags, risk scores or reason codes, and any notes made by staff.
> 2. Details of whether the decision was made solely by automated means and, if so, meaningful information about the logic involved.
> 3. The purposes of processing, the recipients of my data, and how long you will keep it.
>
> Please send the information by email in a commonly used electronic format. If you need anything further to confirm my identity, please tell me as soon as possible.
>
> [your name]

### Asking for a human to review an automated decision

UK GDPR gives people protection against decisions based solely on automated processing that have legal or similarly significant effects. The [ICO's guidance for the public](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/your-rights-relating-to-decisions-being-made-about-you-without-human-involvement/) says that where such a decision is made, you can ask for human intervention, express your view and challenge the decision. That page says it is under review because of changes made by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025.

The ICO says all stages of that Act are now in force ([ICO: how does this affect me](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/what-we-do/legislation-we-cover/data-use-and-access-act-2025/the-data-use-and-access-act-2025-how-does-this-affect-me/)). Under the new rules, organisations can make significant automated decisions in more situations, but must put safeguards in place. The ICO's [summary of the changes](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/what-we-do/legislation-we-cover/data-use-and-access-act-2025/the-data-use-and-access-act-2025-duaa-summary-of-the-changes/data-protection/) lists them: give you information about the decision, let you make representations, let you obtain human intervention, and let you contest the decision.

Two honest caveats:

- **It only applies to decisions made solely by automated means.** If a person meaningfully reviewed the ban, this right does not apply, though the SAR still does.
- **Whether a ban is "significant" is not settled for every case.** Losing an account you rely on for income is a strong argument that it is. Losing a casual account is a weaker one. We are not aware of an ICO ruling on marketplace bans specifically.

**How to ask:** add a short paragraph to your SAR, or send a separate message: "If the decision to ban my account was based solely on automated processing, I ask that it is reviewed by a person, and I wish to make the following representations: [your points]." The ICO says you can make these requests verbally or in writing, and suggests confirming a verbal request in writing.

## What goes wrong

- **Opening a new account.** Vinted's [terms](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms-and-conditions) allow only one account, plus one separate Pro account where Pro is available. The only exception they mention is when your original account was blocked because someone unlawfully took it over.
- **Wasting the one appeal.** Draft it, gather evidence, then send it.
- **Paying a "recovery service".** No outside service has special access to Vinted's appeals.
