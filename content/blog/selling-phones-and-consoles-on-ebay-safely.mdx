This is for anyone selling a used phone, tablet, games console or similar device on eBay UK, whether it is your own old handset or stock you buy to resell. By the end you will know how to remove your data and accounts using the manufacturers' own instructions, what to record before the parcel goes, how to describe condition so it does not come back as "not as described", which checks to run, and the scam patterns to watch for. It is general information, not legal advice.

## The short version

- Back up, then **remove your accounts before you reset**. On an iPhone that means turning off Find My; on Android it means removing your Google Account, or the next owner hits Factory Reset Protection.
- For consoles, deactivate or unlink your account, then use the full factory reset, not a "keep my files" option.
- Take out SIM and memory cards, and erase any eSIM when you wipe.
- Record the **serial number and IMEI** before you post, and photograph them. They are how you prove which device you sent.
- Describe condition, battery health and network lock status plainly. Test what you can and say what you have not tested.
- eBay does not allow "kill switch-activated" devices to be sold ([encouraging illegal activity policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/prohibited-restricted-items/encouraging-illegal-activity-policy?id=4339)). A locked phone is not a "spares or repairs" loophole.
- Post only to the address on the order, with tracking, and with signature confirmation for orders of £450 or more.

## Step 1: back up and check your accounts

The National Cyber Security Centre's guide to [buying, selling and donating second-hand devices](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/buying-selling-second-hand-devices) says to do three things before erasing anything:

1. **Back up** the personal data you want to keep, because the wipe removes all of it.
2. **List the accounts** you use on the device (email, banking, shopping, social media) and make sure you know the logins, because the device will not log you in afterwards.
3. **Move two-factor authentication** to another device, and check it works, before you erase the one you are selling. The same goes for any smart home devices you control from the phone.

This is the step people skip, then find they are locked out of their own bank app because the codes went with the handset.

## Step 2: remove accounts and wipe the device

### iPhone and iPad

Apple's guide to [what to do before you sell or give away your iPhone or iPad](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/109511) covers it. In summary:

1. Unpair any Apple Watch.
2. Back up the device.
3. Sign out of iCloud and the App Store.
4. If moving to a non-Apple phone, deregister iMessage.
5. Go to **Settings > General > Transfer or Reset [device] > Erase All Content and Settings**. If the device uses an eSIM, choose to erase the eSIM profile too.
6. Remove the device from your list of trusted devices.

Apple says that once this is done, Find My and **Activation Lock** are turned off. Activation Lock is the one that matters for a sale: if it is still on, the buyer cannot set the phone up without your Apple Account. If you forget, Apple explains how to [remove Activation Lock](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/108934) remotely.

### Android phones and tablets

Google's page on [Factory Reset Protection](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9459346?hl=en-GB) explains the trap. Protection switches on when a Google Account is added and a screen lock is set. After a reset, whoever sets the device up must get past the previous screen lock or enter the password of a Google Account previously synced to it.

So the order matters: **remove your Google Account from the device first**, and remove the screen lock, then factory reset from Settings. Menu names vary by manufacturer. If the phone also has a manufacturer account, remove that too.

### PlayStation

PlayStation's page on [deactivating a console](https://www.playstation.com/en-gb/support/account/deactivate-playstation-console/) gives the steps:

- **PS5:** Settings > Users and Accounts > Other > Console Sharing and Offline Play > Disable.
- **PS4:** Settings > Account Management > Activate as Your Primary PS4 > Deactivate.

Deactivating does not delete data, so initialise (factory reset) the console afterwards. If you have already let the console go, you can deactivate remotely, but PlayStation says you can only do that **once every six months**. Do it on the console before you post.

### Xbox

Microsoft's guide to [what to do before you sell or gift your Xbox](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/accounts-billing/security/before-you-recycle-sell-or-gift-your-xbox-or-windows-pc) says to back up, reset the console to factory defaults, then sign in to your Microsoft account's devices page and remove and unlink the Xbox so it no longer counts against your device limit.

### Nintendo Switch

Nintendo says the way to unlink all Nintendo Accounts from a console is to restore it to factory settings ([Nintendo UK support](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Support/Troubleshooting/How-to-Unlink-a-Nintendo-Account-From-a-Nintendo-Switch-2-or-Nintendo-Switch-Console-1228926.html)). Back up save data first if you want to keep it.

### Everything else

For laptops, smart watches, speakers and TVs, the [NCSC guide](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/buying-selling-second-hand-devices) says to follow the manufacturer's factory reset instructions. It also warns that if the reset offers to keep your personal files, do not choose that option when the device is leaving you. Take out SIM cards and memory cards before you reset.

## Step 3: record what you are sending

Before the device goes in the box, record:

- **Serial number and IMEI.** On an iPhone, Apple says the serial number is in Settings > General > About, and the IMEI is also on the SIM tray of iPhone 13 and later and on the original box ([Apple: find the serial number or IMEI](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/108037)). Android phones show them in the About section of Settings.
- **Photos of the identifiers** on the device, box and settings screen.
- **Photos of every mark,** and of the screen switched on.
- **The parcel weight** from your postage label.

In our view, showing the serial or IMEI in listing photos is sensible for higher-value items, because it ties that exact device to your listing. Some sellers prefer to show part of it and keep the full number in their records. Either way, keep the full record.

This is your defence if a different, broken or blocked device comes back as a return. Our post on [when an eBay return comes back swapped, empty or damaged](/blog/ebay-returns-switched-or-damaged) covers what to do then.

## Step 4: check the device can be sold

### Kill switch and lock status

eBay's [encouraging illegal activity policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/prohibited-restricted-items/encouraging-illegal-activity-policy?id=4339) lists "kill switch-activated electronic devices, including smartphones and tablets" as not allowed. If a phone is stuck on Activation Lock or Factory Reset Protection and you cannot remove it, do not list it on eBay.

If you bought the device to resell, check it is fully signed out and resets cleanly before you pay, or at least before you list.

### Blocked or reported devices

Networks can block a phone reported lost or stolen. Citizens Advice tells owners to report a lost or stolen phone to their network "so they can block it and stop anyone else using it" ([Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/phone-internet-downloads-or-tv/what-to-do-if-your-mobile-phone-is-lost-or-stolen/)). A blocked phone will not work on a mobile network even after a clean reset.

There are paid IMEI check services that report whether a device has been recorded as lost, stolen or blocked. If you buy phones to resell, running a check on each one and keeping the result is a sensible habit. eBay's [stolen property policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/prohibited-restricted-items/stolen-property-policy?id=4334) does not allow stolen goods to be listed.

## Step 5: describe it honestly

Most tech returns start with a gap between the listing and the device. Close it.

- **Model, storage and colour** exactly as the device reports them, not as you remember.
- **Network status.** Say whether it works on any network or is tied to one. If you have not checked, say so.
- **Battery health.** On an iPhone, Apple says it is in **Settings > Battery > Battery Health**, shown as maximum capacity relative to new ([Apple: iPhone battery and performance](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/101575)). Give the figure and a screenshot. If the phone shows a message that battery health is significantly degraded, say so. Where an Android phone or console does not show battery health, say that too rather than guessing.
- **Screen and body.** Scratches, dead pixels, screen burn, cracked back glass, worn buttons. Photograph them with the screen on.
- **What you tested.** "Calls, camera, Wi-Fi and charging tested; Face ID not tested" is honest and specific. "Works perfectly" is a promise.
- **What is included.** Charger, cable, controller, box. Missing items are a common "not as described" reason.
- **Replaced parts.** A non-original screen or battery should be in the description.

Our guides to [descriptions that sell](/guides/descriptions-that-sell) and [photos on a phone](/guides/photos-on-a-phone) go further.

## Scam patterns that target tech sellers

These come up again and again with phones and consoles.

**"Can you post it to a different address?"** eBay's [seller protections](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-protections?id=4345) for item not received need tracking uploaded before the latest estimated delivery date, a "delivered" or "attempted delivery" status, and a delivery address that matches the one on the order details page. Post to a different address and you lose that protection. Say no, politely.

**"Let's do it off eBay."** Requests to pay by bank transfer, or to swap phone numbers before the sale completes, break eBay's [policy on buying or selling outside eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/payment-policies/offering-buy-sell-outside-ebay-policy?id=4272), and eBay says those transactions are not covered by its protection programmes or the Money Back Guarantee.

**Payment "confirmation" by email.** Only post when Seller Hub shows the order as paid. An email is not proof of payment.

**High-value, no signature.** For orders of £450 or more, eBay's [signature confirmation policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behaviour-policies/signature-confirmation-policy?id=5154) says you need signature confirmation to be protected against an item not received claim. The £450 is the total the buyer paid, including postage.

**The swapped return.** A "not as described" return arrives with an older model, a blocked handset, or a phone with your parts swapped out. This is why Step 3 matters.

**"It's locked to your account."** A buyer claims the phone is Activation Locked or FRP locked. If you followed Step 2, you can show your screenshots and check your Apple or Google account's device list. If you missed a step, remove the device from your account remotely and tell the buyer.

If a message or offer feels wrong, run it through our [scam check](/tools/scam-check) before you reply.

## Fees and business selling

If you sell your own old devices now and then, you are a private seller, and selling is free in most cases ([private seller fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822)). If you buy phones or consoles to resell, you are likely to count as a business seller; see [when eBay says you are a business seller](/blog/when-ebay-says-you-are-a-business-seller). eBay's [business fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/business-selling-fees?id=4809) set lower final value fee rates for mobile and smart phones and for video game consoles than for many other categories. The [eBay fee calculator](/tools/fees/ebay) shows the current rates on a sale.

## What goes wrong

- **Resetting before signing out.** It leaves the phone locked to you and a buyer with a brick.
- **Choosing "keep my files".** Your data leaves with the device.
- **No record of the serial or IMEI.** Then a swapped return is your word against the buyer's.
- **"Fully working" without testing.** Test, and say what you did not test.
- **Posting somewhere other than the order address** because a buyer asked nicely.

If you are unsure about a device or a buyer, ask in [eBay buyers and disputes](/community/c/ebay-buyers-and-disputes).
