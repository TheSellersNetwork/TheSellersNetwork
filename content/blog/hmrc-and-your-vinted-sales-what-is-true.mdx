This is for anyone who sells on Vinted, eBay or similar sites and has seen a headline saying HMRC is coming for their sales. We take the claims that keep coming back, test each against what HMRC and GOV.UK actually say, and end with a plain checklist of when you do need to tell HMRC. It is general information, not tax advice; if your situation is unusual, HMRC's own checker or an accountant is the next step.

## The short version

- **"HMRC will tax your Vinted sales."** Not as a rule. There is no new tax. HMRC says the online selling tax rules have not changed.
- **"Platforms now report you to HMRC."** True, once you reach 30 sales or about £1,700 (2,000 euros) in a calendar year. Being reported does not mean you owe tax.
- **"Selling your old stuff is taxable now."** Not usually. Selling your own unwanted belongings is not normally trading.
- **"Go over £1,000 and you must register."** Only half right. The £1,000 is the trading allowance, and it applies to gross trading income, not to money from selling your own things.
- **"Big-ticket items are a problem."** Possibly. Capital Gains Tax can apply when a personal possession sells for £6,000 or more.
- **"A £40m crackdown on Vinted sellers."** We could not find this figure anywhere on GOV.UK.

## Claim 1: "HMRC will tax your Vinted sales"

**What is true.** Since 1 January 2024, UK digital platforms have had to collect seller details and report them to HMRC ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform)). That is a reporting rule, not a tax.

**What HMRC says.** HMRC's online selling guidance states that "the online selling tax rules haven't changed" and that you can still make up to £1,000 a year tax-free from side hustles, as you have since 2017 ([HMRC](https://taxhelpforhustles.campaign.gov.uk/buying-or-making-things-to-sell-and-online-selling-tax-rules)). In December 2024 the government published a press release titled [No tax changes for online sellers](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/no-tax-changes-for-online-sellers) to say the same thing. Vinted's own [HMRC page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/1149-hmrc-requirements-what-you-need-to-know) says completing a seller report "doesn't mean there's an obligation to pay taxes on your Vinted sales".

**Verdict.** False as a general statement. Tax depends on what you are selling and why, exactly as it did before.

## Claim 2: "Sell 30 items and HMRC gets your details"

**What is true.** Platforms do not have to report you if you make fewer than 30 sales of goods and receive 2,000 euros (about £1,700) or less in a calendar year ([GOV.UK reporting rules](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/reporting-rules-for-digital-platforms)). Pass either limit and you will be reported. The report includes your name, address, date of birth and tax identification number, which for UK individuals is your National Insurance number ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform)). Platforms report by 31 January for the previous calendar year and give you a copy, so you can see what HMRC has.

**What it does not mean.** GOV.UK says it plainly: "A platform reporting your details to HMRC does not automatically mean you owe tax."

**Verdict.** True, and worth knowing, but it is information-sharing. Our [platform reporting check](/tools/tax?tab=reporting) tells you whether you are likely to be reported.

## Claim 3: "Selling your old stuff is taxable now"

**What HMRC says.** "If you're just selling unwanted personal belongings from time to time, like old toys and clothes, whether it's online or in person, you don't usually need to tell HMRC" ([HMRC](https://taxhelpforhustles.campaign.gov.uk/buying-or-making-things-to-sell-and-online-selling-tax-rules)). GOV.UK adds that you are unlikely to pay tax if you sell personal items from your home, like the contents of a loft or garage ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform)).

The line HMRC draws is between clearing out and **trading**. If you buy or make goods to sell at a profit, you are likely to be trading. HMRC decides this with the badges of trade in its [Business Income Manual](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/business-income-manual/bim20205), which look at things like your profit motive, how often you sell, whether you bought the item in order to sell it, whether you changed or repaired it, and how you sold it. No single badge decides it.

Vinted draws a similar line for its own reasons. Its [commercial selling page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/1120-commercial-selling) says that if you buy or source items to resell at a profit, you need a Pro account. See [Vinted Pro and selling as a business](/guides/vinted-pro-and-selling-as-a-business).

**Verdict.** False for genuine clear-outs. True for reselling, but that was always taxable.

## Claim 4: "Sell something valuable and you pay tax on it"

**What is true.** Your own belongings can be subject to Capital Gains Tax, not Income Tax, in some cases. GOV.UK says you may have to pay Capital Gains Tax if you make a profit when you sell a personal possession for £6,000 or more ([GOV.UK: personal possessions](https://www.gov.uk/capital-gains-tax-personal-possessions)). The £6,000 applies to a single item, or to a set of things sold together, such as matching chairs.

Things to know:

- It is about **profit**. If you sell a £7,000 watch for less than you paid, there is no gain.
- Your **car** is exempt unless you used it for business, and so are possessions with a **limited lifespan**, such as clocks, unless used for business.
- There is also an annual tax-free allowance for gains, currently £3,000 on the [Capital Gains Tax allowances page](https://www.gov.uk/capital-gains-tax/allowances).

HMRC's side hustle page puts this in shorthand: it is only if you have sold a single personal item or collection for more than £6,000 that you need to tell them. The GOV.UK guide is the detailed version: check it if you are close to the line.

**Verdict.** True, but only for high-value personal possessions sold at a gain. The ordinary Vinted wardrobe is nowhere near it.

## Claim 5: "Go over £1,000 and you must register"

**What is true.** The £1,000 figure is the **trading allowance**: a tax exemption of up to £1,000 a year of trading income ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tax-free-allowances-on-property-and-trading-income)).

The detail the headlines leave out:

1. **It only counts trading income.** Money from selling your own unwanted belongings is not trading income, so it does not count towards the £1,000 at all.
2. **It is gross, not profit.** GOV.UK says gross income is the total before any allowances or expenses are taken off. Say you buy stock for £700 and sell it for £1,200: your profit is £500, but your gross trading income is £1,200, which is over the allowance.
3. **It is one allowance across all your trading.** HMRC says anything you earn from different side hustles counts towards the same £1,000.
4. **Over £1,000, you choose.** You can deduct the £1,000 allowance or your actual expenses, whichever works better for you, but not both.

In the example above, you would deduct your £700 of actual expenses rather than the £1,000 allowance, leaving £500 of taxable profit to report. Your Personal Allowance and other income then decide whether any tax is due.

**A change announced but not yet in force.** In March 2025 the government announced that the Self Assessment reporting threshold for trading income would rise from £1,000 to £3,000 gross "within this parliament", with a new simple online service for people who owe tax ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/boost-for-side-hustlers-as-300000-people-to-be-taken-out-of-tax-returns-government-announces)). We have not found a start date on GOV.UK, so until one is published, work to £1,000.

**Verdict.** Half true. Over £1,000 of gross **trading** income, you need to tell HMRC. Over £1,000 from selling your own things, you do not.

## Claim 6: "A £40m crackdown on Vinted sellers"

This figure appears in articles and social posts. We searched GOV.UK and HMRC's publications and could not find any announcement of a £40 million campaign aimed at Vinted or eBay sellers. What GOV.UK does show is the platform reporting regime above, and HMRC statements that the tax rules for online sellers have not changed.

That does not mean HMRC ignores the data. It receives it so it can compare what platforms report with what people declare, and people who are trading and have not registered should expect that gap to be noticed. But the specific number is not something we could verify, so treat any story built on it with caution.

**Verdict.** Unverified. The data sharing is real; the figure is not on any official page we found.

## When you do need to tell HMRC

Work through these in order. HMRC's [online platforms checker](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-tell-hmrc-about-your-income-from-online-platforms) covers the same ground.

1. **Are you selling things you bought or made to sell?** If no, and they are your own belongings, you usually do not need to tell HMRC. Go to step 4.
2. **Was your gross trading income over £1,000 in the tax year (6 April to 5 April)?** Add up everything you took from trading, across every platform and every side hustle. If it was £1,000 or less, you may not need to tell HMRC.
3. **If it was over £1,000, register for Self Assessment** as a sole trader, if you are not already registered. The deadline is 5 October after the end of the tax year. For the 2025 to 2026 tax year, that is **5 October 2026**, and GOV.UK says you could get a penalty if you tell them later ([register for Self Assessment](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-self-assessment)).
4. **Did you sell a single personal possession or set for £6,000 or more at a profit?** If so, read the [Capital Gains Tax guidance](https://www.gov.uk/capital-gains-tax-personal-possessions) and work out your gain.

Our [tax dates tool](/tools/tax) lists the registration, filing and payment deadlines, and our guide to [bookkeeping and tax for resellers](/guides/bookkeeping-and-tax-for-resellers) covers records, expenses and the rest.

## What goes wrong

- **Panicking about the report.** It is a copy of your sales, not a tax bill. Check it matches your own records.
- **Counting profit instead of takings.** The £1,000 test uses gross income.
- **Forgetting other platforms.** One allowance covers all your trading, on every site.
- **Mixing personal and resale sales without records.** If you do both, keep a simple list of which items were bought to resell. It makes the question easy to answer later.
- **Relying on a headline.** Check the GOV.UK page it should have linked to.
