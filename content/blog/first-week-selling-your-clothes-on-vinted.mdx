This is for anyone opening a Vinted account to sell clothes they no longer wear. It breaks your first week into one job a day, so by day seven you will have a set of good listings, know how postage and offers work, have posted your first parcel on time and know when the money reaches your bank. Everything here comes from Vinted's own Help Centre, and Vinted changes its pages often, so check the linked page if something looks different. It is general information, not legal or financial advice.

## The short version

- Selling on Vinted costs you nothing. The buyer pays postage and a fee on top of your price.
- Turn on extra security and keep every message and payment inside Vinted.
- Photograph each item in daylight, show every flaw, and add measurements, not just the size on the label.
- Price by looking at similar items, then leave a little room for offers.
- When something sells you have 5 business days to post it, using the prepaid label, at a drop-off point, never a postbox.
- The money reaches your balance after the buyer confirms, then up to 5 business days to your bank.
- A standard account is for selling your own things. If you start buying clothes to resell, you need Vinted Pro.

## Day 1: set up your account safely

Download the app or sign up on the website, then do three things before you list anything.

1. **Turn on extra security.** Vinted's [phishing advice](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/628-how-to-avoid-and-report-phishing-messages-scams) recommends adding multi-factor authentication.
2. **Activate your Vinted Balance.** Vinted says you need to activate it to start selling ([Vinted Balance](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/437-vinted-balance-how-it-works)). Use your legal name as your bank has it, because withdrawals can be blocked if the names do not match ([withdrawals](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/68-how-to-withdraw-earnings)).
3. **Learn what a scam looks like.** Vinted's emails come from an address ending in @vinted.co.uk. Its advice is to avoid leaving Vinted to talk to other members or to send payments, and not to share personal details in messages ([Vinted](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/628-how-to-avoid-and-report-phishing-messages-scams)). A buyer who asks for your email, phone number or bank details, or sends you a link or QR code to "receive payment", is not a real buyer.

While you are in settings, choose a username that does not include your full name.

## Day 2: sort and clean

Pull out everything you want to sell and split it into three piles: ready to list, needs a wash or a press, and not worth selling. Check each item against Vinted's [catalogue rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules). Most everyday clothes are fine. The exceptions to know:

- Underwear bottoms can only be sold new, with tags or original packaging showing.
- Worn bras, swimwear, tights and socks can be sold if properly washed.
- Items that do not meet hygiene standards are not allowed.

Wash and dry what needs it. A clean, pressed item photographs better and is less likely to come back with a complaint about a smell or a mark.

## Day 3: photos

Vinted allows up to 20 photos per listing ([Vinted](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/48-what-photos-you-should-upload)). Its guidance:

- The first photo shows the whole item. No collages.
- Use natural daylight, not lamplight, and no flash or filters.
- Add close-ups of the logo, care label and stitching.
- Show any defects. Vinted warns that if you do not mention them, the buyer may want to cancel and return the item.

The [catalogue rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules) also say photos must be your own. Do not use pictures from the brand's website.

A simple routine that works: hang or lay the item flat against a plain wall or sheet near a window, take the front, the back, the label, and any flaw. Four to six photos is plenty for most clothes. Our guide to [photos on a phone](/guides/photos-on-a-phone) covers the rest.

## Day 4: measurements and descriptions

Sizes vary between brands, and older clothes shrink or stretch. Measurements are what stop "does this fit?" messages and "it does not fit" disappointments. Measure each item flat: across the chest from armpit to armpit, and length from shoulder to hem for tops; waist and inside leg for trousers. Our guide to [measuring clothes for listings](/guides/measuring-clothes-for-listings) shows every measurement step by step.

For the description, keep it plain:

- Brand, item, size on the label and colour.
- Measurements.
- Condition, honestly. "Worn a few times, small bobbling under the arms, see photo 5" is better than "good condition".
- Material, if the care label shows it.

Pick the right category and condition. An accurate listing is your best protection if a buyer later says the item is not as described.

## Day 5: price it

There are no selling fees on Vinted; the buyer pays a fee on top of your price at checkout ([How it works](https://www.vinted.co.uk/how_it_works)). What you set is what you receive, before any discount you agree.

Vinted's [pricing page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/376-how-to-set-a-correct-price) gives a starting point based on condition:

| Condition | Vinted's suggested range |
| --- | --- |
| New or like new | 60% to 80% of the retail price |
| Very good | 40% to 60% of the retail price |
| Good or satisfactory | 20% to 40% of the retail price |

Then check the real market. Search Vinted for the same brand and item to see how other members price it, as Vinted suggests, and look at what similar items actually sold for on eBay using the sold filter. Our guide to [pricing from sold listings](/guides/pricing-from-sold-comps) explains how. Asking prices tell you what people hope for; sold prices tell you what buyers pay.

Say a jacket cost £60 new and is in very good condition. Vinted's range suggests £24 to £36. If similar jackets are listed at around £25, pricing at £28 leaves room for an offer and still sits in the range.

## Day 6: postage, offers and bundles

### Postage

On Vinted the buyer chooses a carrier at checkout and pays for the label ([shipping methods](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/234-shipping-methods)). You choose which carriers you are happy to use in your settings, under Postage ([Vinted](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/447-managing-shipping-options-for-selling)). Turn on the ones with a drop-off point near you. Our guide to [Vinted postage options](/guides/vinted-postage-options) compares them.

Choose the parcel size carefully when you list. Vinted says to use packaging that is about the same size as the item, and that once a buyer has paid, the size and carrier cannot be changed ([parcel size](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/51-choosing-the-right-parcel-size)).

### Offers

Buyers can press "Make an offer". Vinted's [offers page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/258-i-want-to-make-an-offer-or-suggest-a-different-price) says:

- Offers can be up to 40% below the listed price.
- You can decline and send your own counter-offer.
- Offers are not binding. The item stays available to everyone until someone presses "Buy now".

Decide your lowest price before the offers arrive, so you are not deciding on the spot.

### Bundles

Buyers can bundle several of your items into one parcel ([bundles](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/257-selling-a-bundle)). You can set a bundle discount in Settings to encourage it ([Vinted](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/422-set-up-a-discount-for-your-closet)). For a wardrobe clear-out, a modest bundle discount is worth switching on: one parcel and one trip to the drop-off for several sales.

## Day 7: your first sale, posting and getting paid

### Posting on time

Vinted says to send the item within 5 business days of the purchase ([shipping an item](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/482-shipping-an-item)). The prepaid label or code appears in your conversation with the buyer and in your email. You must use that label: Vinted says a different label or the wrong shipping option means the order is cancelled automatically ([shipping methods](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/234-shipping-methods)).

1. Pack the item neatly in a bag or box that fits.
2. Take photos of the item and the packed parcel, inside and out. Vinted recommends this ([selling basics](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/26-selling-step-by-step)).
3. Drop it off at the right place for the carrier. For Royal Mail, Vinted says do not use a postbox, because Royal Mail cannot register the collection. Use a Post Office, Collect+ shop, locker or customer service point ([Vinted](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/528-royal-mail-standard-2nd-class-shipping)).
4. Keep the receipt until the order is complete.

If a parcel sent with a Vinted label is confirmed lost or damaged, you can get compensation up to £500, as long as it was properly packed ([Vinted](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1018)).

### Getting paid

The money moves in stages ([getting paid](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/235-getting-paid-for-a-completed-sale)):

| Stage | When |
| --- | --- |
| Order completes | When the buyer presses "Everything is OK", or 2 days after they get it if they do nothing |
| Money reaches your available balance | Within 2 days of the order completing |
| Money reaches your bank | Up to 5 business days after you press Withdraw |

The minimum withdrawal is £0.10, it goes in pounds to an account with a UK account number and sort code, the account name should match your Vinted Balance details, and you cannot use one bank account for several Vinted accounts ([withdrawals](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/68-how-to-withdraw-earnings)). One thing to watch: if you buy something on Vinted, your balance is used first automatically ([Vinted Balance](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/437-vinted-balance-how-it-works)). Withdraw before you shop if you want the money in the bank.

### If the buyer has a problem

The buyer has 2 days to report an issue. Vinted refunds for items that are lost, arrived damaged, or are significantly not as described. An item that matches its description but does not fit or suit the buyer is not eligible for a refund ([refund policy](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/465)). This is why honest photos and measurements matter.

## After week one: when you would need Vinted Pro

A standard account is for selling your own things. Vinted's [commercial selling page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/1120-commercial-selling) says standard accounts "can't be used to sell items professionally", and that if you sell on Vinted to make a profit you need to sign up as a Pro seller.

Clearing your own wardrobe, however big, is not that. Buying clothes at car boots or charity shops to sell on is. If you find you enjoy it and start sourcing stock, read our guide to [Vinted Pro and selling as a business](/guides/vinted-pro-and-selling-as-a-business) first. Tax works the same way: selling your own things is not usually taxable, and buying to resell usually is. Our post on [HMRC and your Vinted sales](/blog/hmrc-and-your-vinted-sales-what-is-true) explains the difference.

## What goes wrong

- **Dropping a Royal Mail parcel in a postbox.** It will not be registered. Use a counter or a locker.
- **Choosing the wrong parcel size.** It cannot be changed after the sale, and the carrier may refuse it.
- **Missing a flaw in the photos.** It is the most common reason for a not as described claim.
- **Moving a conversation to WhatsApp or email.** You lose Vinted's protection, and it is a common scam.
- **Waiting too long to post.** Five business days goes quickly. Post the same or next day if you can.
