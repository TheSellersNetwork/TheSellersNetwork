This is for resellers who want clean product photos on a white or plain background for eBay, Vinted, Etsy and elsewhere, and are deciding between a paid app, a free tier or a free tool. It compares five well-known services and this site's own free background remover, using only what each provider publishes on its own pricing pages, help centre, terms and privacy policy. It covers price, what the free version really allows, output size, bulk editing, where your photos go, and whether you are allowed to use the results for selling. We have no arrangement with any provider, and our own pick, at the end, rests only on the published facts. It is general information, not legal advice.

## The short version

- Two of the free versions are for **non-commercial use only**: Photoroom's help centre says the free plan cannot be used commercially, and remove.bg restricts its free previews to non-commercial use. Selling is commercial use.
- Paid plans start at £8 a month (remove.bg, 40 images), about £100 a year (Canva Pro), £9.98 a month (Adobe Express Premium), and $10 to $12.99 a month for Pixelcut and Photoroom.
- Every other service here either says your photos are uploaded to it, or does not say where they are processed. Three privacy policies (Photoroom, remove.bg and Canva) say uploads may be used to train or improve their AI, with a setting to opt out in some cases.
- Our [background remover](/tools/background-remover) runs on your own device, is free and handles big batches, but it is basic. Paid tools may do a better job on hair, fur, see-through items and busy scenes.
- Output size limits vary a lot, from 0.25 megapixels on remove.bg's free previews to 50 megapixels on its paid credits.
- For most listing photos, a clear, well-lit picture of the real item matters more than the tool.
- For simple product shots on a budget we would choose our free background remover; for fur, hair, glass or shiny items we would test a paid tool. See our verdict below.

## What matters most for resellers

1. **Commercial use on the plan you are on.** If you are selling, you need a plan whose terms allow it.
2. **Free tier limits.** Number of images, resolution and whether you can use the result for selling.
3. **Output size.** Enough for zoom on eBay and Etsy, without huge files.
4. **Bulk editing.** Whether you can do a whole batch at once, and on which plan.
5. **Where photos are processed.** On your device, or uploaded, and what the privacy policy says about training.
6. **Price in pounds, including VAT.** Several are shown in US dollars.

## Photoroom

Photoroom's [pricing page](https://www.photoroom.com/pricing) showed us US dollar prices: Pro at $12.99 a month or $89.99 a year, Max at $34.99 a month or $249.99 a year, and Ultra at $99 a month or $990 a year. Its FAQ says VAT is already included.

**Good**

- Batch editing and exporting multiple images at once on paid plans ([plans](https://help.photoroom.com/en/articles/6976012-what-are-photoroom-s-plans)).
- Paid export allowances are published: 1,000 a month on Pro, 3,000 on Max and 10,000 on Ultra ([export limits](https://help.photoroom.com/en/articles/15284788-export-limit)).
- Apps for iPhone, Android and the web.

**Bad**

- The free plan allows 100 exports a month, and Photoroom's plans article says of the free plan: "You cannot use Photoroom for commercial use".
- The pricing FAQ says export limits apply to images up to 2K resolution; 4K is listed only on Ultra.
- The [privacy policy](https://www.photoroom.com/legal/privacy) says Photoroom uses the images you upload to improve and train its products. You can opt out in your account settings.

## remove.bg

remove.bg is now a Canva brand, run by Canva Austria GmbH. Its [pricing page](https://www.remove.bg/pricing) lists a Personal subscription at £8 a month for 40 images, or £7.20 a month billed yearly, and Professional plans from £35 a month for 200 images. Pay-as-you-go credits start at £2.70 for 3 images. One full-resolution image uses one credit.

**Good**

- High resolution on paid credits: up to 50 megapixels in and out, with files up to 22 MB ([help](https://www.remove.bg/help/a/what-is-the-maximum-image-resolution-file-size)).
- Commercial use is allowed on subscriptions and on pay-as-you-go full-resolution images ([commercial use](https://www.remove.bg/help/a/can-i-use-remove-bg-for-commercial-purposes)).
- Prices in pounds, with a pay-as-you-go option if you only need a few images.
- Its [privacy policy](https://www.remove.bg/privacy) says uploads are processed and then deleted "shortly after".

**Bad**

- Free use gives previews only, up to 0.25 megapixels (about 625 by 400 pixels), and the free plan is non-commercial only.
- Subscription credits last only while the subscription is active ([plans compared](https://www.remove.bg/help/a/what-is-the-difference-between-the-free-plan-subscription-and-pay-as-you-go)).
- The same privacy policy says account content and uploads may be analysed to train its algorithms and AI.
- The pricing page does not say whether VAT is included.

## Pixelcut

Pixelcut's [pricing page](https://www.pixelcut.ai/pricing) lists Free at $0, Pro at $10 a month or $8 a month billed yearly, and Business at $30 a month or $24 a month billed yearly.

**Good**

- The free plan lists limited background removal and "Free export without watermark".
- Pro lists unlimited background removal, 1,000 batch exports a month and a commercial licence.

**Bad**

- The commercial licence is listed on Pro and Business, not on the free plan.
- Output resolution is not published on the pricing page.
- The [privacy policy](https://www.pixelcut.ai/privacy) names cloud storage services for hosting but does not say whether photos are processed on your device or on its servers, or how long images are kept.
- Priced in US dollars; VAT is not mentioned.

## Canva's background remover

Canva's [help centre](https://www.canva.com/help/background-remover/) says the background remover "is included with paid plans". Canva's [UK pricing page](https://www.canva.com/en_gb/pricing/) lists Pro at £100 a year for one person and Business at £180 a year per person, with a free trial of both.

**Good**

- Prices in pounds, and the tool sits inside a design app many sellers already use for banners and social posts.
- The pricing table lists background removal "including at scale" on paid plans.

**Bad**

- Not on the free plan.
- It only works on photos under 9 MB, photos over 10 megapixels are downscaled to 10 megapixels after removal, and folders with more than 50 images cannot use it.
- Canva's [privacy policy](https://www.canva.com/policies/privacy-policy/) says it may analyse media you upload to train its algorithms and AI. You can manage this in your privacy settings.
- We found no statement on commercial use specific to this tool. Canva's help centre covers commercial use of designs in general.

## Adobe Express

Adobe's [UK pricing page](https://www.adobe.com/uk/express/pricing) lists "Remove Image Background" on the Free, Premium and Teams plans. Premium is £9.98 a month including VAT, billed monthly, with a 30-day free trial. The background remover page now opens in Adobe Firefly.

**Good**

- Free with an Adobe account, with daily generation limits on the free plan ([background remover](https://www.adobe.com/uk/products/firefly/features/remove-background.html)).
- Accepts JPEG, PNG or WEBP up to 50 MB and downloads a PNG with transparency.
- The UK price is shown including VAT.

**Bad**

- The page says batch background removal is offered to enterprise users, or through the Firefly AI Assistant, not as a standard bulk tool.
- Output resolution is not published.
- Photos are uploaded to Adobe. Its [privacy policy](https://www.adobe.com/privacy/policy.html) says Adobe may perform analytics with content stored in its cloud.
- The pricing FAQ says outputs from generative AI features can generally be used commercially, except beta features Adobe marks as personal use. It does not single out the background remover.

## Our free background remover

This site's [background remover](/tools/background-remover) is free and runs in your browser. The first time you use it, your browser downloads a small image model and runs it on your device, so the page says your photos are never uploaded.

**Good**

- Free, with no credits to buy. You can add up to 500 photos at once, or a whole folder, and pause, carry on or cancel.
- White, light grey or transparent backgrounds; square or 4:5; longest side automatic up to 2,000 pixels, or 1,080, 1,600 or 2,000 pixels.

Our view: for a plain item photographed on a light background, try the free route first. If most of your stock is fur, hair, glass or jewellery, a paid tool's free trial is worth a test on your hardest photos.

## How to choose

Ask yourself:

1. Am I selling? If so, is the plan I am on allowed for commercial use?
2. How many photos a month? A few dozen suits pay-as-you-go or a free tool; hundreds suit a batch tool.
3. What do I mostly sell? Plain items are easy for any tool; hair, fur and see-through items are where tools differ.
4. Am I happy for photos to be uploaded and possibly used for training? If not, check the opt-out, or use a tool that runs on your device.
5. What size do I need? Check each platform's own photo guidance, and our guide to [photos on a phone](/guides/photos-on-a-phone).

## Common mistakes

- **Using a free, non-commercial plan for stock you are selling.** Read the plan terms, not just the price.
- **Hiding the truth of the item.** A clean background is fine; hiding marks, wear or colour is not, and invites not-as-described claims.
- **Upscaling small photos.** Take a sharper photo instead.
- **Forgetting the halo.** Zoom in on edges before listing.
- **Paying for a year before testing on your hardest photos.**
