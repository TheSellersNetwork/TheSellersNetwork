This is for resellers whose stock has outgrown the spare room and who are weighing up a self storage unit. It compares five of the main UK chains, Big Yellow, Safestore, Access Self Storage, Shurgard (which now runs the former Lok'nStore sites) and Storage King, on the things that matter when the unit holds stock rather than furniture: how the price is set, minimum terms, the compulsory contents cover and what it costs, access hours, and whether staff will accept deliveries for you. Every figure comes from the chain's own website. Prices vary by site, size and date, and most chains only give a price through a quote. We have no arrangement with any of them. This is general information, not financial or legal advice.

## The short version

- Only Shurgard publishes starting prices on its own site: from £5.31 a week (£23 a month) for under 40 sq ft, up to from £23.54 a week (£102 a month) for over 130 sq ft. The others price by quote.
- Every chain requires your goods to be insured. Big Yellow's cover starts at £6.25 a week for £4,000, Access's at £25 a month for £4,000, and Shurgard's at £26 a month for £4,000. Safestore and Storage King do not publish a price.
- Big Yellow, Access and Storage King say staff can take deliveries for you, with limits. Safestore says many of its sites can. Shurgard says it does not accept deliveries, but you can give suppliers their own access.
- Minimum terms are short everywhere: from one day at Safestore and Access, 14 days at Big Yellow and Storage King, and an initial month at Shurgard.
- Safestore and Storage King say their units are for storage, not shops, offices or workshops. Check the licence terms at the others before you plan to work in a unit.
- On published terms, we would choose Big Yellow: staff take deliveries 7 days a week, access from 5am to 11pm, 14 days' notice and a published cover price. Compare local quotes first. See our verdict below.

## What matters most for resellers

1. **Total monthly cost.** Rent plus contents cover plus a lock, including VAT.
2. **Deliveries.** Whether staff will sign for stock from suppliers when you are not there.
3. **Access hours.** Whether you can get in before work, after work and at weekends.
4. **Notice period.** How quickly you can leave or downsize after a busy season.
5. **Cover for stock.** How the chain values business stock if there is a claim.
6. **What you can do in the unit.** Packing orders is different from running a shop.

## Big Yellow

Big Yellow's [pricing FAQs](https://www.bigyellow.co.uk/faqs/pricing/) say it has no price list: you get a weekly quote including VAT, charged every 4 weeks in advance, and the quote is held for 28 days. New customers can get 50% off the first invoice, covering 4, 8 or 12 weeks, with a 14-day minimum stay for the offer.

**Good**

- Its [business services page](https://www.bigyellow.co.uk/business-storage/services/) says staff are on site 7 days a week, can accept deliveries or hold goods for courier collection, and that access runs from 5am to 11pm. Free pallet trucks and trolleys; forklifts may be charged.
- Contents protection is published: from £6.25 a week for £4,000 of cover, and £8.00 a week for £6,000, which Big Yellow says is its average customer's level ([contents protection FAQs](https://www.bigyellow.co.uk/faqs/insurance-contents-protection/)).
- You can use your own insurer if the policy meets its conditions.

**Bad**

- No published prices, so every comparison needs a quote.
- Its [terms](https://www.bigyellow.co.uk/terms-and-conditions/) say deliveries are unchecked and at your own risk, a holding charge may apply if they are left more than 24 hours in the loading bay or 7 days in a secure location, and anything unclaimed after 30 days is disposed of.
- 14 days' written notice to leave.

## Safestore

Safestore's [FAQs](https://www.safestore.co.uk/faqs/) say it has 139 UK storage centres and prices by the floor area of the room. The quote includes rent and VAT, you are invoiced every four weeks with the first payment in advance, and there is no deposit. It lists introductory offers such as 50% off for 8 weeks or £1 for the first month, which vary by location.

**Good**

- Flexible terms: a variable contract under two weeks needs no notice, and a flexible contract over two weeks needs 14 days' written notice.
- Its [business storage page](https://www.safestore.co.uk/storage-types/business-storage/) says many locations accept deliveries on your behalf, and some have office space to rent.
- A lowest price guarantee: it will beat a cheaper comparable local quote by 10% for the first 8 weeks, subject to conditions.

**Bad**

- The price of its StoreProtect cover is not published on the FAQs or [StoreProtect page](https://www.safestore.co.uk/storeprotect/); it depends on the value you declare.
- For business customers' stock, Safestore says you must declare a value that takes account of age, quality and use, which is not the same as a new-for-old value.
- Units are not to be used as shops, offices or workshops, and standard units have no electricity.
- Access is during opening hours; extended access is only at some stores.

## Access Self Storage

Access's [FAQs](https://www.accessstorage.com/faqs) say prices depend on size, location and length of stay, are quoted including VAT, and have no deposit, admin fee or reservation fee. Contracts roll monthly. It lists pre-pay discounts for 2, 6 or 12 months.

**Good**

- Its [business storage page](https://www.accessstorage.com/business-services/business-storage) says staff can accept deliveries once you have signed a disclaimer, and hold them for up to 24 hours free of charge. It also says business units can be rented for as little as seven days.
- Free 24-hour access at many stores (Access says it charges for it only at Birmingham Selly Oak), and a forklift service at most stores, with costs agreed with the store.
- Short notice for domestic customers: from 7 days.

**Bad**

- Contents protection is compulsory, with a minimum of £4,000 of cover at £25 a month even if your goods are worth less.
- One month's notice on units of 500 sq ft, and 90 days over 5,000 sq ft.
- Reception hours are generally 08.30 to 18.00 on weekdays, 08.30 to 17.00 on Saturdays and 10.00 to 16.00 on Sundays, so deliveries need someone there in those hours.

Shurgard is the only chain here that publishes starting prices, on its [self storage prices page](https://www.shurgard.com/en-gb/self-storage/why-shurgard/self-storage-prices).

| 100 to 130 sq ft | £19.85 | £86.00 |

Shurgard says prices differ by location and include VAT.

**Good**

- Published prices and published cover prices. [SHURProtect](https://www.shurgard.com/en-gb/self-storage/why-shurgard/goods-protection) costs £20 a month for £2,000, £26 for £4,000, £36 for £7,500, £48 for £10,000 and £60 for £15,000 (and £6 for £500 on lockers only).
- Access 7 days a week, 365 days a year through its app, with hours set by each store ([accessing your unit](https://www.shurgard.com/en-gb/self-storage/why-shurgard/faqs/accessing-your-storage-unit)).
- You can give suppliers their own access codes and ask for a log of who entered.
- No deposit; the £20 admin fee is waived if you rent online ([pricing FAQs](https://www.shurgard.com/en-gb/self-storage/why-shurgard/faqs/pricing-and-promotions)).

**Bad**

- Shurgard says it does not receive deliveries for private or business customers ([business storage FAQs](https://www.shurgard.com/en-gb/self-storage/why-shurgard/faqs/business-storage)).
- An initial one-month rental, then monthly, with notice at least 15 days before the monthly anniversary date ([contracts FAQs](https://www.shurgard.com/en-gb/self-storage/why-shurgard/faqs/contracts-and-admin)).
- Without SHURProtect, its liability is £50 unless agreed otherwise, and SHURProtect stops at £15,000.
- It does not price match.

## Storage King

Storage King's [FAQs](https://www.storageking.co.uk/faqs/) say the quoted price is per month including VAT, invoiced for a full calendar month in advance, with no security deposit. The minimum storage period is 14 days and it asks for 14 days' notice, refunding unused storage except during a promotional period.

**Good**

- Store pages list "Deliveries accepted" where the reception will take them; the [Walsall (Wednesbury) store page](https://www.storageking.co.uk/stores/walsall/), for example, says the team will accept deliveries at reception on your behalf.
- Extended access is usually 7am to 9pm, 7 days a week, depending on local planning, and 24-hour access can be arranged on special request.
- Its insurance summary says recoverable value can go up to £100,000 ([summary of insurance](https://www.storageking.co.uk/important-stuff/summary-of-insurance/)).

**Bad**

- The insurance premium is not published, and the insurer's excess is £100 per claim (£250 for flood at two named sites).
- Normal office hours are 8:30am to 6pm on weekdays and 10am to 4pm on Saturdays, so deliveries depend on those hours.
- Storage King says its rooms are not suitable for offices or work premises.
- No refunds of unused rent during a promotional period.

## Alternatives, briefly

**Your own garage.** No rent, and access whenever you want. Two things to check first: whether your home insurance covers business stock, and whether business rates could apply. GOV.UK says you do not usually pay business rates if you use a small part of your home for business or sell goods by post, but you might if you sell to people who visit, employ people there, or have changed your home for the business, such as converting a garage ([business rates when working from home](https://www.gov.uk/introduction-to-business-rates/working-at-home)).

**Shipping container storage.** Offered by some self storage sites and by standalone container yards, usually as drive-up units. We have not compared them here because prices and terms are site by site. Check the same things as above, plus damp, lighting and whether you can reach the container in the dark.

**A small warehouse or industrial unit.** More space and your own hours, but a lease and business rates, which GOV.UK says are charged on most non-domestic properties, including warehouses ([introduction to business rates](https://www.gov.uk/introduction-to-business-rates)). Big Yellow and Access both say there are no business rates to pay on their units.

## How to choose

Ask yourself:

1. Do suppliers deliver to me, or do I collect? If they deliver, the delivery policy matters more than the rent.
2. What will I pay each month in total, with cover and VAT? Get quotes for the same size from two or three sites.
3. How much stock am I storing, at replacement value? That sets your cover cost.
4. When will I visit? Match access hours to your routine, not the brochure.
5. How fast might I need to leave or shrink? Compare notice periods.
6. Will I pack orders there? Check the rules on working in the unit. Our [storage and stock numbers guide](/guides/storage-and-stock-numbers) covers setting up a unit so every item can be found.

## Our verdict

If we had to choose one chain for a typical UK reseller, we would choose **Big Yellow**, subject to a local quote. By typical we mean a sole trader with a small unit of stock, some deliveries from suppliers or wholesale lots, and visits before or after a day job. Rent varies by site and only Shurgard publishes prices, so this pick rests on published terms, not price. Get quotes for the same size from two or three nearby sites before you decide.

Why:

- Deliveries: its business page says staff are on site 7 days a week and can accept deliveries or hold goods for courier collection.
- Access hours: extended access from 5am to 11pm on its business page, which fits around most working days.
- Notice: a 14-day minimum stay and 14 days' notice, so you can leave or change size after a busy season.
- Cover: its contents protection price is published, from £6.25 a week for £4,000, and you can use your own insurer if the policy meets its conditions.

By situation:

- **If you want to visit at any hour:** Access Self Storage, which offers free 24-hour access at many stores, and whose staff hold deliveries for up to 24 hours free after you sign a disclaimer.
- **If you want to see prices before you ask for a quote:** Shurgard, the only chain here with published starting prices (from £23 a month) and published cover prices, and you can give suppliers their own access codes.
- **If you might only need a unit for a few days:** Safestore, where a contract under two weeks needs no notice, from one day.
- **If you store high-value stock:** Storage King, whose insurance summary gives recoverable value up to £100,000. Its premium is not published, so ask for it with your quote.

What we would avoid:

- **Shurgard if you rely on staff signing for deliveries.** It says it does not receive deliveries for private or business customers.
- **Safestore or Storage King if you plan to work in the unit.** Both say their units are not for use as shops, offices or workshops.

## Common mistakes

- **Comparing rent without cover.** Cover is compulsory at every chain here and can add a lot to a small unit.
- **Undervaluing stock to cut cover costs.** Storage King, for example, limits what can be recovered to the value you declared.
- **Assuming deliveries will be signed for.** Shurgard does not, and others have time limits and charges.
- **Taking a bigger unit than you need.** Price is set by floor area, and racking lets you use the height instead.
- **Forgetting the offer ends.** Half-price periods run out; budget for the full rate.
