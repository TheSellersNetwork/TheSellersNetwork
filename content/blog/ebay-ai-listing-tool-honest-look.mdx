This is for anyone selling used items on eBay UK who has seen the "use AI description" button, or the option to start a listing from a photo, and wondered whether to trust it. By the end you will know what eBay says its AI tools do, which ones UK private and business sellers can use, why the listing is still your responsibility, and what to check before it goes live. It is general information, not legal advice, and eBay changes these tools often, so check the current pages before relying on any detail here.

## The short version

- eBay offers AI-written item descriptions in the eBay app, a photo-first listing flow in the app that suggests a category and item specifics, and an AI bulk listing tool in Seller Hub on desktop.
- eBay itself says the AI "doesn't always get it right" and that sellers are responsible for checking the accuracy of their listings.
- In law and under eBay's Money Back Guarantee, the item has to match the listing. It does not matter who, or what, wrote the words.
- AI is at its weakest on exactly what matters most for used goods: the actual condition, the flaws, the exact model and the size.
- Treat the AI output as a first draft. Check the title, item specifics, condition, measurements, category, price and postage every time.
- For one-off, worn, faulty or unusual items, writing it yourself is usually quicker than correcting the AI.

## What eBay says the tools do

eBay uses the word "magical" for several related features, which makes it hard to tell what is available to whom. Here is what we could confirm from eBay's own pages, feature by feature.

| Feature | What eBay says it does | UK availability we could verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI item descriptions | Suggests an item description at the touch of a button, which you can use, edit or ignore | "All UK sellers listing in the majority of categories on the eBay app" ([UK Seller Centre, September 2023](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/news/2023-september/ai-enabled-description)) |
| Background removal | Puts your photo on a white background | In the eBay app and the advanced listing tool (same page) |
| Photo-first listing on mobile | Starts with photos and a title, then suggests the category and item specifics | eBay app in the US, UK and Germany, for private sellers across all categories ([eBay, April 2025](https://innovation.ebayinc.com/stories/ebay-reduces-the-time-to-list-on-mobile-with-new-simplified-selling-tool-now-featuring-magical-listing-ai-technology/)) |
| AI bulk listings | Scans batches of photos and creates draft listings with category, title and item specifics | Seller Hub on desktop, rolling out to all UK sellers by the end of March 2025 ([UK Seller Centre, January 2025](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/news/2025-january/ai-bulk-listings)) |

A few honest caveats on that table.

**Business sellers and the photo-first app flow.** eBay's April 2025 announcement names private sellers only. We could not find an eBay page confirming that UK business sellers get the photo-first flow in the app. Industry coverage in early 2026 ([Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-ai-magical-listing-revisited/)) reported a newer version going to new and reactivated sellers, with no stated timeline for business sellers. If you run a business account, look in your own app rather than assuming.

**The bulk tool.** eBay's own [listing tools page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/listings/listing-tools) says the photo upload tool works best for standardised items such as trading cards. That tells you where eBay expects it to be reliable, and by implication where it is less so. We could not confirm whether every UK account can see it today. If it is not in your Seller Hub, it is not available to you yet.

**Price suggestions.** eBay's [original 2023 announcement](https://innovation.ebayinc.com/stories/magical-listing-tool-harnesses-the-power-of-ai-to-make-selling-on-ebay-faster-easier-and-more-accurate/) of the photo tool, which was for the US iOS app, described it suggesting a price and postage as well as the title and description. We could not find a UK page setting out exactly how the AI arrives at a suggested price, so treat any figure it offers as unexplained until you have checked it against sold listings.

## Who is responsible for the listing

You are. eBay says so plainly on the page that launched AI descriptions in the UK: "sellers are responsible for checking the accuracy of their listings" ([UK Seller Centre](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/news/2023-september/ai-enabled-description)). The bulk listing announcement says eBay strongly recommends checking all listing details before publishing.

The rules the listing has to meet do not change because a tool drafted it:

- eBay's [item description policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/listing-policies/item-description-policy?id=4372) says descriptions must be accurate and the condition selected must match the item and be consistent across the whole listing.
- eBay's [condition guidance](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/creating-managing-listings/item-conditions-category?id=4765) says signs of wear on used items should be shown and described in the listing.
- Under the [Money Back Guarantee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy?id=4210), a buyer can get their money back if the item does not match the listing. That applies whatever your returns policy says.
- In law, a private seller must supply goods that match their description ([Sale of Goods Act 1979, section 13](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/54/section/13)), and a trader selling to a consumer has the same duty under the [Consumer Rights Act 2015, section 11](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/11), plus others.

So if the AI writes "fully working" on a radio with a crackling speaker, or "no signs of wear" on a jacket with a worn cuff, that is your description. ## Where AI text tends to go wrong on used items

The AI works from your photos, your title and eBay's catalogue data. It cannot hold the item, test it, measure it or smell it. In practice the mistakes fall into a few patterns.

**Describing features the item does not have.** If the AI matches your item to a catalogue product, it may describe what the product came with new: the box, the charger, the second lens, the detachable hood. Your used one may have none of those. This is the mistake most likely to turn into a not as described return.

**Generic condition wording.** Phrases like "in great condition" or "gently used" appear because they are safe-sounding filler, not because the AI has seen your item's scuffs. A buyer who opens a parcel and finds a stain you did not mention does not care where the phrase came from.

**Wrong model, edition or size.** Similar-looking products get confused. Coverage of eBay's own tool in 2026 reported items being misidentified altogether, with the wrong title, specifics and description following from the first wrong guess ([Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-ai-magical-listing-revisited/)). On used goods this shows up as the wrong generation of a phone, the wrong year of a console, a UK size read as a US one, or a size taken from the catalogue rather than the label.

**Wrong category.** A near miss on category means the wrong item specifics are offered, which means the listing may not show in the filters buyers use.

**Padding.** AI descriptions often run long and say little. Buyers skim. Two lines of true detail beat a paragraph about "timeless style".

eBay said in 2025 that the accuracy of suggested item specifics would continue to improve over time. That is a fair thing for eBay to say, and it is also an admission that the suggestions are not yet something to accept without looking.

## The checklist before you press list

Run through this every time you use any of the AI features. It takes a couple of minutes.

1. **Title order.** Does it lead with what a buyer would type: brand, then item type, then model, size, colour and the key detail? Remove filler words. Titles can be up to 80 characters. Our guide to [eBay titles, item specifics and search](/guides/ebay-titles-item-specifics-and-search) covers the order in detail.
2. **Model and identity.** Check the model number, edition or year against the item itself: the label, the underside, the settings screen. Never against the AI's guess.
3. **Item specifics.** Go through every suggested specific. Correct anything that came from the catalogue rather than your item, and fill in the ones buyers filter by (size, colour, material, brand). Leave a specific blank rather than guess, and do not put "does not apply" where the real value is available.
4. **Condition and flaws.** Pick the condition grade yourself. Then write every flaw in plain words and photograph it: marks, chips, missing parts, fading, smells, repairs. Delete any generic condition phrase the AI added.
5. **What is included.** List exactly what is in the parcel. Delete any accessory the AI mentioned that you do not have.
6. **Measurements.** Add your own measurements for clothing, shoes, furniture and anything where size matters. AI cannot measure your item. Pit to pit and length for tops, waist and inside leg for trousers.
7. **Category.** Check that the category is the one buyers of this item would browse, and that the specifics offered fit the item.
8. **Price.** Ignore the suggested price until you have looked at what the same item actually sold for. Go to [Advanced search](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sch/ebayadvsearch), tick completed listings, and look at the sold prices, as eBay's [pricing help page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/selling/pricing-items?id=4133) suggests. Compare like with like: same model, same condition, same completeness.
9. **Postage.** Weigh and measure the packed parcel yourself. A suggested service based on a typical item can be wrong for yours, and undercharging comes out of your margin.
10. **Read it as the buyer.** Read the whole listing once from top to bottom. If anything would surprise the person opening the parcel, fix it.

## When to write it yourself

Our view, based on what eBay says the tools are for and where they are weakest: the AI earns its keep on items that closely match a catalogue product, are new or nearly new, and have little to say about condition. Think sealed media, boxed toys, standard branded items with a clear model number.

Write it yourself, or at least write the description yourself, when:

- **The item is worn, faulty or sold for parts.** The condition is the listing, and the AI cannot see it.
- **The item is one-off.** Vintage, handmade, unbranded or rare items have no catalogue match to lean on, so the AI guesses.
- **Size and fit decide the sale.** Clothing, shoes and furniture need your own measurements anyway.
- **The value is high.** On a pricey item, one wrong word can cost more than the time saved.
- **You have a house style that works.** If your descriptions already sell, a generic draft is a step backwards.

One middle route: let the AI suggest the category and specifics, which saves clicks, then delete its description and write your own few lines on condition, contents and measurements. Our guide to [descriptions that sell](/guides/descriptions-that-sell) covers how to write those lines quickly.
