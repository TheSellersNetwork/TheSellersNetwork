This is for anyone about to register an Amazon UK seller account, or stuck partway through verification. By the end you will know what documents Amazon asks for, the rules each one has to meet, what happens on a verification video call if you are asked to take one, the mismatches that most often stop an application, and what to do if it fails. Everything here comes from Amazon's own public registration and verification pages; where Amazon does not publish something, we say so. This is general information, not legal advice.

## The short version

- Amazon verifies the person registering (the primary contact), their address, their bank account and, for business sellers, the business itself ([Amazon verification guide](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/verification-compliance)).
- You need a valid government photo ID, a proof of address dated within the last **180 days**, a chargeable credit or debit card and a bank account.
- The name and address on your documents must match what you type into Seller Central. Most failures come back to this.
- Upload colour scans or photos showing all four corners. No screenshots, no blurry images, nothing cropped.
- Some sellers are asked to join a video call to show their original documents. Have the originals in front of you.
- Do not open a second account to get round a failed verification. Amazon's standard policy is one account per seller.

## What Amazon verifies

Amazon's [verification compliance page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/verification-compliance) sets out four steps:

| Step | What is checked |
| --- | --- |
| Identity verification | Everyone registered on the account: the primary contact, legal representative, beneficial owners and so on |
| Address verification | The same people's addresses |
| Bank account verification | The name and number of the bank account you registered |
| Business verification | Documents vary by business type; not needed for individual sellers |

Amazon says identity verification is required by law to use its payment service, and that if you do not supply the information it needs, your account will be restricted and you will not be able to sell.

## The documents

### Identity document

Amazon's [registration guide](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/sell-online/registration-guide) asks you to use a government-issued ID, such as a passport, to enter your full legal name (including any middle name), country of citizenship, country of birth, date of birth, ID details and residential address.

The [verification page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/verification-compliance) lists acceptable IDs as a full valid passport, national identity card, driving licence (regional differences may apply) or residence permit. The document must:

- be a government-issued ID with a photo;
- be valid and in date;
- show your full name, date of birth, place of birth and country of citizenship;
- show an ID number and expiry date;
- be signed where required.

Check your document against every point before you choose it. If it does not show all of the required information, Amazon says it may ask for a supporting document, such as a birth certificate.

### Proof of address

Individual sellers must provide a proof of address such as a recent bank statement or utility bill ([registration guide](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/sell-online/registration-guide)). The [verification page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/verification-compliance) accepts a utility bill (gas, electricity, internet), a bank or credit card statement, a residence confirmation, or a building society or credit union statement, and says the document must:

- show the provider's name and your name;
- include the date of issue and your address;
- have a name and address that match what you entered in Seller Central;
- be issued by a company or government authority and dated within 180 days;
- not be handwritten or a screenshot.

Amazon's [guide for UK sellers registering in the US store](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/global-selling/registration-guide) adds that you can hide the account number and money amounts on a statement, but the rest must be visible, and that e-statements are accepted only if unaltered, for example not cropped.

### Card and bank account

You need a credit or debit card: Amazon accepts American Express, Visa and Mastercard, and does not accept prepaid cards, gift certificates, cheques or online payment systems ([registration guide](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/sell-online/registration-guide)). You also enter the bank account where your sales proceeds will be paid. If Amazon cannot verify the bank account automatically, it asks for a bank statement dated within the last 180 days.

### Uploading

Amazon's instruction is plain: use high-quality colour scans or photos that show all four corners and have legible text, do not use screenshots or blurry images, and make sure documents are signed where relevant ([registration guide](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/sell-online/registration-guide)).

## The selfie check and the video call

Amazon's current UK [registration guide](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/sell-online/registration-guide) describes a biometric check: you take a photo of your face, then photos of your ID, and Amazon says most verifications are completed within 48 hours. It also says identity verification usually takes three business days or less.

Some sellers are instead, or also, asked to join a video call. Amazon describes this in its [guide for UK sellers registering in the US store](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/global-selling/registration-guide): the primary contact is prompted to take a video call, where an Amazon representative reviews the original documents you uploaded and the information you gave at registration. You can join the next available call or book an appointment. After the call, Amazon may contact you within two business days if it needs clarification. If you miss your appointment, Seller Central prompts you to rebook.

An Amazon staff post on the [UK seller forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/03a80cda-d340-4823-aeab-792a27300eb5) gives more detail on preparing:

- choose between an instant call or a scheduled call booked a few business days ahead;
- calls typically last 10 to 20 minutes;
- have the original documents with you, not copies, as the representative will ask to see both sides;
- make sure your ID is valid and signed, and all information is clear with nothing cut off;
- use a quiet space and a device with a front camera and microphone.

That post is a forum announcement rather than a formal help page, so check the instructions in your own Seller Central notification, which take priority.

## Why verification fails

Amazon does not publish failure statistics or a full list of reasons. The requirements above point straight at the likely problems, and they match what sellers describe in Amazon's own seller forums.

| Problem | Why it fails | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name mismatch | Middle name missing in Seller Central, or a shortened first name on the bank statement | Enter your name exactly as on the ID, including middle names |
| Address mismatch | Flat number, house name or postcode written differently in Seller Central, or a statement sent to an old address | Copy the address format from your proof of address |
| Different addresses | ID shows one address, statement another, Seller Central a third | Use a proof of address for the address you entered, and update your details before you upload |
| Old document | Statement or bill older than 180 days | Download the latest statement |
| Cropped or edited | Edges cut off, a screenshot of a banking app, an e-statement that has been cropped | Upload the full, unaltered PDF or a photo showing all four corners |
| Poor image | Blurry, glare over the photo or ID number | Retake in good light on a plain surface |
| Expired or unsigned ID | Fails the ID criteria | Use an in-date, signed document |
| ID details typed wrongly | Passport number or date of birth does not match the document | Check each field against the ID before submitting |

**A worked example.** Say your passport reads "Sarah Jane Smith", you registered as "Sarah Smith", and your bank statement goes to your old flat. That is two mismatches before anyone looks at the photo. Correct the name in Seller Central to include "Jane", update your address with the bank, download a new statement showing the current address, and only then upload.

## What happens after a failure

Amazon's [verification page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/verification-compliance) says that when it needs more information, a notification appears on your Account Info page, and it may give more detail in your performance notifications. If you do not supply what it needs, the account is restricted and you cannot sell.

Amazon's public pages do not describe a formal appeal route for a failed registration. In practice, what you can do depends on what Seller Central shows you:

1. **Read the notification in full.** It may name the document or field that failed.
2. **If you can resubmit, fix the cause first.** Correct the details, get the right document, then upload once. Several rushed uploads of the same flawed document do not help.
3. **If there is no option to resubmit,** contact Seller Support from within the account and ask what is needed.

Two other rules are worth knowing. Amazon can ask you to **re-verify** later, for example after you change account details. It allows up to 60 days to complete re-verification, and your ability to sell and receive funds may be restricted if you do not ([verification page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/verification-compliance)). And if Amazon posts a code to your address, its [US registration guide](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/global-selling/registration-guide) says you get three attempts to enter it correctly, after which you may not be able to continue with that registration.

## Why not to open a second account

It is tempting to start again with a new email. Do not.

An Amazon staff post on the [UK seller forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/498ddc70-cbf1-4852-a410-232ca24b752d) says Amazon's standard policy is to allow one Seller Central account per seller, with more than one allowed only for a legitimate business need. It also says that if one account is deactivated for a policy breach, that can affect all associated accounts, and that the original account has to be reinstated before a secondary one can be.

A new account opened by the same person to get round a failed verification is not a legitimate business need. Amazon holds your ID, address, bank, card and device details from the first attempt, and a second application with the same details is likely to be linked to the first. Our [Amazon account health guide](/guides/amazon-account-health) covers why a second account is treated as the more serious problem.

## Before you start

- Pick an ID that shows everything on Amazon's list, and check it is in date and signed.
- Download a fresh bank statement as a PDF, showing your current address.
- Make sure the name and address on the ID, the statement and Seller Central are written identically.
- Have the originals to hand in case of a video call.
- Use an email and phone number you will keep; Amazon will contact you on both.

Our guide to [starting Amazon FBA in the UK](/guides/how-to-start-amazon-fba-uk) covers the rest of the setup, and you can ask about a stuck verification in [Account health and suspensions](/community/c/amazon-account-health-and-suspensions).
