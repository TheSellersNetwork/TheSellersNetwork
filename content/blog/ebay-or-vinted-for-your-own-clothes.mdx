This is for anyone with a wardrobe to clear who is choosing between eBay and Vinted, or wondering whether to use both. By the end you will know what each site charges you and your buyer, how postage and payment work, what happens when a buyer is unhappy, and which kinds of clothes tend to suit which site. It covers private sellers selling their own things, not businesses. Fees change, so check the linked pages before relying on a figure. It is general information, not legal or financial advice.

## The short version

- Neither site charges a UK private seller a selling fee on ordinary clothes. On both, the buyer pays a fee on top of your price.
- eBay's buyer fee is published as a fixed table. Vinted's varies, usually 3% to 8% plus £0.30 to £0.80.
- Both give you a prepaid label and cover for lost parcels when you use their postage.
- Vinted gives the buyer 2 days to report a problem. eBay gives them 30 days, and you pay return postage if the item does not match your listing.
- Vinted is built around clothes and quick listing. eBay has a wider audience, auctions and more listing fields to fill in.
- In our opinion, everyday high street clothes suit Vinted, and branded, collectable or unusual items are worth trying on eBay.

## Fees: what you get and what the buyer pays

**eBay.** eBay's [private seller fees page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822) says it is free for UK-based private sellers to sell, excluding cars, motorcycles and vehicles. You get 300 free listings a month, then pay 35p a listing. You pay 3% if the buyer's delivery address is outside the UK, and you pay for optional extras such as a subtitle or promoted listings if you choose them.

The buyer pays a [Buyer Protection fee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/buying/paying-items/buyer-protection-fee?id=5594), which eBay includes in the price shown while browsing. eBay's [seller page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/selling/pricing-items/buyer-protection-information-sellers?id=5595) says the fee does not come out of your money: list at £10 and you receive £10.

**Vinted.** Vinted's [How it works page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/how_it_works) says there are zero selling fees. The buyer pays a fee at checkout. That page shows it as 5% of the price plus £0.70, while Vinted's [fee help page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1654) says it is usually 3% to 8% plus £0.30 to £0.80 and varies by item, order value and whether it is a bundle. From 8 October 2026 it is called the Vinted fee rather than the Buyer Protection fee; our post on [Vinted's new terms](/blog/vinted-terms-october-2026) covers the change.

### A worked example

Say you sell a dress for £20, and then a coat for £50. You receive £20 and £50 on either site. The buyer's fee, before postage, would be:

| Your price | eBay buyer fee | Vinted buyer fee, using the How it works rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| £20 | £0.10 + £1.40 = £1.50 | £1.00 + £0.70 = £1.70 |
| £50 | £0.10 + £1.40 + £1.20 = £2.70 | £2.50 + £0.70 = £3.20 |

Vinted's real fee on a given item may be higher or lower, within the 3% to 8% range. The point is that the fees are in the same region on both sites, and neither comes out of your pocket. What differs is how the total looks to the buyer, since eBay shows it in the headline price. Our [where to sell tool](/tools/where-to-sell) compares what you keep across platforms for any price.

## Postage

**eBay.** Private sellers of eligible items, priced up to £750, use [Simple Delivery](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/posting-items/setting-postage-options/simple-delivery?id=5575). When the item sells, eBay gives you a prepaid label or QR code. You choose when listing whether the buyer pays for postage or you offer it free. Carriers include Evri, Royal Mail, DHL and InPost. You are protected if the parcel is lost or damaged from the first carrier scan until it is marked delivered.

**Vinted.** The buyer picks a carrier at checkout and pays for the label, choosing from the carriers you have switched on ([Vinted](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/234-shipping-methods)). You post within 5 business days. If a parcel sent on a Vinted label is confirmed lost or damaged and was properly packed, you can get compensation up to £500 ([Vinted](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1018)). Our guide to [Vinted postage options](/guides/vinted-postage-options) explains each carrier.

Both work in a similar way for you. The difference is that on eBay you can choose to offer free postage, which can make a listing look cheaper, while on Vinted the buyer always pays for the label.

## Protection and disputes

This is the biggest practical difference.

**Vinted.** The buyer has 2 days after Vinted says the item should have arrived to report a problem. Refunds cover items that are lost, arrived damaged or are significantly not as described. An item that matches its description but does not fit or suit the buyer is not eligible, and the buyer pays for return postage ([refund policy](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/465)).

**eBay.** eBay's [Money Back Guarantee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy?id=4210) lets a buyer report an item that does not match the listing within 30 days of delivery, even if you do not offer returns. When that happens, you pay the return postage. If you do offer change of mind returns, your own policy decides who pays. Our post on [eBay private sellers and the law](/blog/ebay-private-sellers-returns-and-the-law) explains what "no returns" does and does not mean.

The shorter window on Vinted means your money is settled sooner and there is less time for a buyer to wear something and send it back. The protection is the same on both: describe honestly, show every flaw, and add measurements.

## Getting paid

**Vinted.** The order completes when the buyer presses "Everything is OK", or 2 days after they receive it if they do nothing. The money moves to your available balance within 2 days, and a withdrawal can take up to 5 business days to reach your bank ([getting paid](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/235-getting-paid-for-a-completed-sale); [withdrawals](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/68-how-to-withdraw-earnings)).

**eBay.** For most private sellers, funds become available 2 calendar days after tracked delivery is confirmed, or within about 24 hours of payment if you meet eBay's eligibility criteria. You then request a payout, which can take 0 to 4 business days for your bank to process ([getting paid](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/getting-paid/getting-paid-items-youve-sold?id=4814)).

The timelines are similar. Expect roughly one to two weeks from sale to bank on either, depending on how quickly you post and the buyer confirms.

## Audience and effort

This section is our opinion, based on how the two sites are built rather than on any published figures.

**Vinted** is a clothes-first app. Listing is quick: photos, a short description, size, condition and price. Buyers browse by size and brand, expect second-hand prices and make offers. Vinted's offers can be up to 40% below your listed price ([offers](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/258-i-want-to-make-an-offer-or-suggest-a-different-price)), so expect haggling. Bundles make it easy for one buyer to take several items.

**eBay** sells everything, and its buyers search by exact brand, style and size. Listings have more fields to fill in, and the item specifics matter for search. You can run an auction, which suits items where you do not know the value. It also has buyers outside the UK, which can help with niche or collectable pieces, at the cost of the 3% international fee.

## Which suits what

| Type of item | Where we would start | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High street clothes and kids' clothes | Vinted | Quick to list, buyers expect these prices, bundles work well |
| Popular brands in good condition | Either, or both | Check sold prices on eBay and similar listings on Vinted |
| Vintage, designer or collectable pieces | eBay | Wider search audience, auctions help find the price |
| Unusual sizes or specialist kit (riding, cycling, dancewear) | eBay | Buyers search for exact items |
| Lots of low value items | Vinted | Bundles and no listing limit |
| Anything you are unsure of the value of | eBay auction | The market sets the price |

You can list on both at once, but if you do, remove the item from one site as soon as it sells on the other. Selling the same item twice means cancelling an order, which is bad for your reputation on either site.

## What goes wrong

- **Pricing without checking the fee the buyer sees.** On eBay the fee is in the headline price. Compare like with like.
- **Offering free postage without working it out.** On eBay the postage then comes out of your price.
- **Ticking "no returns" on eBay and thinking it covers everything.** It does not cover items that do not match your listing.
- **Forgetting that this is for your own things.** If you start buying to resell, both sites treat you as a business. See our post on [when eBay says you are a business seller](/blog/when-ebay-says-you-are-a-business-seller) and Vinted's [commercial selling rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/1120-commercial-selling).
