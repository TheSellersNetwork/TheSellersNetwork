This is for eBay UK sellers who have a sale sitting at "awaiting payment" and are not sure how long to wait, what to click, or whether cancelling will count against them. By the end you will know the payment window, how to cancel correctly, how to automate it, what happens to the listing and your fees, and how to stop the same buyers doing it again. It is general information about eBay's published policies, not legal advice.

## The short version

- Buyers must pay within **4 calendar days** ([unpaid item policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/payment-policies/unpaid-item-policy?id=4271)).
- After 4 full calendar days, the "Buyer hasn't paid" cancellation reason becomes available. You must cancel within **30 days** of the sale ([cancelling an order](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/getting-paid/sellers-can-cancel-order?id=4136)).
- Cancelling for non-payment does **not** affect your seller standards, and any feedback the buyer left is removed.
- You can set unpaid orders to cancel automatically after 4 or more calendar days in your preferences for items awaiting payment.
- A single-quantity Buy It Now item is relisted automatically unless you say no. For auctions you can send a second chance offer to the next highest bidder.
- Buyer requirements and your blocked buyers list (up to 5,000 usernames) keep repeat non-payers out.
- Never use "Buyer hasn't paid" for any other reason. eBay treats it as misuse.

## How long the buyer has to pay

eBay's [unpaid item policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/payment-policies/unpaid-item-policy?id=4271) is short: "Buyers must pay for the items they purchase on eBay within 4 calendar days." It also says placing a bid on an auction is a commitment to buy.

eBay sends the buyer reminder emails at 24 and 48 hours, and you can message the buyer yourself ([resolving unpaid items](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/getting-paid/resolving-unpaid-items-buyers?id=4137)). A polite nudge on day two often works: some buyers simply forget, or their payment failed.

There is one exception worth knowing. eBay Live auctions require immediate payment, and the seller can cancel straight away if payment fails ([unpaid item policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/payment-policies/unpaid-item-policy?id=4271)).

### Requiring immediate payment

For Buy It Now listings, you can require immediate payment so the item is not reserved until the buyer pays. eBay's [best practices for sellers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/best-practices-avoiding-handling-issues-buyers?id=4113) suggest setting payment preferences to require immediate payment, and its [seller protections page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-protections?id=4345) mentions it as a way to avoid unpaid orders. It does not stop every problem, but it removes most of the "bought it and vanished" cases on fixed-price items.

## Cancelling an unpaid order by hand

From eBay's [cancellation guide](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/getting-paid/sellers-can-cancel-order?id=4136) and [order cancellation policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behavior-policies/order-cancellation-policy?id=5298):

1. Wait until 4 full calendar days have passed. The "Buyer hasn't paid" reason does not appear before then.
2. Go to the order in Seller Hub or My eBay and choose to cancel.
3. Select **"Buyer hasn't paid"** as the reason.
4. Decide whether to relist (see below).
5. Do it within 30 days of the sale, the limit for seller cancellations.

What happens next, according to eBay:

- An **unpaid cancellation** is recorded on the buyer's account. Buyers with excessive unpaid cancellations may have buying limits or restrictions put on their account and any linked accounts ([unpaid item policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/payment-policies/unpaid-item-policy?id=4271)).
- **Your seller standards are not affected**, and any feedback the buyer left is removed ([cancelling an order](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/getting-paid/sellers-can-cancel-order?id=4136)). eBay's [seller protections](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-protections?id=4345) say the same, and add that related cancelled order defects are removed.

Compare that with cancelling because the item is out of stock or you sold it elsewhere, which **is** a transaction defect ([order cancellation policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behavior-policies/order-cancellation-policy?id=5298)). Our post on [eBay seller levels](/blog/ebay-seller-levels-and-below-standard) explains why that matters.

### Do not misuse the reason

eBay's order cancellation policy says a seller should never select an incorrect reason, "including falsely claiming that the buyer failed to pay". The unpaid item policy says sellers who use "Buyer hasn't paid" when payment was received face reversed credits, loss of access to fee credits, and possible suspension. If you want out of a paid sale, that is a different cancellation with different consequences.

## Setting it up to happen automatically

You do not have to watch the clock. In your **preferences for items awaiting payment**, you can set unpaid orders to cancel automatically after 4 or more calendar days ([resolving unpaid items](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/getting-paid/resolving-unpaid-items-buyers?id=4137)). This applies to orders paid through eBay checkout.

Our opinion: for most sellers, turning on automatic cancellation at the minimum period is the right call. It gets stock back on sale sooner and removes the temptation to cancel early with the wrong reason. If you sell high-value items where buyers sometimes need an extra day to move money, a slightly longer period is reasonable.

## What happens to your fees

This depends on your account type.

**Private sellers.** eBay says selling is free for UK private sellers in most cases, with no final value fee or regulatory operating fee when items sell ([private seller fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822)). Fees can still apply for listings over your free allowance, optional upgrades, or overseas delivery. For an unpaid domestic sale with no upgrades, there is usually nothing to reclaim.

**Business sellers.** eBay's [cancellation guide](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/getting-paid/sellers-can-cancel-order?id=4136) and [order cancellation policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behavior-policies/order-cancellation-policy?id=5298) say you "may be eligible for a fee credit" when an order is cancelled, under the [fee credits policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fee-credits?id=4128). That fee credits page sets out which fees are credited when a buyer asks to cancel and when you cancel for reasons in your control, but at the time of writing it does not list the non-payment case separately. So check your financial statement a few days after cancelling. Credits are typically added to your available funds. If a fee for an unpaid order has not been credited, contact eBay and quote the order number.

## Relisting

eBay's [resolving unpaid items page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/getting-paid/resolving-unpaid-items-buyers?id=4137) says:

- A **single-quantity Buy It Now** item is relisted automatically when you cancel, unless you choose not to relist.
- For an **auction**, you can make a **second chance offer** to the next highest bidder instead of starting again.

A second chance offer is often the quickest route for an auction with a close underbidder. If the bidding was thin, relisting may reach a better price. Our guide to [relisting and sell similar](/guides/relisting-and-sell-similar) covers the options, and [offers to watchers](/guides/offers-to-watchers) can move a relisted item quickly.

## Stopping repeat non-payers

### Buyer requirements

eBay lets you set [buyer requirements](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/creating-managing-listings/setting-buyer-requirements?id=4152) that apply to your listings. They include blocking buyers who:

- have a history of cancelled orders because they did not pay;
- have a delivery address in a location you exclude from postage;
- are currently winning, or have bought, a set number of your items in the last 10 days.

By default the settings apply to future listings. You can choose to apply them to active listings too. There is also an exemption list for buyers you want to let through anyway, such as a regular customer. The exact thresholds on offer are shown in the settings rather than on the help page, so check them there.

### Blocking individual buyers

If one buyer causes trouble, add their username to your blocked list on the **Block bidders or buyers from your listings** page. You can block up to 5,000 usernames, and a blocked buyer cannot bid on or buy your items until you remove them ([blocking bidders and buyers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/resolving-buyer-issues/blocking-buyer-ebay?id=4082)).

### Reporting

A buyer who wins your auctions and never pays, again and again, is breaching eBay's rules: the unpaid item policy says not paying breaks the abusive buyer policy. Report them through eBay's reporting tools linked from the [abusive buyer policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/rules-policies-buyers/abusive-buyer-policy?id=4374), then block them.

## Buyers who offer to pay another way

Some non-payers message to ask for bank details or a different way to pay. Say no. eBay's [policy on offers to buy or sell outside eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/payment-policies/offering-buy-sell-outside-ebay-policy?id=4272) does not allow it, and transactions outside eBay are not eligible for eBay's protection programmes. If the buyer cannot pay through eBay checkout, cancel and move on.

## What goes wrong

- **Cancelling on day two with the wrong reason** to get the item relisted faster. That is a defect, and misusing the reason can cost you more.
- **Leaving it past 30 days.** You then cannot cancel as a seller.
- **Posting before payment clears.** Only post when the order shows as paid in Seller Hub, not because of an email. Our guide on [eBay payment holds](/guides/ebay-payment-holds) explains when funds are released.
- **Setting no buyer requirements at all.** Five minutes in the settings saves a lot of relisting.

If a buyer's behaviour does not fit any of this, ask in [eBay buyers and disputes](/community/c/ebay-buyers-and-disputes).
