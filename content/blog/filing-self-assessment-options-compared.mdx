This is for UK resellers and other sole traders who have to send a Self Assessment tax return and want to know the realistic ways to do it. By the end you will know what HMRC's own service does for free, what the HMRC app can and cannot do, what filing software and accountant services charge on their own pages, and which routes stop working once Making Tax Digital applies to you. We checked every page in September 2026, and at the end we say which route we would choose and why. This is general information, not tax or financial advice.

## The short version

- HMRC's online service is free and HMRC calculates what you owe from what you report ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns)). For a straightforward sole trader return it is enough for many people.
- The HMRC app does not file a return. It shows your UTR and what you owe, takes payments and sets payment reminders ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/download-the-hmrc-app)).
- Filing software on HMRC's supplier list costs from £42 (TaxCalc, up to six returns) to £69.99 a tax year (GoSimpleTax, subscription) on the providers' own pages.
- Accountant-led online services publish fixed prices, for example Taxfix from £169 (discounted when we checked) and Crunch at £240 plus VAT. High street accountants' fees vary and are usually quoted per client.
- Once Making Tax Digital for Income Tax applies to you, you must submit your tax return using MTD software ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax/submit-your-tax-return)). HMRC's free online return is no longer the route.
- Whoever files, the figures are your responsibility ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-to-choose-a-tax-agent)).
- Our pick for a typical reseller below the MTD thresholds is HMRC's free online service, used with tidy records.

## What matters most for resellers

- **Is your return simple?** One trade, maybe some employment income, no capital gains or foreign income: most routes work. Partnerships, trusts and non-residents cannot use HMRC's online service at all ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/log-in-file-self-assessment-tax-return)).
- **Are your records in order?** Every route needs your total sales and allowable expenses. Software and accountants do not fix missing records.
- **Does MTD apply to you?** Turnover over £50,000 from April 2026, over £30,000 from April 2027 and over £20,000 from April 2028 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-youre-eligible-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)).
- **Do you want advice, or just filing?** Software and HMRC file what you enter. Only a person can look at your situation and suggest what to claim.
- **Deadlines.** Online returns and payment by 31 January; paper returns by 31 October; online by 30 December if you want the bill collected through your tax code ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/deadlines)).

## HMRC's online service

HMRC's own route, through your HMRC online account.

**Good**

- Free.
- Available if you are self-employed, or not self-employed but still need to send a return ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/log-in-file-self-assessment-tax-return)).
- HMRC works out what you owe from what you report.
- Deadline is 31 January, three months later than paper.

**Bad**

- Not available for partnerships, trusts and estates, non-residents and a few other cases. Those need commercial software or paper.
- It is a form, not a bookkeeping tool. You bring the totals.
- Once MTD for Income Tax applies to you, you must file the return using MTD software instead.

## The HMRC app

Useful alongside any route, but not a way to file.

**Good**

- Free. Shows your UTR and how much tax you owe.
- Lets you pay and set reminders to pay.
- Lets you tell HMRC you no longer need to send a return, and claim a refund if you have overpaid ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/download-the-hmrc-app)).

**Bad**

- The guidance does not list filing a Self Assessment return among its features.

## Commercial filing software

HMRC keeps a [list of commercial software suppliers for Self Assessment](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/self-assessment-commercial-software-suppliers/self-assessment-online-commercial-software-suppliers). It updated the list on 29 September 2026 and says it cannot recommend or endorse any one product. The products below are on that list or, for Coconut, owned by the same directors as one that is.

### GoSimpleTax

**Good**

- On HMRC's supplier list for SA100 and schedules including SA103 (self-employment), SA105 (property) and SA108 (capital gains).
- Individual plan £69.99 a tax year on subscription, £113.99 without; prices include VAT ([GoSimpleTax pricing](https://www.gosimpletax.com/pricing)).
- Free trial without a card. Past years available at £113.99 a return.

**Bad**

- Subscriptions renew automatically on 1 April.
- Not on HMRC's MTD software finder under its own name. MTD runs through Coconut at £12.99 a month.

### TaxCalc Individual

**Good**

- On HMRC's supplier list for all SA100 schedules, plus partnership and trust returns.
- £42 including VAT prepares up to six SA100 returns for one tax year, including non-resident pages ([TaxCalc shop](https://shop.taxcalc.com/individual)).
- Runs on Windows, Mac and Linux, with UK telephone support ([TaxCalc](https://www.taxcalc.com/products/individual-tax-return-software)).

**Bad**

- Desktop software you install, not a bookkeeping app. You bring the totals.
- Priced per tax year, so a new purchase each year.
- For MTD, HMRC lists TaxCalc as bridging software that connects to your records, not as record-keeping software.

### Coconut

**Good**

- The +Self Assessment plan (£129.99 a year) adds filing to bookkeeping, with bank feeds and receipt capture, and supports all the main schedules ([Coconut pricing](https://www.getcoconut.com/pricing)).
- On HMRC's MTD list for quarterly updates and the tax return.
- 24 months free for eligible new customers who open a Revolut or Zempler account through the app.

**Bad**

- The Bookkeeping plan charges £29.99 extra to file.
- Coconut is not on HMRC's Self Assessment supplier list under its own name. It says it is owned by the same directors as GoSimpleTax ([Coconut](https://www.getcoconut.com/gosimpletax-mtd)).

### ANNA

**Good**

- ANNA Self Assessment is on HMRC's supplier list and on the MTD list.
- Current offer: your 2026 to 2027 MTD filings free, and your 2025 to 2026 Self Assessment filed free if you sign up for the MTD offer ([ANNA](https://anna.money/plus-taxes/self-assessment/)).

**Bad**

- Requires signing up to ANNA's MTD service and connecting a bank account.
- The page does not say what it will cost after 2026 to 2027.

Bookkeeping software such as Xero, FreeAgent and Sage can also file returns. We compare those in [bookkeeping and tax software compared](/blog/bookkeeping-and-tax-software-compared).

## Accountant-led online services

These use qualified accountants and publish a fixed price.

### Taxfix (formerly TaxScouts)

**Good**

- An accountant prepares and files your return. Tiers were shown at £169, £249 and £499 with discounts to £99, £149 and £349 when we checked ([Taxfix](https://taxfix.com/en-uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/)).
- The middle tier adds a consultation call about tax optimisation. The top tier adds year-round accountant access.
- An accuracy guarantee: if the return is filed wrongly, it is fixed at no extra cost, subject to terms.

**Bad**

- The discounts are time-limited offers; budget on the full price.
- Advice on the entry tier is limited to messaging your accountant during filing.

### Crunch Self Assessment

**Good**

- Filed by an ACCA accountant, with HMRC submission included and a fixed price rather than hourly billing ([Crunch](https://www.crunch.co.uk/what-we-do/self-assessment)).
- You approve the return before it is filed.

**Bad**

- £240 plus VAT, the highest fixed price here for a standard return.
- A one-off filing service, not MTD quarterly updates.

## Your own accountant

**Good**

- Advice on your whole position: what you can claim, payments on account, whether to register for VAT.
- Can act as your agent with HMRC once you authorise them ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/appoint-tax-agent)).

**Bad**

- Fees vary by firm and by how tidy your records are. We found no published fee range from a professional body.
- HMRC does not regulate agents, and anyone can call themselves one. GOV.UK suggests checking qualifications or professional body membership, and how much the agent charges and when ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-to-choose-a-tax-agent)).
- The figures are still your responsibility, and you must not sign a blank return.

## A worked example of the choice

Say you sell on eBay and Vinted and your trading turnover last tax year was £14,000, with no other income except a part-time job. You are below every MTD threshold. HMRC's free online service handles an employment page and a self-employment page, and it calculates the tax for you. If your records are a monthly spreadsheet with sales and costs, that may be all you need.

Now say your turnover was £34,000. From 6 April 2027, MTD for Income Tax applies to turnover over £30,000, based on a return HMRC has already received. For the April 2026 start, HMRC says it looked at the 2024 to 2025 return ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-youre-eligible-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)). Once you are in MTD, you send quarterly updates and file the year-end return from MTD software, so the free HMRC form stops being an option.

## How to choose

Ask yourself:

1. Is my return simple enough for HMRC's form, or does it include things HMRC's online service cannot handle?
2. Will MTD apply to me in the next two years? If so, is it worth moving to MTD software now?
3. Do I want someone to check my claims, or just a tool to file?
4. What is the full price after any offer, and does it renew automatically?
5. Would I rather pay once a year for filing, or monthly for bookkeeping that also files?

Our view: for a reseller below the MTD thresholds with tidy records, the free HMRC route is hard to beat on cost. Paid help earns its fee where your situation has changed, or where you are unsure what counts as trading. ## Our verdict

If we had to choose one for a typical UK reseller, we would choose **HMRC's free online service**. By typical we mean a sole trader selling on eBay and Vinted, perhaps with a job as well, whose turnover is below the MTD thresholds and who keeps a monthly record of sales and costs.

Why:

- **It costs nothing.** Paid filing software here starts at £42 a year, and ANNA's free filing is tied to signing up for its MTD service.
- **HMRC works out the tax** from what you report, which is the same thing the paid software does for a simple return.
- **It handles the usual reseller return**: a self-employment page, plus an employment page if you have a job.
- **The deadline is 31 January**, three months later than paper.

Picks by situation:

- **If MTD applies to you, or will from April 2027:** Coconut, because it is on HMRC's MTD list for quarterly updates and the tax return, includes bank feeds and receipt capture, and is free for 24 months for eligible new customers who open a Revolut or Zempler account through the app. Our [bookkeeping software comparison](/blog/bookkeeping-and-tax-software-compared) covers other MTD options.
- **If your return is one HMRC's online service cannot take**, such as a partnership return or one with non-resident pages: TaxCalc Individual, because £42 including VAT covers all SA100 schedules, partnership and trust returns and non-resident pages.
- **If you want an accountant to prepare and file it for a fixed fee:** Taxfix, because its entry price (£169, shown at £99 when we checked) is below Crunch's £240 plus VAT and it has an accuracy guarantee.
- **If your situation has changed or you are unsure what counts as trading:** your own accountant, because only a person advising you can look at your whole position.

What we would avoid, and why:

- **Relying on the HMRC app to file**, because it does not file a return.
- **Staying on HMRC's online return once MTD applies**, because the return then has to come from MTD software.

## Common mistakes

- **Registering late.** Tell HMRC by 5 October after the tax year you first need to file ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/deadlines)).
- **Leaving it to late January** and then paying for a rushed service.
- **Assuming the app files.** It does not.
- **Keeping the free route after MTD applies.** The return has to come from MTD software.
- **Signing without reading.** Whoever prepares it, the figures are yours.
- **Buying software on an intro price** and forgetting it renews.
