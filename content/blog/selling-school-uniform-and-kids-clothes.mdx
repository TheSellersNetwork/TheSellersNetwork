This is for parents with a cupboard of things their children have outgrown: clothes, school uniform, toys and baby equipment. The end of the summer holidays is when most families sort through uniform, but everything here works at any time of year. By the end you will know where each kind of item sells best, what Vinted, eBay and Facebook Marketplace allow, which items safety organisations say should not be passed on to strangers, and the checks to do before you list. It is general information, not legal or safety advice for a particular product.

## The short version

- Children's clothes sell well on Vinted, especially in bundles by size. Selling your family's own things is not normally taxable.
- Plain school uniform sells like any children's clothing. Logo items usually do better through the school's own second-hand scheme or with local families.
- In England, schools must make sure second-hand uniform is available to parents, so your school may already run a swap or sale.
- Do not sell a used child car seat online. eBay bans them, and RoSPA's advice to parents is never to buy one second-hand from a stranger.
- eBay also bans used cots. Several baby sleep products are banned on eBay and Vinted.
- Before listing any toy or baby item, check it has not been recalled, and include every part and the instructions.

## Clothes

Children's clothes are some of the easiest things to sell second-hand, because children grow out of them before they wear them out.

**Where.** In our opinion, Vinted suits most children's clothing: it is quick to list, buyers search by age and size, and bundles let a parent buy a whole season in one parcel. Buyers can combine several of your items into one order, and you can set a bundle discount in your settings ([Vinted](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/422-set-up-a-discount-for-your-closet)). eBay works well for branded outdoor wear and bulk "job lots" by age. Facebook Marketplace suits local collection, for example a bag of clothes for a neighbour.

**How.**

- Group by age and season: "Girls' 5 to 6 years, summer bundle, 12 items" is easy for a parent to buy.
- Photograph each item, or lay a bundle out flat so every piece is visible. Show stains, bobbling and repairs.
- Add measurements for coats and school trousers, where age labels vary most. Our guide to [measuring clothes for listings](/guides/measuring-clothes-for-listings) shows how.
- Wash everything first. Vinted's [catalogue rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules) ban items that do not meet hygiene standards.

**Tax.** GOV.UK says you are unlikely to pay tax if you sell personal items from your home ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform)). Your children's old clothes are exactly that. Our post on [HMRC and your Vinted sales](/blog/hmrc-and-your-vinted-sales-what-is-true) explains the reporting rules, and why being reported is not the same as owing tax.

## School uniform

### Plain items

Grey trousers, white polo shirts, plain jumpers in standard colours and black school shoes are ordinary children's clothes. They sell best in bundles by size, listed in late summer when parents are shopping. Vinted's [catalogue rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules) ban official work uniforms, but we found nothing there about school uniform, and plain uniform is listed like any other children's clothing.

### Logo items

Blazers, jumpers and PE kit with a school badge have a much smaller market: families at that school. They can still sell online if you put the school's name in the title, but in practice they often move faster locally.

Before listing, try the school first. The Department for Education's [statutory guidance on the cost of school uniforms](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cost-of-school-uniforms/cost-of-school-uniforms), which applies to state schools and academies in England, says:

- Schools must make sure second-hand uniform is available for current and prospective parents, and publish information about it on the school's website.
- Schools can run this themselves, ask the parent-teacher association to, or join another established scheme.
- From September 2026, schools must limit compulsory branded items of uniform and PE kit to 3 or fewer, or 4 or fewer for secondary and middle schools if one is a tie.

So check the school's website or ask the office or PTA. They may run a uniform swap, a second-hand rail or a sale, and will often take donations. Fewer compulsory logo items should also mean more of your child's uniform is plain and easy to sell. The rules are different in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, and GOV.UK's [school uniform page](https://www.gov.uk/school-uniform) links to each.

### Name labels

Remove or cover name tapes before you photograph and sell anything, especially uniform with a school badge. A child's name and school together is not something to post publicly.

## Toys

Most toys are fine to sell on all three platforms, but check a few things first.

- **Recalls.** Search GOV.UK's [product recalls and alerts](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/product-recalls-and-alerts). eBay's [product safety policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/prohibited-restricted-items/product-safety-policy?id=4300) and Facebook's [commerce policies](https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/commerce) both ban recalled products.
- **Safety marks.** RoSPA's [second-hand goods advice](https://www.rospa.com/home-safety/product-safety/second-hand-goods) tells buyers to check for a CE or UKCA mark, and to wash stuffed toys in hot water. Photograph the mark and the age warning if you can find them.
- **Every part.** Count the pieces, include the instructions if you still have them, and say clearly if anything is missing.
- **Batteries.** Vinted allows used rechargeable batteries under 100 watt-hours, which covers most toys ([catalogue rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules)). Take out ordinary batteries or say whether they are included.
- **Baby toys on eBay.** eBay's [eligibility requirements](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/prohibited-restricted-items/products-eligibility-requirements-policy?id=5271) say baby toys can only be sold by business sellers registered in the UK who meet its selling history requirements. As a private seller, list baby toys on Vinted or Facebook instead.

## Prams, cots and other baby equipment

Vinted's [catalogue rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules) allow children's toys, furniture and childcare equipment. Big items such as prams, cots and high chairs are often easiest on Facebook Marketplace for local collection, since posting them is awkward. Read our guide to [Marketplace collection safety](/guides/facebook-marketplace-collection-safety) before anyone comes to your door.

What the platforms do not allow:

| Item | eBay | Vinted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Used cots | Not allowed; new cots must meet BS EN 716 | Not named; children's furniture is allowed |
| Cot bumpers, inclined sleepers, baby sleeping bags that can cover the head, weighted blankets, swaddles and sleep sacks | Not allowed | Unsafe sleeping bags (no arm holes, hoods, poor fit) not allowed |
| Feeding and self-feeding pillows | Check eBay's policy | Not allowed |
| Mattresses | Check eBay's policy | Allowed only in kids' categories |
| Used helmets | Check eBay's policy | Not allowed; only new |
| Safety harnesses | Check eBay's policy | Not allowed |

Sources: eBay's [product safety policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/prohibited-restricted-items/product-safety-policy?id=4300) and Vinted's [catalogue rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules).

The Lullaby Trust's [second-hand baby products advice](https://www.lullabytrust.org.uk/baby-safety/baby-product-information/second-hand-baby-products/) tells parents to check that second-hand items are complete, in good condition, have their instructions, and have not been recalled. It also advises a new mattress for each baby, because research suggests a second-hand mattress from outside the family home increases the risk of sudden infant death. If you are selling a cot or Moses basket, think about selling it without the mattress, and say so in the listing.

For a pram or pushchair, the same checks apply: check for recalls, include the instructions and every part, and describe honestly how the brakes, harness and folding mechanism work, with photos.

## Car seats: the special case

Child car seats are different from everything else here.

- **eBay** says used car seats are not allowed. New ones can only be listed by eligible sellers, and must meet UN Regulation No. 44 or No. 129 ([eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/prohibited-restricted-items/product-safety-policy?id=4300)).
- **RoSPA** runs the [Child Car Seats](https://www.childcarseats.org.uk/choosing-using/second-hand-child-seats/) website, produced with the support of the Department for Transport. Its advice is not to buy a second-hand child seat, because it may have been in a crash with damage you cannot see, the instructions are often missing, and older seats may not meet current standards. If a parent must use one, it says to accept it only from a family member or friend, and not to buy one from a second-hand shop, through the classified ads or online.
- **The Lullaby Trust** also advises parents not to use a second-hand car seat ([Lullaby Trust](https://www.lullabytrust.org.uk/baby-safety/baby-product-information/second-hand-baby-products/)).

We did not find car seats named in Vinted's catalogue rules or Facebook's commerce policies. Even so, given that the UK's main safety advice is that parents should not buy them from strangers online, our view is simple: do not sell a used car seat online. If a friend or relative wants it and you can tell them its full history, pass it on with the instructions. Otherwise, check your local council's guidance on disposing of it.

## What goes wrong

- **Listing logo uniform before asking the school.** The school's second-hand scheme may be the quickest home for it.
- **Leaving name tapes in.** Remove them before you take photos.
- **Selling a used car seat or cot on eBay.** Both are banned.
- **Missing a recall.** A two minute search on GOV.UK avoids it.
- **Listing baby toys on eBay as a private seller.** They are for eligible business sellers only.
- **Sending a bundle without showing every item.** Parents want to see what they are buying.
