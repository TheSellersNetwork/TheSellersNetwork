This is for Amazon sellers, new or established, who are adding up their subscriptions and wondering which ones earn their keep. It explains the main types of tool, what each one does, the free route for each job, and what the paid versions cost according to the vendors' own pricing pages. Tools are named as examples so you can check the prices yourself, not because we recommend them: there are others in every category, and we have no arrangement with any of them. Prices change often and some are in US dollars or euros, so check the page before you subscribe. This is general information, not financial advice.

## The short version

- There are five main types: **price history**, **sourcing and profit calculators**, **profit and accounting**, **repricers**, and **listing and research suites**.
- Every one of them has a free route, from Amazon's own revenue calculator and Seller Central reports to spreadsheets and this site's free tools. The paid versions are faster, not magic.
- Prices checked in September 2026 run from about £14 a month plus VAT for the cheapest sourcing plan to several hundred dollars a month for the biggest research suites.
- A tool is a monthly overhead whether or not anything sells. Add up what you pay and compare it with your monthly profit, not your sales.
- In our opinion most new resellers need one paid tool at most in their first months, and should add others only when a job is clearly taking too long by hand.

## The types of tool, and the free route for each

### Price history

**What it does:** shows how a product's price, sales rank and number of sellers have moved over time. This is how resellers judge whether something actually sells and whether today's price is normal or a spike. Our guide to [reading price history charts](/guides/amazon-reading-price-history-charts) explains what to look for.

**Free route:** the product's current sales rank and offers are on the Amazon listing itself, and your own sales history is in Seller Central's reports. What you cannot see for free on Amazon is the history for products you have not sold, which is why this is the tool many sellers pay for first.

**Example:** Keepa's own page lists Keepa Pro at 29 euros a month.

### Sourcing and profit calculators

**What they do:** you scan or look up a product, enter the buy price, and the tool shows the fees, profit and return on investment, usually with eligibility and restriction warnings.

**Free route:** Amazon's revenue calculator, linked from its [pricing page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/pricing), shows the fee category and size tier Amazon will use. Our [FBA calculator](/tools/calculator/amazon-fba) adds the fuel surcharge, storage and VAT on fees and tells you the most you can pay. Both work one product at a time. The paid tools are quicker when you are checking dozens of items in a shop or on a retailer's website.

**Example:** SellerAmp lists three UK plans at £13.95, £21.95 and £34.95 a month, each plus VAT, with two months free on annual billing and a 14-day free trial.

### Profit tracking and accounting

**What they do:** a profit tracker pulls your Amazon sales, fees, refunds and storage charges into one dashboard, matched against what you paid for the stock. Accounting software keeps your books, and some sellers use a connector that posts Amazon settlements into it.

**Free route:** Amazon's settlement and transaction reports in Seller Central, a spreadsheet, and this site's tools. Our [profit report](/tools/sales-reports) reads the sales report you download from Amazon and shows sales, fees and what you took home by month and by UK tax year, in your browser. Our [free spreadsheets](/tools/downloads) include a bookkeeping sheet, a stock tracker and a sourcing log.

**Examples:** Sellerboard lists plans from $19 to $79 a month billed monthly, or from $179 to $759 a year billed annually, with a one month free trial. Xero's UK page lists plans from £18 to £70 a month excluding VAT after an introductory discount.

### Repricers

**What they do:** change your price automatically in response to other sellers and the featured offer, within a minimum and maximum you set.

**Free route:** Amazon's Automate Pricing tool in Seller Central. Amazon's [Automate Pricing FAQ](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G202015620?locale=en-GB) says it is included in the Professional plan at no extra charge. Our guide to [repricing without a race to the bottom](/guides/amazon-repricing-without-a-race-to-the-bottom) explains how to set a price floor from your real costs and set it up. Paid repricers add more rule types and strategies.

**Example:** RepricerExpress lists £79 a month billed monthly, or £59 a month on annual billing as an introductory offer, for up to 1,000 SKUs, with a 14-day free trial.

### Listing and research suites

**What they do:** bundles of tools for finding products to develop, researching keywords, writing listings and managing adverts. They are built mainly for sellers creating their own branded products rather than reselling existing ones.

**Free route:** Amazon's own tools. Amazon's [seller tools page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/tools) describes Product Opportunity Explorer, which uses Amazon search and purchase data, and Brand Analytics for sellers with a registered brand.

**Example:** Helium 10 lists Platinum at $129 a month billed monthly or $99 a month billed yearly, and Diamond at $359 a month billed monthly or $279 a month billed yearly.

## Add up your own

List every subscription, convert it to a monthly figure in pounds including VAT, and add them up. Then compare that total with your monthly profit after stock and all Amazon fees, not with your sales. Our guide to [Amazon cash flow and overheads](/guides/amazon-cash-flow-and-overheads) suggests a check: if overheads are more than a third of net profit, start cutting with the one you use least.

Two things are easy to miss:

- **Annual plans save money but lock it up.** Two months free is a good deal on a tool you will use all year, and an expensive one on a tool you drop in March.
- **Free trials renew.** Put the renewal date in your calendar the day you start the trial.

## What to add, and when: our opinion

This is opinion, based on how sellers we know have built up, not a rule. Your products and routine may point elsewhere.

1. **Starting out.** Use the free routes: Amazon's revenue calculator, our [free tools](/tools), Seller Central reports and a spreadsheet. If you pay for one thing, make it the tool that helps you decide what to buy, because buying badly costs more than any subscription. For most resellers that is price history.
2. **Sourcing regularly.** When checking products one at a time in a free calculator is clearly slowing you down, a paid sourcing calculator is the next step. Use the free trial on a real sourcing session before paying.
3. **More than a handful of live products.** Try Amazon's Automate Pricing before paying for a repricer. Move to a paid one only if it cannot do something you actually need.
4. **When the spreadsheet stops working.** A profit tracker, or accounting software with an Amazon connector, earns its keep once reconciling by hand takes hours each month, and certainly by the time you are heading for VAT registration.
5. **Building your own brand.** Research suites are built for this. Most resellers selling existing products do not need one.

At every stage, the test is the same: does the tool save more time or make more money than it costs, measured over the last three months? If you cannot tell, you probably do not need it yet.
