This is for anyone selling their own things on eBay UK with a private (personal) account, who has ticked "no returns" and assumed that settles it. By the end you will know what the law expects of a private seller, what eBay's Money Back Guarantee adds on top, who pays for the return postage, which seller protections exist, and what to do if something comes back that is not what you sent. It is general information, not legal advice.

## The short version

- "No returns" on a private listing lets you turn down a buyer who has simply changed their mind. It does not let you refuse a return when the item does not match your listing.
- In law, a private seller must supply goods that match their description ([Sale of Goods Act 1979, section 13](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/54/section/13)). The wider "satisfactory quality" duty only applies to people selling in the course of a business.
- eBay's [Money Back Guarantee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy?id=4210) goes further than the law: a buyer can return an item that is faulty, damaged or not as described, whatever your returns policy says.
- When an item is returned as not as described, damaged or faulty, the seller pays the return postage.
- Your best protection is an accurate description with every flaw photographed, and tracked postage uploaded on time.
- If a return comes back swapped, keep the evidence, contact eBay before the refund deadline, and report fraud to Report Fraud.

## Private seller or business seller: why it matters

The law treats a sale between two private individuals differently from a sale by a trader to a consumer.

The [Consumer Rights Act 2015](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/2) applies when a "trader" sells to a "consumer". A trader is someone acting for purposes relating to their trade, business, craft or profession. If that is you, you have business seller duties, including the 14 day right to cancel for online sales ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds)) and the 30 day short-term right to reject faulty goods ([Consumer Rights Act 2015, section 22](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/22)). eBay's [business seller policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/business-seller-policy?id=4710) says anyone who sells items they bought to resell, or makes items in order to sell them, is a business seller. Those sellers should read our guide to [returns without losing money](/guides/returns-without-losing-money) and our post on [when eBay says you are a business seller](/blog/when-ebay-says-you-are-a-business-seller).

This post is about genuine private sales: selling your own belongings.

## What the law asks of a private seller

For a private sale, the main law is the Sale of Goods Act 1979.

**The item must match its description.** Section 13 says that where goods are sold by description there is an implied term that they will correspond with the description ([section 13](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/54/section/13)). An eBay listing is a sale by description: your title, item specifics, condition, photos and text are the description. Citizens Advice even publishes a [template letter](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/template-letters/letters/consumer-template-letters/letter-to-complain-about-misdescribed-goods-including-cars-bought-from-a-private-seller/) for buyers complaining about misdescribed goods from a private seller.

**There is no general quality promise.** Section 14 starts from the rule that there is "no implied term about the quality or fitness" of goods, and then adds the satisfactory quality term only "where the seller sells goods in the course of a business" ([section 14](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/54/section/14)). So a private seller who honestly describes a used item as used is not promising it is in shop condition.

**There is no cooling-off period.** Citizens Advice says a buyer has no cooling-off period when buying from a private individual, and names online auction sites as an example ([Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/changed-your-mind/changing-your-mind-about-something-youve-bought/)).

In short, a private seller's core legal duty is to be accurate. Scotland differs a little on remedies, but the duty to match the description still applies.

## What "no returns" does on eBay

eBay lets private sellers choose not to accept returns. Here is what that setting does and does not do, from eBay's own pages on [setting a returns policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/handling-return-requests/setting-return-policy?id=4368) and [returning an item](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/buying/returns-refunds/returning-item?id=4041).

| Buyer's reason | With "no returns" | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Changed their mind, does not fit, found it cheaper | You can decline | Your stated policy applies |
| Item does not match the listing | Buyer can still return it | eBay Money Back Guarantee |
| Item arrived damaged | Buyer can still return it | eBay Money Back Guarantee |
| Item is faulty | Buyer can still return it | eBay Money Back Guarantee |
| Item did not arrive | Buyer can claim a refund | eBay Money Back Guarantee |

eBay puts it plainly: even if you specify "no returns accepted", under the Money Back Guarantee the buyer can still return an item if it does not match the listing description.

The "faulty" row is where eBay goes further than the law, which does not make a private seller promise quality. The practical answer is the same: if you know about a fault, put it in the listing. An item faulty in the way you described matches its description.

## How the Money Back Guarantee works

These figures come from eBay's [Money Back Guarantee policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy?id=4210). eBay changes its policies, so check the page before relying on a detail.

**Item not received.** The buyer can report it up to 30 calendar days from the estimated delivery date. If the claim succeeds, the buyer gets a full refund including the original postage.

**Not as described, damaged or faulty.** The buyer can request a return within 30 calendar days of delivery, or within your stated returns window if that is longer. With "no returns" you have no window, so the 30 days applies.

**Your response time.** You should respond within 3 business days of the request. If it is not resolved, the buyer can ask eBay to step in from 3 business days after the request, and up to 21 business days after it.

**Your options.** When a return request opens, eBay's [seller guide](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/handle-return-request-seller?id=4115) lists what you can do: accept the return, offer a partial refund so the buyer keeps the item, give a full refund without a return, send a message, or offer a replacement. You can only decline if your policy says no returns and the buyer changed their mind. Once you get the item back, you should send the refund within 2 business days.

**Appeals.** If eBay decides against you, you can appeal with new information, such as tracking or photos eBay did not see, within 30 calendar days of the case closing ([eBay: appeal as a seller](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/appeal-outcome-case-seller?id=4369)).

## Who pays the return postage

eBay's [return postage page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/buying/returns-items-not-received-refunds-buyers/returning-item/return-postage?id=4066) sets it out:

- If the item was damaged, faulty or does not match the listing, **the seller pays** the return postage.
- If the buyer changed their mind and the seller's policy says buyers pay, the buyer pays. As a private seller with "no returns", you can simply decline a change-of-mind request, so this rarely comes up.
- If the seller offers free returns, the seller pays.

So a not as described claim costs you the return postage on top of the refund. On a low-value item, a partial refund can be cheaper for both of you.

## The seller protections that exist

eBay's [seller protections page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-protections?id=4345) lists several that apply to private sellers.

**Item not received.** You are protected if you send within your stated dispatch time and upload tracking before the estimated delivery date. The tracking has to show "delivered" or "attempted delivery", the delivery date, and an address matching the order. For orders of £450 or more, you also need signature confirmation ([eBay signature confirmation policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behaviour-policies/signature-confirmation-policy?id=5154)). The £450 is the total the buyer paid, including postage.

**Returned in worse condition.** If an item comes back used or damaged by the buyer, eligible sellers can deduct up to 50% from the refund to cover the lost value. For sellers who are not registered businesses, eBay says this applies to sellers who are not Below Standard and whose listings offer free returns. If you list with "no returns", assume this deduction is not open to you. Also note that eBay says you can no longer deduct if you let the case escalate and eBay steps in.

**Abusive buyers.** Where eBay decides a buyer has abused the process, it says it will remove related negative or neutral feedback, defects and open cases from your service metrics.

**The buyer's side.** eBay's [condition of returned items policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behavior-policies/condition-returned-items-policy?id=4763) says the exact item purchased must be returned for a full refund.

## How to protect yourself before you sell

Almost every not as described claim is decided on the gap between the listing and the item. Close that gap.

1. **Describe the item you actually have.** Brand, model, size, colour and condition, in plain words. Our guide to [descriptions that sell](/guides/descriptions-that-sell) covers the structure.
2. **Photograph every flaw.** Marks, chips, missing parts, wear. A flaw in a photo and in the text is part of the description. See [photos on a phone](/guides/photos-on-a-phone).
3. **Say what you have not tested.** "Powers on, not tested further" is honest; "works perfectly" is a promise.
4. **Record identifiers.** Serial numbers, IMEI numbers, batch codes, distinctive marks. Put them in your notes, and in the listing where it is safe to. These are how you prove what you sent.
5. **Use tracked postage and upload the tracking** before the estimated delivery date. Use signature confirmation for £450 or more.
6. **Photograph the item as you pack it.** For higher-value items, some sellers film the packing.

## If a return comes back swapped

It happens: a buyer opens a not as described request, and what comes back is an older model, a fake, a broken version of your item, or an empty box.

1. **Keep everything.** Do not throw away the packaging or label. Photograph the parcel before and after opening, and photograph what you received next to your original listing photos. Note any serial number that does not match.
2. **Contact eBay before the refund deadline.** You normally have 2 business days to refund once the return is marked delivered. Use the return's options to ask eBay to step in, explain that the item returned is not the one you sent, and upload your evidence. Our guide to [item not received and cases](/guides/item-not-received-and-cases) covers how cases run.
3. **Report the buyer** through eBay's reporting tools, so the pattern is on record.
4. **Report the fraud.** In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, report to [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/reporting-a-fraud/), the national service that replaced Action Fraud. In Scotland, contact Police Scotland on 101. Keep the reference number.
5. **Appeal if the decision goes against you,** within 30 calendar days, with any new evidence, including the fraud reference ([eBay: appeal as a seller](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/appeal-outcome-case-seller?id=4369)).

## What goes wrong

- **Treating "no returns" as a shield.** It only stops change-of-mind returns.
- **Describing from memory.** A wrong size or model number is a not as described claim waiting to happen.
- **Hiding a flaw in photo six.** Say it in the text as well.
- **Posting without tracking** to save a little on a valuable item, then losing an item not received claim.
