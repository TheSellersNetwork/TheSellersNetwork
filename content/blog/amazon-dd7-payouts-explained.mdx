This is for UK Amazon sellers who look at the Payments dashboard, see sales, and wonder why so little of it is available to transfer. By the end you will know what the "DD+7" delivery date reserve is, what Amazon's payment agreement says about transfers to your bank, how the two fit together on a real order, and how to plan stock buying around the gap. Everything here comes from Amazon's own agreement, its help pages and posts by Amazon staff on its seller forums. This is general information, not financial advice.

## The short version

- DD+7 means Amazon holds the proceeds of each order until **seven days after the delivery date**. Amazon staff call this the "default standard setting" reserve period ([Amazon staff post on account level reserves](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/aac4b8e9-c776-418e-8373-9484fbf8baa1)).
- The delivery date is the carrier's actual delivery date where Amazon can read the tracking, or the latest estimated delivery date where it cannot. Untracked orders therefore wait longer.
- Amazon can extend the reserve period for an account "following an assessment of your overall risk and historical performance".
- Once funds are available, Amazon's UK payment agreement says it will transfer your balance automatically on a fourteen day cycle, and you can request a transfer of your available balance once in every 24 hours ([Selling on Amazon Payments User Agreement, section 2.7](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GTVFVCWBJ6RJGKHM?locale=en-GB)).
- Reserved amounts show as **Deferred transactions** on the Payments dashboard, with an estimated release date per order.
- The reserve grows when your sales grow. A busy month means more of your money is in transit at any one time.

## What DD+7 actually is

Amazon's staff explanation is short. It reserves your funds "until a shipment is delivered, plus a reserve period", and the standard reserve period is seven days after delivery. The stated reason is to make sure you have enough money in your account to cover refunds, claims and chargebacks ([Amazon staff post](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/aac4b8e9-c776-418e-8373-9484fbf8baa1)).

Amazon gives its own example: you sell something on 1 January, it is delivered on 6 January, and under DD+7 the funds "will become available for disbursement starting on January 14". In other words, the seven days run after the delivery date and the money is released once they have passed.

Three details in the same post matter more than the headline:

1. **The delivery date depends on tracking.** Where you use an integrated shipping provider, Amazon uses the actual delivery date. Without valid tracking data, it uses the latest estimated delivery date, which is usually later.
2. **The period can be longer for your account.** Amazon says the reserve period "may be extended following an assessment of your overall risk and historical performance". It does not publish the criteria.
3. **The reserve scales with your sales.** Because the amount held is the value of orders still inside their reserve window, it "will increase or decrease over time, depending on your sales volume". New sellers, or sellers who have just moved to DD+7, often see all of their funds held at first. Amazon says that is normal.

## When it started for UK sellers

Amazon describes delivery date reserves as its standard policy rather than something new, and it moved UK and European accounts on to it in stages rather than on one day. Copies of Amazon's notices that sellers have posted on the seller forums give different dates for different accounts, some postponed and some with extensions on request. Because the date varied by account, we are not going to give you one. If you want to know when your account changed, look for the reserve policy notice in your Seller Central notifications and email.

What we can date is the dashboard change. Amazon announced on its forums that delivery date reserve amounts would show as **Deferred transactions** in the Total balance box on the Payments dashboard, instead of under the account level reserve ([Amazon announcement on deferred transactions](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/5eb04dd2-c1e7-47bb-9bf4-57049bc7d1fe)). Clicking the deferred balance shows the orders that make it up, the deferral reason and the estimated release date for each one. You may still see a separate account level reserve if an order has a claim or chargeback, or if your account is under review.

## Settlement, availability and disbursement

These three words get used interchangeably, and they are not the same thing.

| Stage | What it means | Where the rule comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Order completed | The sale is recorded on your account | Your Payments dashboard |
| Deferred | Proceeds held until delivery date plus the reserve period | [Amazon staff post](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/aac4b8e9-c776-418e-8373-9484fbf8baa1) |
| Available | Released from the reserve, can be transferred | Same post |
| Disbursed | Sent to your bank, automatically on the cycle or when you request it | [Payments User Agreement, 2.7](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/GTVFVCWBJ6RJGKHM?locale=en-GB) |

The UK payment agreement (last updated 28 August 2026 when we read it) says Amazon "will automatically transfer your Account balance to your Bank Account fourteen (14) days after the first business day on which the corresponding transactions were completed". It also says you "may initiate a transfer of your Account balance (excluding any amounts that are held in Reserve or are otherwise unavailable)" once in every 24-hour period, with a minimum of £1. Amazon's own seller site puts it more simply: it "pays you available funds every two weeks" ([Sell on Amazon UK](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/sell-online)).

The same section lets Amazon restrict access to your balance for as long as it reasonably thinks necessary in certain situations, including pending chargebacks, a suspected breach of the agreement, identity checks that cannot be completed, or a dispute on the account. Section 2.6 lets Amazon require a separate reserve and raise, lower or remove it at any time.

Amazon does not promise how long a transfer takes to reach your bank once it has been sent. Your Payments dashboard shows the date each transfer was initiated; allow for your bank's own processing on top.

## Worked timeline: an FBA order

This is an example to show how the rules stack up, not a promise of dates. We count in days from the order.

| Day | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Customer orders your item, fulfilled from Amazon's warehouse |
| 2 | Amazon delivers it and the delivery is tracked |
| 3 to 9 | Proceeds sit in Deferred transactions (the seven reserve days) |
| 10 | Funds become available, following the pattern in Amazon's own example |
| 10 onwards | You can request a transfer that day, or wait for the next automatic transfer on your fourteen day cycle |

With FBA, Amazon handles delivery and the tracking, so the delivery date is the real one. That is the fastest the policy allows. If you wait for the automatic cycle, the money might not leave Amazon for up to about two more weeks, depending on where Day 10 falls in your schedule.

## Worked timeline: an FBM order

Say you dispatch yourself with a one day handling time.

**With valid tracking** (for example a Royal Mail Tracked service or an Evri parcel bought through Buy Shipping):

| Day | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Order placed |
| 1 | You dispatch and confirm with the tracking number |
| 3 | Delivery scan |
| 4 to 10 | Seven reserve days |
| 11 | Funds become available |

**Without valid tracking** (say an untracked large letter), Amazon uses the latest estimated delivery date from your shipping template instead. If that date is Day 6, the reserve runs from Day 7 to Day 13 and the money is available on Day 14, three days later than the tracked version, even if the item arrived on Day 3.

The lesson is simple. On FBM, tracking is not just about claims. It also moves your money forward. Our post on [FBM tracking with Royal Mail and Evri](/blog/amazon-fbm-royal-mail-evri-tracking) covers which services count.

## What to do about it: cash flow tips

Amazon's own advice for getting paid faster is to dispatch as soon as you can and confirm dispatch, provide valid tracking, use an integrated carrier because the actual delivery date is usually earlier than the latest estimate, and choose faster delivery ([Amazon staff post](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/aac4b8e9-c776-418e-8373-9484fbf8baa1)). Beyond that:

- **Count the reserve as stock you have sold but not been paid for.** It is a real asset, but you cannot spend it. When you plan a buying trip, work from your available balance and your bank, not your total balance.
- **Expect a squeeze when you grow.** If your sales double this month, the deferred amount roughly doubles too, because more orders are inside their seven day window at once. This is when sellers run out of cash while looking profitable. Our [cash flow and overheads guide](/guides/amazon-cash-flow-and-overheads) shows how to build a simple forecast that includes it.
- **Plan for peak season early.** Q4 means more orders sitting in the reserve at once. Buy stock for November from money you have in hand by October.
- **Use the transfer button deliberately.** Requesting a transfer of your available balance can shorten the wait compared with the fourteen day cycle. It does not touch deferred funds.
- **Read the Deferred transactions report weekly.** It lists each order and its estimated release date, which is the most accurate forecast you will get of what is coming.
- **Keep a buffer for fees and refunds.** Amazon deducts fees, refunds and claims from your balance, and if the balance is negative at the end of a settlement period it can charge your card ([registration guide](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/sell-online/registration-guide)).

If you are starting out with a fixed budget, our worked example of [what £1,000 buys on Amazon FBA](/blog/what-1000-pounds-buys-on-amazon-fba) shows how long money is tied up across a whole cycle, from buying stock to the payout.

## What goes wrong

- **Treating total balance as money.** The top line on the dashboard includes deferred amounts. Only the available balance can be moved.
- **Posting FBM untracked to save pennies.** You lose days of cash on every order, and you lose protection in claims.
- **Assuming a long hold means a problem.** New accounts and fast growing accounts will see most of their money deferred at first. A separate account level reserve, on the other hand, can signal a claim, chargeback or review, and is worth checking in Account Health.
- **Not reading your own notice.** If your reserve period is longer than seven days, Amazon will have told you. Check before you plan around DD+7.
