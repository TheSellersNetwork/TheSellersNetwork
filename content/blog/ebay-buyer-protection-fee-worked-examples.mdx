This is for private sellers on eBay UK who have noticed that buyers see a higher price than the one they typed in, and for anyone comparing private and business listings. By the end you will know how the Buyer Protection fee is calculated, what it adds at £5, £20 and £100, how it shows up in offers and auctions, and how to set a price with it in mind. It is general information based on eBay's help pages, not financial advice, and eBay can change the bands at any time, so check the current page before relying on a figure.

## The short version

- On items from **UK private sellers**, buyers pay a Buyer Protection fee to eBay. Business sellers' items do not carry it ([Buyer Protection fee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/buying/paying-items/buyer-protection-fee?id=5594)).
- The fee is **10p per item plus 7% of the first £20, 4% of the part from £20 to £300, and 2% of the part from £300 to £4,000**. Nothing is added above £4,000. The figures include VAT.
- It is worked out on the item price only, not postage.
- **You receive the price you set.** eBay's example: list at £10, receive £10 ([information for sellers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/selling/pricing-items/buyer-protection-information-sellers?id=5595)).
- Buyers see the price including the fee while browsing, and offers and bids already include it.
- Worked out from the bands: £5 shows as **£5.45**, £20 as **£21.50** and £100 as **£104.70**, plus postage.
- If you care about the price buyers see, work backwards from it. The examples below show how.

## Who pays it and who does not

eBay's [Buyer Protection fee page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/buying/paying-items/buyer-protection-fee?id=5594) says:

- The fee applies when buying from a **UK-based private seller**.
- Buyer Protection is included **at no cost** when buying from business sellers.
- There is no fee on items from private sellers based outside the UK.
- Cars, Motorcycles and Vehicles, Classified Ads and Property are excluded.

For the seller, eBay's [information for sellers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/selling/pricing-items/buyer-protection-information-sellers?id=5595) is clear that the fee "is paid by the buyer to eBay". It does not come out of your money. Combined with free selling for most UK private sellers ([private seller fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822)), a typical domestic private sale has no eBay fee on the seller's side at all. When eBay introduced the fee, it also changed payout timing for private sellers; our change post on [the Buyer Protection fee](/blog/ebay-buyer-protection-private-sellers) covers that history.

## The bands

From the [Buyer Protection fee page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/buying/paying-items/buyer-protection-fee?id=5594):

| Part of the item price | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| Every item | 10p flat fee |
| Up to £20 | 7% |
| From £20 to £300 | 4% of that part |
| From £300 to £4,000 | 2% of that part |
| Above £4,000 | Nothing more |

The bands work like income tax bands: each rate only applies to the part of the price inside its band. A £100 item pays 7% on the first £20 and 4% on the next £80, not 4% on the lot.

Other rules on the same page:

- **Postage is not included** in the calculation.
- If a buyer buys **several of the same item** from one listing, the 10p flat fee is charged once.
- If the buyer gets a **full refund**, the fee is refunded too. A partial refund includes a proportionate share of the fee.

## Three worked examples

These use the bands above. The £5 and £20 results match the examples eBay gives on its own page.

### Say you list an item at £5

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Flat fee | £0.10 |
| 7% of £5 | £0.35 |
| **Buyer Protection fee** | **£0.45** |
| **Price the buyer sees** | **£5.45 plus postage** |
| You receive | £5.00 |

At this price the fee is 9% of your price. The 10p flat fee is what makes low-priced items look proportionally dearer.

### Say you list an item at £20

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Flat fee | £0.10 |
| 7% of £20 | £1.40 |
| **Buyer Protection fee** | **£1.50** |
| **Price the buyer sees** | **£21.50 plus postage** |
| You receive | £20.00 |

That is 7.5% of your price.

### Say you list an item at £100

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Flat fee | £0.10 |
| 7% of the first £20 | £1.40 |
| 4% of the next £80 | £3.20 |
| **Buyer Protection fee** | **£4.70** |
| **Price the buyer sees** | **£104.70 plus postage** |
| You receive | £100.00 |

That is 4.7% of your price. The higher the price, the smaller the fee as a share of it.

### A multi-buy

eBay's own example is two identical £20 T-shirts from one listing: 7% of £20 twice (£2.80) plus a single 10p, so £2.90 in fees and £42.90 shown, plus postage ([Buyer Protection fee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/buying/paying-items/buyer-protection-fee?id=5594)).

## Why buyers see a higher price

eBay says the price buyers see "will always include the Buyer Protection fee so there are no surprises at checkout" ([information for sellers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/selling/pricing-items/buyer-protection-information-sellers?id=5595)). So your £20 listing appears as £21.50 in search results, next to a business seller's identical item at £20 with no fee added.

The same applies to offers and auctions:

- When a buyer bids or makes an offer, **the fee is included in their amount**. A buyer who offers £21.50 is offering you £20.
- **You see the offer or bid without the fee**, so you know what you will receive.
- On a counteroffer, you see the buyer's breakdown: the amount you receive and the fee paid to eBay.

This matters for how you read offers. If you are used to thinking "they offered £18 on my £20 item", check which figure you are looking at. Our guide to [Best Offer strategy](/guides/best-offer-strategy) and the [offer calculator](/tools/offer-calculator) help with the rest.

## How to price with it in mind

eBay's pages do not tell you to change your prices. Whether to do so is a judgement call. Here is how we would think about it.

### Decide which number you are targeting

Look at sold prices for your item. Our guide to [pricing from sold comps](/guides/pricing-from-sold-comps) explains how. Then ask: are the comparable sales mostly from private sellers (whose shown prices already include the fee) or business sellers (whose do not)?

- If you are competing mostly with **other private sellers**, your shown prices are on the same footing. You can price as you normally would.
- If you are competing with **business sellers** on identical items, your listing will look dearer by the fee. You may want to set your price a little lower so the buyer's price lines up.

### Working backwards from a target shown price

Say you want buyers to see about £25 (before postage) for an item.

1. Anything over £20 pays £1.50 on the first £20 (10p plus £1.40).
2. Every pound above £20 costs the buyer £1.04 (the pound plus 4p).
3. So: £25 minus £1.50 is £23.50. Take off the first £20 and you have £3.50 to cover the part above £20. £3.50 divided by 1.04 is about £3.37.
4. Set a price of about **£23.37**. Check: 10p + £1.40 + 4% of £3.37 (about 13p) comes to about £1.63, so the buyer sees about £25.00.

For an item under £20, divide your target (less 10p) by 1.07. A target of £10 shown means a price of about £9.25.

eBay's page does not say how the fee is rounded, so treat these as approximate and check the shown price on your live listing.

### When not to bother

- On most everyday items the fee is a few per cent. Photos, title and condition notes usually move a sale more than a small price change.
- If you are a genuine private seller, you already keep the whole price. Cutting it to match a business seller's shown price means taking less than a business seller would after fees. Our [eBay fee calculator](/tools/fees/ebay) shows what a business seller keeps from the same sale.
- If you are really selling as a business, you should be on a business account anyway. See [when eBay says you are a business seller](/blog/when-ebay-says-you-are-a-business-seller).

## What goes wrong

- **Pricing to match a buyer's offer figure.** Offers include the fee. Read the amount you will receive.
- **Using a single percentage.** The flat 10p and the bands mean no single rate is right. Work it through, or compare with the shown price on your listing.
- **Forgetting postage is separate.** Free postage does not change the fee, because it is calculated on the item price.
- **Assuming the bands never change.** eBay sets and can change them. Check the [current page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/buying/paying-items/buyer-protection-fee?id=5594) before repricing a lot of stock.
