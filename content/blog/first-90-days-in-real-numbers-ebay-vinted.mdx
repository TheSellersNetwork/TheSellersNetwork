This is for anyone who has spent roughly three months buying things to resell on eBay, Vinted or both, and for anyone about to start who wants to see what the early months actually cost. It sets out a case-study format for the forum, "My first 90 days on eBay and Vinted, in real numbers", with a template to fill in and a simple way to work out what you really made. Sourcing is split by where you bought, because a car boot haul, a charity shop run and an online bundle have very different costs. This is general information, not tax or financial advice.

## The short version

- The template splits spending by source (car boot, charity shop, online and other), fees by platform, postage and packaging, and time.
- It asks for sold and unsold stock side by side. Unsold stock is where most first-quarter profit quietly goes.
- Use your own records only: platform reports, your bank statement, receipts and a log of your trips.
- Post finished accounts in [Wins and case studies](/community/c/wins-and-case-studies), or keep a running thread in [Diaries and challenges](/community/c/diaries-and-challenges).
- No links to your listings, and crop personal data out of any screenshot.
- A loss or a break-even is a useful post. It is the part of reselling that haul videos and adverts rarely show.

## Why this format

Most of what people see about reselling is the highlight: the £2 find that sold for £40. It is real, and it is also the exception that gets filmed. What almost nobody shows is the whole of a first quarter: what went on entry fees and fuel, how much of the stock is still in a box, how many evenings went on photos and packing.

We have a matching format for Amazon sellers in [my first 90 days on Amazon FBA, in real numbers](/blog/first-90-days-in-real-numbers-amazon). This one is built for secondhand and clearance reselling on eBay and Vinted, where sourcing costs are small per item but add up, and where the fee picture depends on how your account is set up.

## Check your account type first

Your fees depend on it, so say which you use in your post.

- **eBay.** eBay's [business seller policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/business-seller-policy?id=4710) treats people who sell items they have bought to resell as business sellers. UK private sellers pay no final value fee in most categories, with motors the main exception ([eBay private seller fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822)). Business sellers pay a final value fee that depends on the category, a per-order fee of £0.30 on orders of £10 or less and £0.40 on orders over £10, and a regulatory operating fee of 0.35%, all worked out on the total including postage and all shown excluding VAT ([eBay business seller fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/business-selling-fees?id=4809)). Our post on [when eBay says you are a business seller](/blog/when-ebay-says-you-are-a-business-seller) covers the switch.
- **Vinted.** Vinted says standard accounts cannot be used to sell professionally, including "buying or sourcing items and reselling them at a profit", and that if you sell to make a profit you need to sign up as a Pro seller ([Vinted: commercial selling](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/1120-commercial-selling)). Vinted says Pro is free for sellers to list and sell ([Vinted Pro](https://www.vinted.co.uk/pro)); the buyer pays a fee at checkout. The detail is in our guide to [Vinted Pro and selling as a business](/guides/vinted-pro-and-selling-as-a-business).

If you are fairly new and have not sorted this out yet, say so plainly in your post. It helps other readers more than a tidy account that skips it.

## Gather your records

- **eBay:** your transaction report or the payments section of Seller Hub, which lists sales and the fees charged.
- **Vinted:** your balance history and sales list.
- **Your bank statement** and any card or cash records for sourcing.
- **Receipts** for stock where you got them, and a note of cash spends where you did not. Car boots and markets are often cash, so write it down on the day.
- **A trip log:** date, where, miles, parking and entry fees.
- **A rough hours log.** If you did not keep one, give an estimate and label it.

## Working out the result honestly

### Profit on what sold

For each sold item, take what the buyer paid for the item, subtract the platform fees on that sale, the postage and packaging you paid, and what you paid for the item. Add these up, then subtract the overheads that are not tied to one sale: entry fees, parking and travel for trips, subscriptions and tools. That is your profit on what sold.

Our [where to sell tool](/tools/where-to-sell) shows what you keep after fees on each platform for the same price, which is useful if you sell on both and want to check a few sales against what the platform actually charged.

### Unsold stock

List the cost of what you still hold, at what you paid for it, not what you hope to get. If a lot of it has been listed for weeks without selling, run a few items through the [stock ageing and price-drop planner](/tools/stock-ageing). It works out the lowest price that still covers your costs and a plan for bringing prices down. In your post, say how much of your first 90 days' spend is sitting in unsold stock. That single figure tells other readers more than any headline profit.

### Cash in the bank

As a cross-check, compare total money in from sales with total money out on everything. On a first quarter with plenty of stock still unsold, this is often negative even when sold items made money. That is normal, and it is worth saying so.

### Your hourly rate

Divide your profit by your total hours. The [is it worth my time tool](/tools/worth-my-time) does this for single items and compares the result with the National Living Wage, which GOV.UK lists as £12.71 an hour for people aged 21 and over from April 2026 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/national-minimum-wage-rates)). This is not a judgement on whether reselling is worth it for you. It is a number you should know.

## Log individual finds in Pickups

The 90-day template is for the whole picture. If you want to share single finds as you go, with what you paid and what they sold for, use [Pickups](/community/pickups). A run of Pickups entries is also a handy record to pull from when you write up your 90 days.

## How to post it

1. **Choose the forum.** Finished accounts go in [Wins and case studies](/community/c/wins-and-case-studies). A loss belongs there too. For a running account, post in [Diaries and challenges](/community/c/diaries-and-challenges) and update as you go.
2. **Title it** "My first 90 days on eBay and Vinted, in real numbers", or just the platform you used.
3. **Fill every line.** Blank is fine, missing is not.
4. **Add context.** Hours a week available, whether you have a job alongside, where you live if it affects sourcing (rural and urban car boots are very different).
5. **Answer questions** about how you got a figure.

## House rules for these posts

These sit on top of the general [house rules](/community/rules).

- **Real numbers only.** From your own records. Label any estimate as an estimate.
- **No links to your listings or shop.** Describe items by type if you want to.
- **No screenshots with personal data.** Screenshots of reports and balances are welcome once you have cropped or covered buyer names, addresses, order numbers and your own bank details. If in doubt, type the figures.
- **Be kind to losses.** A clear account of a bad quarter is one of the most useful things anyone can post. If a reply mocks or piles on, flag it.
