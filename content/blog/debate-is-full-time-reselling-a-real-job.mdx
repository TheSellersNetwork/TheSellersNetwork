Tell someone you resell full time and you may get admiration, a blank look, or "that's not a proper job though, is it?". Full-time resellers often feel they have to defend their work to family, friends and sometimes their bank. Others in the community quietly agree that it is not quite a job, because of what it lacks rather than how hard it is. This post sets out both sides, the facts about being self-employed in the UK, and ends with a poll. It is general information, not tax, pension, mortgage or financial advice.

## The short version

- If you resell for a living you are almost certainly self-employed: running a business, not holding a job in the employment law sense.
- Self-employed people pay Class 4 National Insurance on profits over £12,570, and profits of £7,105 or more protect your State Pension record automatically.
- GOV.UK says the self-employed do not get holiday or sick pay when they are not working.
- There is no employer adding to your pension. Tax relief on your own contributions still applies.
- Lenders will usually want proof of income, such as HMRC tax calculations, and many self-employed people find this the hardest part.
- Supporters say it is a real business with real skills. Sceptics say it lacks the security that makes something a job.

## What is happening

Reselling has grown from a side hustle into a full-time living for some people, helped by marketplaces that make it easy to sell from home. Many sellers start part-time and move to full time when the numbers seem to work. Our guide to [going full time without burning out](/guides/going-full-time-without-burning-out) covers the practical side of that step.

What changes when you go full time is not only the hours. You move from having an employer who handles tax, pays you when you are ill and adds to your pension, to doing all of that yourself.

### What GOV.UK says about being self-employed

GOV.UK's [working for yourself](https://www.gov.uk/working-for-yourself) page says you are probably running a business if you take responsibility for its success or failure, have several customers at the same time and can decide how, where and when you work. A full-time reseller fits all three. If you buy to resell, HMRC will usually see you as trading; see [HMRC and your Vinted sales](/blog/hmrc-and-your-vinted-sales-what-is-true) for how the platform reporting rules fit in.

On rights at work, GOV.UK's page on [self-employed status](https://www.gov.uk/employment-status/selfemployed-contractor) says employment law does not cover self-employed people in most cases because they are their own boss, and that they do not get holiday or sick pay when they are not working.

### National Insurance and the State Pension

For the 2026 to 2027 tax year, GOV.UK's [self-employed National Insurance rates](https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-national-insurance-rates) are:

| Profits | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Under £7,105 | Nothing to pay. You can choose to pay voluntary Class 2 contributions (£3.65 a week) to protect your record |
| £7,105 or more | Class 2 is treated as paid, so your National Insurance record is protected without paying it |
| £12,570 to £50,270 | Class 4 at 6% on profits in this band |
| Over £50,270 | Class 4 at 2% on profits above this |

Those qualifying years matter. GOV.UK says you need [10 qualifying years to get any new State Pension](https://www.gov.uk/new-state-pension/how-its-calculated), and [35 qualifying years for the full rate](https://www.gov.uk/new-state-pension/what-youll-get) if your record starts after April 2016. The full rate is £241.30 a week. A reseller whose profits stay under £7,105 for several years, and who does not pay voluntary contributions, may have gaps.

### Sick pay and other support

With no employer, there is no statutory sick pay. GOV.UK says self-employed people may be able to get [New Style Employment and Support Allowance](https://www.gov.uk/employment-support-allowance/eligibility) if they have paid enough National Insurance, usually in the last 2 to 3 years, with credits counting. Self-employed parents may be able to get [Maternity Allowance](https://www.gov.uk/maternity-allowance/eligibility) instead of statutory maternity pay, if they meet the conditions.

### Pensions

GOV.UK describes a [workplace pension](https://www.gov.uk/workplace-pensions/about-workplace-pensions) as one arranged by your employer, who in most cases also adds money. Self-employed resellers have no employer to do that. They can still pay into a private pension, and GOV.UK says you can get [tax relief on contributions](https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-your-private-pension/pension-tax-relief) worth up to 100% of your annual earnings, with basic rate relief of 20% added by the provider.

### Mortgages and proof of income

GOV.UK says you might be asked for an [SA302 tax calculation](https://www.gov.uk/sa302-tax-calculation) as evidence of income, for example when applying for a mortgage as a self-employed person, and that you can get these for the last four years once your return has been filed. Each lender sets its own rules on how many years of figures it wants and whether it accepts printed copies.

## The case for: it is a real job, and a real business

Full-time resellers who reject the stigma make these points.

**It uses real skills.** Sourcing, pricing from sold data, photography, writing listings, customer service, stock control, bookkeeping and dealing with platform policy are all skills that employers pay for. A full-time reseller does all of them every week.

**It is a business, which is more than a job.** Supporters say calling it "not a real job" misses the point. Running a business with stock, customers, tax returns and accounts is at least as serious as working for someone else, and it is recognised as such by HMRC.

**It pays tax and National Insurance.** A profitable full-time reseller files a Self Assessment return, pays Class 4 National Insurance and builds State Pension years like anyone else who works.

**Flexibility is a benefit, not a weakness.** Many full-time resellers chose it because it fits around children, caring, health conditions or other work in a way an employer would not. Supporters say that is a reason to take it seriously, not to dismiss it.

**It is good for the circular economy.** Keeping goods in use is useful work, and supporters see no reason it should count for less than making something new.

## The case against: it lacks what makes a job a job

Sceptics, including some full-time sellers, make these points.

**No safety net.** No sick pay, no holiday pay, no employer pension contributions and no redundancy pay. GOV.UK is clear on the first two. A bad month is your problem alone, and so is a broken wrist.

**Income is uncertain.** Sales swing with the season, platform changes, account problems and the economy. One policy change or a frozen account can stop income overnight. Critics say that is the difference between a job and a gamble.

**Borrowing is harder.** Lenders usually want a track record of accounts or tax calculations. Sceptics say anyone planning to buy a home should know that leaving employment for reselling can make it harder for a while.

**Pensions get forgotten.** Without automatic enrolment, many self-employed people put off saving. Sceptics say that is where the real long-term cost of "being your own boss" shows up.

**Hours are unpaid and unlimited.** Sourcing trips, late-night listing and weekend packing do not stop at 5pm. Some full-time sellers say that once they count every hour, their hourly rate is lower than a job they left.

## What the rules and the law say

- **You are self-employed, not an employee.** Employment law mostly does not apply, per [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/employment-status/selfemployed-contractor). That means no statutory sick pay or holiday pay.
- **You must register and report.** GOV.UK says if you are running a business you need to [set it up properly and report your income to HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/working-for-yourself).
- **National Insurance and State Pension.** The thresholds and rates above apply for 2026 to 2027 and usually change each April. Check the [current GOV.UK page](https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-national-insurance-rates) and your own State Pension forecast.
- **Proof of income.** HMRC SA302 tax calculations are a common form of evidence. Lenders set their own rules, so ask before you need it.

## Questions to think about

- If you resell full time, what reaction do you get when you tell people, and has it changed?
- What makes something a "real job" to you: hours, income, security, or skill?
- Have you set up a pension, and did being self-employed make you more or less likely to?
- Did going full time make it harder to get a mortgage, a tenancy or credit?
- What safety net would you want in place before going full time, or recommend to someone else?
