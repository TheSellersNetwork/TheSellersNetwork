This is for Vinted sellers who have seen the City of London Trading Standards fact sheet on Vinted shared around and want to know what it really says. By the end you will know the main points of the fact sheet, which of them touch sellers directly, how they compare with Vinted's terms, including the version that applies from 8 October 2026, and what you can do in practice. We have been careful to separate what Trading Standards says (its view) from what Vinted's terms say (the contract) and from the law (which only a court can settle). This is general information, not legal advice.

## The short version

- City of London Trading Standards published a [Vinted fact sheet for consumers](https://www.cityoflondon.gov.uk/assets/Business/fact-sheet-2026-vinted.pdf), version 3, updated March 2026. It is written mainly for buyers, but has a section on sellers.
- Its main concerns: buyers paying return postage on items that are not as described, the two-day window to report a problem, and whether "Buyer Protection" was a misleading name.
- On return postage and the two-day rule, Trading Standards gives its **opinion** that the terms are unfair. The fact sheet itself says only the courts can interpret the law with authority.
- For sellers, it records complaints about buyers falsely claiming items are not as described or fake, and keeping the goods without paying.
- Vinted's terms from 8 October 2026 rename the fee and the refund policy, but keep buyer-paid return postage and the two-day window.
- The practical response for sellers is evidence, speed and knowing the escalation route, not relying on the fact sheet to win a dispute.

## Who wrote it and why

The fact sheet says City of London Trading Standards has been monitoring complaints about Vinted made to the Citizens Advice consumer service, because Vinted is associated with an address in the City of London. It also says it has looked at complaints posted on public review sites.

It is a local authority's guidance, not a law, a court ruling or a regulator's enforcement decision. It ends by saying that any legal opinions in it are Trading Standards' own view and that only the courts can interpret the law with authority. Read everything below with that in mind.

## What the fact sheet says

### The complaints it has seen

Trading Standards lists the main categories of complaint it has seen:

- faulty or damaged goods;
- fake or counterfeit goods, which it says continued to be a problem in 2025 and into 2026;
- buyers of misdescribed, unsatisfactory or fake goods having to pay to send them back;
- the two-day window for raising a problem being too short;
- buyer protection not being what customers expect;
- delivery problems.

### Consumer to consumer sales

The fact sheet points out that most Vinted purchases are probably a contract between two consumers, and that in that case the law only requires the goods to match their description. This matters for sellers: a private seller is not in the same position as a shop. The fact sheet does not cite a specific Act for this point.

### The fee and "buyer protection"

The fact sheet says selling on Vinted is free and that buyers pay what Vinted called a Buyer Protection Fee. In Trading Standards' view, based on the complaints it has seen, there was "no real buyer protection", and the name was potentially misleading. Its main example is return postage: a buyer who receives an item significantly not as described has to pay to send it back unless the seller agrees to pay.

### Return postage on items not as described

Trading Standards says that, in its opinion, a term making the buyer pay return postage for misdescribed items is not a fair term, and it encourages buyers to challenge it. This is the point most often repeated online as if it were settled law. It is not. It is Trading Standards' opinion, and the fact sheet does not name the provision it relies on.

### The two-day window

Vinted completes an order and releases payment to the seller unless the buyer raises a problem within two days. Trading Standards says it feels this is unfair and far too short for a buyer to discover problems. Again, this is its view.

### Vinted's response

The fact sheet says Trading Standards met Vinted and raised these concerns, and that Vinted will not be making significant changes to the platform or its policies.

### The escalation route it suggests

It says Vinted is not committed to any alternative dispute resolution scheme, and suggests buyers:

1. use the complaints process in the Vinted app;
2. if that fails, email Vinted's legal team (the address is in the fact sheet);
3. if they paid by card, raise a disputed transaction with their bank or card provider, and if the bank refuses a chargeback, complain to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

It also explains that a claim can be taken to the County Court, that you need the other party's name and address and can only get that by formally asking Vinted, and it names Vinted Platform Limited as the UK entity for claims against Vinted itself.

## The parts that affect sellers

### Complaints from sellers

The fact sheet has a short section on sellers. It says Trading Standards has received complaints from sellers where a buyer falsely claims that goods are not as described, counterfeit or damaged, and that in the cases it has seen the buyer was allowed to keep the goods without paying.

Its suggested route for sellers is the same first two steps (the in-app complaints process, then Vinted's legal team). After that, the only option may be a County Court claim against the buyer. It warns this will not be easy: you need the buyer's name and address, Vinted may be unwilling to provide them, and the buyer may be outside the UK.

### Chargebacks aimed at Vinted

Trading Standards encourages buyers to use chargebacks and the Financial Ombudsman Service. For sellers, that means more card disputes may start outside Vinted. Vinted's [chargeback help page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1624-chargebacks-for-vinted-purchases) asks buyers to contact Vinted before opening a chargeback, and says an account may be restricted if a dispute is opened outside Vinted first. The fact sheet does not say what happens to a seller's money when a buyer's chargeback succeeds, and Vinted's help page does not set that out either, so we do not know.

## How it lines up with Vinted's terms

Vinted's terms change on 8 October 2026; our [summary of the October 2026 terms](/blog/vinted-terms-october-2026) sets out the detail. Here is how the fact sheet's points compare.

| Fact sheet point | Terms until 8 October 2026 | Terms from 8 October 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name of the buyer's fee | Buyer Protection fee | Vinted fee, described as a mandatory platform fee that helps cover the cost of running the site ([section 15](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms-and-conditions)) |
| Name of the refund rules | Buyer Protection policy | Refund policy, which the terms say is not insurance or a legal protection service and is in addition to your other legal rights ([section 11](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms-and-conditions)) |
| Two-day window to report a problem | Two days | Two calendar days from Vinted's notice that the item was delivered or seems lost ([section 11](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms-and-conditions)) |
| Return postage on items not as described | Buyer pays unless agreed with the seller | Buyer pays unless agreed with the seller ([section 11](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms-and-conditions); [Vinted help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/272-returning-an-item)) |
| Counterfeit claims with strong evidence against the item | Seller has 24 hours to prove authenticity | Seller has 48 hours; if not, the order is cancelled and the buyer does not have to return the item |

So the renaming answers the fact sheet's complaint about the word "protection" in the fee's name, at least on paper. Vinted has not said whether that was the reason. The two points Trading Standards called unfair, buyer-paid return postage and the two-day window, stay in the new terms.

The counterfeit rule is the closest match to the sellers' complaints in the fact sheet. Under both versions of the terms, where Vinted has strong evidence an item is fake and the seller cannot prove otherwise in time, the buyer is refunded and keeps the item. The new terms give sellers 48 hours instead of 24. Our guide to [fake and not as described claims on Vinted](/guides/vinted-fake-or-not-as-described-claims) walks through each outcome.

## What sellers can do

1. **Describe and photograph as if you will have to prove it.** In a consumer to consumer sale, the description is what you are held to. Photograph labels, flaws, measurements and serial numbers before listing.
2. **Keep proof of authenticity before you list branded items.** Receipts, order confirmations, tags. You have 48 hours under the new terms, which is not long enough to go looking.
3. **Consider covering return postage when a claim is genuinely your fault.** Vinted lets sellers offer a return label. Nothing requires you to, but it removes the point Trading Standards most objects to, and a buyer who feels treated fairly is less likely to take a chargeback route.
4. **Use Vinted's process first, then escalate in writing.** Follow the in-app route, keep screenshots, then use the escalation step the fact sheet describes.
5. **Know the court route is there, and its limits.** Trading Standards is frank that small claims may not be worth pursuing, that you need the buyer's details from Vinted, and that you may struggle to get paid even if you win. The GOV.UK page on [making a court claim for money](https://www.gov.uk/make-court-claim-for-money) explains the process and fees.
6. **Report problems.** The fact sheet says the [Citizens Advice consumer service](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/about-us/information/contact-the-consumer-service/) passes developments to Trading Standards. Sellers who have lost items to false claims can report too.

## What goes wrong

- **Quoting the fact sheet as if it were law.** A buyer or seller who tells the other side "Trading Standards says this term is illegal" is overstating it. It is an opinion from an enforcement body, not a ruling.
- **Assuming the new terms fixed the complaints.** The fee has a new name. Return postage and the two-day window have not changed.
- **Waiting to gather evidence until a claim arrives.** By then the window may have closed.
- **Arguing in the chat.** Keep messages short and factual; they may end up in front of Vinted, a bank or a court.
