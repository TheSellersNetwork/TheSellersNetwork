This is for eBay UK sellers on a private account who have started buying things to sell, and are wondering whether eBay expects them to register as a business. By the end you will know what eBay's own rules say, why there is no published sales threshold, how eBay's account type differs from your tax position with HMRC, what changes on fees, returns and your listings when you switch, and the steps to switch without a mess. It is general information, not legal or tax advice.

## The short version

- eBay does not publish a sales number or turnover figure that makes you a business seller. Its test is what you sell and why.
- eBay's [business seller policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/business-seller-policy?id=4710) says you are a business seller if you sell items you bought to resell, make items in order to sell them, or buy items for your business.
- Your eBay account type and your tax position are two separate questions. HMRC has its own test, the badges of trade, and its own thresholds.
- As a business seller you pay final value fees, a per-order fee and a regulatory operating fee, and you must meet consumer law on returns, including the 14 day right to cancel.
- Buyers of business sellers pay no Buyer Protection fee, so your price is what they pay before postage.
- eBay says an upgrade from a personal to a business account cannot be reversed.

## What eBay actually says

eBay's wording is consistent across its pages. The [business seller policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/business-seller-policy?id=4710) defines business sellers as people who "sell items they have bought to resell", "make items in order to sell them", or "buy items for their business". Its [business account page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/selling/start-selling/create-ebay-business-account) adds that if you want to sell large quantities, have items you have made or bought to resell, or already have a business outside eBay, you need a business account, and that casual sellers of things they no longer want should use a private account.

Notice what is missing: a number. We could not find any eBay UK page that says "over X sales" or "over £Y a year" makes you a business. Figures you see quoted online are not eBay's. The question eBay asks is about intent: did you get this item in order to sell it?

In practice:

| What you are doing | What eBay's policy points to |
| --- | --- |
| Clearing out your own clothes, books and gadgets | Private |
| Selling a large clear-out of your own things over a few months | Private, as long as they are your own things |
| Buying at car boots or charity shops to sell on | Business |
| Making items to sell | Business |
| Running a shop or trade elsewhere and selling its stock on eBay | Business |

The policy also says business sellers must provide full business contact details, clear price information, a VAT number if the business is VAT registered, and a returns policy. Breaking the policy can lead to listings being removed, warnings, restrictions or suspension.

## HMRC's test is a different test

eBay decides which account type you should have. HMRC decides whether you are trading for tax. They often point the same way, but they are separate.

HMRC uses the **badges of trade**, set out in its [Business Income Manual](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/business-income-manual/bim20205): profit-seeking motive, the number of transactions, the nature of the asset, similar trading transactions or interests, changes made to the asset, the way the sale was carried out, the source of finance, the interval between purchase and sale, and the method of acquisition. HMRC says no single badge is decisive; it looks at the overall picture.

HMRC's own [guidance for online sellers](https://taxhelpforhustles.campaign.gov.uk/buying-or-making-things-to-sell-and-online-selling-tax-rules) says that if you are just selling unwanted personal belongings from time to time, you do not usually need to tell HMRC. Buying or making things to sell is different: trading income counts towards the £1,000 trading allowance, which is measured on gross income, before expenses ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tax-free-allowances-on-property-and-trading-income)).

Some combinations that show why the two questions are separate:

- **Business on eBay, no tax to pay yet.** You buy a few items to resell and your gross trading income for the tax year stays at £1,000 or less. eBay's policy says business account; HMRC says you may not need to tell them.
- **Private on eBay, still reported to HMRC.** You sell 40 of your own old things in a year. eBay will report you under the platform rules because you passed 30 sales ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform)), but selling your own belongings is not usually trading, and the report creates no tax on its own.
- **Business on eBay, business for HMRC.** You source stock regularly and your takings are well over £1,000. You need a business account and, if you are not already registered, to register for Self Assessment.

Our guide to [bookkeeping and tax for resellers](/guides/bookkeeping-and-tax-for-resellers) covers the tax side properly, and the [platform reporting check](/tools/reporting-check) helps you see whether you are likely to be reported.

## What changes when you register as a business

### Fees

Private sellers based in the UK pay no final value fee or regulatory operating fee in most categories (motors are the main exception), and get 300 free listings a month ([eBay private seller fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822)). The buyer pays a Buyer Protection fee on top, which goes to eBay: £0.10 per item, plus 7% of the item price up to £20, 4% of the portion from £20 to £300, and 2% of the portion from £300 to £4,000 ([eBay Buyer Protection information for sellers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/selling/pricing-items/buyer-protection-information-sellers?id=5595)).

Business sellers pay on each sale ([eBay business seller fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/business-selling-fees?id=4809)):

- a final value fee, a percentage set by category, charged on the total amount of the sale, which includes the item price, postage and any taxes;
- a per-order fee of £0.30 for orders of £10 or less, and £0.40 for orders over £10;
- a regulatory operating fee of 0.35% of the total amount of the sale;
- VAT on those fees, because eBay's figures exclude VAT.

Buyers of business sellers pay no Buyer Protection fee.

**A worked example.** Say you sell a book and the buyer pays £20 in total, including postage. Books, Comics and Magazines has a final value fee of 9.9%.

| Fee | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Final value fee | 9.9% of £20 | £1.98 |
| Per-order fee | Order over £10 | £0.40 |
| Regulatory operating fee | 0.35% of £20 | £0.07 |
| Total before VAT | | £2.45 |
| Total with VAT at 20% | | £2.94 |

As a private seller of your own book, you would pay no selling fee on that sale, and the buyer would pay the Buyer Protection fee on top of your item price. Categories differ, some have lower rates above a price threshold, and eBay changes fees, so use the [eBay fee calculator](/tools/fees/ebay) with your own category and prices, and check the fee page before relying on a figure.

Listing allowances differ too. Without a shop subscription, business sellers get fewer free listings than private sellers, and pay an insertion fee after that; the current numbers are on the [business fees page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/business-selling-fees?id=4809).

### Returns

This is the bigger change. Once you are selling as a business to consumers at a distance, the [Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/contents) apply. GOV.UK summarises the effect ([accepting returns](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds), [online and distance selling](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses)):

- A customer can cancel within 14 days of receiving the item, for any reason.
- You must refund within 14 days of getting the item back, including standard delivery.
- If you do not tell customers about the right to cancel, it can extend up to 12 months.

On top of that, the [Consumer Rights Act 2015](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/22) gives consumers a 30 day short-term right to reject faulty goods, and goods must be of satisfactory quality and as described. So "no returns" is no longer an option. Our guide to [returns without losing money](/guides/returns-without-losing-money) explains which eBay returns setting to choose and who pays postage in each case.

### What buyers see

Business sellers must show business information, including contact details, and a VAT number if registered for VAT ([business seller policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/business-seller-policy?id=4710)). These go in the business seller information section of your listings, where buyers can see them. If you work from home, think about which address you will use before you switch.

## How to switch cleanly

eBay's [account help page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/account/signing-ebay-account/signing-ebay-account?id=4191) describes the upgrade. The steps below follow it, with the preparation that makes it go smoothly.

1. **Decide your legal form.** eBay asks whether you trade as a sole proprietor or a legal entity such as a limited company. Most people starting out are sole traders.
2. **Match your bank account.** eBay says your bank account type should match your eBay account type, and the name on the bank account must match your eBay account to pass verification ([eBay: registering as a seller](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/getting-paid/registering-seller?id=4792)). Sort this first.
3. **Write your returns policy.** You will need one during the upgrade, and it must meet the 14 day minimum.
4. **Work out your fees on current stock.** Run a few typical items through the [fee calculator](/tools/fees/ebay). If your prices were set with no selling fees in mind, some may need to change.
5. **Upgrade the account.** In My eBay, go to Account settings, then Personal information, then Edit next to Account type, confirm the upgrade, choose your business type and enter your business details and returns policy.
6. **Check your listings afterwards.** Confirm your returns policy, business details and postage show correctly on live listings.

eBay says that once you have upgraded a personal account to a business account, you cannot go back to a personal account. If you also have genuinely personal things to sell, that is worth thinking about before you switch.

## What goes wrong

- **Waiting for a number.** There is no eBay threshold to wait for. If you are buying to resell, the policy already applies.
- **Mixing up eBay and HMRC.** A business account does not mean you owe tax, and a private account does not mean you do not.
- **Forgetting the returns duty.** A business seller cannot rely on "no returns".
- **Keeping old prices.** Selling fees and VAT on fees come out of every sale once you switch.
- **A bank account in another name.** Mismatched names hold up payouts.
