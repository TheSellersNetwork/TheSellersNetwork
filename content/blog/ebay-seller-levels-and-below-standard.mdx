This is for anyone selling on eBay UK who has seen a seller level on their dashboard and wants to know what it means, what moves it, and what it costs when it goes wrong. By the end you will know the three levels, the measures behind them, how the evaluation period works, the extra fees for Below Standard sellers and for very high "not as described" rates, and the practical steps to recover. It is general information about eBay's published policies, not legal or financial advice.

## The short version

- eBay puts every seller into one of three levels each month: **Top Rated**, **Above Standard** or **Below Standard** ([eBay seller levels](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/seller-levels-performance-standards/seller-levels-performance-standards?id=4080)).
- The level is based on three measures: transaction defect rate, cases closed without seller resolution, and late delivery rate.
- Evaluation happens on the **20th of each month**. If you had more than 400 transactions in the last three months, eBay looks at those three months. Otherwise it looks at the last 12 months ([seller standards policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-standards-policy?id=4347)).
- Business sellers who are Below Standard on the 20th pay an **extra 6 percentage points** on top of the final value fee the following month, rising to 7 points after four months in a row ([business selling fees](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/business-selling-fees?id=4809)).
- A separate **extra 4 percentage points** applies to business sellers rated "Very high" for item not as described requests, rising to 5 points after four months in a row.
- Most defects come from two things you control: cancelling because you cannot fulfil the order, and letting a buyer's problem go to eBay unresolved.

## The three levels

**Top Rated** is the best level. Only Top Rated status is visible to other members; your other levels and service metrics are private to you ([eBay seller levels](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/seller-levels-performance-standards/seller-levels-performance-standards?id=4080)). You must be registered as a business seller to be Top Rated, so a private seller cannot reach it ([seller standards policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-standards-policy?id=4347)).

**Above Standard** means you meet eBay's minimum standards. Most sellers who do the basics well sit here.

**Below Standard** means you have missed the minimum on at least one measure. This is the level that costs money and visibility.

You can see your current level and your projected level for the next evaluation on the [seller dashboard](https://sellerstandards.ebay.co.uk/dashboard).

## What eBay measures

These definitions come from the [seller standards policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-standards-policy?id=4347).

**Transaction defect.** eBay counts a defect when either the seller cancels an order unexpectedly (for example, it was out of stock or sold elsewhere), or the buyer reports an issue and the seller does not resolve it.

**Case closed without seller resolution.** The buyer's issue was not resolved by you, eBay stepped in to review it, and found you responsible. Each of these is also a transaction defect, so one bad case hits two measures.

**Late delivery.** With tracking, an item counts as late when the delivery scan is after the latest estimated delivery date **and** there is no carrier scan within your dispatch time. Without tracking, eBay asks the buyer when they leave feedback whether the item arrived after the estimated date.

That last point matters. If your tracking shows a carrier scan within your dispatch time, a late delivery by the carrier does not count against you. Untracked items leave it to the buyer's memory.

You may see older guides talk about a separate "late shipment rate" and "tracking uploaded" rate. The current policy page sets its thresholds on late delivery rate, and eBay's seller help still offers late shipment removal requests. Check your own dashboard for the measures it shows you today.

## The thresholds

From the [seller standards policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-standards-policy?id=4347):

| Measure | Above Standard | Top Rated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transaction defect rate | No more than 2% of transactions | No more than 0.5%, associated with no more than 3 different buyers |
| Cases closed without seller resolution | No more than 2 (or 0.3% of transactions) | No more than 2 (or 0.3% of transactions) |
| Late delivery rate | No minimum | No more than 5 (or 3% of transactions) |
| Other | | Account active for at least 90 days, at least 100 transactions and £1,000 in sales in the last 12 months, registered as a business seller |

Two details are easy to miss:

- You will only be rated Below Standard on transaction defects if those defects are associated with **more than 4 different buyers**. A single bad week with one buyer will not put a small seller under on its own.
- A high late delivery rate **on its own** will not make you Below Standard. It only stops you being Top Rated.

## The evaluation period

eBay evaluates on the 20th of each month ([seller standards policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-standards-policy?id=4347)):

- **More than 400 transactions in the last three months:** eBay looks at the last three months.
- **400 or fewer:** eBay looks at the last 12 months.

This is why low-volume sellers find recovery slow. A defect from ten months ago still counts if you sell fewer than 400 items a quarter. It is also why the "2 cases" allowance matters more than the percentage for most small sellers: 0.3% of a few hundred transactions is less than one case.

One change to note: eBay says that from 15 October 2026, eBay Live transactions will no longer count in seller standards or service metrics, including transactions completed before that date, and eBay Live performance will be reported separately ([eBay seller levels](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/seller-levels-performance-standards/seller-levels-performance-standards?id=4080)).

## What Below Standard does

The [seller standards policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-standards-policy?id=4347) lists the consequences. Your items may rank lower in search. Your selling limits may be reduced. You cannot use Promoted Listings. Funds from your orders may be held. You may pay higher final value fees from the month after the evaluation. Your shop subscription could be downgraded, and your account could be restricted.

### The extra fee for Below Standard

eBay's [business selling fees page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/business-selling-fees?id=4809) says a seller who is Below Standard on the 20th of a month pays an **additional 6 percentage points** on top of the final value fee for the following calendar month. If you stay Below Standard for four or more consecutive months, it becomes **7 percentage points**.

Say you are a business seller and you sell a lamp for £40 in the "home, furniture and DIY" category, where the fee on that page is 11.9% (our [fee figures](/tools/calculator/ebay) come from the same page). As an example only, ignoring the per-order fee, the regulatory operating fee and VAT:

| | Above Standard | Below Standard (first 3 months) | Below Standard (4+ months) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Final value fee rate | 11.9% | 17.9% | 18.9% |
| Fee on £40 | £4.76 | £7.16 | £7.56 |

That is an extra £2.40 on one £40 sale, every sale, for as long as the level lasts. Business fees on that page exclude VAT, so the real extra cost is higher if you are not VAT registered. Check the current fee table before relying on the rate for your category.

### The extra fee for very high "not as described" rates

This one is separate from your seller level. eBay's [service metrics policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/service-metrics-policy?id=4769) compares your item not as described rate with sellers who have a similar profile, on the 20th of each month, and rates it Low, Average, High or Very High. The same lookback applies: three months if you had more than 400 transactions, otherwise 12 months. Returns where the buyer simply changed their mind are not counted.

A Very High rating will not affect your account unless it involves at least 10 reports or requests from 10 different buyers. Once it does, the [business selling fees page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/business-selling-fees?id=4809) adds **4 percentage points** to your final value fees the following month, rising to **5 points** after four or more consecutive months. The service metrics policy also lists other restrictions: longer estimated delivery dates, funds held until tracking shows the item is on its way, and limits on deducting from refunds for returned items.

A seller could, in theory, pay both extras in the same month.

### What private sellers should know

The fee figures above are on eBay's business selling fees page. The [private seller fees page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822) says selling is free for UK private sellers in most cases and does not mention these extra fees. The other consequences of Below Standard in the seller standards policy, such as lower search placement and held funds, are not limited to business sellers.

## How to recover

The fastest route is to stop new defects, then let old ones age out of the window.

1. **Look at the dashboard first.** Find which measure is failing and how many defects, and from which buyers. The [seller dashboard](https://sellerstandards.ebay.co.uk/dashboard) shows your projected level before the next 20th.
2. **Stop cancelling for stock.** A cancellation because the item is out of stock is a defect. If you sell the same item elsewhere, end or update the eBay listing the moment it goes. Our guide on [multi-channel selling](/guides/multi-channel-selling) covers keeping stock in sync.
3. **Never let a case reach eBay if you can settle it.** eBay's policy advises focusing on cases closed without seller resolution and cancellations first. Respond to every return or not received request within the 3 business days eBay expects, and offer a refund, return or replacement yourself. Our guide on [item not received and cases](/guides/item-not-received-and-cases) walks through the options.
4. **Get a carrier scan inside your dispatch time.** Late delivery is judged on the first scan as well as the delivery date. If you cannot post same day, set a dispatch time you can keep.
5. **Upload tracking promptly.** It protects you on late delivery and on item not received claims.
6. **Ask for removals where they apply.** Seller Help is available to anyone who has listed or sold in the past 120 days and can be used to request defect, late shipment or feedback removals ([eBay seller levels](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/seller-levels-performance-standards/seller-levels-performance-standards?id=4080)). eBay's [seller protections](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-protections?id=4345) also say it removes related defects and feedback when it finds a buyer has been abusive, and adjusts late delivery for severe weather or carrier disruption.
7. **Appeal cases you should have won.** You can appeal a case outcome with new information within 30 calendar days of the case closing ([appeal as a seller](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/appeal-outcome-case-seller?id=4369)).
8. **Fix the listings behind not as described returns.** If one product keeps coming back, the description is wrong, not the buyers. Our guide to [descriptions that sell](/guides/descriptions-that-sell) and [returns without losing money](/guides/returns-without-losing-money) cover how.

If you are close to the edge, one case can tip you over. A partial refund to keep a case out of eBay's hands is often cheaper than a month of extra fees.

## What goes wrong

- **Watching the percentage and missing the count.** For small sellers the "2 cases" limit is usually the binding one.
- **Cancelling to avoid a tricky buyer.** A cancellation because you cannot or will not fulfil the order is still a defect. If the buyer has not paid, use the "buyer hasn't paid" route instead, which eBay says does not affect your seller standards ([cancelling an order](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/getting-paid/sellers-can-cancel-order?id=4136)).
- **Letting a return sit.** Once eBay steps in and finds against you, the defect is recorded.
- **Assuming a bad month is forgotten next month.** With fewer than 400 transactions a quarter, it stays for a year.

If you want a second view on your dashboard, post the numbers (not the buyer names) in [eBay account health and policy](/community/c/ebay-account-health-and-policy).
