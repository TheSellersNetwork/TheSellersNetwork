This is for anyone who has a pile of presents they will never use and is thinking about selling them online after Christmas. By the end you will know whether selling them has any tax consequences, which gifts Vinted and eBay allow you to list, how to price and photograph them so they sell, and what your obligations are if a buyer is unhappy. It is general information, not tax or legal advice.

## The short version

- Selling your own unwanted gifts is not normally trading, so you do not usually need to tell HMRC. A platform reporting your sales to HMRC is not a tax bill.
- Early January is busy. Vinted calls the first Sunday in January "Sell-It Sunday" and says it is the busiest day of the year for people listing unwanted gifts.
- Perfume, make-up and gift sets can go on Vinted only if they are new and still sealed. Used cosmetics are not allowed.
- Gift cards and vouchers are banned on Vinted and restricted on eBay. Check the rules, or try the shop first.
- Price from similar items, not from what the giver paid. Photograph the real item, including the box and any seal.
- As a private seller, a buyer who simply changes their mind has no automatic right to return. An item that does not match your listing is a different matter.

## Do you owe tax on selling your presents?

Usually not. GOV.UK says you are unlikely to pay tax if you sell personal items from your home, like the contents of a loft or garage ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform)). A gift you were given and do not want is a personal item. You did not buy it to make a profit, which is the main thing HMRC looks at when deciding whether someone is trading.

Two things are worth knowing:

1. **Platforms report to HMRC once you pass certain limits.** Vinted, eBay and the rest must share your details with HMRC if you make 30 or more sales, or receive more than 2,000 euros (about £1,700), in a calendar year. GOV.UK is clear that this "does not automatically mean you owe tax" ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform)). Our [platform reporting check](/tools/reporting-check) tells you whether you are likely to be reported.
2. **Very valuable items are different.** Capital Gains Tax can apply if you make a profit when you sell a personal possession for £6,000 or more ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/capital-gains-tax-personal-possessions)). Most Christmas presents are nowhere near that.

The line moves if you start buying things in the sales to sell on. That is trading, and it has its own rules. Our post on [HMRC and your Vinted sales](/blog/hmrc-and-your-vinted-sales-what-is-true) goes through the headlines one by one.

## Why January is a good time

Lots of people have the same idea in the first week of January. Vinted has labelled the first Sunday of the year "Sell-It Sunday" and said it is the busiest day for post-Christmas listings, with toiletries, perfume, jewellery, nightwear and make-up among the most listed categories ([ChannelX, reporting Vinted's announcement](https://channelx.world/2025/12/vinted-pinpoints-4th-january-as-sell-it-sunday/)).

That cuts both ways. More buyers are browsing, but you are also competing with a lot of near-identical gift sets. In our opinion, listing a few days early, while items are fresh and before prices settle, is better than joining the rush on the day. There is no rule either way.

## What you can and cannot sell

Each platform has its own list. Check the item before you photograph it.

| Perfume and aftershave | New only, in original packaging, seals unbroken | Allowed, subject to eBay's policies | Check the carrier's rules before posting |
| Make-up, skincare, gift sets | New only, sealed if it came sealed | Allowed, subject to eBay's policies | Used cosmetics are banned on Vinted |
| Gift cards and vouchers | Not allowed | Restricted: see below | Try the shop first |
| Event tickets | Not allowed | Check eBay's rules | |

| Toys and games | Allowed | Allowed; baby toys have extra rules | Must not be recalled |

### Perfume, make-up and gift sets

Vinted's [catalogue rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules) say standard sellers can list only new cosmetics, including perfume, make-up, creams, lotions and hair care, and that they must be in their original packaging. If the item or its packaging came sealed, the seal must not be broken. Its [list of items not allowed](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/52-what-can-i-sell-on-vinted) includes used cosmetics on hygiene grounds.

So a boxed gift set still in its film is fine. A perfume you sprayed once to try is not, on Vinted. Facebook's [commerce policies](https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/commerce) also ban cosmetics that have been used or are not in their original packaging.

Perfume also needs care in the post. Carriers have their own rules for flammable liquids, and Vinted says carriers prohibit perfume and aftershave on sales between the UK and the US ([Vinted](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1555-us-uk-international-sales)). Check the carrier's restricted items list before you choose a postage option, and pack glass bottles in a box with padding, as Vinted's [packaging rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/4/65-vinted-go-packaging-rules-and-prohibited-items-list) show for fragile items.

### Gift cards and vouchers

This is the category most people get wrong.

- **Vinted** lists "tickets for events, gift cards, discount coupons, subscriptions" as prohibited ([catalogue rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules)).
- **eBay's** [vouchers policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/prohibited-restricted-items/vouchers-policy?id=4292) says you can list up to 25 vouchers worth £100 in total in a 30 day period, only physical paper vouchers can be listed, and scans or photos that could be copied or redeemed are not allowed. Expired vouchers are not allowed either. Read the whole policy before you list a gift card.
- **Facebook** does not allow gift cards or vouchers to be sold except by approved sellers ([commerce policies](https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/commerce)).

Often the simpler answer is to ask the shop whether it will swap the card or let you spend it on something you want, or give it to someone who will use it.

### Anything that could be unsafe

Do not list anything that has been recalled. GOV.UK keeps a searchable list of [product recalls and alerts](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/product-recalls-and-alerts), and both eBay's [product safety policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/prohibited-restricted-items/product-safety-policy?id=4300) and Facebook's commerce policies ban recalled items.

## Try the shop before you list

If the gift came with a gift receipt, a swap or refund from the shop may be quicker than selling. Citizens Advice explains that you have no automatic right to return something just because you have changed your mind, but many shops choose to allow it within their own returns window ([Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/changed-your-mind/changing-your-mind-about-something-youve-bought/)). Faulty items are different: the shop has to deal with those.

## Pricing: what it is worth now, not what it cost

What someone paid for your present is not what a stranger will pay for it second-hand, even if it is unopened.

1. **Search for the same item.** Vinted's [pricing advice](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/376-how-to-set-a-correct-price) suggests searching the catalogue for similar items to see how other members price them. On eBay, filter search results by sold items to see what actually sold, not just what is listed. Our guide to [pricing from sold listings](/guides/pricing-from-sold-comps) walks through it.
2. **Allow for the rush.** In early January there may be dozens of the same gift set listed. In practice, the ones that sell first are usually priced a little under the crowd.
3. **Remember who pays the fees.** On Vinted there are no selling fees; the buyer pays a fee on top at checkout ([How it works](https://www.vinted.co.uk/how_it_works)). On eBay, UK private sellers pay no final value fee on most items, and buyers pay a Buyer Protection fee that is already included in the price they see ([eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/selling/pricing-items/buyer-protection-information-sellers?id=5595)). Say you list a gift set at £10 on eBay: you receive £10, and the buyer sees a slightly higher price that includes their fee.
4. **Price to allow a small offer.** Buyers on both sites like to make offers. Leave a little room.

## Photos and descriptions

- **Photograph the real item.** Vinted's photo guidance asks that the first photo shows the whole item, taken in daylight, with no filters ([Vinted](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/48-what-photos-you-should-upload)). Do not use pictures from the shop's website.
- **Show the seal, the box and the batch details** for cosmetics and perfume. Buyers want proof it is new.
- **Show any marks.** A dented box or a scuffed tin is fine if you say so. It is a problem if the buyer finds it first.
- **Say it was a gift.** "Unwanted gift, never used, still sealed" is a clear, honest description that buyers understand.
- **Give the size and measurements** for clothes. A label alone is not enough.

Our guide to [photos on a phone](/guides/photos-on-a-phone) covers light and backgrounds.

## If a buyer wants to send it back

When you sell your own things you are a private seller, and the rules are lighter than for shops.

- **Changing their mind.** Citizens Advice says the cooling-off period does not apply when you buy from a private individual, including on auction sites and social media ([Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/changed-your-mind/changing-your-mind-about-something-youve-bought/)). On Vinted, an item that matches its description but does not fit or suit the buyer is not eligible for a refund ([Vinted refund policy](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/465)).
- **Not as described.** If the item is damaged, missing, or not what you described, the buyer can claim. Vinted gives buyers 2 days after delivery to report a problem ([Vinted refund policy](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/465)). eBay's [Money Back Guarantee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy?id=4210) lets a buyer return an item that does not match the listing even if you do not offer returns, and in that case the seller pays for return postage.

The protection is the same on both: describe honestly and photograph everything. Our post on [eBay private sellers and returns](/blog/ebay-private-sellers-returns-and-the-law) explains the law in more detail.

## What goes wrong

- **Listing a tester or a sprayed perfume on Vinted.** It will be removed. Only new, sealed cosmetics are allowed.
- **Trying to sell a gift card on Vinted.** It is banned. Try the shop.
- **Pricing at the shop price.** Unopened is not the same as new from the shop. Check what similar items sold for.
- **Using stock photos.** Buyers and platforms both want to see your item.
- **Buying more in the January sales to sell on.** That turns a clear-out into trading, with different tax and platform rules.
