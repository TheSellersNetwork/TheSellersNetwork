This is for UK sellers who post their own Amazon orders (Fulfilled by Merchant, or FBM) with Royal Mail or Evri. By the end you will know the two delivery metrics Amazon measures you on, what buying labels through Buy Shipping protects you from and what it does not, which common services come with tracking, and how tracking and proof of delivery are used when a buyer says a parcel never arrived. The figures come from Amazon's UK Seller Central help pages and the carriers' own service pages, read in September 2026; Amazon changes these pages, so check them before relying on a number. This is general information, not legal advice.

## The short version

- Amazon expects a **Valid Tracking Rate (VTR) of 95%** for domestic FBM shipments, measured per category over 30 days. Offers in a category below that may be deactivated ([VTR policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201817070?locale=en-GB)).
- Amazon expects a **minimum 90% On-Time Delivery Rate (OTDR)** and recommends 95% or higher ([OTDR policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200847280?locale=en-GB)). Only tracked shipments with a delivery scan count.
- Buying labels through **Buy Shipping** and dispatching on time protects your metrics from delivery related A-to-z claims, but Amazon says you are "still responsible for the financial loss" of those claims ([Buy Shipping](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200202220?locale=en-GB)).
- Royal Mail Tracked 24, Tracked 48 and Special Delivery are tracked. Evri says every delivery has full tracking. Royal Mail 1st and 2nd Class are not tracked, and Signed For gives a signature and online delivery confirmation rather than tracking in transit.
- Untracked Royal Mail 1st and 2nd Class **letter** postage is exempt from VTR only if you buy it through Buy Shipping.
- In an A-to-z claim, Amazon may ask for proof of delivery, tracking, the carrier and the customer's signature. If receipt is unclear, Amazon says the claim is more likely to go to the customer.

## The two tracking metrics

### Valid Tracking Rate

VTR is the share of your eligible FBM shipments, in each category, confirmed with a valid tracking ID over a 30-day period. Amazon's example: 190 of 200 shipments with valid tracking gives 95% ([VTR policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201817070?locale=en-GB)).

You must keep 95% VTR for:

- **Domestic shipments**, from a UK address to a UK address.
- **Cross-border shipments of £20 or more** (including postage, excluding VAT), which must use a tracked service with at least two scans, including a delivery or attempted delivery scan.

Amazon does not calculate VTR for:

- Cross-border shipments under £20 to and from a list of EU countries sent by a letter service.
- **Untracked Royal Mail 1st and 2nd Class Letter stamps and franking, if bought through Buy Shipping** (Amazon says at no extra cost).
- Digital products and self-delivered shipments.

Below 95%, your FBM offers in that category may be deactivated. You appeal from Account Health, per category. If you get a tracking number wrong, you can edit it in Manage Orders before the delivery date and re-confirm the shipment; Amazon says to allow 72 hours for the metric to update.

### On-Time Delivery Rate

OTDR is the share of tracked FBM units delivered on or before your promised "Deliver by" date. That date comes from your handling time and transit time, before any extra days Amazon adds to the date shown to customers ([OTDR policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200847280?locale=en-GB)).

| Point | What Amazon's page says |
| --- | --- |
| Target | Minimum 90%; 95% or higher recommended |
| Window | Rolling 14 days: units with a promised date in the last 21 days, excluding the most recent 7 |
| What counts | Only tracked shipments with a valid delivery scan |
| Small sellers | Fewer than 20 shipped units in the 14-day period are exempt |
| Evaluation | Monthly, with a warning first and 30 days to improve |
| 80% to 90% | Individual listings below 90% deactivated; you can reactivate them yourself from Account Health |
| 60% to 80% | Listings below 80% deactivated; if it does not improve, further listings follow and reactivation needs a plan |
| Below 60% | You can lose the ability to list FBM products on Amazon.co.uk until a reactivation request is accepted |

There is one important exemption. Orders have no negative effect on OTDR if you use all three of Amazon's automation tools: Shipping Settings Automation on your templates, Automated Handling Time (or a 0 to 1 day handling time), and labels bought through Buy Shipping. You are still expected to dispatch on time.

Amazon's page also says that from 19 January 2026 the maximum transit time in standard domestic shipping templates for mainland UK went from five days to four, with non-mainland and some remote areas excluded. If your templates were built before then, check the promise you are making now. Our guide on [when FBM makes sense](/guides/amazon-fbm-when-it-makes-sense) covers templates and the other FBM metrics, including late dispatch and cancellations.

## What Buy Shipping protects, and what it does not

Buy Shipping is Amazon's own label tool in Manage Orders. In the UK, Amazon lists **DPD, Evri and Royal Mail** as supported carriers ([carrier setup](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200204090?locale=en-GB)). You can use Amazon's negotiated drop-off rates with no setup, or link your own Royal Mail or Evri business account to use your own rates.

The protections, from Amazon's [Buy Shipping page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200202220?locale=en-GB):

- **Feedback.** If you ship on time with tracking via Buy Shipping and the buyer leaves feedback only about a late or undelivered parcel, you can ask for a review. If approved, it is removed from your Order Defect Rate and struck through.
- **A-to-z claims.** If you buy through Buy Shipping and dispatch on time, delivery related A-to-z claims do not affect your Order Defect Rate.
- **OTDR.** As above, when combined with the two automation settings.

What it does not do: pay for the loss. Amazon's wording is that you are "still responsible for the financial loss of these claims". Buy Shipping protects your account health. Your stock and your money are a separate question, which is where carrier compensation comes in.

## A-to-z Guarantee claims and proof of delivery

The A-to-z Guarantee covers orders you fulfil yourself, for both timely delivery and the condition of the item ([About A-to-z Guarantee claims](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G27951?locale=en-GB)). For a parcel that has not arrived, the process runs like this:

1. **The customer contacts you first** through Buyer-Seller Messages. Except for instant refunds, they cannot claim without doing this.
2. **You have 48 hours** from their first message to respond and try to resolve it.
3. **They can claim** once that 48 hours has passed and either three days after the estimated delivery date or once delivery is confirmed by valid tracking. Amazon's [not received page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G65221?locale=en-GB) says customers have 90 days from the maximum estimated delivery date to file, and Amazon can accept later claims.
4. **Amazon investigates** and may ask you for the dispatch method, proof of delivery, tracking number, delivery address, the customer's signature confirmation and carrier details ([Respond to a claim](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G1781?locale=en-GB)). If you do not reply within 48 hours, the claim is granted to the customer and your account is debited.
5. **If it goes against you,** you have 30 days to appeal.

Amazon automatically grants a claim, among other cases, if you did not ship by the expected date, did not use a trackable method and did not provide a valid tracking ID, did not deliver by the maximum estimated delivery date, or delivered to the wrong address.

Where proof of delivery helps most is the "delivered but not received" case. Amazon says that if it is unclear whether the customer received the item, "for example, if there is no signature confirmation", the claim is more likely to be granted, and may be granted even where the item was delivered. You can appeal "if you have documentation to demonstrate that the item was received by the customer". So a delivery scan and photo help; a signature is stronger; and any message where the buyer acknowledged receipt is worth keeping, because Amazon lists that correspondence as evidence too.

The text box in the claim response does not take attachments. Amazon says to attach documents in Buyer-Seller Messages and say in your response that you have done so.

## A worked example

Say you sell an item for £30 and post it Tracked 48 bought through Buy Shipping, dispatched on time. The tracking shows a delivery photo at the right address. The buyer says it never came and, after your 48 hours, files a claim.

- Your Order Defect Rate is protected because you used Buy Shipping and dispatched on time.
- Amazon may still refund the customer and debit you, because of the "delivered but not received" rule.
- You can appeal within 30 days with the delivery photo and scan.
- If the parcel was genuinely lost, not delivered, Royal Mail's claims process and the £75 included cover on Tracked 48 are your route to recover the cost. Keep proof of posting.

## What goes wrong

- **Posting untracked parcels outside Buy Shipping.** They count against VTR and give you nothing in a claim.
- **Promising delivery you cannot hit.** OTDR uses your own promise. Long handling times hurt less than missed dates.
- **Missing the 48 hours.** Both the buyer message window and the claim information request are 48 hours. Check messages daily.
- **Assuming Buy Shipping means Amazon pays.** It protects your metrics, not your money.

For help with a claim or a metric warning, ask in [Amazon FBA and FBM](/community/c/amazon-fba-and-fbm).
