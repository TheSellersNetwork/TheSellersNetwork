This is for sellers who have been told to film every parcel they pack because "if you have a video, you can't lose a claim". We test that claim against what Vinted, eBay and Depop publish about evidence in disputes. By the end you will know what each platform asks for, what a packing video can and cannot prove, how to record one that is actually useful, and which other records often carry more weight. It is based on each platform's Help Centre as published in September 2026. It is general information, not legal advice.

## The short version

- **The claim:** film yourself packing and you will always win a dispute.
- **The reality:** none of the three platforms' help pages we read says a video decides a case, or even names seller videos as evidence. They ask mainly for photos, tracking and documents.
- Vinted recommends photos of the item and the packed parcel, inside and outside, before shipping. It asks buyers for photos and videos when they claim damage.
- eBay's seller protection for "not received" claims turns on tracking that meets specific rules, with signature confirmation on orders of £450 or more.
- Depop says a seller may need to provide photos showing the item is as described, and that its support team decides on the evidence.
- A good video is still worth taking for higher value items, but as one piece of proof. It shows what went into the box, not what happened after.

## Where the claim comes from

It is easy to see why the idea spreads. Many disputes are one person's word against another's, and a video feels like it settles the matter. Sellers share stories of a claim dropped the moment they mentioned a video.

But a platform deciding a dispute is not a court weighing a film. Each one has its own process, its own list of what it wants, and its own discretion. Depop says in terms that its decision on a not as described case "will be final" ([Depop help](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360038455993-What-does-Depop-consider-significantly-not-as-described)). So the useful question is not "does video win?" but "what does each platform actually look at?"

Two things stand out. First, the word "video" appears in the pages we read only when Vinted tells **buyers** what to send. Second, photos and tracking come up on every platform. If you only do one thing, take the photos.

## When a video helps

A continuous packing video is at its best in three situations:

- **Missing items or empty box claims.** It shows each item going in and the parcel being sealed. That supports what the tracking weight and your photos say.
- **"Not the item I ordered" or switched items.** If the serial number, a distinctive flaw or a label is on camera going into the box, it links that exact item to that parcel. On eBay, where a buyer might return something different, the same habit on the way back in matters too; see [eBay returns: switched or damaged items](/blog/ebay-returns-switched-or-damaged).
- **Counterfeit claims on branded goods.** Close shots of labels, codes and hardware on film, alongside your listing photos and receipt, help show the item you sent was genuine. Under Vinted's terms from 8 October 2026, you have 48 hours to prove authenticity when Vinted has strong evidence against you ([our summary](/blog/vinted-terms-october-2026)). Proof you already hold is the only proof that counts in 48 hours.

## Where a video falls short

This is where the myth breaks down.

- **It proves what went in, not what came out.** Damage in transit happens after the camera stops. For those claims, Vinted asks the buyer for packaging photos and looks to the carrier. Your video helps show you packed it well, not that it arrived well.
- **It cannot fix the listing.** Most not as described claims are about a flaw that was real but not shown or described. A video of you packing a stained top does not help if the stain is not in your listing photos. See [Vinted fake and not as described claims](/guides/vinted-fake-or-not-as-described-claims).
- **It may have nowhere to go.** Vinted's pages ask sellers for photos. We did not find a Vinted, eBay or Depop page that explains how a seller uploads a video to a case. Take stills from the same session, or separate photos, so you have something you can attach.
- **It is one side's recording.** A platform may reasonably wonder whether the parcel in the video is the one that was posted. Showing the printed label and tracking number on camera answers that.
- **Staff read quickly.** A clear, short case with three good photos and a tracking number is easier to act on than a link to a ten-minute film.

## How to record one well

If you are going to film, make it count. This is our practical checklist, not a platform requirement.

1. **One continuous take.** No cuts, no edits. A break in the footage is the first thing anyone will question.
2. **Start with the label.** Show the printed shipping label or code, with the order and tracking number readable, before anything goes in.
3. **Show the item properly.** Front, back, labels, sizes, serial numbers, and any flaw you listed. Hold it close enough to read.
4. **Pack on camera.** Every item in a bundle, one at a time, then any protective wrapping.
5. **Seal and label on camera.** Show the tape going on and the label fixed to the outside.
6. **Weigh it.** Put the sealed parcel on your scales with the reading visible. A weight is useful later if a buyer says the box was empty or something was missing.
7. **Keep it plain.** Good light, steady phone, no music, no commentary needed.
8. **Keep the file** until the sale is complete and past any claim window. Name it with the order number so you can find it.

For the packing itself, see [packaging cheaply](/guides/packaging-cheaply). Good packing prevents the damage claims that no video can win.

## The evidence that often matters more

In practice, the strongest cases are built from ordinary records made at the time:

- **Your listing.** Every flaw photographed and described, measurements given, labels shown. On Vinted, the list of what counts as not as described is largely about what was not shown ([Vinted SNAD page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1090-significantly-not-as-described-items-at-vinted)).
- **Tracking.** On eBay, tracking that meets the rules is what protects you on "not received" claims. Upload it in time. For orders of £450 or more, use signature confirmation ([eBay seller protections](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-protections?id=4345)). Our guide to [item not received cases](/guides/item-not-received-and-cases) goes through it.
- **The platform's label.** Depop's seller protection applies only if you use the Depop label within 5 days and send it to the address on the receipt. On Vinted, you must use the label for the order.
- **Drop-off receipts.** Keep them, or a screenshot of the locker or shop confirmation.
- **Weights.** Your own reading, and the carrier's record where the service shows one.
- **Serial numbers and receipts.** Note serials in the listing or your records. Keep purchase receipts for branded goods.
- **Packing photos.** Exactly what Vinted recommends: the item, and the packed parcel inside and outside.
- **Messages.** Keep everything on the platform, polite and factual. Support reads the conversation.

## When the item comes back

Evidence matters on returns too. On eBay, you can deduct up to 50% from a refund when a return comes back used, damaged or with parts missing ([eBay condition of returned items policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/member-behavior-policies/condition-returned-items-policy?id=4763)), and you have 3 business days to respond to a return request ([eBay help](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/handle-return-request-seller?id=4115)). On Vinted, you have 2 days once the return reaches you to confirm it is OK, and if you do nothing the buyer is refunded when that deadline passes ([Vinted: returning an item](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/272-returning-an-item)). Opening returns on camera, next to your original listing photos, is where a video most often earns its keep. For more on handling returns, see [returns without losing money](/guides/returns-without-losing-money).

## Verdict

**Myth, mostly.** A packing video is useful evidence, especially for missing items, switched items and branded goods. It is not a guarantee. No platform page we read promises that a video wins, each platform decides at its own discretion, and the things they actually ask for are photos, tracking, labels and documents. Take the photos every time, film the parcels where the value justifies it, and put your effort into an accurate listing, because that is where most claims are won or lost.
