This is for Amazon sellers who have had an account deactivated or listings removed and are being offered help, often within hours, by people who say they can get it back. Some of that help is honest. Some of it is not. By the end you will know what an appeal to Amazon actually involves, why no one outside Amazon can guarantee the result, the warning signs in how these services are sold, and what you can do yourself before paying anyone. We do not name any service; test the claim, not the company. This is general information, not legal advice.

## The short version

- An appeal is something you submit to Amazon from Account Health. Amazon reviews it and decides. No third party has a lever that Amazon does not.
- Amazon publishes its own guidance on what a good appeal contains: the root cause, the specific actions you have taken with dates, and supporting evidence for every affected product ([Amazon appeal guidance](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201623610?locale=en-GB)).
- "Guaranteed reinstatement" is a promise about a decision the seller of the service does not make. UK advertising rules say the word "guarantee" must not be used in a way that causes confusion, and significant limits must not be left out ([CAP Code section 3](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html)).
- Never give anyone your Seller Central password. Amazon's staff advice is not to share it, and to add outside service providers as authorised partners, not as users ([Amazon staff tip](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/d575bdfe-df5d-4b0d-9bd8-6d4ce66cfe8e)).
- Large fees upfront with no refund, pressure to act "before it's too late", and a request for your login are the three biggest red flags.
- Many appeals can be written by the seller, using Amazon's own guidance, for free.

## What an appeal actually is

Amazon's [appeal page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200370560?locale=en-GB) sets out the process:

1. In Seller Central, go to **Account Health**.
2. Click **Reactivate your account** at the top of the page.
3. Follow the instructions. Amazon says you may be asked to "submit a questionnaire, acknowledge a violation, complete a quiz or provide supporting documents".
4. Submit, and watch your email. If Amazon asks for more, use **View Appeal** in Account Health. Amazon says to expect a reply within two days.

For a removed listing, you start from the Product Policy Compliance tab in Account Health, or Manage Inventory for inactive listings, and either appeal or fix the listing.

Amazon's [guidance on appeal questionnaires](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201623610?locale=en-GB) says what it wants to see. In summary:

- **Be clear and concise.** Factual and direct, about the events that led to the issue, without introducing your business or using emotive language.
- **Explain every issue.** Look for the root causes, such as unverifiable suppliers, inventory problems or quality control failures, and give the specific actions you have taken, "making sure that you include dates of changes or documentation".
- **Include supporting evidence for all items.** For every product under review, from the list at the bottom of the notification, within the time frame requested. Amazon notes that some products need invoices from the last 365 days.
- **Highlight the important parts** of your documents, such as the ASINs, the supplier name or supplier terms.

Where Amazon asks for a "plan of action", its own policies describe the same shape. For example, its [On-Time Delivery Rate policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200847280?locale=en-GB) says a reactivation request must identify the root causes, detail the specific steps being taken, give a timeline and demonstrate understanding of the requirements.

That is the whole job. A service can help you write it and organise the evidence. It cannot submit anything Amazon would not accept from you, and it cannot make the decision.

## Claim 1: "We guarantee reinstatement"

**Reality:** only Amazon decides whether an account is reinstated. Its policies are full of wording that keeps the decision with Amazon: IP appeals say "your content may be reinstated"; repeat infringers are terminated "in appropriate circumstances" ([Amazon IP Policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201361070?locale=en-GB)). Some decisions cannot be appealed at all: Amazon says a "Your account does not qualify" answer on a product approval is final ([approval page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200333160?locale=en-GB)).

**What the rules say:** the CAP Code covers non-broadcast marketing, including online ads. Rule 3.55 says marketing "must not use the word 'guarantee' in a way that could cause confusion about a consumer's rights". Rule 3.56 says it must not mislead "by omitting significant limitations to an advertised guarantee". Rule 3.7 says marketers must hold evidence for objective claims before they publish them, and rule 3.1 says marketing "must not materially mislead or be likely to do so" ([CAP Code section 3](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html)).

If a "guarantee" turns out to mean "we will keep writing appeals" or "partial refund if Amazon says no, after deductions", that is a limitation you should have been told about before you paid. Ask for the full terms in writing.

## Claim 2: "We have contacts inside Amazon"

**Reality:** Amazon's published process is the appeal from Account Health, and Amazon reviews it. Nothing on Amazon's public appeal pages describes a faster route through a third party. A claim of inside contacts is either untrue, or describes something you should not want your account associated with.

**What to ask:** "What will you submit, and through which part of Seller Central?" An honest answer describes the appeal process above.

## Claim 3: "Pay now, before it's too late"

**Reality:** time matters, but panic is how bad decisions get made. Amazon's page says it expects to reply to an appeal within two days, and when it wants more information it tells you in Account Health. A rushed, generic appeal can be worse than a considered one, because Amazon will look for the root cause and the evidence either way.

**Red flag:** a large fee demanded upfront, "non-refundable", with a deadline set by the seller of the service rather than by Amazon. You are usually buying as a business, and business buyers can have fewer statutory rights than consumers, so the contract terms matter more. Read them.

## Claim 4: "We just need your login"

**Reality:** you should not share your password. Amazon's agreement says you may not disclose your password to any third party, other than those you have authorised to use your account in line with the agreement, and that you are responsible for any use of it ([Business Solutions Agreement](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201190440?locale=en-GB)). Amazon staff advice is blunt: "Don't share your password with other people", grant the minimum permissions, and "Don't add external third-party service providers or developers as users"; add them as authorised partners instead ([Amazon staff tip on user permissions](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/d575bdfe-df5d-4b0d-9bd8-6d4ce66cfe8e)).

Someone with your login can see your customers' data, change your bank details and submit things in your name. There is no appeal you need a stranger to type for you.

## The red-flag checklist

| Red flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| "Guaranteed" reinstatement or approval | They do not control the outcome; check what the guarantee actually promises (CAP rules 3.55 and 3.56) |
| Success rates with no evidence | Objective claims need evidence before publication (CAP rule 3.7) |
| Fee in full upfront, no refund | You carry all the risk; business buyers may have fewer rights |
| Asks for your password or to be added as a user | Against Amazon's advice and your agreement |
| Contacts you first after your suspension | Ask how they knew, and be wary |
| Promises to "reset" or open a new account | A second account usually makes things worse; see our [verification post](/blog/amazon-seller-verification-uk) |
| Will not explain what they will submit | An appeal is not a mystery; it is documents and a plan |

## What you can do yourself first

1. **Read the notification properly.** It names the policy, the ASINs and usually the documents Amazon wants. Most failed appeals do not answer the question Amazon asked.
2. **Find the real root cause.** Not "a mistake happened", but which supplier, which listings, which process failed.
3. **Fix it before you write.** Remove the problem listings, change supplier, change your checks. Then write down what you did and when.
4. **Gather evidence for every ASIN.** Invoices within the time frame, matching names and addresses, highlighted.
5. **Write it plainly.** Follow Amazon's guidance: facts, root cause, actions with dates, evidence.
6. **Get a second pair of eyes.** Post your draft, with identifying details removed, in [Account health and suspensions](/community/c/amazon-account-health-and-suspensions) before you submit.

Our guide to [Amazon account health](/guides/amazon-account-health) explains the metrics and notices that lead to suspension, and our post testing [claims in Amazon course adverts](/blog/amazon-course-claims-tested) covers how guaranteed ungating and reinstatement claims sit with the advertising rules.

## If you decide to pay for help

Some sellers do, for complex cases. If you do:

- get the price, what is included, and the refund terms in writing before you pay;
- keep control of your login and add them, if at all, as an authorised partner with the least access needed;
- ask to see and approve every word before it is submitted;
- pay by a method that gives you some protection.

If an advert makes a claim you think is misleading, you can [complain to the ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/make-a-complaint.html). If you have lost money to a scam, report it to [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/) (in Scotland, contact Police Scotland on 101).
