This is for UK resellers selling on Amazon, eBay or anywhere else who are wondering whether they need business insurance and, if so, what kind. By the end you will know what each common type of cover is for, when Amazon makes liability insurance compulsory, and how seven providers aimed at small online sellers describe their cover and prices on their own pages. This is general information, not insurance advice. The Sellers Network is not an insurance intermediary: we do not arrange or sell policies, and we earn nothing from any provider named here. At the end we give our opinion on which provider we would look at first, based only on what the providers publish. A broker or the insurer can look at your own situation.

## The short version

- Amazon's UK agreement requires liability insurance of at least £400,000 within 30 days of your gross proceeds going over £4,000 in any month, and Amazon can ask any seller for it. Our [product liability insurance post](/blog/product-liability-insurance-for-amazon-sellers) covers the detail.
- Product liability covers claims that something you sold caused injury or damage. Public liability covers injury or damage caused by your business activities.
- Home insurance may not cover business stock, computers or visitors ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/run-business-from-home)). Stock cover is a separate policy or section.
- Published starting prices range from £5 to £14.70 a month, but each is for a different mix of cover. They are not comparable, and your own quote depends on what you sell, your turnover and where you are.
- Three of the seven providers here are brokers who arrange cover with insurers. Four are insurers selling direct, or trading names of one.
- Check any firm on the [FCA register](https://register.fca.org.uk/s/) before you buy.
- Our pick for a typical reseller is Hiscox, for the lowest published price on public and product liability together and limits well above Amazon's minimum.

## The cover types resellers actually consider

**Product liability.** Pays legal costs and compensation if a product you sold is said to have injured someone or damaged their property. Providers here say it applies to goods you did not make yourself. For resellers of branded goods, being able to name the manufacturer or importer also matters under UK law, as our Amazon post explains.

**Public liability.** Pays claims from members of the public hurt, or whose property is damaged, because of your business activities: a courier tripping on your step, or someone hurt at a car boot pitch. Usually sold together with product liability.

**Stock and contents.** Pays to repair or replace stock and equipment that is stolen, lost or damaged. GOV.UK warns that home insurance may not cover business stock or computers. Several providers add seasonal increases for November and December.

**Goods in transit.** Covers stock while it is being moved. Providers differ on whether that means your own vehicle only, or parcels with Royal Mail and couriers too. Some couriers offer their own cover, which Hiscox notes may have limitations.

**Business use of your home.** GOV.UK says you may need permission from your mortgage provider or landlord, and that home insurance may not cover your business ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/run-business-from-home)). Tell your home insurer you trade from home.

**Business use of your car.** If you drive to source stock or drop parcels, tell your car insurer how you use the car. The Financial Ombudsman Service has warned that giving an insurer the wrong information can reduce or stop a payout, using a case about commuting cover ([Financial Ombudsman Service](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/who-we-are/data-insight/news/financial-ombudsman-service-warns-risks-getting-insurance-details-wrong)).

**Employers' liability.** Only relevant if you take on staff, including temporary help. It is then usually a legal requirement.

## What Amazon requires

Section 8 of the [Amazon Services Europe Business Solutions Agreement](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G201190440?locale=en-GB) says that if your gross proceeds exceed the Insurance Threshold in any month, you must have liability insurance within 30 days and keep it. For the UK store the threshold is £4,000 and the minimum limit is £400,000 per occurrence and in aggregate, including products liability and bodily injury. Amazon's [insurance policy page](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/help/hub/reference/external/G200386300?locale=en-GB) adds an excess of no more than £10,000, occurrence-based cover in most cases, and your legal entity name on the policy.

Say your amazon.co.uk orders in November come to £4,300 including VAT and postage. That is over £4,000 in one month, so the 30-day clock starts, even if December is quieter.


## What matters most for resellers

- **Your product range.** Batteries, electricals, children's products, cosmetics and supplements are the questions insurers ask about. Tell them everything you list.
- **Where you sell.** Some policies cover US and Canadian sales, some limit them.
- **Limits that match any platform rule.** For Amazon UK, at least £400,000 of products liability and an excess of £10,000 or less.
- **Where your stock lives.** Home, a unit, or a prep centre or third-party warehouse.
- **How parcels travel.** Your own car, or carriers.
- **Turnover limits.** Several online retailer policies have a turnover ceiling.
- **Direct insurer or broker.** A broker compares insurers; a direct insurer sells its own policy.

## Simply Business

**Good**

- Compares quotes from a range of insurers and says it covers online shops with annual turnover below £3m.
- Public liability limits of £1m, £2m or up to £5m.
- States that stock cover should include goods you transport yourself, though limits vary in transit.

**Bad**

- The £8.59 figure is for up to £2m of public liability only; 10% of online retailers paid £103.10 a year or less between January and June 2026. Product liability and stock cost extra.
- As a broker it does not underwrite the policy; the insurer's terms are what count.

## Hiscox

**Good**

- Public and product liability from £5.20 a month, with limits up to £10m ([Hiscox](https://www.hiscox.co.uk/business-insurance/product-liability-insurance)).
- Says its online retailer policy covers selling through Amazon, Etsy, eBay or your own website.
- Representation costs up to £250,000 if a criminal claim is made against your business.

**Bad**

- Hiscox says it cannot give an average cost, and the £5.20 is based on a share of its public liability policies.
- Transit cover "depends on your specific policy"; you have to ask.
- The policy is not intended for businesses with a permanent physical shop, and products liability cannot extend to sellers with a dubious source of supply.

## AXA

**Good**

- One policy can combine public and products liability, contents and employers' liability.
- Contents cover includes a 30% seasonal increase between November and December, or at a time that suits you.
- Goods in transit available as an optional extra, on the road or in the post.

**Bad**

- The £7 a month figure is for AXA business insurance in general: 10% of customers paid it or less between April and June 2026 ([AXA](https://www.axa.co.uk/business-insurance/)).
- AXA's online retailer page says marketplaces such as Amazon do not require cover. Amazon's own UK agreement does, above £4,000 in a month.

## Direct Line for Business

**Good**

- Public and product liability from £1m to £10m, with defence costs.
- Stock cover at home or premises includes an extra 25% for two months you choose.
- No admin fees for policy changes, and 0% APR on monthly payments, subject to eligibility.

**Bad**

- No starting price published for online retailers.
- The site says it is proposing to transfer insurance business from U K Insurance Limited to Aviva Insurance Limited, which may affect customers. Check who the insurer will be at renewal.

## Superscript

**Good**

- Combined public and product liability of £2m or £5m, including goods you did not make.
- Stock up to £75,000, at your premises or a third-party warehouse, with a 30% seasonal increase.
- £5,000 goods in transit in your own vehicles, plus optional £5,000 with Royal Mail, Evri, DPD and other carriers.
- Up to 25% of turnover from the US and Canada can be covered.

**Bad**

- No starting price published.
- North American claims carry a £2,500 excess.
- Dropshipping cover depends on inspecting a 5% sample or having recourse against a UK or EU manufacturer or wholesaler.

## Markel Direct

**Good**

- Public liability from £1m to £5m, plus product liability; stock and equipment optional.
- Says its policies suit anyone from a sole trader turning over £1,000 a month to a company of 15.
- Legal expenses cover up to £100,000 available.

**Bad**

- The "from £5 a month" price is based on £50,000 of cyber and data risks cover, not liability or stock.

## PolicyBee

**Good**

- The most detailed published pricing of the seven: £14.70 a month for £1m public and £100,000 products liability, £4.90 for £5,000 of stock and property, £2.94 for employers' liability, based on income up to £100,000 and including insurance premium tax.
- Says its policy can cover selling on Amazon USA.
- Quotes online for turnovers up to £2m.

**Bad**

- The example products liability limit of £100,000 is below Amazon UK's £400,000 minimum. You would need a higher limit to meet Amazon's rule.
- The page also says the policy is for turnovers below £500,000, so check which limit applies to you.

## How to choose

Ask yourself, and write down the answers before you quote:

1. What do I sell, and does any of it carry extra risk?
2. Have I crossed, or am I near, Amazon's £4,000 a month? What limit and excess do I need?
3. Where is my stock, and what is it worth at its peak?
4. Do I post everything, or do I drive stock around?
5. Do I sell to the US or Canada?
6. Would I rather compare insurers through a broker, or buy direct?
7. Is the firm on the FCA register under the name and number on its website?

Our view: for most resellers the first question is liability, because a claim can far exceed the value of your stock. Stock cover matters more once your stock is worth more than you could afford to lose.

## Our verdict

If we had to choose one for a typical UK reseller, we would choose **Hiscox**. By typical we mean a sole trader selling second-hand goods from home on eBay, Amazon or both, who wants public and product liability first and may cross Amazon's £4,000 a month threshold.

Why:

- **The lowest published price for the cover that matters most.** Public and product liability together from £5.20 a month. The other low headline prices here are for different cover: Markel's £5 is cyber cover and Simply Business's £8.59 is public liability only.
- **Limits that clear Amazon's rule.** Up to £10m, against Amazon UK's £400,000 minimum.
- **Marketplaces named.** Hiscox says the policy covers selling through Amazon, Etsy, eBay or your own website.

Picks by situation:

- **If you post a lot of stock with couriers:** Superscript, because it publishes £5,000 of goods in transit in your own vehicles plus an optional £5,000 with Royal Mail, Evri, DPD and other carriers, and stock cover up to £75,000.
- **If your stock peaks before Christmas:** AXA, because its contents cover includes a 30% seasonal increase for November and December and goods in transit by post is an optional extra.
- **If you sell to the US or Canada:** Superscript, because it can cover up to 25% of turnover from those countries.
- **If you would rather compare several insurers at once:** Simply Business, because it is a broker that compares quotes for online shops with turnover below £3m.

What we would avoid, and why:

- **A products liability limit of £100,000 if you sell on Amazon UK**, as in PolicyBee's published example, because it is below Amazon's £400,000 minimum.
- **Choosing on the headline price alone**, because each one is for a different mix of cover.

Hiscox says its £5.20 is based on a share of its policies, not an average, so get a quote with every product type you sell listed, and ask about transit, which it says depends on your policy.

## Common mistakes

- **Comparing headline prices** that cover different things.
- **Assuming home or car insurance covers the business.**
- **Buying a products liability limit below a platform's minimum.**
- **Not listing every product type.** An undisclosed category can leave a claim uncovered.
- **Forgetting parcels.** Stock cover may stop at your door.
- **Not checking the FCA register.** The [FSCS](https://www.fscs.org.uk/what-we-cover/insurance/) protects 90% of accepted claims for non-compulsory insurance such as public liability and property if an authorised insurer or broker fails.
