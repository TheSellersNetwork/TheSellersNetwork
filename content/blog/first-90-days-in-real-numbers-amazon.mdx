This is for anyone who has sold on Amazon FBA for about three months, and for anyone thinking of starting who wants to see what the early months really look like. It introduces a case-study format for the forum, "My first 90 days on Amazon FBA, in real numbers", gives you the template to fill in, and explains how to work out a profit figure that includes every cost. The aim is simple: a growing set of honest first-quarter accounts from real sellers, written the same way so they can be compared. This is general information, not tax or financial advice.

## The short version

- Adverts for Amazon courses show revenue screenshots and best cases. Very few people publish a full first 90 days with every cost in it.
- The template below lists the money you put in (stock, each Amazon fee type, tools, prep, postage) and the money that came back to you, plus units, returns, hours and lessons.
- Fill it in from your own records only: Amazon's reports, your bank statement and your receipts. No estimates dressed up as results.
- Post it in [Wins and case studies](/community/c/wins-and-case-studies) if the 90 days are done, or keep a running version in [Diaries and challenges](/community/c/diaries-and-challenges) as you go.
- No links to your listings, and crop personal data out of any screenshot.
- A loss is a useful post. So is a break-even. They are the results adverts leave out.

## Why we are asking for this

If you have watched Amazon course adverts, you will have seen the pattern: a sales dashboard, a monthly figure, a line about how quickly students get there. We tested the common claims against Amazon's own pages and the UK advertising rules in [the claims in Amazon course adverts, tested](/blog/amazon-course-claims-tested). The short answer is that revenue is not profit, and a best result is not a typical one.

What is missing is the other side: ordinary sellers showing their first three months with the fees, the returns, the stock that did not sell and the hours it took. Nobody has to share, and nobody should feel they need a good result to post. But every honest account makes it harder for an inflated one to look normal, and it gives someone thinking of starting a real picture before they spend.

Ninety days is long enough to have bought stock, sent it in, sold some of it, had returns and seen a couple of payouts. It is short enough that most people still remember what went wrong.

## Before you start: gather your records

You will need:

- **Amazon's own reports** for the period, which show each sale and each fee Amazon charged. Seller Central has payment and transaction reports you can download for a date range.
- **Your bank statement** for the account you use for the business, so you can check what actually left and arrived.
- **Receipts** for stock, tools, prep materials and postage.
- **A rough log of your hours.** If you did not keep one, say so and give your best estimate, clearly labelled as an estimate.

Our [profit report tool](/tools/sales-reports) takes an Amazon sales report and shows sales, fees and profit by month in your browser. Nothing is uploaded. It is a quick way to check your totals before you post.

## How to work out a true profit figure

There are two honest ways to do this. Pick one, say which you used, and stick to it.

### Method one: cash in the bank

Take the total Amazon paid out to you in the 90 days, add any other money that came back, and subtract everything you spent, including stock you have not sold yet. This is the harshest measure and usually the most revealing for a new seller, because early months are heavy on stock and light on payouts. Amazon also holds some money back between settlements, so a payout total can lag behind your sales.

### Method two: profit on what sold, plus stock on hand

Work out the profit on the units you actually sold: sale price, minus every fee on those units, minus what you paid for them. Then list separately the cost of the stock you still hold, and the overheads (plan fee, tools, prep materials) for the period. This shows whether your individual sales make money, while being clear that the unsold stock is still a cost until it sells.

Do not value unsold stock at the price you hope to sell it for. Use what you paid. A price you hope for is not a result.

### Check the per-unit sums

Our [FBA calculator](/tools/calculator/amazon-fba) applies the referral fee, fulfilment fee, fuel surcharge, storage and VAT on fees for a single product. Run a few of your best and worst sellers through it and compare with what Amazon actually charged in your reports. If they differ, find out why: the most common reasons are a different size tier than you expected, storage building up on slow stock, and returns. Our walk-through of [every Amazon fee on one sale](/blog/every-amazon-fee-on-one-sale) explains each line, and [where the money leaks on Amazon](/guides/amazon-where-the-money-leaks) covers costs that never show on a single order.

### Count your hours honestly

Divide your profit by your total hours. It is often the most sobering number in the whole post, and it is the number adverts almost never show. If you are comfortable, include it.

## How to post it in the forum

1. **Choose the forum.** If your 90 days are complete, post in [Wins and case studies](/community/c/wins-and-case-studies). The name says "wins", but a clear account of a loss belongs there too. If you are part way through, start a thread in [Diaries and challenges](/community/c/diaries-and-challenges) and update it as you go, then post the finished template at the end.
2. **Use the title format** "My first 90 days on Amazon FBA, in real numbers", and add your start month if you like.
3. **Paste the template** and fill every line. Blank is fine. Missing is not.
4. **Add a few lines of context.** How many hours a week you had, whether this was alongside a job, what you knew before you started.
5. **Answer questions.** People will ask how you got a figure. That is the point.

## The house rules for these posts

These apply on top of the general [house rules](/community/rules).

- **Real numbers only.** Every figure must come from your own records. If something is an estimate, label it as one. Do not round a loss up to break-even or a good month into a typical one.
- **No links to your listings.** The house rules already say no links to your listings or live streams. You can name the category or the type of product if you want to. You do not have to.
- **No screenshots with personal data.** Screenshots of reports are welcome, but crop or cover buyer names, addresses, order numbers, your own bank details and anything else that identifies a person. If in doubt, type the figures instead.
- **Be kind to losses.** Someone who posts a loss has done everyone a favour. If a reply mocks or piles on, flag it.
