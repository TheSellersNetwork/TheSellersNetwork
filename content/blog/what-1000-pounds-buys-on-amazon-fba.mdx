This is for anyone with about £1,000 who is thinking of starting on Amazon FBA in the UK and wants to know where the money goes in the first month. It builds a budget line by line, separating the costs Amazon fixes from the ones you choose, shows what is left for stock, and explains why that stock money does not come back quickly. It does not tell you what you will earn, because nobody can: that depends on what you buy, what it sells for and how fast. This is general information, not financial or tax advice.

## The short version

- Amazon's fixed cost to get started is the **Professional plan: £25 a month plus VAT**, so £30 for a seller who is not VAT registered.
- Paid tools are optional. Amazon's own revenue calculator and this site's calculators are free. If you add a sourcing tool, SellerAmp's lowest UK plan is **£13.95 plus VAT** a month on its pricing page.
- Prep supplies, labels and shipping to Amazon are real costs, but you choose how much. We show them as example amounts for you to replace.
- Amazon's fees per sale are not paid upfront. They come off each sale, so they reduce what comes back, not what you can spend on day one.
- Money from a sale is normally held until **seven days after delivery** under Amazon's DD+7 reserve policy, and can be held longer. Plan on your first stock money being tied up for several weeks.
- Keep a buffer. A budget with nothing left over has no room for a return, a price drop or a slow seller.

## The fixed costs

These are the ones you cannot choose to skip if you sell through FBA on the Professional plan.

| Cost | Amount | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Professional selling plan | £25 a month excluding VAT | [Amazon pricing](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/pricing) |
| VAT on the plan | 20%, so £5 | [Amazon: VAT on fees](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/fe8e800e-d95c-42ae-a98b-e6e682547f90) |
| Plan total, not VAT registered | £30 a month | |

Since 1 August 2024 Amazon charges VAT on its fees to UK-established sellers, and "VAT will be charged on your Amazon fees" in Amazon's own words. If you are not VAT registered you cannot reclaim it, so it is part of the cost.

**Could you start on the Individual plan instead?** It has no monthly fee and charges £0.75 plus VAT per item sold. On fees alone it is cheaper below about 34 sales a month. The plans do not include the same features, so compare what each includes on Amazon's [pricing page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/pricing) before choosing.

## The costs you choose

### Tools

You can start without paying for any tool. Amazon's revenue calculator is linked from its [pricing page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/pricing), and our [FBA calculator](/tools/fba-calculator) works out fees, profit and the most you can pay for an item. What paid tools add is speed and data, such as price and sales rank history.

We do not recommend any tool. As a fact from the vendors' own pricing pages, checked on 29 September 2026:

- **Price history:** Keepa's site lists Keepa Pro at 29 euros a month. It is charged in euros, so the sterling cost moves with the exchange rate.
- **Sourcing calculator:** SellerAmp lists UK plans at £13.95, £21.95 and £34.95 a month, each plus VAT, with a 14-day free trial.

Our post on [what Amazon seller tools cost](/blog/amazon-seller-tools-what-they-cost) covers the other categories.

### Prep supplies

Amazon has packaging and prep requirements for stock it receives, set out in Seller Central. What you need depends on what you sell: bags or protective wrap for some items, and boxes and tape for shipping in. The amount is yours to set, so the budget below uses an example figure.

### Labels

Amazon has to be able to identify every unit by its barcode, and its labelling requirements in Seller Central say when you must add your own label. You can print labels yourself or pay Amazon to apply them. The [July 2026 rate card](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/02/sell/images/260630-FBA-Rate-Card-EN1.pdf) lists Amazon's label service at £0.38 a unit for envelope-size items and £0.78 for parcels. On 100 parcel-size units that is £78 before VAT, which is why most small sellers print their own.

### Shipping to Amazon

If you use the Amazon Partnered Carrier programme, the cost is shown in Send to Amazon before you accept it. An Amazon staff post says UK sellers can choose DPD for small parcel shipments at Amazon-negotiated rates, paid inside Seller Central ([Amazon forum post](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/2796f436-18f1-4375-bb42-ae06b073da50)). Amazon's [pricing page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/pricing) also lists £80 off inbound shipments through the programme as a new seller incentive, with conditions.

The rate card also has a manual processing fee of £0.15 a unit if you do not give box content information, so fill that in when you create the shipment.

## What is not on the table, and why

**Amazon's fees on each sale.** Referral, fulfilment, the fuel surcharge, the digital services fee and VAT come off each sale as it happens, so they do not reduce your starting pot. They do reduce what comes back. Our worked example of [every Amazon fee on one sale](/blog/every-amazon-fee-on-one-sale) shows a £24.99 toy paying about £8.54 in fees through FBA. Put your own products through the [FBA calculator](/tools/fba-calculator) before you buy, not after.

**Storage.** Charged monthly per cubic foot. Standard-size stock outside clothing is £0.76 a cubic foot from January to September and £1.51 from October to December on the [rate card](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/02/sell/images/260630-FBA-Rate-Card-EN1.pdf). Say your first shipment takes up 10 cubic feet: that is £7.60 a month in September and £15.10 in October, before the digital services fee and VAT. The FBA New Selection programme on the [pricing page](https://sell.amazon.co.uk/pricing) offers free storage on eligible new-to-FBA products, so check whether yours qualify.

**Your own time, travel and anything else.** Petrol for retail arbitrage, a scale, a label printer if you do not have one. None is required by Amazon, and all of it is real money.

## Why the cash is tied up

This is the part that surprises new sellers most. Here is the path of a pound spent on stock:

1. You buy the stock and it is delivered to you.
2. You prep, label and ship it to Amazon.
3. Amazon receives and checks it in. This can take longer in busy periods.
4. It sells. That can be days or weeks, depending on the product and your price.
5. It is delivered to the buyer.
6. **Amazon holds the money until seven days after delivery.** An Amazon moderator describes the default as "7 days after delivery date ('DD+7' reserve policy)", using the carrier's delivery date or, with no valid tracking, the latest estimated delivery date ([Amazon on account level reserves](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/aac4b8e9-c776-418e-8373-9484fbf8baa1)). The same post says the reserve period "may be extended following an assessment of your overall risk and historical performance".
7. Amazon pays out on your settlement cycle, or when you request a transfer. An Amazon staff post says payment dates vary by seller based on registration date, and that it can take up to five working days for the money to reach your bank after Amazon starts the payment ([Amazon: getting paid](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/20b49169-ea34-4b7c-8014-b6ed3a4e0684)).

Add those steps up and the first money from your first stock is several weeks away at best. Meanwhile the plan fee and any tools renew monthly. If you spend the whole £1,000 on stock in week one, you have nothing for the second shipment until the first one has sold and paid out. Many sellers split the stock money across two or three smaller buys for this reason, so there is always something arriving, something selling and something paying out.

Our guide to [Amazon cash flow and overheads](/guides/amazon-cash-flow-and-overheads) covers reserves, withdrawals and a monthly check on whether the business is growing.

## Before you spend anything

- Read [how to start Amazon FBA in the UK](/guides/how-to-start-amazon-fba-uk) for account setup, verification and the order to do things in.
- Check whether the brands and categories you want need approval before you buy stock you cannot list.
- Run every product through a calculator with VAT on fees switched on.
- Decide your buffer before you shop, not after.
