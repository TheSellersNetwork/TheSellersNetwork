Selling your own things on eBay, Vinted or Depop in the UK now costs the seller little or nothing in selling fees. Instead, the buyer pays a fee on top of the item price: eBay's Buyer Protection fee on private sellers' items, the Vinted fee, and Depop's Marketplace fee. On paper, the buyer pays. Many sellers say that in practice they pay too, because buyers look at the total and haggle the item price down. This post sets out the facts, both sides of the argument and what the drip pricing rules say, and ends with a poll. It is general information, not legal or financial advice, and the fee figures can change, so check each platform's page before relying on them.

## The short version

- eBay charges buyers of UK private sellers a Buyer Protection fee of £0.10 plus a sliding percentage. Business sellers' buyers do not pay it.
- Vinted charges buyers a fee at checkout. Vinted's How it works page gives 5% of the item price plus £0.70; its Help Centre says usually 3% to 8% plus £0.30 to £0.80.
- Depop charges UK buyers a Marketplace fee of up to 5% of the item price plus up to £1.
- The platforms say the fees pay for things such as customer support, fraud monitoring and refund protection.
- Since April 2025, UK law says mandatory fees must be included in the price shown to consumers, not added late in the purchase.
- There is no published data on how much sellers have cut prices because of these fees.

## What is happening

### What each platform charges

| | eBay (private sellers) | Vinted | Depop (UK) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Name | Buyer Protection fee | Vinted fee (called the Buyer Protection fee before 8 October 2026) | Marketplace fee |
| Fixed part | £0.10 per item | £0.70 on the How it works page; usually £0.30 to £0.80 in the Help Centre | Up to £1 per transaction |
| Percentage | 7% of the price up to £20, 4% from £20 to £300, 2% from £300 to £4,000 | 5% on the How it works page; usually 3% to 8% in the Help Centre | Up to 5% of the item price |
| Seller's selling fee | None for UK private sellers, except motors | None | None; Depop charges sellers a payment processing fee of 2.9% plus £0.30 |
| Source | [eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/buying/paying-items/buyer-protection-fee?id=5594) | [How it works](https://www.vinted.co.uk/how_it_works), [Help Centre](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1654) | [Marketplace fee](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/21752555753361-What-is-the-Marketplace-fee), [seller fees](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001791127-Seller-fees-and-charges) |

eBay's business sellers are different: their buyers pay no Buyer Protection fee, and the seller pays final value and other fees instead ([eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/selling/pricing-items/buyer-protection-information-sellers?id=5595)). Vinted Pro sellers' buyers pay a separate Buyer Protection Pro fee.

### What it adds, worked through

Say you list an item at £10, and another at £50. Using each platform's published figures (Vinted's How it works rate, and Depop's maximum):

| Item price | eBay private | Vinted | Depop (at most) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| £10 | £0.10 + £0.70 = £0.80 | £0.50 + £0.70 = £1.20 | £0.50 + £1 = £1.50 |
| £50 | £0.10 + £1.40 + £1.20 = £2.70 | £2.50 + £0.70 = £3.20 | £2.50 + £1 = £3.50 |

Postage comes on top in each case. Our post on the [eBay Buyer Protection fee worked through](/blog/ebay-buyer-protection-fee-worked-examples) goes into eBay's bands in more detail.

### What buyers get

eBay lists 24/7 customer service, "risk monitoring and fraud detection" and encrypted payments, and refunds the fee in full when a buyer gets a full refund ([eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/buying/paying-items/buyer-protection-fee?id=5594)). Vinted says its fee pays for connecting buyers and sellers, supporting safer transactions, refund protection for items that are lost, damaged or significantly not as described, and member support ([Vinted](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1654)). Depop says its fee funds Depop Protection, customer support, new features and marketing to attract more sellers, and that it is refunded in full if the item is refunded ([Depop](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/21752555753361-What-is-the-Marketplace-fee)).

### How the fee is shown

eBay says the fee "is already included in the item price that you see when browsing". When we looked at Vinted's catalogue in September 2026, each listing showed the seller's price and a second, higher price marked "incl.", and the difference matched 5% plus £0.70. Depop says the exact amount is shown at checkout; we did not check how it appears in Depop's search results.

## The case for: the buyer pays, and gets something for it

Supporters of the model, including the platforms, argue that it is simply honest about who benefits. Buyers are the ones protected if a parcel goes missing or an item turns up broken, so buyers pay for that protection. Sellers of their own things keep the full price they asked for.

They say it made selling easier. Before these changes, private sellers had to work out a fee on every sale. Now a seller clearing out a wardrobe can price an item at £10 and receive £10. For casual sellers, that is a real simplification, and supporters say it encourages more people to list, which is good for everyone selling there.

They also argue that the fee is visible. eBay builds it into the price buyers see while browsing, and Vinted's catalogue shows a price including the fee next to the seller's price. Buyers can compare like with like, and anyone who does not want to pay it can buy elsewhere.

Finally, supporters point out that sellers have not lost anything they had before. Private eBay sellers used to pay final value fees; Depop sellers used to pay a 10% selling fee, which Depop removed for new UK listings from March 2024 ([Depop](https://news.depop.com/company-news/evolving-our-fee-structure-with-zero-selling-fees-on-depop/)). Whatever pressure the buyer fee puts on prices, it replaced a fee that came straight out of the seller's pocket.

## The case against: the seller pays in the end

Many resellers argue that the label on the fee does not decide who pays it. Buyers think in totals. If a buyer will spend no more than £12 on a jumper, and the fee is 5% plus £0.70, the seller has to price at about £10.75 or less to make the sale. In their words: the money still comes out of our side, it is just harder to see.

They say the effect is worst at the bottom. On a £3 item, a fixed part of £0.70 or £1 is a large share of the price, so cheap items look expensive and buyers bargain harder. That pushes sellers towards bundling or away from low-value stock altogether.

They also question the protection itself. Buyers pay the fee on every order, but most orders go smoothly. When something does go wrong, sellers say, the refund often comes out of the seller's item, not the platform's fee income. From that point of view, the fee looks less like insurance and more like the platform's main revenue under a friendlier name. Vinted's own change of name, from Buyer Protection fee to Vinted fee, and its description of what the fee pays for, is taken by some as support for that view.

Finally, critics say the comparison with business sellers is lopsided. On eBay, a business seller's listing shows no buyer fee, so a private seller's item can look more expensive than an identical business listing even when the private seller's price is lower.

## What the rules and the law say

**Drip pricing.** Since 6 April 2025, the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 has banned drip pricing. The Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) summarises the rule plainly: "It's illegal to hide additional fees, taxes or other charges that the customer will have to pay until later in the purchase process", and a charge is mandatory "if the customer must pay it to buy the product" ([CMA](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/price-transparency-cma209/providing-clear-and-accurate-information-about-prices-summary)). Where a fee cannot be worked out in advance, there must be enough information alongside the headline price for customers to work out the total. The CMA can fine traders up to 10% of turnover or £300,000, whichever is greater. Its full guidance is in [CMA209](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/price-transparency-cma209).

We have not seen any CMA statement or decision about the buyer fees on eBay, Vinted or Depop, and it is not for us to say whether any platform's display complies. How the fee is shown is a question for the platforms and, if it came to it, the regulator.

**Your side as a seller.** The platforms set their fees and can change them. If you sell as a business, you pay seller fees instead, and consumer law gives your buyers more rights. Our guide to [pricing from sold comps](/guides/pricing-from-sold-comps) explains how to check what buyers actually pay.

## Questions to think about

- When you price an item, do you think about the fee the buyer will pay?
- Would you rather pay a selling fee yourself and have buyers see only your price?
- Does the fee put buyers off cheap items, or do they not notice?
- Is a fixed part of up to £1 fair on low-value items?
- Do buyer fees give private sellers an edge or a handicap against business sellers?
