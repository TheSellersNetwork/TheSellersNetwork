This page explains the rules in plain words. It is not tax advice.

## The short version

- Since [1 January 2024](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform), online marketplaces collect details about their sellers and send HMRC a yearly report of what each seller sold.
- The first reports, covering 2024, went to HMRC [by 31 January 2025](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform). It now happens every January.
- You are not reported if you made fewer than 30 sales and received less than €2,000 (about £1,700) in the calendar year.
- Being reported [does not automatically mean you owe tax](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform). HMRC said plainly in December 2024 that [there are no tax changes](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/no-tax-changes-for-online-sellers).
- What matters is whether you are trading. That rule has not changed.

## Who it affects

The reporting covers anyone selling goods or services through a digital platform. The tax question only matters for some of them. This table sums up where you probably sit, using HMRC's own guidance.

| Which one are you | Reported to HMRC? | Tax to think about |
|---|---|---|
| Clearing out your own clothes, toys or furniture, under 30 sales and under €2,000 | No | [None in most cases](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-tell-hmrc-about-your-income-from-online-platforms) |
| Clearing out your own things, 30 or more sales or €2,000 or more | Yes | Usually none. Selling your own possessions is not trading |
| Selling a single personal item, or a set, for more than £6,000 | Depends on your totals | [Possibly Capital Gains Tax](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-tell-hmrc-about-your-income-from-online-platforms) |
| Buying stock to resell, or making things to sell, trading income £1,000 or under in the tax year | Depends on your totals | Covered by the [£1,000 trading allowance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-tell-hmrc-about-your-income-from-online-platforms) |
| Buying stock to resell, or making things to sell, trading income over £1,000 in the tax year | Depends on your totals | You need to tell HMRC through Self Assessment |

Note the last two rows. Your duty to tell HMRC comes from the trading rules, not from the platform report. A reseller with 20 sales worth £1,500 is not reported but still needs to tell HMRC. Someone clearing a loft with 40 sales of old clothes is reported but usually owes nothing.

### Selling your own things vs trading

HMRC's line is about intent. You are [probably trading](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-tell-hmrc-about-your-income-from-online-platforms) if you buy goods intending to sell them for a profit, or if you make items to sell, including hobby items. Buying charity shop stock to list on Vinted is trading. Selling the coat you wore for three winters is not.

HMRC's [side hustle guide](https://taxhelpforhustles.campaign.gov.uk/buying-or-making-things-to-sell-and-online-selling-tax-rules/) makes the same split, and adds that unwanted personal items do not need reporting unless a single item goes for over £6,000.

## What changed

| | Before 1 January 2024 | Since 1 January 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| What platforms tell HMRC about you | No routine yearly report | Name, address, date of birth and tax ID (National Insurance number for UK residents), plus [sales details](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform) |
| When | Not applicable | Each year by 31 January, covering the previous calendar year |
| Copy to you | No | [Platforms must give you a copy](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform) |
| Tax rules for sellers | Trading rules, £1,000 trading allowance | Exactly the same |

The change is about information, not tax. HMRC can now see what it could previously only ask for. Anyone who was already trading over £1,000 and not telling HMRC is now much more visible.

## What it means for your money

If you were already doing things properly, nothing changes. If you are trading over £1,000 and have not registered, the report makes it far more likely HMRC will notice.

One practical catch: the platform report runs on a [calendar year](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform) (1 January to 31 December), but your tax return uses the tax year (6 April to 5 April). The figure on your platform's report will not match your tax return, and it should not.

Example: your report for 2025 covers January to December 2025. Your 2025 to 2026 tax return covers 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026. To get the tax year figure, take your own sales records for 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026, not the platform's annual total.

## What to do now

1. Give your platform the details it asks for. They are required to collect them.
2. Keep the copy of the report your platform sends you, and check it against your own records.
3. Decide honestly whether you are trading. If you buy to resell or make things to sell and your trading income is over £1,000 in a tax year, [you need to tell HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-tell-hmrc-about-your-income-from-online-platforms).
4. If you sell both personal items and stock, keep them apart in your records so you can show which is which.
5. If you are unsure, use HMRC's [online platform income checker](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-tell-hmrc-about-your-income-from-online-platforms).

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|

| 31 January 2025 | First reports to HMRC, covering 2024 |
| 31 January each year | Reports for the previous calendar year |
