## The short version

- From 15 December 2025, FBA fulfilment fees for parcels in the UK fell by an [average of 26p a unit](https://www.aboutamazon.eu/news/empowering-small-business/update-to-european-referral-and-fulfilment-by-amazon-fees-for-2026).
- On the same date, Clothing and Accessories referral fees dropped from 8% to 5% on items up to £15, and from 15% to 10% on items between £15 and £20.
- From 1 February 2026, some fees went the other way: monthly storage, return-to-seller and liquidation fees rose on several tiers.
- A further set of cuts for Home, Pet, Grocery and Vitamins items and Low-Price FBA came in on 5 January 2026. That has [its own page](/blog/amazon-low-price-fba-and-referral-cuts-2026).
- For most UK sellers who turn stock quickly, this is a net saving. If you hold a lot of slow stock through the autumn, the storage rises eat into it.

## Who it affects

**In:** anyone using FBA on Amazon.co.uk with parcel-sized products. Anyone selling Clothing and Accessories priced at £20 or less, whether FBA or merchant fulfilled. Anyone storing stock in Amazon's warehouses, sending stock back, or liquidating it.

**Not in:** the 26p cut is for parcel-sized items; the announcement does not claim it for envelopes or oversize items. Clothing above £20 keeps its existing rate. The small net fee rise Amazon mentions applies to the Netherlands, Sweden, Belgium, Ireland and Poland stores, not the UK.

Amazon's framing is that across all its European stores the whole package lowers fees by about 15p a unit on average. The cuts go to fast-selling parcel items and cheap clothing. The rises land on stock that sits, stock you pull back and stock you give up on.

## What it means for your money

A T-shirt at £12 used to cost 96p in referral fee at 8%. At 5% it costs 60p, a saving of 36p a sale. A £18 hoodie used to cost £2.70 at 15%. At 10% it costs £1.80, a saving of 90p a sale.

On the storage side, if you hold 100 cubic feet of ordinary standard-size stock through October to December, you now pay £151 a month instead of £137, or £42 more across the quarter. That is before any aged inventory surcharge.

## What to do now

1. Check your fee preview or the revenue calculator on your best sellers. The 26p is an average across parcel tiers, so your own figure may be more or less.
2. If you sell clothing between £15 and £25, look at whether pricing at £20 or less, or £15 or less, now works out better after fees.
3. Plan the autumn stock run with the new Oct to Dec storage rate in mind. Send in what will sell before the new year, not six months of cover.
4. Clear slow stock before it needs removing. Return-to-seller and liquidation both cost more per unit now, so the cheapest exit is usually selling it while it is still worth something.
5. Update any profit spreadsheet that uses fixed fee figures from 2025.

## What we do not know yet

- Amazon gave the 26p as an average. The rate card shows the fee for each size and weight band, so check yours rather than assuming 26p.
- Amazon has changed its European fees every year recently, so 2027 will likely bring another round. Check the current rate card before relying on any figure here.
