## The short version

- From [13 December 2024](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/general-product-safety-regulations-northern-ireland/eu-regulation-2023988-on-general-product-safety-factsheet), EU Regulation 2023/988, the General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR), applies to consumer products sold into Northern Ireland and the EU.
- It covers second-hand goods sold by businesses, unless they are clearly sold as needing repair, or are antiques and collectables that nobody expects to meet modern safety standards.
- Online listings aimed at NI or EU buyers must show the manufacturer's name and contact details, product identification with a picture, and any safety warnings.
- There must be a responsible person established in Northern Ireland or the EU. A PO box does not count.
- If you only sell within England, Scotland and Wales, this does not apply to you. The older [General Product Safety Regulations 2005](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/general-product-safety-regulations-northern-ireland) still apply in Great Britain.

## Who it affects

**In:** business sellers who offer consumer products to buyers in Northern Ireland or the EU, including through eBay, Etsy, Vinted Pro or their own site. The EU text says an offer counts as aimed at a place if you direct your selling there by any means, such as offering delivery there. Simply having a website that can be seen there is not enough on its own ([Article 4 and recital 21](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R0988)).

**In, for resellers:** the regulation applies to second-hand, repaired and reconditioned products that go back on sale "in the course of a commercial activity" (recital 16). If you trade, your used stock is covered.

**Out:**
- Sales only to buyers in Great Britain. GOV.UK says the regulation does not apply to products placed on the GB market.
- Products explicitly sold as needing repair or reconditioning before use.
- Antiques, collectors' items and works of art that consumers cannot reasonably expect to meet current safety standards. GOV.UK notes a work of art does not have to be 100 years old to count.
- Food, medicines, living plants and animals and some other categories, which have their own rules.
- Products already on the market before 13 December 2024 that met the rules in force at the time.

This page is not legal advice. The rules are detailed and the official guidance is the place to check your own case.

## What changed

| | Before 13 December 2024 | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Law in Northern Ireland | General Product Safety Regulations 2005 | EU Regulation 2023/988 |
| Law in Great Britain | General Product Safety Regulations 2005 | No change |

Under the new regulation, for anything offered to NI or EU buyers:

- **Online listings** must clearly show the manufacturer's name and postal and email address; the responsible person's details if the manufacturer is outside the EU; information to identify the product, including a picture, its type and any other identifier; and any warnings or safety information, in a language buyers there can understand ([Article 19](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R0988)).
- **A responsible person** must be established in NI or the EU, meaning a registered office or permanent place of business there.
- **Safety incidents** must be reported to the authorities.

A reseller who buys and sells on is usually a **distributor** under the rules. GOV.UK's [detailed guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/general-product-safety-regulations-northern-ireland/eu-regulation-2023988-on-general-product-safety-detailed-guidance) says distributors must check that the manufacturer and importer have met their traceability and labelling duties before selling. If you are based in NI and bring goods in from GB, you are likely to be the **importer**, which carries more duties.

## What it means for your money

There is no fee, so no calculator. The cost is time and, for some, lost sales. Every listing offered to NI or EU buyers needs manufacturer details and a responsible person. For used goods bought without packaging, finding the manufacturer's postal and email address can take longer than the sale is worth. A GB seller with no presence in NI or the EU also needs someone established there to act as the responsible person.

For many small resellers the practical choice is between doing that work for NI and EU buyers, or not offering those destinations.

## What to do now

1. Decide whether you sell to Northern Ireland or EU buyers at all. If you do not, check your postage settings on each platform actually exclude them.
2. If you do, add manufacturer name, postal and email address, a clear picture and product identifier, and any safety warnings to those listings. Check your marketplace's help pages for where it wants this information.
3. Sort out a responsible person established in NI or the EU. The GOV.UK [factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/general-product-safety-regulations-northern-ireland/eu-regulation-2023988-on-general-product-safety-factsheet) explains who can fill the role.
4. For items sold as spares or repairs, say so clearly and explicitly in the listing, as that is what takes them out of scope.
5. Keep records of where you bought stock, so you can trace a product if there is a safety problem.

## What we do not know yet

How strictly the rules are enforced against small second-hand sellers is not set out in the guidance, and each marketplace decides how it collects the information. The GOV.UK guidance has been updated since launch, most recently on 4 November 2025, and may change again. Check the current GOV.UK pages before relying on this.
