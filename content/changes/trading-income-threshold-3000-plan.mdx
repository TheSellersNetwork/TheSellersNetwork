This page explains a government plan in plain words. It is not tax advice.

## The short version

- In March 2025 the government [announced a plan](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/boost-for-side-hustlers-as-300000-people-to-be-taken-out-of-tax-returns-government-announces) to raise the Self Assessment reporting threshold for trading income from £1,000 to £3,000 gross, "within this parliament".
- It is about filing a tax return, not about tax. The government says [people's tax liability will not change](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/summary-of-tax-update-spring-2025-simplification-administration-and-reform/tax-update-spring-2025-simplification-administration-and-reform-summary).
- There is no start date. Until the law changes, the current rule stands: if your trading income is over £1,000, you need to tell HMRC.
- People under the new threshold who still have tax to pay would report through a new, simpler online service instead of a full return.
- Mostly good news for small side sellers, but nothing to act on yet.

## Who it affects

In: people with trading income, such as buying stock to resell or making things to sell, whose gross takings are between £1,000 and £3,000 a year. The government estimates [up to 300,000 people](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/boost-for-side-hustlers-as-300000-people-to-be-taken-out-of-tax-returns-government-announces) would no longer need to file a return, around 90,000 of whom have no tax to pay.

Not in:

- People only selling their own unwanted things. That is not trading income, so neither threshold applies to them.
- Sellers with trading income over £3,000. You would still file a Self Assessment return as now.
- Anyone who needs a return for another reason, for example other untaxed income. The £3,000 figure is only about when trading income on its own triggers a return.

## What changed

Nothing has changed in law yet. This is the plan compared with today.

| | Today | Under the plan |
|---|---|---|
| Trading income before you must file a return | Over [£1,000 before expenses](https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/who-must-send-a-tax-return) | Over £3,000 gross |
| Trading allowance (tax-free) | [£1,000](https://www.gov.uk/income-tax/tax-free-and-trading-allowances) | Not changed by this announcement |
| Property and "other taxable" income thresholds | Separate rules | [Aligned at £3,000 gross each](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/summary-of-tax-update-spring-2025-simplification-administration-and-reform/tax-update-spring-2025-simplification-administration-and-reform-summary) |
| How to report tax due below the threshold | Self Assessment return | A new digital reporting service, or stay in Self Assessment if you prefer |
| Start date | In force | "Within this parliament", no date set |

Two separate numbers get mixed up here. The £1,000 trading allowance is the amount you can earn tax-free. The £3,000 figure is a reporting threshold: the point at which you would have to file a full return. The plan moves the reporting threshold. The announcement does not say the tax-free allowance is going up, so earning £2,500 would not suddenly become tax-free.

HMRC's [side hustle guide](https://taxhelpforhustles.campaign.gov.uk/buying-or-making-things-to-sell-and-online-selling-tax-rules/) says the new online reporting tool is on the way by 2029. The [transformation roadmap](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/hmrc-transformation-roadmap/hmrcs-transformation-roadmap) from July 2025 repeats the plan but gives no start date.

## What it means for your money

For now, nothing. Say you buy stock and your gross sales in the tax year are £2,400. Today that is over £1,000, so you [must register for Self Assessment by 5 October](https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/who-must-send-a-tax-return) after the tax year ends and file a return.

If the plan becomes law, the same seller would not need a full return. If they still had tax to pay on their profit, they would report it through the new service. The tax itself would be worked out the same way as today.

## What to do now

1. Keep following the current rule. If your trading income goes over £1,000 in a tax year (6 April to 5 April), register and file.
2. Keep records of sales and costs whatever your turnover. You will need them under either system.
3. If you are only clearing out your own things, you do not need to act on this plan. See HMRC's [online selling guide](https://taxhelpforhustles.campaign.gov.uk/buying-or-making-things-to-sell-and-online-selling-tax-rules/) for the difference between selling your own things and trading.
4. Watch for HMRC announcing a start date before you change what you do.

## What we do not know yet

- The start date, and which tax year it would first apply to. We could not find legislation or a confirmed date on GOV.UK as of late September 2026.
- How the new reporting service will work, and whether it will need a separate sign-up.
- Whether the date by which you must tell HMRC will change for people under £3,000.

Check the current GOV.UK pages before relying on this.
