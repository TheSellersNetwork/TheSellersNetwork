This page explains the rules in plain words. It is not tax advice. If your situation is unusual, check the GOV.UK pages linked below or speak to a qualified adviser.

## The short version

- Since 6 April 2026, sole traders and landlords whose [qualifying income was over £50,000 in the 2024 to 2025 tax year](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-and-when-you-need-to-use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax) must use Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax.
- Qualifying income is [your income before expenses, also called turnover](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/work-out-your-qualifying-income-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax), not your profit. For resellers this catches far more people than the headline suggests.
- You keep digital records, use compatible software, and send HMRC a summary every quarter, then still do a tax return after the year ends.
- The threshold falls to [£30,000 from April 2027 and £20,000 from April 2028](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-and-when-you-need-to-use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax).
- From September 2026, [HMRC is signing people up](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/making-tax-digital-for-income-tax) who should be in for 2026 to 2027 but have not joined.

## Who it affects

You are in scope if you are a sole trader, a landlord, or both, and your combined self-employment and property income before expenses is over the threshold for the relevant year. HMRC uses the figures on the tax return you sent for the earlier year.

For resellers, the important word is "before". If you sold £52,000 of stock on eBay and Vinted in 2024 to 2025 but made £9,000 profit after buying stock and paying fees, your qualifying income is £52,000. You are in.

Who is not in:

- Anyone whose qualifying income is [£20,000 or less, plus partnerships and some other groups](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-you-can-get-an-exemption-from-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax), who are exempt automatically.
- People who cannot reasonably use software because of age, health, disability, religious beliefs or lack of internet access. They [can apply for an exemption](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-you-can-get-an-exemption-from-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax); it is not automatic.
- People who sell their own unwanted things and are not trading. They do not have trading income to count.

## What changed

| | Before 6 April 2026 | Now, if you are in scope |
|---|---|---|
| Records | Any format, paper allowed | Digital records of income and expenses |
| Software | Not required | [Compatible software](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-software-thats-compatible-with-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax) |
| Reporting during the year | Nothing | Four [quarterly updates](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax/send-quarterly-updates) |
| End of year | Self Assessment return | Tax return through your software, after the quarterly updates |

The phased start, from GOV.UK's [timing page](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-and-when-you-need-to-use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax):

| Qualifying income | Based on the tax year | You must use MTD from |
|---|---|---|
| Over £50,000 | 2024 to 2025 | 6 April 2026 |
| Over £30,000 | 2025 to 2026 | 6 April 2027 |
| Over £20,000 | 2026 to 2027 | 6 April 2028 |

A quarterly update is not a tax bill. It is [totals for each income and expense category](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax/send-quarterly-updates). HMRC does not see your individual receipts or invoices.

Quarterly deadlines for the standard periods (the updates are cumulative from 6 April):

| Period covered | Deadline |
|---|---|
| 6 April to 5 July | 7 August |
| 6 April to 5 October | 7 November |
| 6 April to 5 January | 7 February |
| 6 April to 5 April | 7 May |

If you choose calendar quarters (1 April to 30 June and so on), [the deadlines are the same dates](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax/send-quarterly-updates).

## What it means for your money

MTD does not change how much tax you pay. It changes how and how often you report. The money side is mostly time and software.

Penalties are points-based. HMRC's [penalties page](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/penalties-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax) says you get a point for each missed quarterly update or tax return deadline, and at 4 points you get a £200 penalty, then another £200 for each further missed deadline. For the 2026 to 2027 tax year, [no penalty points are given for late quarterly updates](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax/send-quarterly-updates), but you still have to send all of them before you can submit your tax return, and late tax returns can still be penalised.

A worked example using GOV.UK's thresholds:

- A seller who turned over £34,000 in 2025 to 2026 and made £8,000 profit is over £30,000, so they must use MTD from 6 April 2027.
- A seller who turned over £18,000 in 2026 to 2027 is under £20,000 and stays out, as long as turnover stays at that level.

There is also a new system for late payment penalties. The details are on the [penalties page](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/penalties-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax); check it before relying on any figure.

## What to do now

1. Add up your turnover (all sales before costs) from your last tax return, plus any rental income before expenses. Compare it with the table above.
2. If you were over £50,000 in 2024 to 2025 and have not signed up, read [what to do if HMRC has signed you up](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-what-to-do-if-hmrc-has-signed-you-up-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax). HMRC will contact you through your online account or by post.
3. Choose software from HMRC's [compatible software list](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-software-thats-compatible-with-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax). The list shows which products cover which features.
4. Catch up any quarterly updates you have missed for 2026 to 2027. The 7 August deadline has passed and 7 November is next.
5. Get your records digital now, even if you are under the threshold. Marketplace sales reports and bank exports make this much easier to keep going than to rebuild later.
6. If you cannot use software for a good reason, [apply for an exemption](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-you-can-get-an-exemption-from-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax) rather than ignoring it.

## Key dates

| Date | What happens |
|---|---|
| 6 April 2026 | MTD starts for qualifying income over £50,000 |
| 7 August 2026 | First quarterly update deadline |
| September 2026 | HMRC starts signing up people who should be in for 2026 to 2027 |
| 7 November 2026 | Second quarterly update deadline |
| 7 February 2027 | Third quarterly update deadline |
| 6 April 2027 | Threshold drops to £30,000 |
| 7 May 2027 | Fourth quarterly update deadline |
| 6 April 2028 | Threshold drops to £20,000 |

## What we do not know yet

- Whether penalty points for late quarterly updates will be switched on as planned from the 2027 to 2028 tax year. GOV.UK says the easement covers 2026 to 2027 only; check the [penalties page](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/penalties-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax) before relying on this.
- How quickly HMRC will work through the September 2026 sign-ups, and when each person will hear. Check your HMRC online account and post.
