## The short version

- UK private sellers pay [no final value fee and no regulatory operating fee](https://www.ebayinc.com/stories/news/ebay-uk-announces-it-is-now-free-to-sell-across-its-categories/) when an item sells.
- Motors (Cars, Motorcycles and Vehicles) are excluded. Property and classified ads have their own fee rules.
- You get [300 free listings a month](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-private-sellers-activated-managed-payments?id=4822). After that each listing costs 35p.
- Sending an item outside the UK costs a [3% fee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-private-sellers-activated-managed-payments?id=4822) on the total sale.
- None of this applies to business accounts. If you buy to resell, you are probably trading, and that matters for eBay and for HMRC.

Very good news for people clearing out their homes. For resellers, the real question is whether you should be on a private account at all.

## Who it affects

In: sellers based in the UK with a **private** eBay account.

Out:

- Business sellers. The removal [does not apply](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-private-sellers-activated-managed-payments?id=4822) to business accounts.
- Sellers living outside the UK, who still pay final value fees.
- Motors listings.

### Private or business: where the line is

eBay's fee pages treat private and business sellers differently but do not spell out a test. What eBay has said is that it will [watch trading activity](https://community.ebay.co.uk/t5/Announcements/Changes-to-selling-fees-for-private-sellers-amp-new-benefits-for/ba-p/7684924/) and help business sellers using a private account to switch, or restrict their selling where needed.

HMRC's line is clearer. [Selling your own unwanted belongings](https://taxhelpforhustles.campaign.gov.uk/buying-or-making-things-to-sell-and-online-selling-tax-rules) from time to time is not usually trading. Buying things in order to sell them on, such as bundles, car boot finds or clearance stock, is. If your trading income is over the [£1,000 trading allowance](https://taxhelpforhustles.campaign.gov.uk/buying-or-making-things-to-sell-and-online-selling-tax-rules) in a tax year, you need to register for Self Assessment. The allowance is a single one across all your side income, and it is [measured on gross income](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tax-free-allowances-on-property-and-trading-income), before expenses, not on profit.

Platforms also now [report sellers to HMRC](https://taxhelpforhustles.campaign.gov.uk/buying-or-making-things-to-sell-and-online-selling-tax-rules) who sell 30 or more items a year. Being reported does not mean you owe tax, but HMRC will see your sales.

A free private account does not change your tax position. This is not tax advice. If you are unsure, read the GOV.UK guidance or speak to a qualified adviser.

## What changed

| | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Final value fee (private, most categories) | Charged | [None](https://www.ebayinc.com/stories/news/ebay-uk-announces-it-is-now-free-to-sell-across-its-categories/) |
| Regulatory operating fee (private) | Charged | [None](https://www.ebayinc.com/stories/news/ebay-uk-announces-it-is-now-free-to-sell-across-its-categories/) |
| Motors | Charged | Still charged |
| Free listings a month | | [300](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-private-sellers-activated-managed-payments?id=4822), or 400 with an eBay Shop |
| Each extra listing | | [35p](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-private-sellers-activated-managed-payments?id=4822) |
| Items sent outside the UK | | [3% of the total sale](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-private-sellers-activated-managed-payments?id=4822) |

Optional upgrades such as subtitles and Gallery Plus, and promoted listings, still cost money. The [private seller fees page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-private-sellers-activated-managed-payments?id=4822) has the current prices.

**Why two dates?** eBay's [press release](https://www.ebayinc.com/stories/news/ebay-uk-announces-it-is-now-free-to-sell-across-its-categories/) was published on 30 September 2024 and said the change was starting that day. eBay's [announcement to sellers](https://community.ebay.co.uk/t5/Announcements/Changes-to-selling-fees-for-private-sellers-amp-new-benefits-for/ba-p/7684924/) gave Tuesday 1 October 2024 as the date private sellers stopped paying transaction fees, and the help page carries a 1 October 2024 date. We use 1 October as the date it took effect.

## What it means for your money

For a private seller, a £30 item with £4 postage to a UK buyer now costs you nothing in eBay selling fees, unless you went over your free listings or paid for upgrades.

Send the same sale to a buyer abroad and the 3% international fee is worked out on the whole amount, including postage and taxes. On £34, that is about £1.02.

Buyers pay a separate [Buyer Protection fee](/blog/ebay-buyer-protection-private-sellers) on private sellers' items, which is added to the price they see. That does not come out of your money, but it does make your listing look dearer.

We have not added a calculator because the headline change is a fee going to zero.

## What to do now

1. Be honest about which kind of seller you are. If you buy to resell, a business account is likely the right one, whatever the fees.
2. Read the [GOV.UK guidance](https://taxhelpforhustles.campaign.gov.uk/buying-or-making-things-to-sell-and-online-selling-tax-rules) and check whether you are over the £1,000 trading allowance this tax year.
3. Keep records of what you buy and sell. Our guide on [bookkeeping and tax for resellers](/guides/bookkeeping-and-tax-for-resellers) covers the basics. Free selling does not change what eBay tells HMRC: see [how platform reporting works](/blog/hmrc-platform-reporting-rules).
4. If you are a genuine private seller, keep an eye on your monthly listing count so you do not pay for listings past 300.
5. Price overseas sales with the 3% fee in mind.
