## The short version

- From [12 February 2026](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/news/2026-january/rate-card-change), business sellers pay 40p per order on orders over £10, instead of 30p.
- Orders of £10 or less stay at 30p.
- Five category groups had their final value fee changed. Business, Office and Industrial went up. Vehicle Parts, Tyres, GPS and Power Tools got new price thresholds, which helps on expensive items and costs more on some others.
- Nothing to switch on. The new rates apply to new and existing listings automatically.
- Private sellers are not affected.

For a typical small business seller this is a small but real cost rise on every order over £10.

## Who it affects

In: sellers with a **business** account on ebay.co.uk. All eBay's figures are [exclusive of VAT](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/news/2026-january/rate-card-change), so if you are not VAT registered you pay VAT on top.

Out: private sellers, who pay no final value fee or per-order fee in most categories. See our page on [free selling for private sellers](/blog/ebay-free-selling-private-sellers).

The order value is the total amount of the sale. eBay's [business fees page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-business-sellers-activated-managed-payments?id=4809) says this includes the item price, postage and any taxes. So a £9 item with £3 postage is a £12 order and pays 40p.

## What changed

Per-order fee ([source](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/news/2026-january/rate-card-change)):

| Order total | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| £10 or less | 30p | 30p |
| Over £10 | 30p | 40p |

Some Collectables and Home, Furniture and DIY listings have a [10p per-order fee on orders up to £10](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-business-sellers-activated-managed-payments?id=4809). Check that page if you sell in those categories.

Category final value fees ([source](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/news/2026-january/rate-card-change)):

| Category | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Business, Office and Industrial | 11.9% | 12.5% |
| Vehicle Parts and Accessories | 8.9% | 9.5% up to £750, 3% above £750 |
| Tyres | 7.9% up to £250, 3% above £250 | 6.9% up to £750, 3% above £750 |
| GPS and Sat Nav, Power Tools and Equipment | 6.9% up to £400, 3% above £400 | 6.9% up to £750, 3% above £750 |

The thresholds are per item. Most other categories were not part of this change. The standard rate in most categories is shown on the [business fees page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/fees-business-sellers-activated-managed-payments?id=4809).

## What it means for your money

The per-order fee is simple: 10p more on every order over £10. If you send 300 such orders a month, that is £30 a month, before VAT.

The category changes cut both ways. Working from the figures above, on the part of the price the rates apply to:

- **Business, Office and Industrial, £100 sale:** £11.90 before, £12.50 after. 60p more.
- **Vehicle Parts, £100 part:** £8.90 before, £9.50 after. 60p more.
- **Vehicle Parts, £1,000 part:** £89.00 before. After, 9.5% of £750 plus 3% of £250 is £78.75. About £10 less.
- **Tyres, £300 set:** before, 7.9% of £250 plus 3% of £50 is £21.25. After, 6.9% of £300 is £20.70. Slightly less.
- **Power Tools, £600 item:** before, 6.9% of £400 plus 3% of £200 is £33.60. After, 6.9% of £600 is £41.40. £7.80 more.

So cheap and mid-priced vehicle parts cost more, very expensive parts and most tyres cost less, and GPS and power tools between £400 and £750 cost noticeably more.

## What to do now

1. Check your most recent invoice to see the 40p fee is being charged as expected.
2. Look at items that sell for just over £10 including postage. A 10p rise matters most on low prices.
3. If you sell in the five category groups above, rework your margins using the new rates, especially power tools and sat navs priced between £400 and £750.
4. If you sell expensive vehicle parts or tyres, check whether the lower fee lets you price more sharply.
5. Remember VAT. If you are not VAT registered, the fees you actually pay are higher than the headline figures.
