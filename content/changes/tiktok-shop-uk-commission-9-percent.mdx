## The short version

- TikTok Shop UK's standard commission is now [9%, including VAT](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=7753824408913665&default_language=en-GB). It was 5% before the rise.
- It is charged on the order total, which includes the shipping the buyer pays and any discount TikTok funds, not just the item price.
- Products in certain Electronics and Beauty and Personal Care categories get an automatic reduction to an effective [5%](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=3337893683398432&lang=en-GB), the old rate.
- New sellers, and sellers who complete TikTok's Seller Missions, can get a lower rate for a time.
- For most resellers selling clothing, collectables or homeware, this nearly doubled the platform fee on every sale.

## Who it affects

**In:** sellers based in the UK and selling through TikTok Shop UK, in every category that does not qualify for the category reduction.

**Partly out:** products in the specified Electronics and Beauty and Personal Care categories, which TikTok says stay at an effective 5%. The exact list is in a spreadsheet on the [commission rates by category page](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=3315312175236897&default_language=en-GB). It depends on how the product is categorised: if TikTok recategorises a product out of an eligible category, the reduction goes.

**Temporarily out:** new sellers during their reduced commission period. TikTok's pages say this exists but do not give the rate or length publicly. Check your Seller Centre for your own figure.

## What changed

| | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard commission, including VAT | 5% | 9% |
| Specified Electronics and Beauty and Personal Care categories | 5% | Effective 5% (9% with a 4% reduction) |
| What it is charged on | Not shown on TikTok's current pages | Order total, including buyer-paid shipping and platform discounts, minus refunds |

TikTok's [commission policy](https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=3337893683398432&lang=en-GB) describes the category reduction as keeping those sales at the previous 5% rate. It also says only one reduction can apply to a product at a time, so category and mission reductions do not stack, and category reductions only start once a new seller's reduced period ends.

The fee is worked out as: (net sales, plus the shipping the customer paid, plus any platform discount) minus (refunded sales and refunded platform discount), multiplied by the commission rate. It comes straight out of your settlement. If a whole order is refunded, you get the full commission back. On a partial refund, you get back the commission on the refunded items only.

## What it means for your money

A round example using TikTok's formula. You sell an item for £20 and the buyer pays £5 shipping, with no discounts. The commission is worked out on £25.

- At 5%, on the same basis, the commission was £1.25.
- At 9%, it is £2.25.

That is £1 more on a £25 order. Because shipping is included, sellers who charge realistic postage pay commission on it too.

If TikTok funds a discount for the buyer, the discount is added back before the rate is applied. So a £20 item that the buyer gets for £15 because of a £5 TikTok discount is still charged commission on the full £20 plus shipping.

## What to do now

1. Check your recent statements in Seller Centre under Finance. The commission shows as "TikTok Shop commission fee" in the fees section of each statement.
2. Rework your prices using 9% of the item price plus shipping, not 9% of the item price alone.
3. Check your products are in the right category. If you sell anything that may fall in the reduced Electronics or Beauty and Personal Care categories, compare your listings against TikTok's category spreadsheet.
4. Look at the Seller Missions offered in your Seller Centre. Only take on a mission if the fee saving is worth the work of the videos or lives it asks for.
5. Compare your total cost per sale with your other channels. Our [multi-channel selling guide](/guides/multi-channel-selling) covers how to decide where each item goes.

## What we do not know yet

- **The exact start date on an official page.** TikTok's current pages confirm the 9% rate and describe 5% as the previous rate, but do not state when the change started. Trade press at the time reported 2 September 2024.
- **New seller rate.** TikTok does not publish the new seller rate or how long it lasts on its public pages.
- **Future changes.** TikTok says there is no set schedule for commission changes and it will tell sellers in writing. Check the current page before relying on this.
